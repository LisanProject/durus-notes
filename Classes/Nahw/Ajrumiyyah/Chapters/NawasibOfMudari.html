The Ten Particles That Cause Nasb

Al-Nawasib النَّوَاصِبُ

Text
اَلنَّوَاصِبُ عَشَرَةٌ، وَهِيَ: أَنْ، وَلَنْ، وَإِذَنْ، وَكَيْ، وَلَامُ كَيْ، وَلَامُ الجُحُودِ، وَحَتَّى، وَالجَوَابُ بِالفَاءِ، وَالوَاوِ، وَأَوْ.
Vocabulary
  • النَّوَاصِبthe particles that cause nasb (accusative/subjunctive)
  • عَشَرَةten
  • أَنْan (the verbal-noun particle)
  • لَنْlan (future negation)
  • إِذَنْidhan (a responsive particle)
  • كَيْkay (causation)
  • لَامُ كَيْlam kay (the lam of causation)
  • لَامُ الجُحُودlam al-juhud (the lam of denial)
  • حَتَّىhatta (until)
  • الجَوَابُ بِالفَاءthe response with fa
  • الوَاوwaw (in this construction)
  • أَوْaw (or)
Translation
The particles of nasb are ten: an, lan, idhan, kay, lam kay, lam al-juhud, hatta, jawab with fa, waw, and aw.

1. أَنْ   2. لَنْ   3. إِذَنْ   4. كَيْ   5. لَامُ كَيْ

6. لَامُ الجُحُودِ   7. حَتَّى   8. الجَوَابُ بِالفَاءِ   9. الوَاوُ   10. أَوْ

(1)

An al-Masdariyyah

Commentary

يُعْجِبُنِي أَنْ يَأْتِيَ زَيْدٌ, "it pleases me that Zayd comes," shows this "an" turning a verb into a verbal noun (masdar): "an ya'tiya Zaydun" is equivalent in meaning to "maji'u Zaydin," the coming of Zayd.

Not every "an" is this particle of nasb. أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ, "I bear witness that there is no god except Allah," uses a different "an" entirely, a lightened form of "inna," meaning "the matter that there is no god..." rather than introducing a verbal noun.

Lan

Commentary

لَنْ يَنَالَ اللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا, "never will their meat reach Allah," shows lan causing nasb, restricting the verb's time reference to the future, and negating the event.

A significant theological debate connects to this particle: the Mu'tazilah argued from لَنْ تَرَانِي, "you will never see Me" (addressed to Musa, peace be upon him), that Allah can never be seen, even in the Hereafter. This was refuted by pointing to the possibility of an exception granted specifically to the Prophet ﷺ during the Mi'raj, meaning lan restricting negation to the future does not by itself settle the broader question of ru'yah in the akhirah.

Idhan

Commentary

إِذَنْ أُكْرِمُكَ, "then I will honor you," only makes sense as a response to something already said, it could not open a conversation on its own. A live exchange shows this: if someone says they will visit you, the reply "idhan akrimuka" only works because it answers that statement.

Kay and Lam Kay

Commentary

كَيْ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةً بَيْنَ الأَغْنِيَاءِ مِنْكُمْ, "so that it not be a fortune circulating solely among the rich among you," shows kay giving the reason or causation for what follows. Lam kay is interchangeable with kay in certain verses, as in لِيَأْكُلُوا مِنْ ثَمَرِهِ, "so that they may eat from its fruit."
