The Ten Particles That Cause Nasb

The Ten Nawasib in Full

اَلنَّوَاصِبُ عَشَرَةٌ، وَهِيَ: أَنْ، وَلَنْ، وَإِذَنْ، وَكَيْ، وَلَامُ كَيْ، وَحَتَّى، وَلَامُ الجُحُودِ، وَالجَوَابُ بِالفَاءِ، وَالوَاوِ، وَأَوْ.
The particles of nasb are ten: an, lan, idhan, kay, lam kay, hatta, lam al-juhud, jawab with fa, waw, and aw.

Matn al-Ajrumiyyah, the ten nawasib.

1. أَنْ (an)

2. لَنْ (lan)

3. إِذَنْ (idhan)

4. كَيْ (kay)

5. لَامُ كَيْ (lam kay)

6. حَتَّى (hatta)

7. لَامُ الجُحُودِ (lam al-juhud)

8. الجَوَابُ بِالفَاءِ (jawab with fa)

9. الوَاوُ (waw)

10. أَوْ (aw)

(1)

An al-Masdariyyah: Turning a Verb into a Verbal Noun

يُعْجِبُنِي أَنْ يَأْتِيَ زَيْدٌ.
It pleases me that Zayd comes.

Worked example of an al-masdariyyah.

An al-Masdariyyah: this "an," together with the verb after it, produces a verbal noun (masdar). "An ya'tiya Zaydun" is equivalent in meaning to "maji'u Zaydin," the coming of Zayd.

(1)

An al-Masdariyyah vs. An al-Mukhaffafah

Not every "an" in Arabic is the particle of nasb. A common point of confusion is distinguishing it from a lightened form of "inna."

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ.
I bear witness that there is no god except Allah.

Worked example distinguishing an al-mukhaffafah.

In this phrase, "an" is not the particle of nasb governing a verb at all. It is a lightened form of "inna," functioning to mean "the matter that there is no god..." rather than introducing a verbal noun the way an al-masdariyyah does.

(1)

Lan: Negation Restricted to the Future

لَنْ يَنَالَ اللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا.
Never will their meat reach Allah.

Qur'anic example of lan.

Lan: causes nasb on the following present-tense verb, restricts its time reference to the future, and negates the event described.

(1)

A significant theological debate was cited in connection with this particle: the Mu'tazilah argued from the verse "lan tarani" (you will never see Me), addressed to Musa (peace be upon him), that this establishes Allah can never be seen, even in the Hereafter. This was refuted by pointing to the possibility of an exception granted specifically to the Prophet ﷺ during the Mi'raj, meaning "lan" restricting negation to the future does not by itself settle the broader question of ru'yah in the akhirah the way the Mu'tazilah claimed.

Idhan: A Response, Never an Opener

إِذاً أُكْرِمُكَ.
Then I will honor you.

Worked example of idhan.

Idhan: comes only as a response to something already said, never at the beginning of speech. It requires a preceding context to function at all.

(1)

A live exchange shows this in practice: if someone says they will visit you, the reply "idhan akrimuka" only makes sense as a response to that statement, it could not open the conversation on its own.

Kay and Lam Kay: Causation

كَيْ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةً بَيْنَ الأَغْنِيَاءِ مِنْكُمْ.
So that it not be a fortune circulating solely among the rich among you.

Qur'anic example of kay.

Kay: gives the reason or causation for what follows it.

(1)

Lam kay was noted as interchangeable with kay in certain verses, citing "li-ya'kulu min thamarihi" (so that they may eat from its fruit) as an example of the lam kay usage specifically.
