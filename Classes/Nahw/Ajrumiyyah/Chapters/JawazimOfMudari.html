The Eighteen Particles That Cause Jazm

The Eighteen Jawazim in Full

الجَوَازِمُ ثَمَانِيَةَ عَشَرَ: لَمْ، وَلَمَّا، وَأَلَمْ، وَأَلَمَّا، وَلَامُ الأَمْرِ وَالدُّعَاءِ، وَلَا فِي النَّهْيِ وَالدُّعَاءِ، وَإِنْ، وَمَنْ، وَمَا، وَمَهْمَا، وَإِذْمَا، وَأَيٌّ، وَمَتَى، وَأَيْنَ، وَأَيَّانَ، وَأَنَّى، وَحَيْثُمَا، وَكَيْفَمَا، وَإِذَا فِي الشِّعْرِ خَاصَّةً.
The jazm particles are eighteen: lam, lamma, alam, alamma, the lam of command and supplication, la in prohibition and supplication, in, man, ma, mahma, idhma, ayy, mata, ayna, ayyan, anna, haythuma, kayfama, and idha, in poetry specifically.

Matn al-Ajrumiyyah, Bab al-Jawazim.

1. لَمْ (lam)

2. لَمَّا (lamma)

3. أَلَمْ (alam)

4. أَلَمَّا (alamma)

5. لَامُ الأَمْرِ وَالدُّعَاءِ (lam of command and supplication)

6. لَا فِي النَّهْيِ وَالدُّعَاءِ (la in prohibition and supplication)

7. إِنْ (in)

8. مَنْ (man)

9. مَا (ma)

10. مَهْمَا (mahma)

11. إِذْمَا (idhma)

12. أَيٌّ (ayy)

13. مَتَى (mata)

14. أَيْنَ (ayna)

15. أَيَّانَ (ayyan)

16. أَنَّى (anna)

17. حَيْثُمَا (haythuma)

18. كَيْفَمَا (kayfama), and idha, in poetry only

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullahi flagged from the outset that understanding these particles is essential for correctly determining i'rab in mudari' verbs going forward, since they are exactly what moves a verb out of its default raf'. (See: The Three Verb Types and Their Base Rulings)

Lam: Negation and Qalb, Flipping Time to the Past

لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ.
He has not begotten, nor was He begotten.

Qur'anic example of lam, Surat al-Ikhlas.

Lam: a particle of jazm, negation, and qalb (flipping). Its entry flips a present or future-oriented verb's meaning into a genuine past negation. In this example the negation is absolute, unbound by time at all, since it describes an attribute of Allah rather than an ordinary event. A distinction was drawn here between negation in everyday examples and this kind of absolute negation used for divine attributes specifically.

(1)

The use of "lam" negates an event only up to the moment of speech. It does not negate the event's future occurrence.

(2)

A student asked whether it is a mistake to say "lam akul" (I have not eaten).

No: a present-tense verb negated with "lam" has its negation confined to the past. It does not negate eating in the present or future at all.

(3)

Lamma: Negation Up to Now, With Future Possibility

وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ.
And faith has not yet entered your hearts.

Qur'anic example of lamma.

Lamma: a particle of jazm giving negation that holds up to the present moment, while leaving open the possibility the event will still occur later.

(1)

The contrast with lam is the whole point: lam denies an event happened, full stop; lamma denies it has happened yet, while keeping the door open.

Alam: The Rhetorical Question

أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ.
Did We not expand your breast for you?

Qur'anic example of alam, Surat al-Sharh.

Alam: the hamzah of istifham (interrogation) joined with lam, most often used for rhetorical questioning (istifham balaghi) or affirmation (taqrir) rather than a genuine request for information.

(1)

The hamzah of istifham can step outside its original meaning of asking a question, serving instead to affirm or to remind the listener of a favor already granted, as in this verse, rather than seeking new understanding from the one being addressed.

One Verb or Two: How the Jawazim Divide

The jazm particles are not all the same in scope. Some make only a single verb jussive. Others make two jussive at once, the fi'l al-shart (the condition) and its jawab (the response to that condition).

(1)
إِنْ تُذَاكِرْ تَنْجَحْ.
If you study, you will succeed.

Worked example of a two-verb jazm particle.

Full parse: "in," a jazm particle introducing a condition; "tudhakir," a present-tense verb made jussive by "in"; "tanjah," the jawab al-shart, also jussive.

وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنْفُسِكُمْ مِنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ.
And whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will find it with Allah.

Qur'anic example of a two-verb jazm particle.

The Signs of Jazm

1. Hadhf al-nun (deletion of the nun), in the five verbs.

2. Hadhf harf al-'illah (deletion of the weak letter), in a verb whose final letter is weak.

(1)

Real Qur'anic examples were used to point out exactly where the weak letter or the nun drops in practice, treating the signs of jazm as something to actually locate in a real text rather than a rule to memorize in the abstract.

Darurat al-Shi'r: An Exception Reserved for Poetry

تَعَلَّمِي.
Learn (addressed to a female, in poetry).

A poetic example of darurat al-shi'r.

Darurat al-Shi'r: a grammatical license permitted within a line of poetry, forming an exception to the standard rules.

(1)

A kasrah can appear on an otherwise jussive verb in poetry, here to avoid two adjacent sukun letters or simply to fit the meter, with the sukun still counted as present in an estimated (muqaddar) sense underneath it. This kind of license does not occur in prose or in the Qur'an, since the Qur'an is transcendent above any linguistic slip or artificial strain of this kind.
