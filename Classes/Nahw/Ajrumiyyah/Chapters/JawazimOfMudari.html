The Eighteen Particles That Cause Jazm

Al-Jawazim الجَوَازِمُ

Text
الجَوَازِمُ ثَمَانِيَةَ عَشَرَ: لَمْ، وَلَمَّا، وَأَلَمْ، وَأَلَمَّا، وَلَامُ الأَمْرِ وَالدُّعَاءِ، وَلَا فِي النَّهْيِ وَالدُّعَاءِ، وَإِنْ، وَمَنْ، وَمَا، وَمَهْمَا، وَإِذْمَا، وَأَيٌّ، وَمَتَى، وَأَيْنَ، وَأَيَّانَ، وَأَنَّى، وَحَيْثُمَا، وَكَيْفَمَا، وَإِذَا فِي الشِّعْرِ خَاصَّةً.

Translation
The jazm particles are eighteen: lam, lamma, alam, alamma, the lam of command and supplication, la in prohibition and supplication, in, man, ma, mahma, idhma, ayy, mata, ayna, ayyan, anna, haythuma, kayfama, and idha, in poetry specifically.

1. لَمْ   2. لَمَّا   3. أَلَمْ   4. أَلَمَّا   5. لَامُ الأَمْرِ وَالدُّعَاءِ

6. لَا فِي النَّهْيِ وَالدُّعَاءِ   7. إِنْ   8. مَنْ   9. مَا   10. مَهْمَا

11. إِذْمَا   12. أَيٌّ   13. مَتَى   14. أَيْنَ   15. أَيَّانَ

16. أَنَّى   17. حَيْثُمَا   18. كَيْفَمَا، وَإِذَا (فِي الشِّعْرِ)

(1)
Commentary

Understanding these particles is essential for correctly determining i'rab in mudari' verbs going forward, since they are exactly what moves a verb out of its default raf'. (See: The Three Verb Types and Their Base Rulings)

Lam, Lamma, and Alam

Commentary

لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ, "He has not begotten, nor was He begotten," shows lam as a particle of jazm, negation, and qalb (flipping): its entry flips a present or future-oriented verb's meaning into a genuine past negation. This negation is absolute here, unbound by time, since it describes an attribute of Allah rather than an ordinary event, a distinction drawn between negation in everyday examples and this kind of absolute negation used for divine attributes. Used elsewhere, lam only negates an event up to the moment of speech, it does not negate the event's future occurrence, so "lam akul" (I have not eaten) says nothing at all about eating in the present or future.

وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ, "and faith has not yet entered your hearts," shows the contrast with lam: lamma denies an event has happened yet, while leaving the door open for it to still occur later.

أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ, "did We not expand your breast for you," shows alam, the hamzah of istifham joined with lam, most often used for rhetorical questioning or affirmation rather than a genuine request for information. The hamzah of istifham can step outside its original meaning of asking a question, serving instead to affirm or to remind the listener of a favor already granted.

One Verb or Two

Commentary

The jazm particles are not all the same in scope. Some make only a single verb jussive. Others make two jussive at once, the fi'l al-shart (the condition) and its jawab (the response). إِنْ تُذَاكِرْ تَنْجَحْ, "if you study, you will succeed," parses as: إِنْ, a jazm particle introducing a condition; تُذَاكِرْ, a present-tense verb made jussive by إِنْ; تَنْجَحْ, the jawab al-shart, also jussive. وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنْفُسِكُمْ مِنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ, "and whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will find it with Allah," shows the same structure.

The Signs of Jazm

1. Hadhf al-nun: deletion of the nun, in the five verbs.

2. Hadhf harf al-'illah: deletion of the weak letter, in a verb whose final letter is weak.

(1)
Commentary

Real Qur'anic examples were used to point out exactly where the weak letter or the nun drops in practice, treating the signs of jazm as something to actually locate in a real text rather than a rule to memorize in the abstract.

Darurat al-Shi'r

Commentary

تَعَلَّمِي, "learn" (addressed to a female, in poetry), shows darurat al-shi'r, a grammatical license permitted within a line of poetry that forms an exception to the standard rules. A kasrah can appear on an otherwise jussive verb in poetry, here to avoid two adjacent sukun letters or simply to fit the meter, with the sukun still counted as present in an estimated (muqaddar) sense underneath it. This kind of license does not occur in prose or in the Qur'an, since the Qur'an is transcendent above any linguistic slip or artificial strain of this kind.
