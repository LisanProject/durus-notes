Hadith 6: The Halal Is Clear and the Haram Is Clear

Text

سَمِعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَقُولُ: إِنَّ الْحَلَالَ بَيِّنٌ، وَإِنَّ الْحَرَامَ بَيِّنٌ، وَبَيْنَهُمَا أُمُورٌ مُشْتَبِهَاتٌ لَا يَعْلَمُهُنَّ كَثِيرٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ، فَمَنِ اتَّقَى الشُّبُهَاتِ اسْتَبْرَأَ لِدِينِهِ وَعِرْضِهِ، وَمَنْ وَقَعَ فِي الشُّبُهَاتِ وَقَعَ فِي الْحَرَامِ، كَالرَّاعِي يَرْعَى حَوْلَ الْحِمَى يُوشِكُ أَنْ يَرْتَعَ فِيهِ، أَلَا وَإِنَّ لِكُلِّ مَلِكٍ حِمًى، أَلَا وَإِنَّ حِمَى اللَّهِ مَحَارِمُهُ، أَلَا وَإِنَّ فِي الْجَسَدِ مُضْغَةً إِذَا صَلَحَتْ صَلَحَ الْجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، وَإِذَا فَسَدَتْ فَسَدَ الْجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، أَلَا وَهِيَ الْقَلْبُ.
I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: the halal is clear and the haram is clear, and between them are doubtful matters that many people do not know. Whoever avoids the doubtful matters has protected his religion and his honor, and whoever falls into the doubtful matters falls into the haram, like a shepherd who grazes his flock around a protected sanctuary, likely at some point to graze within it. Truly every king has a sanctuary, and truly Allah's sanctuary is what He has prohibited. Truly in the body there is a morsel of flesh which, if sound, makes the whole body sound, and if corrupt, makes the whole body corrupt. Truly, it is the heart.

Narrated by Abu 'Abdillah al-Nu'man ibn Bashir; agreed upon by al-Bukhari and Muslim.

"Sami'tu Rasulallahi ﷺ" (I Heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ): Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin highlighted the weight of this opening phrase specifically. It signals the narrator heard the report directly with his own ears, one of the strongest possible forms of ittisal (connection). He noted that scholars would sometimes physically gesture to their ears when citing a hadith transmitted this way, a small physical act testifying to direct hearing rather than a weaker mode of transmission.

(1)

The Three-Part Map: Halal, Haram, and the Doubtful Zone

The hadith does not describe a spectrum with a fuzzy edge. It describes three distinct zones: clearly permitted, clearly forbidden, and a middle zone most people cannot confidently sort into either. The instruction is not to figure out which side the doubtful matters actually belong to. It is to avoid that zone altogether, precisely because most people lack the knowledge to sort it correctly.

The Hima: A Wise Soul Keeping Its Flock Away from the Edges

A hima was a protected tract of land a ruler would fence off, where grazing was forbidden to everyone but him. The Prophet ﷺ uses it as an image for Allah's prohibitions.

Hima (Sanctuary): a protected boundary. Here, a metaphor for the line around what Allah has made haram, one that grazing too close to inevitably breaches.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin expanded the image further in session: he likened a wise soul to a shepherd who deliberately keeps his flock away from the very edges of a king's reserved property, not merely avoiding the property itself, but keeping a margin of distance from it as a matter of course, never allowing the flock to test how close it can safely get.

Wara': Leaving Even Some Halal to Protect What Matters

This is where the hadith's practical instruction lands: al-wara' (scrupulousness). The Shaykh discussed this at length as one of the foundations the hadith establishes.

Wara': voluntarily leaving off even some permissible things, not because they are haram, but to keep clear distance from doubt and avoid spiritual stagnation.

(1)

Wara' Over Personal Judgment: Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin stressed that true spiritual growth requires actually practicing wara', not simply relying on one's own personal judgment about what is technically lawful. Knowing that something is technically permissible is not the same as it being spiritually safe to indulge in.

(2)

The Heart: Why the Hadith Closes Where It Does

The hadith ends by naming the heart (al-qalb) as the morsel of flesh whose soundness determines the soundness of the whole body. The Shaykh discussed the spiritual significance of this closing at length in session: the entire structure of the hadith, from clear halal and haram, through the doubtful zone, through the hima metaphor, is building toward this final point, that the outward avoidance of doubtful matters is ultimately in service of protecting the heart, since it is the heart's condition that determines whether the rest of a person's actions are sound.

Mustalah Note: Widely Narrated and Still Fully Sahih

This hadith is muttafaq 'alayh, agreed upon by both al-Bukhari and Muslim, and it meets every condition of sahih li-dhatihi discussed alongside it in this session, reinforced here by the narrator's own explicit "sami'tu" (I heard) opening. Unlike Hadith 1, which is famous despite being gharib at its origin, this hadith's fame and its technical soundness point the same direction. (See: Sahih li-Dhatihi and the Divisions of Accepted Hadith)
