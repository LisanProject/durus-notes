Hadith 5: Rejecting Innovations

Text
عَنْ أُمِّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أُمِّ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَائِشَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهَا قَالَتْ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ: مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ. رَوَاهُ الْبُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ. وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ لِمُسْلِمٍ: مَنْ عَمِلَ عَمَلًا لَيْسَ عَلَيْهِ أَمْرُنَا فَهُوَ رَدٌّ.
The Mother of the Believers, Umm 'Abdillah 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Whoever introduces into this affair of ours something that does not belong to it, it is rejected." Narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. In a narration of Muslim: "Whoever performs an act that our affair does not sanction, it is rejected."

Narrated by 'A'ishah; recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Why 'A'ishah Is Called Umm 'Abdullah

'A'ishah and the Prophet ﷺ had no children together, so her kunya, Umm 'Abdullah, is not drawn from a child of her own. It comes instead from her nephew, 'Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, the son of her sister Asma' bint Abi Bakr and al-Zubayr ibn al-'Awwam. She was especially close to him, and took the kunya in reference to him rather than to any child of her own.

The Chain of This Hadith Itself

This hadith is a useful live example of the classification terms covered under Review Lesson I. At its origin, it is narrated only by 'A'ishah from the Prophet ﷺ, making it gharib at that first generation. In later generations, the number of narrators widens considerably, to the point that its inclusion in both Bukhari and Muslim reflects the report's mashhur status among later scholars, even though it began as a solitary narration.

Defining Bid'ah

Technically, bid'ah refers to any innovation introduced into the religion that has no basis in the Qur'an or Sunnah. This technical sense is narrower than the ordinary linguistic sense of the word, which simply means anything newly begun or introduced, with no judgment attached to it either way. Bid'ah does not always carry a negative meaning; only that which "does not belong" to the religion, ma laysa minhu, meaning it lacks any real basis (asl) in revealed sources, is what the hadith is actually rejecting.

The Five Categories of Bid'ah

Drawing on the same five-fold division used throughout fiqh for ordinary actions, scholars including al-Shafi'i and later jurists such as al-Mudabbiri classified bid'ah into five legal categories, rather than treating it as a single uniform prohibition.

Wajib: an innovation that becomes obligatory because it is now necessary to preserve or transmit the religion correctly. Compiling and formalizing Arabic grammar is given as the example, since precise language became essential once native fluency in classical Arabic could no longer be assumed.

(1)

Mandub: a recommended innovation. Building madrasahs is the example given, a practice with no direct precedent in the earliest generation but broadly beneficial and encouraged.

(2)

Mubah: a permissible innovation, neither encouraged nor discouraged. Variety in food is the example given.

(3)

Makruh: a disliked innovation. Excessive decoration of mosques is the example given.

(4)

Muharram: a forbidden innovation, which is what this hadith is actually addressing. Introducing new theological positions with no basis in revelation is the example given.

(5)

Ibadat vs. Mu'amalat: Where Innovation Is and Isn't Allowed

A distinction closed out this discussion, and it does real work in explaining why the five categories above are not a contradiction of the hadith's apparent blanket rejection.

Ibadat (acts of worship): must strictly follow the Prophet's ﷺ own example. No new form of worship can be introduced here, regardless of how beneficial it might seem, since the form of worship itself is what this hadith is protecting.

(1)

Mu'amalat (civil dealings and contracts): allow for real innovation, so long as any new arrangement avoids explicitly prohibited conditions. New contract structures, new institutions, and new tools are not bid'ah in the sense this hadith addresses, since mu'amalat was never restricted to a fixed inherited form the way worship is.

(2)

This is why compiling Arabic grammar or building a madrasah can be wajib or mandub bid'ah without conflicting with the hadith at all, they belong to the domain of mu'amalat and supporting infrastructure, not to the form of worship itself. Introducing a new theological position or a new form of ritual worship falls squarely within ibadat, which is exactly the category this hadith is guarding.

Mashhur in Two Different Senses

A distinction raised while discussing this hadith's own transmission history is worth keeping in view generally: mashhur in the ordinary linguistic sense, meaning simply "well known among people," is not the same as mashhur in the technical mustalah sense, which specifically requires an actual chain of narration meeting the stated conditions. Ibn Hajar draws this distinction directly: a statement can become famous on people's tongues with no isnad behind it at all, while the technical term always presupposes a real sanad.
