Hadith 10: Allah Is Tayyib and Accepts Only What Is Tayyib

Text

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّباً، وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ المُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ المُرْسَلِينَ، فَقَالَ تَعَالَى: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ كُلُوا مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَاعْمَلُوا صَالِحاً﴾، وَقَالَ تَعَالَى: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ﴾»، ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ الرَّجُلَ يُطِيلُ السَّفَرَ أَشْعَثَ أَغْبَرَ، يَمُدُّ يَدَيْهِ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ: يَا رَبِّ، يَا رَبِّ، وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَغُذِيَ بِالحَرَامِ، فَأَنَّى يُسْتَجَابُ لَهُ؟ رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ.
On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Allah the Exalted is tayyib and accepts only what is tayyib. Allah has commanded the believers with what He commanded the messengers. He said: 'O messengers, eat from the tayyib things and do righteous deeds.' And He said: 'O you who believe, eat from the tayyib things We have provided you.'" Then he mentioned a man who travels at length, disheveled and dusty, stretching his hands toward the sky: "My Lord, my Lord," while his food is haram, his drink is haram, his clothing is haram, and he has been nourished by the haram. So how could he be answered? Narrated by Muslim.

Narrated by Abu Hurayrah and collected by Muslim. Hadith ten of Imam al-Nawawi's collection. The two verses are al-Mu'minun 23:51 and al-Baqarah 2:172.

Why Keep "Tayyib" in Arabic

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin recommended leaving tayyib untranslated, because no single English word carries its layers. It can mean pure or lawful, yet saying "Allah is lawful" would not express what is meant. In this hadith it also covers what is good, sound, righteous, kind, ethical, and agreeable to the Sharia. The theme is the believer's pursuit of the tayyib through an inwardly sound intention and righteous conduct, and the command to seek it applies to the believers just as it did to the messengers.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes keeping tayyib in Arabic in translation where possible, since one English equivalent loses its other meanings.

"Ya Rabbi": Riwayah and Dirayah Together

A student compared editions and asked whether the invocation is read with kasra because it means "my Lord." The Shaykh said the editions he consulted, including Dar al-Minhaj and Ibn Rajab's commentary, have the kasra. The original form carries the possessive ya:

يَا رَبِّيْ ← يَا رَبِّ

When that ya is dropped, the kasra remains and still means "my Lord." A nominative reading could sound like a general address, "O Lord." Since the traveler is calling on his own Lord, the kasra fits the context best.

He allowed that a different vocalization may have reached an editor through a chain, but stressed that riwayah and dirayah should be considered together. Grammar, morphology, syntax, figurative usage, and balagha can serve as corroborating considerations when scholars assess a hadith's wording. One should be cautious about accepting, as the Prophet's ﷺ speech, wording that would be extraordinarily weak in Arabic, without claiming that every unusual reading is unsupported.

The Traveler Who Has Every Cause, and One Obstacle

The man in the hadith displays several of the causes associated with dua being answered: he is traveling, he is dusty and disheveled, he raises his hands, and he calls on Allah again and again. The Prophet ﷺ deliberately sets these against a heavier obstacle: haram food, drink, clothing, and nourishment. The outward signs of devotion do not make up for unlawful provision or inner corruption.

Outward devotional practice is part of the Sunnah and deserves respect, but its deeper meanings must be understood and lived. A long beard, a short garment, long recitation, night prayer, or the miswak does not by itself prove that a person's inward state is sound. The Shaykh recalled serving as an imam in New York City and seeing people with conspicuous devotional appearance consume money belonging to the masjid, which he called a grave haram.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that outward devotional appearance does not compensate for unlawful earnings, unlawful consumption, or a corrupt intention.

The Inward Guides the Outward

The Sharia is holistic: inward and outward both matter, but the inward guides and validates the outward. Love of the Prophet ﷺ and submission to him are acts of the heart that lead a person to obey his outward commands. After the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, the Companions were disappointed not to complete the 'umrah as they had hoped, yet they obeyed his instruction to finish the rites and shave their heads, out of love and submission. This connects to the hadith of the heart: when it is sound, the whole is sound. The heart is the house of intention and sincerity, and the leader of the limbs.

Avoiding the haram comes before piling up voluntary acts built on unlawful earnings, show, pride, sectarianism, or mistreatment of Muslims. The Shaykh presented this priority as agreed upon by the scholars (compare Hadith 9). He criticized sectarian attacks and said Ahl al-Sunnah should not defend someone merely because he uses a Sunni label. Allah accepts only what is tayyib, and a sound intention is an obligation on everyone, the messengers and ordinary believers alike. Seeking knowledge, teaching, or pursuing leadership for display, money, or status corrupts the intention.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that this does not make outward acts unimportant. Both matter, but the heart's intention and submission lead the outward acts.

(1)

Benefits

  • Allah accepts only what is tayyib: pure, lawful, good, sound, and righteous.
  • The command to seek the tayyib applies to believers as it did to the messengers.
  • Haram food, drink, clothing, and nourishment are serious obstacles to dua being answered.
  • Outward signs of devotion do not make up for unlawful conduct or a corrupt intention.
  • Inward and outward practice both matter, and the inward guides and validates the outward.
  • Avoiding haram comes before multiplying voluntary acts while persisting in wrongdoing.
  • Sound intention is required in seeking and teaching knowledge and in taking positions of leadership.

From the Opening of Class: Harithah's Morning

The Shaykh opened with the account of Harithah, whom the Prophet ﷺ asked how he had woken that morning. Harithah replied that he had woken a true believer, describing how he had turned away from the dunya toward Allah and the akhirah, praying at night, fasting by day, and seeing the Throne and the people of Paradise and the Fire as if he witnessed them. The Shaykh drew from it a lesson in humility: do not make claims about your faith that you cannot live up to.

The Prophet's ﷺ reply is reported as "asabta" (you have got it right) and in another version "'arafta" (you have known), and then "falzam" (so hold to it). The knowledge meant is deeper than information: a lived knowledge engaging the soul, spirit, intellect, and senses, all directed toward the truth and to Allah. "Falzam" means remaining with one's Lord, keeping one's face, intentions, and self turned toward Him. Companionship with Allah and the Prophet ﷺ in this sense is not limited to those who lived in his generation. The class closed this reflection with a prayer that Allah fill their hearts with iman, and closed the session asking Allah to count their intentions, deeds, and outcomes among the tayyib.
