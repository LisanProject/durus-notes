Asahh al-Asanid: The Golden Chains and the Ranks of Sahih li-Dhatihi

The Logical Structure of the Sahih li-Dhatihi Definition

Ibn Hajar pauses on his own earlier definition of sahih li-dhatihi to explain its grammatical and logical structure word by word.

تَنْبِيهٌ: قَوْلُهُ: «وَخَبَرُ الآحَادِ»؛ كَالجِنْسِ، وَبَاقِي قُيُودِهِ كَالفَصْلِ. وَقَوْلُهُ: «بِنَقْلِ عَدْلٍ»؛ احْتِرَازٌ عَمَّا يَنْقُلُهُ غَيْرُ العَدْلِ.
A note: his saying "khabar al-ahad" functions like the genus, and the rest of its restrictions function like the differentia. And his saying "by the transmission of an upright narrator" is a precaution against what is transmitted by other than an upright person.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the logical structure of the sahih li-dhatihi definition.

This is the same jins/fasl (genus/differentia) logic used across the sciences to define a term: "khabar al-ahad" names the broad category, and every condition after it, 'adl, dabt, ittisal, freedom from 'illah and shudhudh, progressively narrows that broad category down to the specific case of sahih li-dhatihi. (See: The Conditions of Sahih li-Dhatihi Explained)

وَقَوْلُهُ: «هُوَ» يُسَمَّى فَصْلاً يَتَوَسَّطُ بَيْنَ المُبْتَدَإِ وَالخَبَرِ، يُؤْذِنُ بِأَنَّ مَا بَعْدَهُ خَبَرٌ عَمَّا قَبْلَهُ، وَلَيْسَ بِنَعْتٍ لَهُ. وَقَوْلُهُ: «لِذَاتِهِ»؛ يُخْرِجُ مَا يُسَمَّى صَحِيحاً بِأَمْرٍ خَارِجٍ عَنْهُ؛ كَمَا تَقَدَّمَ.
And his saying "huwa" ("it") is called a fasl, a separator sitting between the subject and predicate, signaling that what follows is a predicate about what came before, not a descriptive qualifier of it. And his saying "li-dhatihi" ("in itself") excludes whatever is called sahih due to something external to it, as preceded.

Ibn Hajar, on the grammatical role of "huwa" and the exclusionary function of "li-dhatihi."

This is why the phrase "li-dhatihi" is doing real work in the definition rather than being decorative: it is precisely what rules out sahih li-ghayrihi, which is sahih only because of an external corroborating factor, from being called sahih li-dhatihi. (See: Sahih li-Dhatihi and the Divisions of Accepted Hadith)

Why Sahih li-Dhatihi Comes in Ranks

وَتَتَفَاوَتُ رُتَبُهُ؛ أَيْ: بِسَبَبِ تَفَاوُتِ هَذِهِ الأَوْصَافِ المُقْتَضِيَةِ لِلتَّصْحِيحِ فِي القُوَّةِ؛ فَإِنَّهَا لَمَّا كَانَتْ مُفِيدَةً لِغَلَبَةِ الظَّنِّ الَّذِي عَلَيْهِ مَدَارُ الصِّحَّةِ؛ اقْتَضَتْ أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهَا دَرَجَاتٌ بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ بِحَسَبِ الأُمُورِ المُقَوِّيَةِ. وَإِذَا كَانَ كَذَلِكَ: فَمَا تَكُونُ رُوَاتُهُ فِي الدَّرَجَةِ العُلْيَا مِنَ العَدَالَةِ وَالضَّبْطِ وَسَائِرِ الصِّفَاتِ الَّتِي تُوجِبُ التَّرْجِيحَ؛ كَانَ أَصَحَّ مِمَّا دُونَهُ.
Its ranks vary, that is, due to the variance in strength of these qualities that necessitate authentication. Since they yield the preponderance of certainty upon which soundness turns, this necessitates that they have degrees, some above others, according to the strengthening factors present. And if that is so, then whatever report's narrators sit at the highest degree of 'adalah, dabt, and the other preference-warranting qualities is more sound than what falls below it.

Ibn Hajar, on why sahih hadith admit of ranking.

Sahih li-dhatihi is not a flat, binary label. Every condition it depends on, 'adalah and dabt above all, is itself a matter of degree, so two chains can both clear the bar for sahih while one still outranks the other based on how far above that bar its narrators sit.

The Golden Chains: Three Descending Tiers

Among the highest rank in this are chains some of the imams have applied the label "the soundest of chains" to directly.

فَمِنَ الرُّتْبَةِ العُلْيَا فِي ذَلِكَ: مَا أَطْلَقَ عَلَيْهِ بَعْضُ الأَئِمَّةِ أَنَّهُ أَصَحُّ الأَسَانِيدِ: كَالزُّهْرِيِّ، عَنْ سَالِمِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عُمَرَ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ. وَكَ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ سِيرِينَ، عَنْ عَبِيدَةَ بْنِ عَمْرٍو، عَنْ عَلِيٍّ. وَكَإِبْرَاهِيمَ النَّخَعِيِّ، عَنْ عَلْقَمَةَ، عَنِ ابْنِ مَسْعُودٍ.
Among the highest rank in that: what some of the imams applied the label "the soundest of chains" to, such as al-Zuhri, from Salim ibn 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar, from his father. And such as Muhammad ibn Sirin, from 'Ubaydah ibn 'Amr, from 'Ali. And such as Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, from 'Alqamah, from Ibn Mas'ud.

Ibn Hajar, the first tier of the golden chains.

وَدُونَهَا فِي الرُّتْبَةِ: كَرِوَايَةِ بُرَيْدِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ أَبِي بُرْدَةَ، عَنْ جَدِّهِ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ أَبِي مُوسَى. وَكَحَمَّادِ بْنِ سَلَمَةَ، عَنْ ثَابِتٍ، عَنْ أَنَسٍ.
Below that in rank: such as the narration of Buraid ibn 'Abdullah ibn Abi Burdah, from his grandfather, from his father Abu Musa. And such as Hammad ibn Salamah, from Thabit, from Anas.

Ibn Hajar, the second tier of the golden chains.

وَدُونَهَا فِي الرُّتْبَةِ: كَسُهَيْلِ بْنِ أَبِي صَالِحٍ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ، عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ. وَكَالعَلَاءِ بْنِ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ، عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ.
Below that in rank: such as Suhayl ibn Abi Salih, from his father, from Abu Hurayrah. And such as al-'Ala' ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, from his father, from Abu Hurayrah.

Ibn Hajar, the third tier of the golden chains.

Why the Ranks Differ

فَإِنَّ الجَمِيعَ يَشْمَلُهُمُ اسْمُ العَدَالَةِ وَالضَّبْطِ؛ إِلَّا أَنَّ المَرْتَبَةَ الأُولَى فِيهِمْ مِنَ الصِّفَاتِ المُرَجِّحَةِ مَا يَقْتَضِي تَقْدِيمَ رِوَايَتِهِمْ عَلَى الَّتِي تَلِيهَا، وَفِي الَّتِي تَلِيهَا مِنْ قُوَّةِ الضَّبْطِ مَا يَقْتَضِي تَقْدِيمَهَا عَلَى الثَّالِثَةِ، وَهِيَ مُقَدَّمَةٌ عَلَى رِوَايَةِ مَنْ يُعَدُّ مَا يَنْفَرِدُ بِهِ حَسَناً؛ كَمُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِسْحَاقَ، عَنْ عَاصِمِ بْنِ عُمَرَ، عَنْ جَابِرٍ، وَعَمْرِو بْنِ شُعَيْبٍ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ، عَنْ جَدِّهِ. وَقِسْ عَلَى هَذِهِ المَرَاتِبِ مَا يُشْبِهُهَا.
All of them are covered by the name 'adalah and dabt alike, except that the first rank among them has, of the preference-warranting qualities, what necessitates preferring their narration over the one following it, and the one following it has, of strength of dabt, what necessitates preferring it over the third, and that third rank is preferred over the narration of one whose solitary reports are only counted as hasan, such as Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from 'Asim ibn 'Umar, from Jabir, and 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, from his father, from his grandfather. And measure whatever resembles these against these same ranks.

Ibn Hajar, explaining the basis for the ranking between the tiers.

The key point: none of these narrators lack 'adalah or dabt, every tier here already qualifies as sahih. The ranking between tiers is a matter of degree within an already sound category, not a matter of some tiers being sound and others not. A fourth tier below all three, exemplified by Muhammad ibn Ishaq and 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb's chains, ranks lower still: their solitary narrations are only accepted at the level of hasan, one step below sahih.

A Caution Against Absolute Claims

وَالمَرْتَبَةُ الأُولَى: هِيَ الَّتِي أَطْلَقَ عَلَيْهَا بَعْضُ الأَئِمَّةِ أَنَّهَا أَصَحُّ الأَسَانِيدِ، وَالمُعْتَمَدُ عَدَمُ الإِطْلَاقِ لِتَرْجَمَةٍ مُعَيَّنَةٍ مِنْهَا.
The first rank is that which some of the imams applied the label "the soundest of chains" to, and the relied-upon position is not to apply this absolutely to any single specific chain-designation among them.

Ibn Hajar, the caution against declaring one single chain absolutely soundest.

Even having named three narrator-lines as the top tier, Ibn Hajar closes with a caution against going further and singling out just one of the three as uniquely "the" soundest chain in an absolute sense. The reliable position keeps all three in a single top tier rather than ranking a winner among them.

نَعَمْ؛ يُسْتَفَادُ مِنْ مَجْمُوعِ مَا أَطْلَقَ الأَئِمَّةُ عَلَيْهِ ذَلِكَ أَرْجَحِيَّتُهُ عَلَى مَا لَمْ يُطْلِقُوهُ.
Yes; what can be drawn from the sum total of what the imams applied this label to is its preponderance over whatever they did not apply it to.

Ibn Hajar, on what can still be salvaged from the imams' disagreement.

So the caution above isn't a full retreat from the label. Even without crowning one single chain as uniquely "the" soundest, the fact that several imams converged on naming these particular chains at all still tells you something real: collectively, this group of chains outranks whatever chains never earned that designation from anyone. The disagreement is only about picking a single winner among the top tier, not about whether the top tier itself is meaningfully stronger than everything else.
