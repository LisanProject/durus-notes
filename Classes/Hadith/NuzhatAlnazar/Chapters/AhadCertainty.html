Ahad and the Question of Certainty

Defining Ahad

وَكُلُّهَا أَيِ الْأَقْسَامِ الْأَرْبَعَةِ الْمَذْكُورَةِ سِوَى الْأَوَّلِ آحَادٌ، وَيُقَالُ لِكُلٍّ مِنْهَا: خَبَرُ وَاحِدٍ. وَخَبَرُ الوَاحِدِ فِي اللُّغَةِ: مَا يَرْوِيهِ شَخْصٌ وَاحِدٌ، وَفِي الِاصْطِلَاحِ: مَا لَمْ يَجْمَعْ شُرُوطَ التَّوَاتُرِ.
All of them, i.e., the four categories mentioned besides the first (mutawatir), are Ahad, and each of them is called khabar al-wahid, a solitary report. Khabar al-wahid linguistically is what a single person narrates. Technically, it is whatever does not gather the conditions of tawatur.

Its Division into Maqbul, Mardud, and Tawaqquf

وَفِيهَا أَيِ الْآحَادِ الْمَقْبُولُ: وَهُوَ مَا يَجِبُ الْعَمَلُ بِهِ عِنْدَ الجُمهُورِ. وَفِيهَا الْمَرْدُودُ وَهُوَ الَّذِي لَمْ يَرْجَحْ صِدْقُ الْمُخْبِرِ بِهِ؛ لِتَوَقُّفِ الِاسْتِدْلَالِ بِهَا عَلَى الْبَحْثِ عَنْ أَحْوَالِ رُوَاتِهَا، دُونَ الْأَوَّلِ، وَهُوَ الْمُتَوَاتِرُ؛ فَكُلُّهُ مَقْبُولٌ؛ لِإِفَادَتِهِ الْقَطْعَ بِصِدْقِ مُخْبِرِهِ، بِخِلَافِ غَيْرِهِ مِنْ أَخْبَارِ الْآحَادِ.
Among the Ahad is al-Maqbul, the Accepted, that which it is obligatory to act upon according to the majority. And among it is al-Mardud, the Rejected, that in which the truthfulness of the reporter has not been given preponderance, since relying on Ahad reports as proof depends on investigating the conditions of their narrators. This differs from the first category, mutawatir, all of which is accepted, since it yields certainty regarding the truthfulness of its reporter, unlike other Ahad reports.
لَكِنْ إِنَّمَا وَجَبَ الْعَمَلُ بِالْمَقْبُولِ مِنْهَا؛ لِأَنَّهَا إِمَّا أَنْ يُوجَدَ فِيهَا أَصْلُ صِفَةِ الْقَبُولِ، وَهُوَ ثُبُوتُ صِدْقِ النَّاقِلِ، أَوْ أَصْلُ صِفَةِ الرَّدِّ، وَهُوَ ثُبُوتُ كَذِبِ النَّاقِلِ، أَوْ لَا. فَالْأَوَّلُ يَغْلِبُ عَلَى الظَّنِّ لِثُبُوتِ صِدْقِ الْخَبَرِ؛ لِثُبُوتِ صِدْقِ نَاقِلِهِ؛ فَيُؤْخَذُ بِهِ. وَالثَّانِي: يَغْلِبُ عَلَى الظَّنِّ كَذِبُ الْخَبَرِ لِثُبُوتِ كَذِبِ نَاقِلِهِ؛ فَيُطْرَحُ. وَالثَّالِثُ: إِنْ وُجِدَتْ قَرِينَةٌ تُلْحِقُهُ بِأَحَدِ الْقِسْمَيْنِ الْتَحَقَ، وَإِلَّا فَيُتَوَقَّفُ فِيهِ، فَإِذَا تُوُقِّفَ عَنِ الْعَمَلِ بِهِ صَارَ كَالْمَرْدُودِ لَا لِثُبُوتِ صِفَةِ الرَّدِّ، بَلْ لِكَوْنِهِ لَمْ تُوجَدْ فِيهِ صِفَةٌ تُوجِبُ الْقَبُولَ، وَاللهُ أَعْلَمُ.
Acting upon the Accepted among them is obligatory only because such reports either contain the basic quality of Acceptance, the established truthfulness of the transmitter, or the basic quality of Rejection, the established falsehood of the transmitter, or neither. The first case: the predominant likelihood favors the truthfulness of the report, due to the established truthfulness of its transmitter, so it is acted upon. The second case: the predominant likelihood favors the falsehood of the report, due to the established falsehood of its transmitter, so it is discarded. The third case: if a corroborating indicator is found attaching the report to one of the two categories above, it is joined to that category. If none is found, judgment is withheld. A report left in that state is then treated like the Rejected in practice, not because the quality of rejection has actually been established in it, but because no quality has been found in it that necessitates acceptance. And Allah knows best.

Case One is where the predominant likelihood favors the truthfulness of the report, due to the established truthfulness of its transmitter. Such a report is acted upon.

Case Two is where the predominant likelihood favors the falsehood of the report, due to the established falsehood of its transmitter. Such a report is discarded.

Case Three is where neither is established. If a corroborating indicator (qarinah) is found attaching the report to one of the two categories above, it is joined to that category. If no qarinah is found, judgment is withheld (tawaqquf). A report in this state is then treated like the Rejected in practice, not because the quality of rejection has actually been established in it, but because no quality has been found in it that necessitates acceptance.

(1)

Footnote by Shaykh Nur al-Din 'Itr, on predominant likelihood (ghalabat al-zann). What is meant is that the report's truthfulness is established with a kind of knowledge whose establishment admits the possibility that the narrator erred, but this possibility is weak and is not given weight. This type of knowledge is regarded by the general public as certainty (yaqin). Some pseudo-researchers have expressed astonishment at the scholars' use of this expression and objected to it, which shows their inability to distinguish decisive, certain knowledge from knowledge based on predominant likelihood, due to their distance from the foundations of knowledge and its standards and ranks. Even more astonishing, some contemporary writers have cited the verses condemning the following of zann as an argument against this. This is the mistake many make. It is a conflation between the meaning intended by the Qur'an, which is following baseless whim and guesswork without proof or argument, and the meaning intended by the scholars, a technical term for a type of knowledge arising from real evidence, but which retains a weak, negligible possibility of error. This possibility has no real value or weight. Reflect on this, for it is important.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin noted very beautifully and from the Adab of our Ulama is that when an imam such as al-Tirmidhi labels a hadith as da'if, he is often not delivering a final verdict, but suspending judgment (tawaqquf), leaving the matter open for subsequent generations of scholars to investigate further. SubhanAllah, this is beautiful foresight and Husn al-Dhann from our Hadith masters.

(3)

In response to a question in class on whether scholars today can reclassify a hadith left in suspension by earlier generations, Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin answered that the theoretical window remains open until the Day of Judgment, but in practice it is very difficult to overturn an established ijma'. He cited Imam al-'Iraqi and Imam Murtada al-Zabidi's work on the Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din as an example, noting that they solidified the status of many narrations earlier generations had left suspended since the foundations of the discipline are sound even where human application of them is never perfect.

(4)

The Report Surrounded by Corroborating Indicators

وَقَدْ يَقَعُ فِيهَا أَيْ فِي أَخْبَارِ الْآحَادِ الْمُنْقَسِمَةِ إِلَى مَشْهُورٍ، وَعَزِيزٍ، وَغَرِيبٍ مَا يُفِيدُ الْعِلْمَ النَّظَرِيَّ بِالْقَرَائِنِ، عَلَى الْمُخْتَارِ خِلَافًا لِمَنْ أَبَى ذَلِكَ.
It may occur within the Ahad reports (which divide into mashhur, 'aziz, and ghareeb) that something yields theoretical knowledge ('ilm nazari) by means of corroborating indicators according to the preferred view contrary to those who reject this.
وَالْخِلَافُ فِي التَّحْقِيقِ لَفْظِيٌّ؛ لِأَنَّ مَنْ جَوَّزَ لَهُمْ إِطْلَاقَ الْعِلْمِ النَّظَرِيِّ قَيَّدَهُ بِكَوْنِهِ نَظَرِيًّا، وَهُوَ الْحَاصِلُ عَنِ الِاسْتِدْلَالِ، وَمَنْ أَبَى الْإِطْلَاقَ خَصَّ لَفْظَ الْعِلْمِ بِالْمُتَوَاتِرِ، وَمَا عَدَاهُ عِنْدَهُ ظَنِّيٌّ؛ لَكِنَّهُ لَا يَنْفِي أَنَّ مَا احْتَفَّ بِالْقَرَائِنِ أَرْجَحُ مِمَّا خَلَا عَنْهَا.
The disagreement upon close examination is merely verbal (lafzi). Whoever permits applying the term knowledge qualifies it as nazari (inferential), that is, knowledge obtained through reasoning. Whoever refuses the term restricts knowledge strictly to mutawatir, considering everything else merely zanni. Even this position does not deny that whatever is accompanied by corroborating indicators is more preponderant than whatever lacks them.

What Bukhari and Muslim Both Narrated (Muttafaq 'Alayh)

وَالْخَبَرُ الْمُحْتَفُّ بِالْقَرَائِنِ أَنْوَاعٌ، مِنْهَا: مَا أَخْرَجَهُ الشَّيْخَانِ فِي صَحِيحَيْهِمَا مِمَّا لَمْ يَبْلُغِ التَّوَاتُرَ، فَإِنَّهُ احْتَفَّ بِهِ قَرَائِنُ؛ مِنْهَا: جَلَالَتُهُمَا فِي هَذَا الشَّأْنِ. وَتَقَدُّمُهُمَا فِي تَمْيِيزِ الصَّحِيحِ عَلَى غَيْرِهِمَا. وَتَلَقِّي الْعُلَمَاءِ لِكِتَابَيْهِمَا بِالْقَبُولِ، وَهَذَا التَّلَقِّي وَحْدَهُ أَقْوَى فِي إِفَادَةِ الْعِلْمِ مِنْ مُجَرَّدِ كَثْرَةِ الطُّرُقِ الْقَاصِرَةِ عَنِ التَّوَاتُرِ.
The report surrounded by corroborating indicators comes in several types. Among them is what the two Shaykhs (Bukhari and Muslim) both narrated in their two Sahihs, from what does not reach tawatur, yet is nonetheless surrounded by qara'in. Those qara'in are listed below.

First, their eminence (jalalah) in this field.

Second, their precedence in distinguishing the authentic from other reports.

Third, the scholars' reception of their two books with acceptance (talaqqi bi'l-qabul). This reception alone is stronger in yielding knowledge than the mere multiplicity of chains that fall short of tawatur.

(5)
إِلَّا أَنَّ هَذَا يَخْتَصُّ بِمَا لَمْ يَنْتَقِدْهُ أَحَدٌ مِنَ الْحُفَّاظِ مِمَّا فِي الْكِتَابَيْنِ، وَبِمَا لَمْ يَقَعِ التَّجَاذُبُ بَيْنَ مَدْلُولَيْهِ مِمَّا وَقَعَ فِي الْكِتَابَيْنِ حَيْثُ لَا تَرْجِيحَ؛ لِاسْتِحَالَةِ أَنْ يُفِيدَ الْمُتَنَاقِضَانِ الْعِلْمَ بِصِدْقِهِمَا مِنْ غَيْرِ تَرْجِيحٍ لِأَحَدِهِمَا عَلَى الْآخَرِ، وَمَا عَدَا ذَلِكَ فَالْإِجْمَاعُ حَاصِلٌ عَلَى تَسْلِيمِ صِحَّتِهِ.
However, this applies only to what no hadith master (one who has reached the rank of a Hafidh in Hadith) has criticized among the contents of the two books, and to what has not fallen into contradiction between the apparent meanings of two narrations found in the two books, in cases where no preponderance can be given, since it is impossible for two contradictory reports to both yield certain knowledge of their truthfulness without one being preferred over the other. Beyond that, ijma' exists on accepting its authenticity.
فَإِنْ قِيلَ: إِنَّمَا اتَّفَقُوا عَلَى وُجُوبِ الْعَمَلِ بِهِ لَا عَلَى صِحَّتِهِ، وَسَنَدُ الْمَنْعِ أَنَّهُمْ مُتَّفِقُونَ عَلَى وُجُوبِ الْعَمَلِ بِكُلِّ مَا أَخْرَجَهُ الشَّيْخَانِ، وَلَوْ لَمْ يُخْرِجْهُ الشَّيْخَانِ صَحَّ، فَلَمْ يَبْقَ لِلصَّحِيحَيْنِ تِلْكَ الْمَزِيَّةُ، وَالْإِجْمَاعُ حَاصِلٌ عَلَى أَنَّ مَا اتَّفَقُوا عَلَيْهِ صَحِيحٌ.
An objection may be raised. The scholars only agreed on the obligation to act upon what Bukhari and Muslim narrated. They did not agree on its authenticity as such. Even material the two Shaykhs never narrated could still turn out to be sound. So the Sahihayn gain no real advantage from this agreement beyond what ijma' already grants to any sound report.
وَمِمَّنْ صَرَّحَ بِإِفَادَةِ مَا خَرَّجَهُ الشَّيْخَانِ الْعِلْمَ الْأُسْتَاذُ أَبُو إِسْحَاقَ الْإِسْفَرَايِينِيُّ، وَأَبُو الْفَضْلِ بْنُ طَاهِرٍ، وَأَبُو عَبْدِ اللهِ الْحُمَيْدِيُّ، وَغَيْرُهُمْ. وَيُحْتَمَلُ أَنْ يُقَالَ الْمَزِيَّةُ الْمَذْكُورَةُ كَوْنُ أَحَادِيثِهِمَا أَصَحَّ الصَّحِيحِ.
Among those who explicitly affirmed that what the two Shaykhs narrated yields real knowledge are Ustadh Abu Ishaq al-Isfara'ini and Abu al-Fadl ibn Tahir and Abu 'Abdillah al-Humaydi. Others held this view too. It may also be said that the true merit is simply that their hadith are the soundest of the sound (asahh al-sahih).

What the author is doing here. He raises the strongest version of the objection against his own position before he answers it. The objector is not denying that Bukhari and Muslim's hadith are sound. He is denying that scholarly ijma' adds anything to that soundness beyond an obligation to act. Ijma' already forces us to act on any report the community agrees is sound whether or not it happens to sit inside the two Sahihs. So if that obligation is all ijma' actually grants them the two books have no special status at all. They would just be two collections that happen to contain material already treated as sound for other reasons.

Ibn Hajar doesn't refute this directly. He just points out that scholars like al-Isfara'ini, ibn Tahir, and al-Humaydi went further than the objector allows and said the two books yield real knowledge simply by being in the Sahihayn. He's not winning through argument alone, he's showing the objector's premise, that ijma' only ever obligates action, is rejected by the very scholars whose consensus is being cited.

(6)

Further Types of Qarina Supported Reports

وَمِنْهَا: الْمَشْهُورُ إِذَا كَانَتْ لَهُ طُرُقٌ مُبَايِنَةٌ سَالِمَةٌ مِنْ ضَعْفِ الرُّوَاةِ وَالْعِلَلِ، وَمِمَّنْ صَرَّحَ بِإِفَادَتِهِ الْعِلْمَ النَّظَرِيَّ الْأُسْتَاذُ أَبُو مَنْصُورٍ الْبَغْدَادِيُّ، وَالْأُسْتَاذُ أَبُو بَكْرِ بْنُ فُورَكَ، وَغَيْرُهُمَا.
Among them is the mashhur report, when it has distinct routes free from narrator weakness or hidden defects.
وَمِنْهَا: الْمُسَلْسَلُ بِالْأَئِمَّةِ الْحُفَّاظِ الْمُتْقِنِينَ، حَيْثُ لَا يَكُونُ غَرِيبًا، كَالْحَدِيثِ الَّذِي يَرْوِيهِ أَحْمَدُ بْنُ حَنْبَلٍ مَثَلًا، وَيُشَارِكُهُ غَيْرُهُ فِيهِ عَنِ الشَّافِعِيِّ، وَيُشَارِكُهُ فِيهِ غَيْرُهُ عَنْ مَالِكِ بْنِ أَنَسٍ، فَإِنَّهُ يُفِيدُ الْعِلْمَ عِنْدَ سَامِعِهِ بِالِاسْتِدْلَالِ مِنْ جِهَةِ جَلَالَةِ رُوَاتِهِ.
And among them is what is musalsal (chained) by meticulous, expert hadith masters at every link where it does not amount to ghareeb such as a hadith narrated by Ahmad ibn Hanbal, for example, shared by another narrator from al-Shafi'i, shared by another narrator from Malik ibn Anas.

Mashhur with sound routes yields theoretical knowledge according to those who affirmed it, among them Ustadh Abu Mansur al-Baghdadi and Ustadh Abu Bakr ibn Furak.

Musalsal bi'l-a'immah yields knowledge for the one who hears it, by inference, on account of the eminence and majesty of its narrators at every generation of the chain.

(7)

Closing Note on Humility, Even Malik's Solitary Report

يَقُومُ مَقَامَ الْعَدَدِ الْكَثِيرِ مِنْ غَيْرِهِمْ، وَلَا يَتَشَكَّكُ مَنْ لَهُ أَدْنَى مُمَارَسَةٍ بِالْعِلْمِ وَأَخْبَارِ النَّاسِ أَنَّ مَالِكًا مَثَلًا لَوْ صَادَقَهُ بِخَبَرٍ أَنَّهُ صَادِقٌ فِيهِ، فَإِذَا انْضَافَ إِلَيْهِ مَنْ هُوَ فِي تِلْكَ الدَّرَجَةِ ازْدَادَ قُوَّةً، وَبَعُدَ مَا يُخْشَى عَلَيْهِ مِنَ السَّهْوِ.
Such a narrator's report stands in the place of a large number of lesser narrators. No one with even the slightest experience in knowledge and in evaluating people's reports would doubt that if Malik, for example, reported something, he would be truthful in it. If someone of that same caliber is added alongside him, the report only grows stronger, and the fear of inadvertent error (sahw) recedes further still.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin closed this point with a note on humility. Even when an imam as great as Malik narrates a report alone, scholars still prefer to find a second supporting route where possible simply to eliminate the small residual possibility of human error, rather than resting on the strength of one narrator's status alone.
