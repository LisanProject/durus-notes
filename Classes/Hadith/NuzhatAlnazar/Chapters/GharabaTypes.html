Gharaba, Fard, and the Difference from Gharib

Who Is Actually Qualified to Judge These Classifications

Before moving into a new layer of classification, Ibn Hajar pauses to make a point about who these classifications are even useful to. Naming a hadith mutawatir, gharib, or sahih is not a mechanical label anyone can apply after memorizing the definitions.

وَهَذِهِ الأَنْوَاعُ الَّتِي ذَكَرْنَاهَا لَا يَحْصُلُ العِلْمُ بِصِدْقِ الخَبَرِ مِنْهَا إِلَّا لِلْعَالِمِ بِالحَدِيثِ، المُتَبَحِّرِ فِيهِ، العَارِفِ بِأَحْوَالِ الرُّوَاةِ، المُطَّلِعِ عَلَى العِلَلِ.
Certain knowledge of a report's truthfulness from these types is not attained except by one learned in hadith, deeply versed in it, knowledgeable of the states of the narrators, and aware of hidden defects.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on who may judge these classifications.

Al-mutabahhir literally describes someone who sails deeply into a sea, here the sea of hadith knowledge. It is a deliberate image: surface familiarity with the terms is not the same as the depth needed to actually apply them to a real chain of narrators.

وَكَوْنُ غَيْرِهِ لَا يَحْصُلُ لَهُ بِصِدْقِ ذَلِكَ لِقُصُورِهِ عَنِ الأَوْصَافِ المَذْكُورَةِ؛ لَا يَنْفِي حُصُولَ العِلْمِ لِلْمُتَبَحِّرِ المَذْكُورِ، وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ.
The fact that someone else does not attain that certainty, due to falling short of the qualities mentioned, does not negate that the aforementioned deeply versed scholar does attain it, and Allah knows best.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, closing note on expertise.

Gharaba: Solitary Transmission at the Root or Mid-Chain

Gharaba (solitariness) is not one thing. It matters a great deal where in the chain the solitary narration actually occurs.

ثُمَّ الْغَرَابَةُ: إِمَّا أَنْ تَكُونَ فِي أَصْلِ السَّنَدِ، أَيْ فِي المَوْضِعِ الَّذِي يَدُورُ الإِسْنَادُ عَلَيْهِ وَيَرْجِعُ - وَلَوْ تَعَدَّدَتِ الطُّرُقُ - إِلَيْهِ، وَهُوَ طَرَفُهُ الَّذِي فِيهِ الصَّحَابِيُّ، (أَوْ لَا) يَكُونُ كَذَلِكَ؛ بِأَنْ يَكُونَ التَّفَرُّدُ فِي أَثْنَائِهِ.
Gharaba is either at the root of the isnad, meaning the point the chain revolves around and returns to, even if the routes multiply, which is its end where the Companion sits, or it is not that, meaning the solitary narration occurs partway through the chain instead.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the two locations of gharaba.

Al-Fard al-Mutlaq (Absolute Solitary): the solitary narration sits at the root of the chain, one Companion narrating alone, no matter how many routes branch out from him afterward.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin specifically cited the hadith prohibiting the sale of wala' (the bond of alliance) as the worked example of al-fard al-mutlaq in this session.

كَحَدِيثِ النَّهْيِ عَنْ بَيْعِ الْوَلَاءِ وَعَنْ هِبَتِهِ؛ تَفَرَّدَ بِهِ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ دِينَارٍ، عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ.
Like the hadith prohibiting the sale of wala' and its gifting: 'Abdullah ibn Dinar was alone in narrating it, from Ibn 'Umar.

Al-Fard al-Nisbi (Relative Solitary): the solitary narration occurs somewhere in the middle of the chain, even if the hadith is well-known overall, and even if a solitary narrator is then narrated alone by a second, later narrator.

(2)

For the relative case, the Shaykh referenced the hadith of the branches of faith (shu'ab al-iman), where Abu Salih was the sole transmitter from Abu Hurayrah, and 'Abdullah ibn Dinar was then sole from Abu Salih, showing solitary transmission occurring at two different levels of the same chain.

وَقَدْ يَنْفَرِدُ بِهِ رَاوٍ عَنْ ذَلِكَ المُنْفَرِدِ؛ كَحَدِيثِ شُعَبِ الإِيمَانِ: تَفَرَّدَ بِهِ أَبُو صَالِحٍ، عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ، وَتَفَرَّدَ بِهِ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ دِينَارٍ، عَنْ أَبِي صَالِحٍ.
Sometimes a narrator is then alone in narrating it from that solitary narrator: like the hadith of the branches of faith, where Abu Salih was alone in narrating it from Abu Hurayrah, and 'Abdullah ibn Dinar was then alone in narrating it from Abu Salih.

(See: Ghareeb Hadith for the fuller treatment of ghareeb mutlaq and ghareeb nisbi.)

A Naming Habit, Not a Real Distinction

Students sometimes assume fard and gharib mark two genuinely different phenomena. They do not. The split is only in which word muhaddithun happen to reach for in each case.

وَيَقِلُّ إِطْلَاقُ الفَرْدِيَّةِ عَلَى الغَرِيبِ وَالفَرْدُ مُتَرَادِفَانِ؛ لِأَنَّ الغَرِيبَ وَالفَرْدَ مُتَرَادِفَانِ لُغَةً وَاصْطِلَاحًا، إِلَّا أَنَّ أَهْلَ الِاصْطِلَاحِ غَايَرُوا بَيْنَهُمَا مِنْ حَيْثُ كَثْرَةُ الِاسْتِعْمَالِ وَقِلَّتُهُ. فَالفَرْدُ: أَكْثَرُ مَا يُطْلِقُونَهُ عَلَى الفَرْدِ المُطْلَقِ. وَالغَرِيبُ: أَكْثَرُ مَا يُطْلِقُونَهُ عَلَى الفَرْدِ النِّسْبِيِّ.
Gharib and fard are synonyms linguistically and technically, except that the people of the terminology distinguished between them by frequency of use, not by meaning: fard is mostly applied to al-fard al-mutlaq, and gharib is mostly applied to al-fard al-nisbi.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the difference between gharib and fard.

Does One Gharib Isnad Disqualify a Hadith from Being Mashhur?

A student asked directly: if a hadith has several routes and one of those routes is gharib, does that disqualify the hadith as a whole from being called mashhur?

Isnad-Level vs. Hadith-Level Grading: Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin's answer was that each isnad is classified individually, one route may be mashhur and another gharib within the very same hadith. The highest valid classification among a hadith's routes applies to the hadith as a whole, but that does not erase or overwrite the individual gharib isnad's own status, it still stands on its own.
