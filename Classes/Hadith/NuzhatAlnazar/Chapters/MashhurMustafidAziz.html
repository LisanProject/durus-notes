Mashhur, Mustafid, and Aziz in Practice

Mashhur: Technical Definition vs. Everyday Fame

Technically, mashhur is a narration reported by more than two narrators at every single generation of its chain.

وَالثَّانِي المَشْهُورُ عِنْدَ المُحَدِّثِينَ، وَهُوَ المُسْتَفِيضُ عَلَى رَأْيٍ.
The second is mashhur among the hadith scholars, and it is the mustafid according to one position.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the definition of mashhur.

A hadith being "famous" in ordinary speech does not guarantee it meets this standard, and it does not guarantee authenticity either. Fame and technical grading are two separate axes entirely. The clearest way to see this is to look at three real mashhur hadiths that carry three different authenticity grades.

مِثَالُ المَشْهُورِ الصَّحِيحِ: حَدِيثُ لَا يَمَسُّ القُرْآنَ إِلَّا طَاهِرٌ، رُوِيَ مِنْ حَدِيثِ عَمْرِو بْنِ حَزْمٍ وَابْنِ عُمَرَ، وَحَكِيمِ بْنِ حِزَامٍ وَعُثْمَانَ وَأَبِي العَاصِ وَثَوْبَانَ. وَمِثَالُ المَشْهُورِ وَهُوَ حَسَنٌ: حَدِيثُ لَا ضَرَرَ وَلَا ضِرَارَ، رُوِيَ مِنْ أَوْجُهٍ كَثِيرَةٍ يَرْتَقِي بِهَا إِلَى الحَسَنِ أَوِ الصِّحَّةِ، وَحَسَّنَهُ النَّوَوِيُّ فِي الأَرْبَعِينَ. وَمِثَالُ المَشْهُورِ وَهُوَ ضَعِيفٌ: حَدِيثُ اطْلُبُوا العِلْمَ وَلَوْ بِالصِّينِ، رُوِيَ مِنْ عِدَّةِ أَوْجُهٍ، وَلَمْ يَخْلُ مِنْ قَدْحٍ شَدِيدٍ.
An example of a sahih mashhur hadith: "None should touch the Qur'an except one who is pure," narrated through 'Amr ibn Hazm, Ibn 'Umar, Hakim ibn Hizam, 'Uthman, Abu al-'As, and Thawban. An example of a mashhur hadith graded hasan: "There is no harm nor reciprocating harm," narrated through many routes that raise it to the level of hasan or sahih, and graded hasan by al-Nawawi in the Arba'in. An example of a mashhur hadith graded da'if: "Seek knowledge even as far as China," narrated through several routes, none of which escape serious criticism.

Nuzhat al-Nazar, footnote on examples of mashhur.

All three of these are genuinely mashhur in the technical sense, meaning all three are widely narrated through multiple chains at every generation. What separates them is only the reliability of those chains, not how well known the hadith is. Fame gets a hadith into wide circulation. It says nothing at all about whether that hadith is actually sound.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin Lashen has suggested memorizing these hadith along with their respective category as a way to actually drill the concept in, rather than leaving mashhur as a purely abstract definition.

Mustafid: A Synonym, With a Minority Refinement

Jurists sometimes use mustafid ("overflowing") as a synonym for mashhur, and the matn itself names this directly. A minority of scholars draw a finer distinction, requiring the number of narrators to stay consistent at every stage of the chain for a report to be called mustafid specifically, rather than simply exceeding two at each generation the way mashhur requires. In practice, though, the two terms are generally treated as synonyms, and the technical debate rarely changes how any actual hadith gets classified.

Aziz: Two at Its Narrowest Point, Not Two Everywhere

Aziz is defined as a narration which, at its narrowest point in the chain, has no fewer than two narrators. This is a common point of confusion worth being precise about.

وَالثَّالِثُ العَزِيزُ، وَهُوَ أَنْ لَا يَرْوِيَهُ أَقَلُّ مِنِ اثْنَيْنِ عَنِ اثْنَيْنِ.
The third is aziz, and it is a narration not carried by fewer than two narrators from two narrators.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the definition of aziz.

What Aziz Does Not Require: exactly two narrators at every single generation of the chain.

(1)

What Aziz Actually Requires: that the number of narrators never drops below two at any generation. A chain can have three, five, or ten narrators at some generations and still be aziz overall, so long as no generation anywhere in the chain narrows to a single narrator.

(2)

Sahih Does Not Require Multiple Narrators

A solitary hadith, one narrated by only a single narrator at some point in its chain, can still be judged sahih (authentic) if that narrator is trustworthy and precise. The number of narrators and the authenticity grade are answering two different questions.

Why More Narrators Still Helps: Imam al-Hakim discussed how having additional corroborating narrators can help remove the status of jahala (an unknown or unestablished narrator) from a source, since independent corroboration confirms that a given narrator was genuinely known and relied upon by others. This is a real benefit, but it is a separate matter from the bare requirement for authenticity, which does not itself demand multiple narrators.

(1)

The hadith "Actions are by intentions" is the clearest possible illustration of this: it is solitary (gharib) at several generations of its chain, most famously narrated only by 'Alqamah from 'Umar at its origin, and yet it is entirely authentic and sits at the very opening of Sahih al-Bukhari. Its solitary status at points in the chain never put its authenticity in question, precisely because authenticity tracks the reliability of the narrators involved, not how many of them there happen to be. (See: Hadith 1: Actions Are Judged by Intentions)
