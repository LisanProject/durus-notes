Mahfuz and Shadh, Ma'ruf and Munkar

Mahfuz and Shadh

Ibn Hajar now names the two sides of a conflict that cannot be reconciled (see Ziyadat al-Thiqah). Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin asked the class to put these under the heading "Additions by Reliable Transmitters" in their notes and list mahfuz, shadh, ma'ruf, and munkar.

Text
(فَإِنْ خُولِفَ بِأَرْجَحَ) مِنْهُ؛ لِمَزِيدِ ضَبْطٍ أَوْ كَثْرَةِ عَدَدٍ أَوْ غَيْرِ ذَلِكَ مِنْ وُجُوهِ التَّرْجِيحَاتِ؛ (فَالرَّاجِحُ) يُقَالُ لَهُ: (المَحْفُوظُ، وَمُقَابِلُهُ) - وَهُوَ المَرْجُوحُ - يُقَالُ لَهُ: (الشَّاذُّ).
مِثَالُ ذَلِكَ: مَا رَوَاهُ التِّرْمِذِيُّ وَالنَّسَائِيُّ وَابْنُ مَاجَهْ مِنْ طَرِيقِ ابْنِ عُيَيْنَةَ، عَنْ عَمْرِو بْنِ دِينَارٍ، عَنْ عَوْسَجَةَ، عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا: «أَنَّ رَجُلاً تُوُفِّيَ عَلَى عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ، وَلَمْ يَدَعْ وَارِثاً إِلَّا مَوْلًى هُوَ أَعْتَقَهُ...» الحَدِيثَ.
وَتَابَعَ ابْنَ عُيَيْنَةَ عَلَى وَصْلِهِ ابْنُ جُرَيْجٍ، وَغَيْرُهُ.
وَخَالَفَهُمْ حَمَّادُ بْنُ زَيْدٍ، فَرَوَاهُ عَنْ عَمْرِو بْنِ دِينَارٍ، عَنْ عَوْسَجَةَ، وَلَمْ يَذْكُرِ ابْنَ عَبَّاسٍ.
قَالَ أَبُو حَاتِمٍ: «المَحْفُوظُ حَدِيثُ ابْنِ عُيَيْنَةَ». انْتَهَى.
فَحَمَّادُ بْنُ زَيْدٍ مِنْ أَهْلِ العَدَالَةِ وَالضَّبْطِ، وَمَعَ ذَلِكَ رَجَّحَ أَبُو حَاتِمٍ رِوَايَةَ مَنْ هُمْ أَكْثَرُ عَدَداً مِنْهُ.
وَعُرِفَ مِنْ هَذَا التَّقْرِيرِ أَنَّ الشَّاذَّ: «مَا رَوَاهُ المَقْبُولُ مُخَالِفاً لِمَنْ هُوَ أَوْلَى مِنْهُ»، وَهَذَا هُوَ المُعْتَمَدُ فِي تَعْرِيفِ الشَّاذِّ بِحَسَبِ الاصْطِلَاحِ.

Translation
"So if he is contradicted by one weightier" than he, through greater precision, a greater number, or other grounds of preference, "then the preponderant one" is called "al-mahfuz, and its counterpart," the outweighed one, is called "al-shadh."
An example is what al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah narrated by way of Ibn 'Uyaynah, from 'Amr ibn Dinar, from 'Awsajah, from Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both: "that a man died in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and left no heir except a client whom he had freed..." to the end of the hadith. Ibn Jurayj and others corroborated Ibn 'Uyaynah in connecting it. Hammad ibn Zayd differed from them, narrating it from 'Amr ibn Dinar, from 'Awsajah, without mentioning Ibn 'Abbas. Abu Hatim said: "The mahfuz is the hadith of Ibn 'Uyaynah." So Hammad ibn Zayd is among the people of uprightness and precision, and even so Abu Hatim preferred the narration of those more numerous than he.
From this explanation it is known that the shadh is "what an acceptable narrator narrates in contradiction to someone more entitled than he." This is the relied-upon definition of shadh in technical usage.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on al-mahfuz and al-shadh.

Commentary

When two acceptable narrators conflict in a way that cannot be reconciled, the critic compares them. The preferred version is mahfuz, the outweighed one shadh. Shadh here does not mean simply rare or solitary, and must not be confused with gharib: it is the result of a critical comparison in which one side is stronger.

In the example the two versions differ technically: Ibn 'Uyaynah's connects the chain through Ibn 'Abbas, Hammad's omits him. Abu Hatim preferred the connected version here given its corroboration. The Shaykh cautioned against turning this into a rule that a connected version always beats a disconnected one: Ibn 'Uyaynah and Hammad ibn Zayd were both major imams of the same generation, and the preference rests on the evidence of this particular comparison. Critics ask who is more precise, who better preserved the shared teacher, whether either side is corroborated and how strongly, whether the routes are independent, and whether a technical slip such as connecting or disconnecting a chain may have occurred. A marfu' and a mawquf form of one transmission cannot simply be treated as the same account.

Mahfuz. The version judged stronger after comparison with a conflicting version from an acceptable narrator.

Shadh. An acceptable narrator's version that conflicts with one more entitled to preference.

Mahfuz is not the same as sahih. It only names the stronger side of this comparison. The editor's footnote notes that al-Bukhari weakened this hadith because of 'Awsajah's standing.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that shadh is a technical term for an outweighed conflicting report, not a synonym for any rare or solitary report, and that connecting or omitting a link in an isnad is a technical claim to be tested against all the evidence.

Ma'ruf and Munkar

(وَ)إِنْ وَقَعَتِ المُخَالَفَةُ (مَعَ الضَّعْفِ): فَـ(الرَّاجِحُ) يُقَالُ لَهُ: (المَعْرُوفُ، وَمُقَابِلُهُ) يُقَالُ لَهُ: (المُنْكَرُ).
مِثَالُهُ: مَا رَوَاهُ ابْنُ أَبِي حَاتِمٍ مِنْ طَرِيقِ حُبَيِّبِ بْنِ حَبِيبٍ - وَهُوَ أَخُو حَمْزَةَ بْنِ حَبِيبٍ الزَّيَّاتِ المُقْرِئِ -، عَنْ أَبِي إِسْحَاقَ، عَنِ العَيْزَارِ بْنِ حُرَيْثٍ، عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «مَنْ أَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ، وَحَجَّ، وَصَامَ، وَقَرَى الضَّيْفَ؛ دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ».
قَالَ أَبُو حَاتِمٍ: «هُوَ مُنْكَرٌ؛ لِأَنَّ غَيْرَهُ مِنَ الثِّقَاتِ رَوَاهُ عَنْ أَبِي إِسْحَاقَ مَوْقُوفاً، وَهُوَ المَعْرُوفُ».
وَعُرِفَ بِهَذَا: أَنَّ بَيْنَ الشَّاذِّ وَالمُنْكَرِ عُمُوماً وَخُصُوصاً مِنْ وَجْهٍ؛ لِأَنَّ بَيْنَهُمَا اجْتِمَاعاً فِي اشْتِرَاطِ المُخَالَفَةِ، وَافْتِرَاقاً فِي أَنَّ الشَّاذَّ: رِوَايَةُ ثِقَةٍ أَوْ صَدُوقٍ، وَالمُنْكَرَ: رِوَايَةُ ضَعِيفٍ، وَقَدْ غَفَلَ مَنْ سَوَّى بَيْنَهُمَا، وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ.

Translation
"And" if the contradiction occurs "with weakness," then "the preponderant one" is called "al-ma'ruf, and its counterpart" is called "al-munkar." An example is what Ibn Abi Hatim narrated by way of Hubayyib ibn Habib, the brother of Hamzah ibn Habib al-Zayyat the Quran reciter, from Abu Ishaq, from al-'Ayzar ibn Hurayth, from Ibn 'Abbas, from the Prophet ﷺ, who said: "Whoever establishes the prayer, gives zakah, performs Hajj, fasts, and hosts the guest will enter Paradise." Abu Hatim said: "It is munkar, because others among the reliable narrators narrated it from Abu Ishaq as mawquf, and that is the ma'ruf."
From this it is known that between the shadh and the munkar there is a partial overlap, generality and specificity in one respect: they meet in requiring contradiction, and differ in that the shadh is the narration of a reliable or truthful narrator, while the munkar is the narration of a weak one. Whoever treated them as the same has overlooked this. And Allah knows best.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on al-ma'ruf and al-munkar.

Commentary
Who narrates the outweighed versionPreferred versionOutweighed version
An acceptable narrator (thiqah or saduq), against one more reliableMahfuzShadh
A weak narrator, against reliable narratorsMa'rufMunkar

Both pairs share contradiction; what separates them is the standing of the narrator whose version loses. The example also shows that raising a report to the Prophet ﷺ (raf') is not automatically preferable to a version that stops at a Companion. Raf' is a technical claim to be tested against narrator reliability, other versions, and the total evidence: a weak narrator can mistakenly raise a statement that reliable narrators preserve as the words of Ibn 'Abbas.

Raf'. Attributing a report to the Prophet ﷺ.

Mawquf. A report stopping at a Companion.

'Umum wa khusus min wajh. Two terms that share one feature but each has a condition the other lacks.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that the early critics did not always use these terms with the precision of later manuals. In early works munkar can have a broader range, so read it in its context rather than imposing a later definition on every early use.
