Chapter 2: The Khabar by Number of Routes

The matn's first division of the khabar is by how many routes it reaches us through: mutawatir, then the three kinds of ahad, mashhur, aziz, and gharib. This chapter also covers what kind of knowledge each yields, how ahad divides into accepted and rejected, how corroborating indicators can raise an ahad report to certainty, and the two kinds of gharaba.

Here is a list of the sections covered in this chapter.

Categories of Knowledge and Mutawatir covers the distinction between necessary ('ilm daruri) and inferential ('ilm nazari) knowledge, and why mutawatir yields the former.

Mutawatir, Mashhur, Aziz, and Gharib covers the classification of hadith by the number of narrators at each generation, with the four conditions of mutawatir.

Real Examples of Mutawatir, and Its Two Forms covers mutawatir lafzi and ma'nawi, and real hadith examples of tawatur.

Mashhur, Mustafid, and Aziz in Practice covers these terms beyond their bare definitions, and why sahih does not require multiple narrators.

Ghareeb Hadith covers the definition of gharib and its division into gharib mutlaq and gharib nisbi.

Ahad and the Question of Certainty covers the division of ahad into maqbul, mardud, and tawaqquf, and how qara'in can raise an ahad report to inferential certainty.

Gharaba, Fard, and the Difference from Gharib covers fard mutlaq and fard nisbi, who is qualified to apply them, and the two places gharaba can occur in a chain.
