The Difference Between Gharib and Fard

A Naming Habit, Not a Real Distinction

Students sometimes assume fard and gharib mark two genuinely different phenomena. They do not. The split is only in which word muhaddithun happen to reach for in each case.

وَيَقِلُّ إِطْلَاقُ الفَرْدِيَّةِ عَلَى الغَرِيبِ وَالفَرْدُ مُتَرَادِفَانِ؛ لِأَنَّ الغَرِيبَ وَالفَرْدَ مُتَرَادِفَانِ لُغَةً وَاصْطِلَاحًا، إِلَّا أَنَّ أَهْلَ الِاصْطِلَاحِ غَايَرُوا بَيْنَهُمَا مِنْ حَيْثُ كَثْرَةُ الِاسْتِعْمَالِ وَقِلَّتُهُ. فَالفَرْدُ: أَكْثَرُ مَا يُطْلِقُونَهُ عَلَى الفَرْدِ المُطْلَقِ. وَالغَرِيبُ: أَكْثَرُ مَا يُطْلِقُونَهُ عَلَى الفَرْدِ النِّسْبِيِّ.
Gharib and fard are synonyms linguistically and technically, except that the people of the terminology distinguished between them by frequency of use, not by meaning: fard is mostly applied to al-fard al-mutlaq, and gharib is mostly applied to al-fard al-nisbi.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the difference between gharib and fard.

That split disappears entirely once you look at the verb rather than the noun. Muhaddithun say "tafarrada bihi fulan" (so-and-so was alone in narrating it) or "aghraba bihi fulan" (so-and-so was singular in it) for both the absolute and relative case, with no attempt to keep the two separate.

Noun vs. Verb: as nouns, "fard" leans toward the absolute case and "gharib" toward the relative case, purely by convention of use. As verbs ("tafarrada bihi", "aghraba bihi"), muhaddithun apply both to either case without distinction.

(1)

Ibn Hajar draws a parallel to munqati' and mursal, which most muhaddithun treat as distinct terms, yet still both collapse into the single verb "arsalahu" once you are describing the act of narrating rather than naming the category. The same pattern recurs across mustalah: technical nouns get fine distinctions that the ordinary verbs used to describe the act do not preserve.
