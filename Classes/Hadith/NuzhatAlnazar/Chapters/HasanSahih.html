"Hasan Sahih" and al-Tirmidhi's Definition of Hasan

When One Hadith Is Called Both Hasan and Sahih

The class reread the passage on sahih li-ghayrihi (see Hasan li-Dhatihi, and Sahih li-Ghayrihi), whose last line, "this is where the description stands alone," leads into the question of a hadith given both descriptions at once, as al-Tirmidhi often does.

Text
وَهَذَا حَيْثُ يَنْفَرِدُ الوَصْفُ.
(فَإِنْ جُمِعَا) أَيِ: الصَّحِيحُ وَالحَسَنُ فِي وَصْفٍ وَاحِدٍ؛ كَقَوْلِ التِّرْمِذِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِ: «حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ»؛ (فَلِلتَّرَدُّدِ) الحَاصِلِ مِنَ المُجْتَهِدِ (فِي النَّاقِلِ): هَلِ اجْتَمَعَتْ فِيهِ شُرُوطُ الصِّحَّةِ أَوْ قَصُرَ عَنْهَا؟! وَهَذَا (حَيْثُ) يَحْصُلُ مِنْهُ (التَّفَرُّدُ) بِتِلْكَ الرِّوَايَةِ.
وَعُرِفَ بِهَذَا جَوَابُ مَنِ اسْتَشْكَلَ الجَمْعَ بَيْنَ الوَصْفَيْنِ فَقَالَ: الحَسَنُ قَاصِرٌ عَنِ الصَّحِيحِ، فَفِي الجَمْعِ بَيْنَ الوَصْفَيْنِ إِثْبَاتٌ لِذَلِكَ القُصُورِ وَنَفْيُهُ!
وَمُحَصَّلُ الجَوَابِ: أَنَّ تَرَدُّدَ أَئِمَّةِ الحَدِيثِ فِي حَالِ نَاقِلِهِ اقْتَضَى لِلْمُجْتَهِدِ أَنْ لَا يَصِفَهُ بِأَحَدِ الوَصْفَيْنِ، فَيُقَالُ فِيهِ: «حَسَنٌ» بِاعْتِبَارِ وَصْفِهِ عِنْدَ قَوْمٍ، «صَحِيحٌ» بِاعْتِبَارِ وَصْفِهِ عِنْدَ قَوْمٍ.
وَغَايَةُ مَا فِيهِ: أَنَّهُ حُذِفَ مِنْهُ حَرْفُ التَّرَدُّدِ؛ لِأَنَّ حَقَّهُ أَنْ يَقُولَ: حَسَنٌ أَوْ صَحِيحٌ، وَهَذَا كَمَا حُذِفَ حَرْفُ العَطْفِ مِنَ الَّذِي بَعْدَهُ.
وَعَلَى هَذَا؛ فَمَا قِيلَ فِيهِ: حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ؛ دُونَ مَا قِيلَ فِيهِ: صَحِيحٌ؛ لِأَنَّ الجَزْمَ أَقْوَى مِنَ التَّرَدُّدِ، وَهَذَا حَيْثُ التَّفَرُّدُ.
(وَإِلَّا) إِذَا لَمْ يَحْصُلِ التَّفَرُّدُ؛ (فَـ)إِطْلَاقُ الوَصْفَيْنِ مَعاً عَلَى الحَدِيثِ يَكُونُ (بِاعْتِبَارِ إِسْنَادَيْنِ) أَحَدُهُمَا صَحِيحٌ، وَالآخَرُ حَسَنٌ.
وَعَلَى هَذَا؛ فَمَا قِيلَ فِيهِ: «حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ»؛ فَوْقَ مَا قِيلَ فِيهِ: «صَحِيحٌ» فَقَطْ، إِذَا كَانَ فَرْداً؛ لِأَنَّ كَثْرَةَ الطُّرُقِ تُقَوِّي.

Translation
This is where the description stands alone. "So if the two are combined," that is, sahih and hasan in one description, as when al-Tirmidhi and others say "a hasan sahih hadith," "then it is due to the hesitation" of the evaluator "about the transmitter": are the conditions of soundness combined in him, or does he fall short of them? This is "where" he is "alone" in that narration.
This answers whoever found it problematic to combine the two descriptions, saying: hasan falls short of sahih, so combining them affirms that shortfall and denies it at once. The gist of the answer is that the hadith imams' differing over the state of its transmitter required the evaluator not to describe it with only one of the two. So it is called "hasan" according to how some describe him, and "sahih" according to how others do. The most that can be said is that the particle of alternatives was dropped, since it should properly be "hasan or sahih," just as the conjunction is dropped in the case that follows.
On this view, what is called hasan sahih is below what is called sahih, because a categorical judgment is stronger than a hesitant one; this is where there is a single route. "Otherwise," if there is no single route, then applying both descriptions to the hadith is "with respect to two chains," one sahih and the other hasan. On this view, what is called "hasan sahih" is above what is called "sahih" alone when that one is a single route, because many routes strengthen.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the expression "hasan sahih."

Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained that Ibn Hajar offers two possibilities, and which one applies depends on whether there is tafarrud, a single route.

SituationWhat "hasan sahih" meansCompared with plain "sahih"
One routeHasan or sahih: critics differ over the narrator, some judging him to meet the sahih standard, others his precision to fall short.Below it, since a categorical judgment is stronger than hesitation.
Two or more routesHasan and sahih: one chain is sahih, another hasan.Above a single-route sahih, since the extra route reinforces it ("sahih plus," as the Shaykh put it).

In the second case the hasan route, though below sahih by itself, adds support to the sahih route. The Shaykh presented this as a comparison within that case, not as a rule that every hasan sahih outranks every sahih.

Mujtahid here. A student asked why Ibn Hajar says "the mujtahid." The Shaykh said it need not mean an absolute mujtahid like al-Shafi'i. In context it means a qualified evaluator, a naqid familiar with the narrators and rijal. He said he was not sure of the more specific reason Ibn Hajar chose the word.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that "hasan sahih" does not have one fixed meaning. Ibn Hajar is offering possibilities, and whether the phrase means "or" or "and" depends on whether the report has one route or several.

An Objection: Al-Tirmidhi's "Hasan Gharib"

فَإِنْ قِيلَ: قَدْ صَرَّحَ التِّرْمِذِيُّ بِأَنَّ شَرْطَ الحَسَنِ: أَنْ يُرْوَى مِنْ غَيْرِ وَجْهٍ؛ فَكَيْفَ يَقُولُ فِي بَعْضِ الأَحَادِيثِ: «حَسَنٌ غَرِيبٌ، لَا نَعْرِفُهُ إِلَّا مِنْ هَذَا الوَجْهِ»؟!
فَالجَوَابُ: أَنَّ التِّرْمِذِيَّ لَمْ يُعَرِّفِ الحَسَنَ مُطْلَقاً، وَإِنَّمَا عَرَّفَ بِنَوْعٍ خَاصٍّ مِنْهُ وَقَعَ فِي كِتَابِهِ، وَهُوَ مَا يَقُولُ فِيهِ: «حَسَنٌ» مِنْ غَيْرِ صِفَةٍ أُخْرَى، وَذَلِكَ أَنَّهُ يَقُولُ فِي بَعْضِ الأَحَادِيثِ: «حَسَنٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «صَحِيحٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «غَرِيبٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «حَسَنٌ غَرِيبٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «صَحِيحٌ غَرِيبٌ»، وَفِي بَعْضِهَا: «حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ غَرِيبٌ».
وَتَعْرِيفُهُ إِنَّمَا وَقَعَ عَلَى الأَوَّلِ فَقَطْ، وَعِبَارَتُهُ تُرْشِدُ إِلَى ذَلِكَ؛ حَيْثُ قَالَ فِي آخِرِ كِتَابِهِ: «وَمَا قُلْنَا فِي كِتَابِنَا: حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ؛ فَإِنَّمَا أَرَدْنَا بِهِ: حُسْنَ إِسْنَادِهِ عِنْدَنَا، كُلُّ حَدِيثٍ يُرْوَى لَا يَكُونُ رَاوِيهِ مُتَّهَماً بِكَذِبٍ، وَيُرْوَى مِنْ غَيْرِ وَجْهٍ نَحْوَ ذَلِكَ، وَلَا يَكُونُ شَاذّاً؛ فَهُوَ عِنْدَنَا حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ».
فَعُرِفَ بِهَذَا أَنَّهُ إِنَّمَا عَرَّفَ الَّذِي يَقُولُ فِيهِ: «حَسَنٌ» فَقَطْ، أَمَّا مَا يَقُولُ فِيهِ: «حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ»، أَوْ: «حَسَنٌ غَرِيبٌ»، أَوْ: «حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ غَرِيبٌ»؛ فَلَمْ يُعَرِّجْ عَلَى تَعْرِيفِهِ؛ كَمَا لَمْ يُعَرِّجْ عَلَى تَعْرِيفِ مَا يَقُولُ فِيهِ: «صَحِيحٌ» فَقَطْ، أَوْ: «غَرِيبٌ» فَقَطْ.
وَكَأَنَّهُ تَرَكَ ذَلِكَ اسْتِغْنَاءً؛ لِشُهْرَتِهِ عِنْدَ أَهْلِ الفَنِّ، وَاقْتَصَرَ عَلَى تَعْرِيفِ مَا يَقُولُ فِيهِ فِي كِتَابِهِ: «حَسَنٌ» فَقَطْ؛ إِمَّا لِغُمُوضِهِ، وَإِمَّا لِأَنَّهُ اصْطِلَاحٌ جَدِيدٌ، وَلِذَلِكَ قَيَّدَهُ بِقَوْلِهِ: «عِنْدَنَا»، وَلَمْ يَنْسُبْهُ إِلَى أَهْلِ الحَدِيثِ كَمَا فَعَلَ الخَطَّابِيُّ.
وَبِهَذَا التَّقْرِيرِ يَنْدَفِعُ كَثِيرٌ مِنَ الإِيرَادَاتِ الَّتِي طَالَ البَحْثُ فِيهَا، وَلَمْ يُسْفِرْ وَجْهُ تَوْجِيهِهَا، فَلِلَّهِ الحَمْدُ عَلَى مَا أَلْهَمَ وَعَلَّمَ.

Translation
If it is said: al-Tirmidhi stated explicitly that the condition of hasan is that it be narrated through more than one route, so how can he say of some hadiths, "hasan gharib, we know it only through this route"? The answer is that al-Tirmidhi did not define hasan in general. He defined a particular kind of it found in his book: what he calls "hasan" with no other description. For he says of some hadiths "hasan," of some "sahih," of some "gharib," of some "hasan sahih," of some "hasan gharib," of some "sahih gharib," and of some "hasan sahih gharib."
His definition applies to the first alone, and his wording points to this, since he said at the end of his book: "Whatever we have called in our book a hasan hadith, we meant by it that its chain is hasan in our view: every hadith narrated whose narrator is not accused of lying, that is narrated through more than one route in a similar way, and that is not shadh, is in our view a hasan hadith." So it is known that he defined only what he calls "hasan" alone. What he calls "hasan sahih," "hasan gharib," or "hasan sahih gharib," he did not take up defining, just as he did not take up defining what he calls "sahih" alone or "gharib" alone.
It is as though he left that as unnecessary, since it was well known to the specialists, and limited himself to defining what he calls "hasan" alone in his book, either because it was obscure or because it was a new term. That is why he qualified it with "in our view" and did not attribute it to the hadith scholars, as al-Khattabi did. With this explanation many objections are answered whose discussion went on at length without the way to resolve them becoming clear. Praise be to Allah for what He inspired and taught.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on al-Tirmidhi's definition of hasan.

Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin noted the two uses of "general" here: mutlaqan first means that al-Tirmidhi did not define hasan in general, while "hasan with no other description" means simply the label hasan, with nothing added. The definition does not claim to cover each combination, and the qualifier "in our view" shows he was framing his own usage, not a universally binding definition.

Al-Tirmidhi's unqualified hasan. Three conditions, as he states them: (1) the narrator is not accused of lying; (2) it is narrated through more than one route in a similar way; (3) it is not shadh. This defines only what he labels "hasan" alone, not hasan gharib, hasan sahih, or any other combination.

(1)

A student asked whether these terms were already known in al-Tirmidhi's time. The Shaykh said hasan was known in substance, but was not widely used as an independent grade until al-Tirmidhi helped establish and popularize it (compare Imam Ahmad and the Term "Hasan"). Earlier descriptions tended toward sahih or da'if, and a report between them might be described by which way it leaned. Formal categories became necessary as knowledge thinned and later students needed distinctions spelled out, just as usul al-fiqh was written down though earlier scholars applied it without documenting it, and dots and vowel marks were added to texts. Al-Tirmidhi made an understood distinction explicit; he did not invent the concept from nothing.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that al-Tirmidhi's stated condition for hasan must not be extended to every grading that contains the word hasan.

(2)
