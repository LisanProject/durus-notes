Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim: Comparison and the Seven Ranks

Why Bukhari Is Given Precedence

The highest single category of accepted hadith is what both Bukhari and Muslim narrated in agreement.

مَا اتَّفَقَ الشَّيْخَانِ عَلَى تَخْرِيجِهِ.
What the two Shaykhs (Bukhari and Muslim) agreed upon narrating.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the highest rank of accepted hadith.

The majority of scholars give precedence to Sahih al-Bukhari in terms of soundness, although both collections are fully accepted.

(1)

Imam Bukhari's stricter condition is requiring evidence the narrator met their teacher at least once (liqa'). Imam Muslim is satisfied with contemporaneity (mu'asarah) between narrator and teacher, without requiring proof they actually met.

(2)

An'ana: use of the word "'an" ("from") in a chain instead of an explicit active form like "sami'tu" ("I heard"), which introduces ambiguity about whether the narration was actually heard directly.

(3)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin illustrated an'ana by walking through how it appears in real chains, and explained the problem of tadlis, a narrator concealing a disconnection in the chain by wording it ambiguously rather than admitting he did not hear the report directly. He gave figures for scale: Bukhari's Sahih includes roughly 480 narrators, of whom about 80 have been criticized, while Muslim's Sahih includes roughly 620 narrators, of whom about 160 have been criticized, and he discussed examples of hadith found in both collections versus hadith unique to each.

Bukhari's Conditions Are More Complete and More Exacting

فَصِفَاتُ الَّتِي تَدُورُ عَلَيْهَا الصِّحَّةُ فِي كِتَابِ البُخَارِيِّ أَتَمُّ مِنْهَا فِي كِتَابِ مُسْلِمٍ وَأَشَدُّ، وَشَرْطُهُ فِيهَا أَقْوَى وَأَسَدُّ.
The characteristics upon which soundness is based in al-Bukhari's book are more complete and stricter than in Muslim's, and his condition therein is stronger and more precise.

Ibn Hajar, on the criteria of authenticity in the two Sahihs.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin spent several minutes elaborating on why Bukhari's conditions are considered superior, tying this back to the narrator and criticism figures above. He gave historical context on the movement from oral to written transmission across the early generations, and encouraged approaching these technical details as bearing spiritual weight, not only academic interest.

The Seven Ranks of Accepted Hadith

ثُمَّ يُقَدَّمُ فِي الأَرْجَحِيَّةِ مِنْ حَيْثُ الأَصَحِّيَّةِ مَا وَافَقَهُ شَرْطُهُمَا؛ لِأَنَّ المُرَادَ بِهِ رُوَاتُهُمَا مَعَ بَاقِي شُرُوطِ الصَّحِيحِ.
Then, in terms of preponderance regarding soundness, what agrees with both of their conditions is given precedence, because what is meant by it is their narrators together with the remaining conditions of sahih.

Ibn Hajar, on ranking hadith relative to the two Sahihs.

1. What both Bukhari and Muslim transmitted.

2. What only Bukhari transmitted.

3. What only Muslim transmitted.

4. What meets both their conditions but was not included in either book.

5. What meets only Bukhari's condition.

6. What meets only Muslim's condition.

7. What meets neither of their specific conditions but is still judged sahih on its own merits.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin clarified that works like al-Hakim's al-Mustadrak were compiled specifically to collect hadith meeting the two Shaykhs' conditions without having been included in either of their books, corresponding to rank four above. He also walked through how to "test" whether a hadith found in a collection like al-Tirmidhi's or al-Nasa'i's actually matches Bukhari's or Muslim's criteria, rather than assuming it does simply because it appears in a major collection.

Does This Ranking Apply Beyond the Two Sahihs?

A student asked directly: does this seven-rank system apply only when evaluating Bukhari and Muslim themselves, or does it extend to judging other canonical collections like al-Tirmidhi's?

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin confirmed that it does extend further: later scholars judged narrations in other collections by whether they met the criteria Bukhari and Muslim had established, using those two conditions as the benchmark even outside the two Sahihs themselves. He referenced al-Hakim's al-Mustadrak specifically as evidence of this, since it was built entirely around applying that same standard to material outside the two books.

(1)

Can An'ana Chains Still Reach Mutawatir?

A second question: since an'ana introduces ambiguity about direct hearing, are chains that rely on it permanently capped below mutawatir status?

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin's answer was no, not necessarily: when narrators known to be trustworthy and known not to practice tadlis use "'an," it functions in practice the same as if they had specified the mode of narration explicitly. Mutawatir status can still be reached through such chains if the isnad's integrity and the narrators' reliability are independently established, the ambiguity of the wording itself is not an automatic ceiling.

(1)

A Note on Scholarly Humility

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin closed with a comment on how Bukhari and Muslim related to each other as scholars. Although Bukhari is technically considered superior in his conditions, benefit and knowledge flowed both ways between them, and no single scholar stands above learning from another. He stressed that technical debates over which collection is stronger should never overshadow humility and gratitude toward all the hadith scholars who preserved this material. He referenced classical expressions of humility from Ibn Hajar and al-Daraqutni as models of the etiquette expected in this field.
