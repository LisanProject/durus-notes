Hasan li-Dhatihi, and Sahih li-Ghayrihi Through Many Routes

"If the Precision Is Lighter"

With the ranks of sahih complete, the matn turns to the next grade. A student read the passage and Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained Ibn Hajar's wording.

Text
(فَإِنْ خَفَّ الضَّبْطُ) أَيْ: قَلَّ، يُقَالُ: خَفَّ القَوْمُ خُفُوفاً: قَلُّوا، وَالمُرَادُ: مَعَ بَقِيَّةِ الشُّرُوطِ المُتَقَدِّمَةِ فِي حَدِّ الصَّحِيحِ؛ (فَـ)هُوَ: (الحَسَنُ لِذَاتِهِ) لَا لِشَيْءٍ خَارِجٍ، وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَكُونُ حُسْنُهُ بِسَبَبِ الاعْتِضَادِ، نَحْوُ حَدِيثِ المَسْتُورِ إِذَا تَعَدَّدَتْ طُرُقُهُ.
وَخَرَجَ بِاشْتِرَاطِ بَاقِي الأَوْصَافِ: الضَّعِيفُ.
Vocabulary
  • فَإِنْ خَفَّso if it is lighter
  • الضَّبْطُthe precision
  • أَيْ: قَلَّmeaning, it is less
  • يُقَالُit is said
  • خَفَّ القَوْمُ خُفُوفاًthe people became few
  • قَلُّواthey were few
  • وَالمُرَادُand what is meant
  • مَعَ بَقِيَّةِ الشُّرُوطِwith the rest of the conditions
  • المُتَقَدِّمَةِalready stated
  • فِي حَدِّ الصَّحِيحِin the definition of sahih
  • فَهُوَ الحَسَنُ لِذَاتِهِthen it is hasan in itself
  • لَا لِشَيْءٍ خَارِجٍnot because of something external
  • وَهُوَ الَّذِيand that (external case) is the one
  • يَكُونُ حُسْنُهُwhose being hasan is
  • بِسَبَبِ الاعْتِضَادِbecause of corroboration
  • نَحْوُsuch as
  • حَدِيثِ المَسْتُورِthe report of a mastur, a partly unknown narrator
  • إِذَا تَعَدَّدَتْ طُرُقُهُwhen its routes are multiple
  • وَخَرَجَand there is excluded
  • بِاشْتِرَاطِby stipulating
  • بَاقِي الأَوْصَافِthe remaining qualities
  • الضَّعِيفُthe weak report
Translation
"So if the precision is lighter," meaning less (one says "the people became khafif" meaning they became few), and what is meant is: together with the remaining conditions already stated in the definition of sahih, "then it is hasan li-dhatihi," not because of something external. That external case is the one whose hasan status comes from corroboration, such as the report of a mastur narrator when its routes are multiple. By stipulating the remaining qualities, the weak report is excluded.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, defining hasan li-dhatihi.

Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained that khaffa al-dabt describes the narrator's level of precision as lighter. It does not mean a chain that was strong and then weakened; it describes the state of the isnad. Everything else in the definition of sahih is still present. The lexical note about a people "becoming few" is an Arabic explanation of khaffa and adds little once rendered in English.

Ibn Hajar's "not because of something external" is what separates hasan li-dhatihi from hasan li-ghayrihi. The first is hasan by its own characteristics; the second gets its strength from outside, such as corroborating routes.

Mastur. Linguistically, covered or hidden, the same root used in fiqh for covering the 'awrah. Technically, a narrator who is partly obscured.

Two situations. (1) There is not enough information about the narrator's character and reliability, though enough to see some potential for truthfulness. (2) The chain does not make clear which person is meant. The Shaykh's example: several narrators from Naysabur in the same generation with similar names or descriptions, one strong and one weak, and a student who narrated from more than one of them. If the abbreviated isnad does not establish which is meant, the report stays weak for now.

When multiple routes support a mastur's report, they can corroborate one another and raise its standing.

(1)

Why "The Remaining Qualities" Is There: Jami' and Mani'

The final line, "by stipulating the remaining qualities, the weak report is excluded," shows Ibn Hajar building the definition to keep foreign things out. The Shaykh explained that a sound technical definition must be jami' (inclusive, taking in every member of what is defined) and mani' (exclusive, keeping out what does not belong), balancing the genus with the distinguishing characteristics (see Genus and Differentia, Illustrated).

His examples were definitions of the human being that wrongly leave out women, non-Arabs, or non-white people, or that wrongly let animals in as if they belonged to the same category. A definition that fails either way is defective. Here, requiring the rest of the sahih conditions is the ihtiraz, the precautionary clause, that keeps the weak report outside hasan.

Jami' wa Mani'. Inclusive and exclusive: a definition takes in all of its subject and nothing foreign to it.

Ihtiraz. A clause in a definition placed to exclude something.

(1)

The Isnad Does Not Change; the Judgment Does

The Shaykh cautioned that an isnad does not change status in itself. What changes is the scholars' judgment as they examine more evidence and find more routes. A judgment of weakness reflects the evidence available at the time and is not necessarily final; a report judged da'if may later be raised, especially to hasan. By contrast, it is rare for an established sahih or hasan judgment by the leading hadith scholars, or by their broad agreement, to be downgraded, because they scrutinize the evidence before judging anything hasan, sahih, or mutawatir.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that a weak report is not banished from all evidentiary value. It retains the possibility that the Prophet ﷺ said it, even though its present probability is judged weak, and many reports first considered weak were later judged otherwise.

Hasan Shares Sahih's Authority, and Its Ranks

وَهَذَا القِسْمُ مِنَ الحَسَنِ مُشَارِكٌ لِلصَّحِيحِ فِي الاحْتِجَاجِ بِهِ - وَإِنْ كَانَ دُونَهُ -، وَمُشَابِهٌ لَهُ فِي انْقِسَامِهِ إِلَى مَرَاتِبَ بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَهَذَا القِسْمُand this category
  • مِنَ الحَسَنِof hasan
  • مُشَارِكٌ لِلصَّحِيحِshares with sahih
  • فِي الاحْتِجَاجِ بِهِin being used as proof
  • وَإِنْ كَانَ دُونَهُeven though it is below it
  • وَمُشَابِهٌ لَهُand resembles it
  • فِي انْقِسَامِهِin its being divided
  • إِلَى مَرَاتِبَinto ranks
  • بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍsome above others
Translation
This category of hasan shares with sahih its use as proof, even though it is below it, and resembles it in being divided into ranks, some above others.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the standing of hasan li-dhatihi.

Commentary

Hasan is authoritative evidence, as sahih is, though weaker. It is not rejected for being below sahih, and its acceptance does not erase differences of strength: some hasan reports sit close to sahih, others near the lower edge of acceptability. A narrator at that lower edge would be judged weak if his precision dropped one more degree.

A student asked why Ibn Hajar singles out precision, as if the other conditions were all-or-nothing. The Shaykh said they too have degrees. There are levels within mutawatir and within sahih, and among narrators: the taqwa, wara', and 'adalah of the Companions are generally higher than those of the Tabi'un, and theirs higher than the generation after, with individual exceptions. Hasan keeps the other conditions and is marked by precision that ranges from nearly that of sahih down to lighter but still accepted.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes a balance between two errors: pushing hasan down and calling it weak because it is below sahih, and pushing it up so that real differences of strength disappear. A difference in strength is not a difference in acceptability.

(1)

Hasan in 'Aqidah

A student asked whether a hasan hadith can be used in matters of belief, or needs something stronger. The Shaykh said hasan reports can be used in the branches of 'aqidah and in the Sharia. For foundational matters tied to iman and kufr, the scholars require mutawatir evidence. A non-mutawatir report should not by itself be used to declare someone a disbeliever for rejecting a conclusion derived from it, and denying a report is different from disputing an interpretation drawn from it.

Branches versus foundations. Whether the Prophet's ﷺ ascension was bodily or spiritual is a branch, a matter of detail. The believers seeing Allah in Paradise is a foundational matter. Detailed disagreements must not be confused with foundational articles of faith.

(1)

In the same answer he distinguished respect for the Companions, a Sharia obligation rooted in respect for the Prophet ﷺ, from treating criticism of an individual Companion as automatically disbelief. The Companions were human and could err. Denouncing the Companions as a whole is a different matter, because it implicates the Prophet's ﷺ own standing. He warned against sectarianism in every camp: a Sunni label or outward religious identity does not excuse attacking Muslims or making careless accusations.

Riwayah Is Not Dirayah

The class also discussed transmitting versus understanding. A narrator authorized only to transmit the wording should preserve it and not paraphrase or interpret beyond that permission. The Shaykh distinguished three things: transmitting the letters of the Quran or Sunnah, explaining a text's general meaning, and deriving practical rulings from it.

Ijazah for riwayah is permission to relay the wording. It does not by itself authorize interpreting the text or giving fatwa. Someone with an ijazah in Quran recitation does not thereby have authority to explain its meanings; someone authorized to transmit the Arba'in may have permission only to relay it.

Ijazah for riwayah and dirayah is a broader permission covering both transmission and understanding.

(1)

Scholars of usul al-fiqh treat narration by meaning with more nuance. An exceptionally qualified scholar who knows the meaning and the limits of paraphrase may transmit by meaning without compromising the substance. The Shaykh named al-Shafi'i, Malik, Abu Hanifa, al-Awza'i, Abu Thawr, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal as scholars whose paraphrasing, had they done it, would not lower a report's precision, given their knowledge and care. He was not claiming any of them actually did so in a given case. Less qualified narrators should keep to the wording. Ijtihad itself has levels: al-Nawawi is said to have described five or six, later scholars subdivided further, and the Shaykh cited al-Suyuti as describing nine.

Grading does not end the process. After a report is found sahih or hasan, scholars must weigh it against other evidence, reconcile, check for conflict and abrogation, distinguish general from specific, and see how it informed practice. A sahih report may not have shaped the practice of the Companions and later scholars while remaining authoritative in itself (see Accepted, But Not Acted Upon).

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that authorization to transmit wording is not authorization to interpret or to give rulings, and that finding a hadith in a collection is not the end of evaluating it.

"Through Many Routes It Is Judged Sahih"

(وَبِكَثْرَةِ طُرُقِهِ يُصَحَّحُ)، وَإِنَّمَا يُحْكَمُ لَهُ بِالصِّحَّةِ عِنْدَ تَعَدُّدِ الطُّرُقِ؛ لِأَنَّ لِلصُّورَةِ المَجْمُوعَةِ قُوَّةً تَجْبُرُ القَدْرَ الَّذِي قَصُرَ بِهِ ضَبْطُ رَاوِي الحَسَنِ عَنْ رَاوِي الصَّحِيحِ، وَمِنْ ثَمَّ تُطْلَقُ الصِّحَّةُ عَلَى الإِسْنَادِ الَّذِي يَكُونُ حَسَناً لِذَاتِهِ لَوْ تَفَرَّدَ إِذَا تَعَدَّدَ.
Vocabulary
  • وَبِكَثْرَةِ طُرُقِهِand through the multiplicity of its routes
  • يُصَحَّحُit is judged sahih
  • وَإِنَّمَا يُحْكَمُ لَهُit is only judged
  • بِالصِّحَّةِto be sound
  • عِنْدَ تَعَدُّدِ الطُّرُقِwhen the routes are multiple
  • لِأَنَّ لِلصُّورَةِ المَجْمُوعَةِbecause the combined configuration has
  • قُوَّةًa strength
  • تَجْبُرُthat makes up for
  • القَدْرَthe amount
  • الَّذِي قَصُرَ بِهِby which fell short
  • ضَبْطُ رَاوِي الحَسَنِthe precision of the hasan narrator
  • عَنْ رَاوِي الصَّحِيحِfrom the sahih narrator
  • وَمِنْ ثَمَّand for this reason
  • تُطْلَقُ الصِّحَّةُsoundness is applied
  • عَلَى الإِسْنَادِto the chain
  • الَّذِي يَكُونُ حَسَناً لِذَاتِهِthat would be hasan in itself
  • لَوْ تَفَرَّدَhad it stood alone
  • إِذَا تَعَدَّدَwhen it is multiplied
Translation
"And through the multiplicity of its routes it is judged sahih." It is judged sound only when the routes are multiple, because the combined configuration has a strength that makes up for the amount by which the precision of the hasan narrator fell short of the sahih narrator's. For this reason, soundness is applied to a chain that would be hasan li-dhatihi had it stood alone, once it is multiplied.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on sahih li-ghayrihi.

Commentary

This is how hasan li-dhatihi rises to sahih li-ghayrihi. The routes are transmissions of the same hadith, each of which would be hasan li-dhatihi on its own. Taken together, their corroborating strength makes up for the gap between the hasan narrator's precision and what is expected of a sahih narrator.

The key phrase is al-surah al-majmu'ah, the combined configuration: the bigger picture, not a change in any individual route. When a student asked him to explain the sentence again, the Shaykh restated it simply and gave an analogy: a group can have a strength that none of its members has alone, and the routes together have a corroborating strength none has by itself.

Al-Surah al-Majmu'ah. The routes considered together, whose shared strength can make up for one narrator's lighter precision.

Sahih li-Ghayrihi. A hadith whose routes would each be hasan li-dhatihi, raised to sahih by their combined strength.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that the route itself does not change. The judgment on the hadith changes because the routes are considered together. (Compare The Four Grades Revisited.)
