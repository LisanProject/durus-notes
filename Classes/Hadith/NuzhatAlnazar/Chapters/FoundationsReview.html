Asking Why

In fiqh, ta'lil asks why a ruling holds. In mustalah the same habit asks why a term is drawn where it is, why a clause sits in a definition, and what a judgment rests on. Try to answer each before opening the reason.

Why did the early works leave so much undefined? They were written for peers and advanced students who already knew the vocabulary. Definitions became explicit when the audience widened, which is why Ibn Hajar wrote a primer and then a commentary on it.

(1)

Why does the usuli definition of sunnah leave out the Prophet's ﷺ physical description and character? Because the usuli is asking what can ground a legal ruling. Those reports are still Sunnah for the muhaddith, whose question is what is attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. The definition follows the discipline's question.

(2)

Why is a transmitted report judged by its isnad and not by whether it seems plausible? Because naqli knowledge reaches us through people, so its truth depends on the reliability of those people. Abu Bakr's acceptance of the Isra' rested on the truthfulness of the one reporting it. Rejecting a well-established report out of discomfort uses the wrong criterion.

(3)

Why is an ijazah from someone who disparages the author's core beliefs only "literal"? Because ijazah is meant to carry a chain of understanding. Constant opposition to the source conveys the words while cutting the understanding the chain exists to carry.

(4)

Why doesn't the lack of an aqidah book by al-Nawawi mean he lacked expertise in it? Because classical scholars were authorities across several sciences at once; what someone wrote is not the measure of what he mastered.

(5)

Commonly Confused

PairThe difference
Khabar vs. hadithUsually synonyms; only on the minority view is khabar the broader term for reports about others.
Sunnah (hadith) vs. Sunnah (usul)Same word, different scope: everything attributed vs. what grounds rulings.
Naqli vs. 'aqliThe classification tracks how the knowledge was reached, not its subject.
Ijazah in wording vs. in understandingPermission to relay the words is not authority to explain them (see Riwayah Is Not Dirayah).

Case 1A report describes the Prophet's ﷺ height and complexion. A student says it "is not Sunnah" because no ruling follows from it. Is he right?

Only in the usuli sense. For the muhaddith, physical description (khalqiyyah) is part of the Sunnah. He has applied one discipline's definition as if it were the only one.

Case 2Why would a beginner find Ibn al-Salah's Muqaddimah repetitive in places, even though it became the standard reference?

Because it was dictated across separate sessions and compiled afterward by students, not written straight through as one planned text.

Case 3Someone accepts a weak report because it feels right, and rejects a sound one because it feels wrong. What single error explains both?

Judging naqli knowledge by personal reaction instead of by the reliability of its transmission.

Questions to Bring to the Shaykh

  • When later scholars made definitions explicit, did any of them change what earlier scholars meant by a term, rather than only spelling it out?
  • Is there a minimum of shared method and belief below which an ijazah should not be sought from a teacher?
  • Where does athar used for a respected Successor's statement sit relative to a mawquf report?
