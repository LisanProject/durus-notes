The Conditions of Sahih li-Dhatihi Explained

'Adl and Taqwa

وَالمُرَادُ بِالعَدْلِ: مَنْ لَهُ مَلَكَةٌ تَحْمِلُهُ عَلَى مُلَازَمَةِ التَّقْوَى وَالمُرُوءَةِ. وَالمُرَادُ بِالتَّقْوَى: اجْتِنَابُ الأَعْمَالِ السَّيِّئَةِ - مِنْ شِرْكٍ أَوْ فِسْقٍ أَوْ بِدْعَةٍ.
What is meant by 'adl: one who has a disposition carrying him to consistently maintain taqwa and muru'ah. What is meant by taqwa: avoiding evil deeds, whether shirk, fisq, or bid'ah.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on 'adl and taqwa.

Muru'ah is the companion quality to taqwa: the dignified comportment expected of an upright person, distinct from ritual piety but bound up with it in practice.

'Adl Is Not Merely the Absence of Major Sin: Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin stressed that 'adl includes both deep God-consciousness and social uprightness together, not simply the fact that a narrator has avoided major sin. A narrator's 'adalah rests on taqwa and muru'ah as a joint disposition, not either one alone.

(1)

The Two Kinds of Dabt

ضَبْطُ صَدْرٍ: وَهُوَ أَنْ يُثْبِتَ مَا سَمِعَهُ؛ بِحَيْثُ يَتَمَكَّنُ مِنِ اسْتِحْضَارِهِ مَتَى شَاءَ. وَضَبْطُ كِتَابٍ: وَهُوَ صِيَانَتُهُ لَدَيْهِ مُنْذُ سَمِعَ فِيهِ وَصَحَّحَهُ إِلَى أَنْ يُؤَدِّيَ مِنْهُ.
Dabt sadr: firmly retaining what one heard, such that one can recall it at will. Dabt kitab: safeguarding the written record from the time of hearing and verifying it until it is transmitted from.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on the two kinds of dabt.

Dabt Sadr is precision of memory, the narrator recalling the hadith from his own retention.

(1)

Dabt Kitab is precision of the written record. Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained this as keeping one's book as accurate as what the teacher actually spoke, never letting an error slip in through neglect between hearing it and transmitting from it.

(2)

Ittisal, Ta'lil, and Shudhudh

وَالمُتَّصِلُ: مَا سَلِمَ إِسْنَادُهُ مِنْ سُقُوطٍ فِيهِ، بِحَيْثُ يَكُونُ كُلٌّ مِنْ رِجَالِهِ سَمِعَ ذَلِكَ المَرْوِيَّ مِنْ شَيْخِهِ. وَالمُعَلَّلُ لُغَةً: مَا فِيهِ عِلَّةٌ، وَاصْطِلَاحاً: مَا فِيهِ عِلَّةٌ خَفِيَّةٌ قَادِحَةٌ. وَالشَّاذُّ لُغَةً: المُنْفَرِدُ، وَاصْطِلَاحاً: مَا يُخَالِفُ فِيهِ الرَّاوِي مَنْ هُوَ أَرْجَحُ مِنْهُ.
Al-muttasil: that whose isnad is free of any drop in it, such that every one of its narrators heard what he narrates from his teacher. Al-mu'allal linguistically: that which has a defect; technically: that which has a hidden, disqualifying defect. Al-shadh linguistically: the solitary one; technically: a narrator going against someone more authoritative than himself.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on ittisal, ta'lil, and shudhudh.

Al-muttasil (connected) means the chain has no drop anywhere in it. On this, the Shaykh noted that any drop in the isnad voids muttasil status entirely, even if only a single narrator is missing at a single point in the chain, there is no partial credit for an otherwise-complete chain.

Al-mu'allal is linguistically simply "that which has a defect," but the Shaykh was specific that not every visible flaw qualifies: the defect has to be subtle and genuinely damaging to the chain, an 'illah khafiyyah qadihah, not any flaw whatsoever, and certainly not an obvious one that would already be caught by checking the other conditions.

Al-shadh is linguistically "the solitary one," which invites confusion with gharaba discussed earlier in this chapter. The Shaykh addressed this directly: shadh is not about number of narrators at all, it is about a narrator, however many people narrated alongside him, going against someone more authoritative than himself.

Two Senses of "Alone": gharaba is about how many people narrated a report. Shudhudh is a comparative failure entirely separate from that count, a narrator being outweighed by someone more reliable who narrated the same report differently, regardless of how many people narrated either version.

(1)

Five Conditions, or Six?

Some accounts of sahih li-dhatihi list five conditions rather than six, treating dabt as one undivided requirement. A student asked directly why dabt sadr and dabt kitab are kept separate here.

Why the Two Kinds of Dabt Are Kept Separate: Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin's answer was that separating them avoids conflation and highlights a real technical difference between the two. Memorization reliability and written/documentary reliability are different things, and both have to be separately checked before a narrator's transmission can be trusted with full confidence. Folding them into one condition risks treating a narrator as fully dabit when only one of the two has actually been verified.

(1)

The same logic applies to keeping mu'allal and shadh distinct rather than combining them into a single closing caveat: each is checking for a different kind of failure ('illah is hidden and internal to the chain, shudhudh is comparative and requires outside knowledge), and treating them as one condition would obscure that distinction the same way merging the two kinds of dabt would.
