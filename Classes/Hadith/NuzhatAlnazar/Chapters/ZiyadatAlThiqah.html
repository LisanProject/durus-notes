Ziyadat al-Thiqah: Additions by a Reliable Narrator

When Is an Addition Accepted?

Sometimes one route of a hadith contains wording or material that another route of the same hadith does not. Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained ziyadah as exactly this: something found in one route and absent from another. Ibn Hajar now gives the rule for additions made by the narrator of a sahih or hasan report.

Text
(وَزِيَادَةُ رَاوِيهِمَا) أَيِ: الصَّحِيحِ وَالحَسَنِ (مَقْبُولَةٌ، مَا لَمْ تَقَعْ مُنَافِيَةً لِـ)رِوَايَةِ (مَنْ هُوَ أَوْثَقُ) مِمَّنْ لَمْ يَذْكُرْ تِلْكَ الزِّيَادَةَ.
لِأَنَّ الزِّيَادَةَ إِمَّا أَنْ تَكُونَ لَا تَنَافِيَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ رِوَايَةِ مَنْ لَمْ يَذْكُرْهَا؛ فَهَذِهِ تُقْبَلُ مُطْلَقاً؛ لِأَنَّهَا فِي حُكْمِ الحَدِيثِ المُسْتَقِلِّ الَّذِي يَنْفَرِدُ بِهِ الثِّقَةُ وَلَا يَرْوِيهِ عَنْ شَيْخِهِ غَيْرُهُ.
وَإِمَّا أَنْ تَكُونَ مُنَافِيَةً بِحَيْثُ يَلْزَمُ مِنْ قَبُولِهَا رَدُّ الرِّوَايَةِ الأُخْرَى؛ فَهَذِهِ الَّتِي يَقَعُ التَّرْجِيحُ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ مُعَارِضِهَا، فَيُقْبَلُ الرَّاجِحُ وَيُرَدُّ المَرْجُوحُ.
وَاشْتَهَرَ عَنْ جَمْعٍ مِنَ العُلَمَاءِ القَوْلُ بِقَبُولِ الزِّيَادَةِ مُطْلَقاً مِنْ غَيْرِ تَفْصِيلٍ، وَلَا يَتَأَتَّى ذَلِكَ عَلَى طَرِيقِ المُحَدِّثِينَ الَّذِينَ يَشْتَرِطُونَ فِي الصَّحِيحِ أَنْ لَا يَكُونَ شَاذّاً، ثُمَّ يُفَسِّرُونَ الشُّذُوذَ بِمُخَالَفَةِ الثِّقَةِ مَنْ هُوَ أَوْثَقُ مِنْهُ.

Translation
"And the addition of their narrator," that is, of the sahih and the hasan, "is accepted, so long as it does not conflict with" the narration "of one more reliable" than the one who did not mention that addition. For an addition either has no conflict with the narration of the one who did not mention it, in which case it is accepted without restriction, since it has the ruling of an independent hadith that a reliable narrator transmits alone and no one else narrates from his shaykh. Or it conflicts, such that accepting it entails rejecting the other narration; this is where it is weighed against what opposes it, and the preponderant is accepted and the outweighed rejected.
It became well known from a group of scholars that additions are accepted without restriction and without distinguishing cases. That is not consistent with the method of the hadith scholars, who stipulate that a sahih report not be shadh, and then explain shudhudh as a reliable narrator contradicting someone more reliable than he.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, on ziyadat al-thiqah.

Commentary
Kind of additionTestRuling
Non-conflictingIt can coexist with the narration that omits it.Accepted, like an independent hadith a reliable narrator transmits alone.
ConflictingAccepting it would require rejecting the other narration.Weighed (tarjih): the stronger is accepted and the weaker rejected in the point of conflict.

The Shaykh stressed that the question is not merely whether one narrator includes wording another leaves out. The real question is whether accepting the extra wording necessarily means rejecting the other narration. He said unrestricted acceptance of every addition is not known from the early hadith imams, who compared reports and weighed them, additions included, and he described unrestricted acceptance as a later minority position. (The editor's footnote notes that al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, in al-Kifayah, attributed that position to the majority of jurists and hadith scholars; Ibn Hajar's point is that it does not fit the hadith scholars' own condition of no shudhudh.)

Ziyadah. Additional material in one route that another route does not mention.

Munafiyah (Conflicting). An addition such that accepting it would require rejecting the competing narration.

Rajih and Marjuh. The preponderant narration, which is accepted, and the outweighed one, which is rejected in the point of conflict.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that an addition is not rejected just because another route omits it. First ask whether it can coexist with the other report; only real conflict calls for weighing.

What Counts as an Addition, and What Counts as Conflict

In the next lesson the class translated this passage in full. A student had rendered "ziyadatu rawihima" as "an addition made by the two"; the Shaykh corrected it to an addition made by a narrator of either of the two categories, sahih or hasan.

Where additions appear. Additions commonly surface when the larger collections preserve several versions of one report. An addition is not limited to the matn: it can be an extra word or clause, an added name or attribution, an additional narrator, or an added link in the isnad.

(1)

Example of a compatible addition. One reliable version says the Prophet ﷺ told the Companions to do items one and two; another reliable version gives one, two, and three. If the third item does not negate the shorter account, it is accepted as an independent report, even if only that narrator transmits it from his teacher.

(2)

The bar for "conflict" is strict. Merely adding information is not a contradiction. The two versions must be unable to coexist coherently, so that accepting one requires rejecting the other. If any coherent way exists for both to stand, they are understood that way before either is rejected. Equally, a narrator whose reports are generally accepted does not have every addition accepted automatically.

When there is real conflict, the comparison can weigh the narrators' precision, which student better preserved a particular teacher, the strength and number of corroborating routes, a narrator's specialization in that teacher's hadith, whether he preserved a written book, and other indicators. A larger number of narrators is not automatically decisive; reliability and the specific evidence matter.

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that rejecting one addition does not make its narrator untrustworthy. A narrator can be reliable in general and still err in a particular detail, or that detail may lack sufficient support.

(3)

The Early Imams, and al-Shafi'i's Own Words

وَالعَجَبُ مِمَّنْ أَغْفَلَ ذَلِكَ مِنْهُمْ، مَعَ اعْتِرَافِهِ بِاشْتِرَاطِ انْتِفَاءِ الشُّذُوذِ فِي حَدِّ الحَدِيثِ الصَّحِيحِ، وَكَذَا الحَسَنُ.
وَالمَنْقُولُ عَنْ أَئِمَّةِ الحَدِيثِ المُتَقَدِّمِينَ - كَعَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ مَهْدِيٍّ، وَيَحْيَى القَطَّانِ، وَأَحْمَدَ بْنِ حَنْبَلٍ، وَيَحْيَى بْنِ مَعِينٍ، وَعَلِيِّ بْنِ المَدِينِيِّ، وَالبُخَارِيِّ، وَأَبِي زُرْعَةَ، وَأَبِي حَاتِمٍ، وَالنَّسَائِيِّ، وَالدَّارَقُطْنِيِّ، وَغَيْرِهِمْ - اعْتِبَارُ التَّرْجِيحِ فِيمَا يَتَعَلَّقُ بِالزِّيَادَةِ وَغَيْرِهَا، وَلَا يُعْرَفُ عَنْ أَحَدٍ مِنْهُمْ إِطْلَاقُ قَبُولِ الزِّيَادَةِ.
وَأَعْجَبُ مِنْ ذَلِكَ: إِطْلَاقُ كَثِيرٍ مِنَ الشَّافِعِيَّةِ القَوْلَ بِقَبُولِ زِيَادَةِ الثِّقَةِ، مَعَ أَنَّ نَصَّ الشَّافِعِيِّ يَدُلُّ عَلَى غَيْرِ ذَلِكَ؛ فَإِنَّهُ قَالَ فِي أَثْنَاءِ كَلَامِهِ عَلَى مَا يُعْتَبَرُ بِهِ حَالُ الرَّاوِي فِي الضَّبْطِ مَا نَصُّهُ: «وَيَكُونُ إِذَا شَرِكَ أَحَداً مِنَ الحُفَّاظِ لَمْ يُخَالِفْهُ، فَإِنْ خَالَفَهُ فَوُجِدَ حَدِيثُهُ أَنْقَصَ؛ كَانَ فِي ذَلِكَ دَلِيلٌ عَلَى صِحَّةِ مَخْرَجِ حَدِيثِهِ، وَمَتَى خَالَفَ مَا وَصَفْتُ أَضَرَّ ذَلِكَ بِحَدِيثِهِ». انْتَهَى كَلَامُهُ.
وَمُقْتَضَاهُ: أَنَّهُ إِذَا خَالَفَ فَوُجِدَ حَدِيثُهُ أَزْيَدَ أَضَرَّ ذَلِكَ بِحَدِيثِهِ؛ فَدَلَّ عَلَى أَنَّ زِيَادَةَ العَدْلِ عِنْدَهُ لَا يَلْزَمُ قَبُولُهَا مُطْلَقاً، وَإِنَّمَا تُقْبَلُ مِنَ الحَافِظِ؛ فَإِنَّهُ اعْتَبَرَ أَنْ يَكُونَ حَدِيثُ هَذَا المُخَالِفِ أَنْقَصَ مِنْ حَدِيثِ مَنْ خَالَفَهُ مِنَ الحُفَّاظِ، وَجَعَلَ نُقْصَانَ هَذَا الرَّاوِي مِنَ الحَدِيثِ دَلِيلاً عَلَى صِحَّتِهِ؛ لِأَنَّهُ يَدُلُّ عَلَى تَحَرِّيهِ، وَجَعَلَ مَا عَدَا ذَلِكَ مُضِرّاً بِحَدِيثِهِ، فَدَخَلَتْ فِيهِ الزِّيَادَةُ، فَلَوْ كَانَتْ عِنْدَهُ مَقْبُولَةً مُطْلَقاً؛ لَمْ تَكُنْ مُضِرَّةً بِحَدِيثِ صَاحِبِهَا، وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ.

Translation
What is astonishing is whoever among them overlooked this while acknowledging that the absence of shudhudh is a condition in the definition of the sahih hadith, and likewise the hasan. What is transmitted from the early hadith imams, such as 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Yahya al-Qattan, Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Yahya ibn Ma'in, 'Ali ibn al-Madini, al-Bukhari, Abu Zur'ah, Abu Hatim, al-Nasa'i, al-Daraqutni, and others, is that they took weighing into account regarding additions and other matters, and unrestricted acceptance of additions is not known from any of them.
More astonishing still is that many Shafi'is state without qualification that the addition of a reliable narrator is accepted, although al-Shafi'i's own text indicates otherwise. In discussing how a narrator's precision is assessed, he said: "And when he shares a narration with one of the huffaz, he does not contradict him. If he differs from him and his hadith is found to be shorter, that is evidence of the soundness of the source of his hadith; and whenever he departs from what I have described, that harms his hadith."
Its implication is that if he differs and his hadith is found to be longer, that harms his hadith. This shows that, for al-Shafi'i, an upright narrator's addition need not be accepted unconditionally; it is accepted from the hafiz. For he required that the differing narrator's hadith be shorter than that of the hafiz he differed from, made the shortfall evidence of its soundness because it shows his carefulness, and made anything else harmful to his hadith, which includes the addition. Had additions been accepted unconditionally in his view, they would not harm the hadith of the one who made them. And Allah knows best.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, citing al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah on additions.

Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin explained that unrestricted acceptance of every addition would empty the condition of "no shudhudh" of any meaning. The early imams Ibn Hajar names compared versions and weighed them. The Shaykh acknowledged that those who held the opposite view had their reasons, while maintaining that the position is wrong.

On al-Shafi'i's criterion: narrating alongside an established hafiz without contradicting him supports a narrator's precision. A shorter version may mean he carried part of the reliable report without introducing anything conflicting. A longer version that conflicts with the established huffaz calls for scrutiny and can harm the report. This does not mean every longer version is rejected automatically; it means the conflict has to be investigated.

Huffaz. Established hadith preservers, a technical rank in hadith studies.

Makhraj al-hadith. The source or route a report comes from.

A Narrowing Phrase: Conflict or Clarification?

A student asked: if one report adds a phrase that narrows who a ruling applies to, while several other narrators give a more general wording, is the addition shadh or accepted? The Shaykh answered that it is not automatically a contradiction. The addition may build on the broader narration rather than negate it, and then it can be accepted.

He cautioned that this is more nuanced in usul al-fiqh. A qualifying phrase in one narration may apply to a particular event or circumstance, clarify a general ruling, or emphasize one aspect, without acting as a universal qualification. The legal reading has to examine the circumstances and scope of the wording rather than mechanically treating every extra phrase as a conflict (compare Accepted, But Not Acted Upon).

Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin emphasizes that an added qualification may clarify or apply to a specific circumstance, so its legal implications need more careful analysis than a simple comparison of wording.
