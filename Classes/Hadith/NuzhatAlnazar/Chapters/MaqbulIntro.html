Chapter 3: The Accepted Hadith (al-Maqbul)

Having divided the khabar by its routes, the matn turns to the accepted report. It defines sahih li-dhatihi, explains each of its conditions, shows how its ranks differ as those conditions grow stronger or weaker, and from that derives the precedence of the two Sahihs. Hasan and the other divisions of the maqbul follow as the lessons continue.

Here is a list of the sections covered in this chapter.

Sahih li-Dhatihi and the Divisions of Accepted Hadith covers the definition of sahih li-dhatihi and the four-way division of al-maqbul into sahih and hasan, each li-dhatihi or li-ghayrihi.

The Conditions of Sahih li-Dhatihi Explained covers 'adalah, the two kinds of dabt, ittisal, freedom from 'illah and shudhudh, and whether there are five conditions or six.

Asahh al-Asanid: The Golden Chains and the Ranks of Sahih li-Dhatihi covers how the ranks of sahih differ with its conditions, and the three descending tiers of the soundest chains.

Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim: Comparison and the Ranks of Sahih covers why al-Bukhari is given precedence, an'anah and tadlis, and the seven-rank hierarchy of accepted hadith.

The Four Grades Revisited: Intrinsic and External Strength covers Ibn Hajar's four-way division in his own words, what can rise from li-dhatihi to li-ghayrihi, sahih al-isnad versus sahih al-hadith, and the ranks of da'if.

When the Outranked Outranks: Relative Strength and Hadith in Fiqh covers when a lower rank is preferred over a higher one, accepted reports not acted upon, and weak hadith across the four madhhabs.

Hasan li-Dhatihi, and Sahih li-Ghayrihi Through Many Routes covers Ibn Hajar's definition of hasan li-dhatihi, the mastur narrator, hasan's authority and ranks, and how multiple hasan routes become sahih li-ghayrihi.

"Hasan Sahih" and al-Tirmidhi's Definition of Hasan covers the two meanings of hasan sahih, with one route or several, and why al-Tirmidhi's definition covers only his unqualified hasan.

Ziyadat al-Thiqah: Additions by a Reliable Narrator covers when an addition in one route is accepted and when it must be weighed against a more reliable narration.

Mahfuz and Shadh, Ma'ruf and Munkar covers the names for the preferred and outweighed sides of a conflict, depending on whether the outweighed narrator is acceptable or weak.

Review of Chapter 3 gathers the chapter's grades, ranks, and terms, with the reasons behind each definition and practice cases.

More sections will be added here as the lessons continue.
