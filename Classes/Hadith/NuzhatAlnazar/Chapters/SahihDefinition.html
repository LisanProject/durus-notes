Sahih li-Dhatihi and the Divisions of Accepted Hadith

A New Axis: From How Many Narrated, to How Reliable

With gharaba covered, the matn turns to a different axis of classification entirely: not how many people narrated a report, but how reliable the report is. This is where the accepted hadith (al-maqbul) first splits into its four types.

وَخَبَرُ الآحَادِ بِنَقْلِ عَدْلٍ تَامِّ الضَّبْطِ، مُتَّصِلِ السَّنَدِ، غَيْرِ مُعَلَّلٍ وَلَا شَاذٍّ، وَهَذَا هُوَ الصَّحِيحُ لِذَاتِهِ.
A khabar ahad transmitted by an upright narrator of complete precision, with a connected chain, free of hidden defect and not anomalous: this is the sahih li-dhatihi, sound in and of itself.

Ibn Hajar, Nuzhat al-Nazar, the definition of sahih li-dhatihi.

Sahih li-Dhatihi: has every condition below at its full strength on its own.

(1)

Sahih li-Ghayrihi: falls short somewhere, but something external compensates, most commonly multiple corroborating routes.

(2)

Hasan li-Dhatihi: falls short with nothing to compensate for it.

(3)

Hasan li-Ghayrihi: a suspended case tipped toward acceptance by a corroborating factor (qarina), without reaching sahih.

(4)

Can Multiple Weak Routes Combine to Strengthen a Hadith?

A student asked this directly: can several independent da'if routes together upgrade a hadith's status?

What Corroboration Can and Cannot Do: Sidi Shaykh Muhyiddin's answer was that multiple independent da'if routes can together raise a report's grade to hasan li-ghayrihi, but not to sahih. He was specific that each supporting route must be genuinely independent for this to count, and that a report cannot jump two grades at once, corroboration moves a report up one tier, from da'if toward hasan li-ghayrihi, it does not leap all the way to sahih regardless of how many routes are stacked.

(5)
