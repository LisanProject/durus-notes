The Obligation of Zakah وُجُوبُ الزَّكَاةِ

The Text

Text
[بَابُ الزَّكَاةِ]
تَجِبُ الزَّكَاةُ فِي الْإِبِلِ، وَالْبَقَرِ، وَالْغَنَمِ، وَالزُّرُوعِ وَالثِّمَارِ، وَالْمَعْدِنِ، وَالرِّكَازِ، وَالتِّجَارَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • تَجِبُ الزَّكَاةُzakah is obligatory
  • الْإِبِلِcamels
  • وَالْبَقَرِand cattle
  • وَالْغَنَمِand sheep and goats
  • وَالزُّرُوعِand crops
  • وَالثِّمَارِand fruits
  • وَالْمَعْدِنِand mined minerals
  • وَالرِّكَازِand buried treasure
  • وَالتِّجَارَةِand trade goods
Translation
The chapter of zakah. Zakah is obligatory on camels, cattle, sheep and goats, crops and fruits, mined minerals, buried treasure, and trade goods.
Commentary

Gold and silver, the two monetary metals (al-naqdan), are not in this opening list, but the author gives their rulings in their own section.

Its Place in the Religion

Zakah is one of the five pillars of Islam. The Prophet ﷺ said:

بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ

"Islam is built on five." Allah commands it alongside prayer:

وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ

"Establish prayer and give zakah." And He says to the Prophet ﷺ:

خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِمْ بِهَا

"Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them to grow." It was also in the laws before us; 'Isa, peace be upon him, said:

وَأَوْصَانِي بِالصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا

"And He enjoined on me prayer and zakah as long as I live."

PositionRuling
Denying the obligation of zakah itselfDisbelief
Denying it in a disputed case, such as a child's wealthNot disbelief; the scholars differed
Acknowledging it but refusing to payThe ruler takes it by force and may fight those who refuse, as Abu Bakr fought the tribes that withheld it

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that denying zakah's foundation differs from disputing a case the scholars differed on, and that refusing to pay differs from denying.

(1)

Meaning

Linguistically, zakah means growth and purification, and also praise, as in:

فَلَا تُزَكُّوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ

"So do not claim purity for yourselves." It purifies the wealth, since the poor person's unpaid right left in it makes it impure; it purifies the giver's heart of attachment to wealth; and it purifies the recipient's heart of resentment toward the wealthy around him.

Zakah. The name for what is given from wealth or from the person, in a specified manner.

Wealth means livestock, crops, fruits, gold, silver, and the rest; "from the person" is zakat al-fitr. "In a specified manner" covers when, how, how much, and to whom.

(2)

"Charity" and "almsgiving" approach the meaning but do not capture it, since zakah has its own precise rules.
