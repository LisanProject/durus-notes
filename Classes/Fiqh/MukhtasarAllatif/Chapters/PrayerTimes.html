The Prayer Times مَوَاقِيتُ الصَّلَاةِ

Background

The five prayers were made obligatory on the Night of the Mi'raj. After the Prophet returned, Jibril taught him the prayers and their times, and the first prayer Jibril led him in was Dhuhr. The name is connected to appearing (zuhur), since it is prayed in full daylight.

Today, apps, timetables, and the adhan make the times easy to know. Traditionally the mu'adhdhin had to track the sun and the sky with real effort and knowledge. The Shaykh related that Abu Bakr said he would have given the adhan himself were he not occupied with the caliphate.

Dhuhr

Text
[مَوَاقِيتُ الصَّلَاةِ]
وَأَوَّلُ وَقْتِ الظُّهْرِ: إِذَا زَالَتِ الشَّمْسُ، وَآخِرُهُ: إِذَا صَارَ ظِلُّ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مِثْلَهُ غَيْرَ ظِلِّ الِاسْتِوَاءِ.
Vocabulary
  • مَوَاقِيتُthe times of
  • وَأَوَّلُand the beginning of
  • وَقْتِthe time of
  • الظُّهْرِDhuhr
  • إِذَاwhen
  • زَالَتِhas declined
  • الشَّمْسُthe sun
  • وَآخِرُهُand its end
  • صَارَbecomes
  • ظِلُّthe shadow of
  • كُلِّ شَيْءٍeach thing
  • مِثْلَهُequal to it
  • غَيْرَother than
  • ظِلِّthe shadow of
  • الِاسْتِوَاءِthe sun's zenith
Translation
The beginning of the time of Dhuhr is when the sun declines, and its end is when the shadow of each thing equals its height, not counting the shadow at the zenith.
Commentary

To the eye, the sun rises in the east, climbs to the middle of the sky, then descends westward. The moment it appears to be at the middle is istiwa'. It does not literally stop; this describes what one sees. Once it moves west of the middle, it has declined (zawal), and Dhuhr begins.

The Shaykh illustrated with a stick (the jurists call it a shakhis) set upright in open ground. As the sun rises, the shadow shortens. At istiwa' the shadow is at its shortest, and may not be zero. After zawal it begins to lengthen. Dhuhr ends when the shadow, minus the small shadow it had at istiwa', equals the stick's height: for a one-meter stick, one meter beyond the istiwa' shadow.

Istiwa'. The sun's apparent arrival at the middle of the sky. Its small shadow is subtracted when measuring.

Zawal. The sun's apparent move westward from the middle, which begins Dhuhr.

Shakhis. An upright object, like a stick, used to observe the shadow.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes subtracting the shadow of istiwa': Dhuhr ends when the shadow's growth since the zenith equals the object's height.

(2)

Asr

وَأَوَّلُ وَقْتِ الْعَصْرِ: إِذَا صَارَ ظِلُّ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مِثْلَهُ وَزَادَ أَدْنَى زِيَادَةٍ، وَآخِرُهُ: غُرُوبُ الشَّمْسِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْعَصْرِAsr
  • وَزَادَand it increased
  • أَدْنَىthe slightest
  • زِيَادَةٍincrease
  • غُرُوبُthe setting of
  • الشَّمْسِthe sun
Translation
The beginning of the time of Asr is when the shadow of each thing equals it and increases by the slightest amount, and its end is sunset.
Commentary

The end of Dhuhr and start of Asr touch. When the shadow equals the height, Dhuhr ends; the moment it exceeds it, by a centimeter on a one-meter stick, Asr begins. Asr lasts until sunset. Sunset is hard to see in a city among buildings, and easier in open country.

Maghrib, and the Old and New Positions

وَأَوَّلُ وَقْتِ الْمَغْرِبِ: غُرُوبُ قُرْصِ الشَّمْسِ، وَآخِرُهُ عَلَى الْمُخْتَارِ: غُرُوبُ الشَّفَقِ الْأَحْمَرِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْمَغْرِبِMaghrib
  • غُرُوبُthe setting of
  • قُرْصِthe disk of
  • عَلَىaccording to
  • الْمُخْتَارِthe chosen position
  • الشَّفَقِthe twilight
  • الْأَحْمَرِred
Translation
The beginning of the time of Maghrib is the setting of the sun's disk, and its end, according to the chosen position, is the disappearance of the red twilight.
Commentary

Here the Shaykh introduced Imam al-Shafi'i's old position (al-qadim), his teaching before he entered Egypt, and his new position (al-jadid), his later rulings in Egypt. They are not two separate schools; they mark earlier and later views on certain issues. The general rule is that the new position is followed, with a known set of exceptions where the old is preferred.

Maghrib is one of those exceptions. In the new position, its time lasts only long enough for an ordinary person to purify himself, cover the 'awrah, give the adhan and iqamah, and pray five rak'ahs (or seven, as some say), roughly thirty minutes. Delaying past that would be impermissible. But this is not the relied-upon position. The chosen and relied-upon position is the old one: Maghrib lasts until the red twilight disappears, which is when Isha enters.

The hadith says the time lasts "until the shafaq disappears" without saying red. The jurists specify red because that is what shafaq means by default in Arabic; twilight can be red, yellow, or white.

Al-Qadim (Old Position). Imam al-Shafi'i's views before entering Egypt.

Al-Jadid (New Position). His later views in Egypt, which are generally followed, except in specific issues where the old is preferred, such as the time of Maghrib.

(3)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the short, thirty-minute Maghrib of the new position is not the relied-upon view. Maghrib lasts until the red twilight disappears.

(4)

Isha and the Two Dawns

وَأَوَّلُ وَقْتِ الْعِشَاءِ: غُرُوبُ الشَّفَقِ الْأَحْمَرِ، وَآخِرُهُ: طُلُوعُ الْفَجْرِ الصَّادِقِ الْمُنْتَشِرِ عَرْضًا.
Vocabulary
  • الْعِشَاءِIsha
  • طُلُوعُthe rising of
  • الْفَجْرِthe dawn
  • الصَّادِقِtrue
  • الْمُنْتَشِرِspreading
  • عَرْضًاacross, horizontally
Translation
The beginning of the time of Isha is the disappearance of the red twilight, and its end is the rising of the true dawn, which spreads horizontally.
Commentary

Picture standing in a desert or looking out to sea, where the horizon is clear. Facing east, the true dawn (al-fajr al-sadiq) spreads across the horizon from north to south. It ends Isha, begins Fajr, and begins the fasting day, so the one fasting must stop eating and drinking.

Before it, another light may appear: the false dawn (al-fajr al-kadhib). It is a narrow, vertical streak. The hadith likens it to the tail of a wolf (dhanab al-sirhan). The false dawn changes nothing: Isha continues, Fajr has not entered, and eating is still permitted.

Al-Fajr al-Sadiq (True Dawn). Light spreading horizontally across the eastern horizon. It ends Isha, begins Fajr, and begins the fast.

Al-Fajr al-Kadhib (False Dawn). An earlier, narrow vertical light, likened to a wolf's tail. It has no effect on prayer or fasting.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the true dawn is the sign, not the false one: horizontal, not vertical.

(6)

Subh, Ada' and Qada'

وَأَوَّلُ وَقْتِ الصُّبْحِ: طُلُوعُ الْفَجْرِ الصَّادِقِ، وَآخِرُهُ: طُلُوعُ الشَّمْسِ.
Vocabulary
  • الصُّبْحِSubh, the morning prayer
  • طُلُوعُthe rising of
  • الْفَجْرِ الصَّادِقِthe true dawn
  • الشَّمْسِthe sun
The beginning of the time of Subh is the rising of the true dawn, and its end is sunrise.

Knowing the times tells us what is ada', performing a prayer within its time, and what is qada', performing it after its time has passed. The words can overlap in ordinary Arabic, but as fiqh terms they mark this distinction.

Asked about waking after Fajr has entered but before sunrise, the Shaykh said to pray right away, before the sun rises. Someone who wakes after sunrise still prays Fajr as soon as he wakes, by the hadith "whoever sleeps through a prayer or forgets it, let him pray it when he remembers." Going back to sleep after waking at the adhan is permissible if he is confident he will wake again in time, though praying at the start is better; if he knows he will not wake in time, it is sinful. Explaining a case in a lesson, the Shaykh added, is not recommending it.

Ada'. Performing the prayer within its time.

Qada'. Performing it after its time has passed.

(7)

Praying at the Start of the Time

وَأَفْضَلُ الْأَعْمَالِ الْمُبَادَرَةُ بِالصَّلَاةِ فِي أَوَّلِ وَقْتِهَا.
Vocabulary
  • وَأَفْضَلُand the best of
  • الْأَعْمَالِdeeds
  • الْمُبَادَرَةُhastening
  • بِالصَّلَاةِto the prayer
  • فِي أَوَّلِat the beginning of
  • وَقْتِهَاits time
The best of deeds is hastening to the prayer at the start of its time.

This follows the hadith answering which deed is best: prayer at the start of its time. One earns this virtue by starting to prepare as soon as the time enters: wudu, covering the 'awrah, adhan, iqamah. If the time enters at 1:00 and preparation takes twenty minutes, praying at 1:20 still counts as the start of the time. Preparing before the time enters is better, but not required for this virtue.

Some apps show a separate "adhan time" and "prayer time". The Shaykh explained that the time's entry is what permits the prayer; the adhan simply announces it. There is no extra waiting period after the adhan before prayer becomes valid.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the adhan announces the time has entered. Prayer is valid from that moment, and starting preparation promptly earns the virtue of the first time.

(8)

Delaying Dhuhr in Intense Heat

وَيُسَنُّ الْإِبْرَادُ بِالظُّهْرِ فِي شِدَّةِ حَرٍّ فِي قُطْرٍ حَارٍّ لِمَنْ يُصَلِّي جَمَاعَةً فِي مَسْجِدٍ بَعِيدٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيُسَنُّand it is recommended
  • الْإِبْرَادُdelaying until the heat eases
  • بِالظُّهْرِwith Dhuhr
  • فِي شِدَّةِin the intensity of
  • حَرٍّheat
  • فِي قُطْرٍin a region
  • حَارٍّhot
  • لِمَنْfor whoever
  • يُصَلِّيprays
  • جَمَاعَةًin congregation
  • فِي مَسْجِدٍin a mosque
  • بَعِيدٍfar away
Translation
It is recommended to delay Dhuhr until the heat eases, in intense heat in a hot region, for one who prays in congregation in a distant mosque.
Commentary

This is an exception to praying at the start of the time. Ibrad comes from barid, cold, but it does not mean waiting for cool weather; it means waiting until the worst of the heat has passed, say from 1:00 to around 3:00 on a scorching day. The Shaykh gave temperatures of 45 or 50 degrees and places like Qatar, Makkah, Arizona, and New Mexico as examples, contrasting them with a mild, rainy place like Washington.

Only Dhuhr. Not Asr, Maghrib, Isha, and not Jumu'ah, where the instruction is to go early.

Intense heat in the hot season. A generally hot city in its cool season does not qualify.

Going to a mosque. Recommended whether he prays there in congregation or alone.

Elsewhere in congregation. Recommended, such as going to someone's home to pray together.

Alone at home. Not recommended; the hardship of going out does not exist.

(9)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the reason behind ibrad: the hardship of going out in severe heat to pray. Where that hardship is absent, such as praying alone at home, there is no ibrad.
