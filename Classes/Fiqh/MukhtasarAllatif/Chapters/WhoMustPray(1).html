Upon Whom Prayer Is Obligatory فِيمَنْ تَجِبُ الصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِ

The Four Conditions

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِيمَنْ تَجِبُ الصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِ]
تَجِبُ الصَّلَاةُ عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ، بَالِغٍ، عَاقِلٍ، طَاهِرٍ.
Vocabulary
  • فَصْلٌa section
  • فِيمَنْconcerning whom
  • تَجِبُis obligatory
  • الصَّلَاةُthe prayer
  • عَلَيْهِupon him
  • عَلَى كُلِّupon every
  • مُسْلِمٍMuslim
  • بَالِغٍwho has reached puberty
  • عَاقِلٍsane, possessing reason
  • طَاهِرٍpure, free of menstruation and postnatal bleeding
Translation
A section on those upon whom prayer is obligatory. Prayer is obligatory upon every Muslim who has reached puberty, is sane, and is pure.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha called these the conditions of the obligation of prayer: when all four are present, prayer is due. Prayer is not demanded in this world from a non-Muslim, a child who has not reached puberty, or someone without reason, and it is not due from a woman in menstruation or postnatal bleeding.

ConditionWho lacks itAre missed prayers made up?
IslamOriginal non-MuslimNo, after accepting Islam
ApostateYes, after returning to Islam

Six conditions of obligation. In the next lesson the Shaykh added two more to the matn's four: soundness of the senses, and that the call of Islam has reached the person. These conditions of obligation (shurut al-wujub) are different from the conditions of validity (see The Conditions of Prayer).

(1)

Expanded and Restricted Obligation

The Shaykh distinguished an obligation being due from the time available to perform it. Dhuhr, from the start of its time, is an expanded obligation (wujub muwassa'): if Dhuhr runs from 12 to 3, the time could hold the prayer more than once. Ramadan fasting is a restricted obligation (wujub mudayyaq): one cannot fit two Ramadans into one, or substitute a voluntary fast for it. A prayer's time also becomes restricted once only enough remains for the prayer and what it requires, such as wudu; if fifteen minutes remain before Asr, that time now belongs to Dhuhr alone.

Hasten or resolve. When the time enters, a person should either pray promptly or resolve in his heart to pray within the time. In the Shaykh's example, someone who resolved to pray and died before doing so is not sinful; someone who neither prayed nor resolved to pray and then died is sinful.

(2)

Islam: The Non-Muslim and the Apostate

A non-Muslim is not required to pray as a worldly demand the way a Muslim is. This is separate from the hereafter: the Shaykh explained that one who dies a non-Muslim is punished for disbelief and also for leaving prayer and zakah. When an original non-Muslim (kafir asli) accepts Islam, he does not make up the prayers from before. An apostate is different: if a Muslim leaves Islam for a period, say a week, and returns, he must make up the prayers missed in that period, and prayers performed during apostasy are not accepted.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that "not obligatory" here concerns the worldly demand to pray, and that accountability in the hereafter is a separate matter.

(3)

Puberty: The Child

Prayer is not obligatory on a child before puberty. After puberty, it is recommended to make up prayers missed during the period of discernment (tamyiz). Scholars differed over prayers missed before discernment; the Shaykh did not name a preferred view.

Tamyiz (Discernment). The child can eat by himself, drink by himself, and clean himself after relieving himself without help.

(4)

Sanity: Insanity, Intoxication, and Unconsciousness

Prayer is not obligatory on someone insane, intoxicated, or unconscious, such as a person in a coma for five, six, or seven days. If he did not bring the state on himself through transgression (ta'addi), the missed prayers are not made up. If he did, for instance by knowingly drinking wine or knowingly consuming something that causes unconsciousness, he makes them up.

No transgression. A thirsty person finds a cup of wine, thinks it is something else, drinks quickly, and becomes intoxicated. Or someone eats something without knowing it will make him ill or unconscious. Neither transgressed, so neither makes up the prayers.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the question is whether the person transgressed in causing the incapacity, not merely whether he was unable to pray.

(6)

Purity: Menstruation and Postnatal Bleeding

A woman in menstruation or postnatal bleeding is not required to pray and does not make up those prayers (see Menstruation and Nifas). If she makes them up anyway, the Shaykh reported a difference without naming the relied-upon view:

Ibn Hajar. Making them up is prohibited, and the prayer does not take effect.

Al-Ramli. It is disliked, but the prayer takes effect.

(7)

Commanding Children to Pray

وَيَجِبُ عَلَى الْوَلِيِّ أَنْ يَأْمُرَ الصَّبِيَّ بِالصَّلَاةِ لِسَبْعِ سِنِينَ، وَيَضْرِبَهُ عَلَى تَرْكِهَا لِعَشْرٍ، وَالصَّبِيَّةُ كَالصَّبِيِّ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَجِبُand it is obligatory
  • عَلَى الْوَلِيِّupon the guardian
  • أَنْ يَأْمُرَto command
  • الصَّبِيَّthe child
  • بِالصَّلَاةِto pray
  • لِسَبْعِ سِنِينَat seven years
  • وَيَضْرِبَهُand to discipline him
  • عَلَى تَرْكِهَاfor leaving it
  • لِعَشْرٍat ten
  • وَالصَّبِيَّةُand the girl
  • كَالصَّبِيِّis like the boy
Translation
The guardian must command the child to pray at seven years, and discipline him for leaving it at ten. A girl is like a boy in this.
Commentary

The wali is the legally responsible guardian, such as a parent. The word for child is masculine, but the matn itself says the girl is included, unlike a ruling such as the urine of an infant boy, where the jurists mean the male specifically. The duty is communal (wujub kifa'i): if one responsible person fulfills it, whether mother, father, grandparent, or a schoolteacher, it falls from the rest.

"Seven" means after completing seven years, and the child must also be discerning; both are required for this duty to attach. Before seven, or after seven without discernment, the guardian is not yet under this obligation, though encouraging a younger child, even a five-year-old, is still fine. At seven, eight, and nine, the command may come with a warning, but striking is not permitted. The Shaykh mentioned a difference over whether ten means completing ten years or entering the tenth year.

Discipline at ten is training, not punishment, and it is restricted. It must not be severe, must not lead to a prohibited harm, and must be expected to benefit the child. If it will make the child more defiant or cause harm, it is not done. The Shaykh said there are six conditions in all, to be covered in a later lesson.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a phrase about striking must not be pulled out of its juristic context to misrepresent Islam. Learn first when it is permitted, how, what it means, and its conditions.

(9)

A student asked why a guardian must command and discipline a child who is not yet obligated. The Shaykh answered that the obligation is on the guardian, not the child. The child's prayer before puberty is valid and rewarded, though not obligatory for him; the purpose is training, and the prayer's conditions must still be met.

When an Impediment Ends Before the Time Ends

وَإِذَا بَلَغَ الصَّبِيُّ أَوْ طَهُرَتِ الْحَائِضُ أَوِ النُّفَسَاءُ، أَوْ أَفَاقَ الْمَجْنُونُ قَبْلَ خُرُوجِ الْوَقْتِ بِقَدْرِ تَكْبِيرَةٍ.. وَجَبَ قَضَاءُ تِلْكَ الصَّلَاةِ، وَيَجِبُ قَضَاءُ الْفَرْضِ الَّذِي قَبْلَهَا إِذَا كَانَ ظُهْراً أَوْ مَغْرِباً.
Vocabulary
  • وَإِذَا بَلَغَ الصَّبِيُّand if the child reaches puberty
  • أَوْ طَهُرَتِor becomes pure
  • الْحَائِضُthe menstruating woman
  • أَوِ النُّفَسَاءُor the woman in postnatal bleeding
  • أَوْ أَفَاقَor recovers
  • الْمَجْنُونُthe insane person
  • قَبْلَ خُرُوجِ الْوَقْتِbefore the time ends
  • بِقَدْرِ تَكْبِيرَةٍby the length of a takbir
  • وَجَبَ قَضَاءُmaking up becomes obligatory
  • تِلْكَ الصَّلَاةِthat prayer
  • الْفَرْضِ الَّذِي قَبْلَهَاthe obligatory prayer before it
  • إِذَا كَانَ ظُهْراً أَوْ مَغْرِباًif it is Dhuhr or Maghrib
Translation
If the child reaches puberty, the menstruating or postnatally bleeding woman becomes pure, or the insane person recovers, before the time ends by the length of a takbir, making up that prayer becomes obligatory, and so does the obligatory prayer before it if that is Dhuhr or Maghrib.
Commentary

The Shaykh called this zawal al-mani', the removal of an impediment. If Maghrib runs from 7:00 to 8:30 and a woman's period ends with enough time left, she must purify herself and pray Maghrib within its time; calling that "qada" would not be accurate, since she is still in the time. If it ends so close to the end that only a takbir's worth remains, she makes the prayer up afterward, because she reached part of the time in a state of purity.

Why the earlier prayer too? Because Dhuhr can be joined with Asr, and Maghrib with Isha. So purity at the end of Asr obliges Asr and Dhuhr, and at the end of Isha obliges Isha and Maghrib. Fajr is never joined with Dhuhr, so purity in Dhuhr's time does not add Fajr.

(10)

When an Impediment Arises After the Time Begins

وَإِذَا دَخَلَ الْوَقْتُ وَمَضَى قَدْرُ مَا يُؤَدَّى فِيهِ الْفَرْضُ، ثُمَّ حَاضَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ أَوْ نُفِسَتْ أَوْ جُنَّ قَبْلَ الصَّلَاةِ.. وَجَبَ قَضَاءُ تِلْكَ الصَّلَاةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَإِذَا دَخَلَ الْوَقْتُand if the time enters
  • وَمَضَىand there passes
  • قَدْرُ مَا يُؤَدَّى فِيهِ الْفَرْضُenough time to perform the obligatory prayer
  • ثُمَّ حَاضَتِ الْمَرْأَةُthen the woman menstruates
  • أَوْ نُفِسَتْor begins postnatal bleeding
  • أَوْ جُنَّor he becomes insane
  • قَبْلَ الصَّلَاةِbefore praying
  • وَجَبَ قَضَاءُ تِلْكَ الصَّلَاةِmaking up that prayer becomes obligatory
Translation
If the time enters and enough time passes to perform the obligatory prayer, and then the woman menstruates or begins postnatal bleeding, or the person becomes insane, before praying, making up that prayer becomes obligatory.
Commentary

This is turu' al-mani', an impediment arising. If Dhuhr enters at 1:00, a woman has not yet prayed, and her period begins fifteen minutes later, she makes up Dhuhr, because enough time had passed for her to pray it. If it begins one minute after the time enters, too little to pray, she does not.

CaseWhat decides it
The impediment ends (zawal al-mani')Was any of the time reached free of it, even a takbir's length?
The impediment begins (turu' al-mani')Did enough time pass to perform the prayer before it began?

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the difference between a prayer that can still be performed in its time and one that must be made up after it, and that in the second case the decisive question is whether enough time to pray had already passed.
