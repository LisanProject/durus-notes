Translation
This is a concise work on what every Muslim must know, or know its equivalent, of the obligations of purification, prayer, and other matters. It is therefore obligatory for those who need it to learn it and to teach it, whether men or women, young or old, free or enslaved.

What "Obligatory Knowledge" Means

طَلَبُ الْعِلْمِ فَرِيضَةٌ عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ
Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim. (Ibn Majah)

The author's purpose is to cover the knowledge a Muslim needs in order to fulfill his obligations. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that the knowledge meant in this hadith is the knowledge that is individually obligatory (fard 'ayn). It does not refer to university degrees or professional training such as engineering or medicine. Those may be beneficial, but the religiously obligatory knowledge is what makes a person's worship valid and teaches him the rulings that apply to him.

Fard 'Ayn Knowledge (Individually Obligatory). Knowledge each Muslim must personally acquire for the obligations that apply to him, including what is needed for his worship to be valid.

(1)

It Depends on Your Circumstances

What a person must learn depends on what is actually required of him. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha gave a series of examples.

  • Whoever must pray has to learn how to pray.
  • Whoever owns wealth on which zakah is due must learn its rulings. A poor person is not required to learn the details of zakah at that time, though learning them in preparation for future wealth is beneficial.
  • A Muslim should learn the rulings of fasting before Ramadan arrives.
  • Whoever becomes able to perform Hajj, and is accepted to go, must learn its rulings.
  • Whoever intends to marry must learn the rulings relevant to marriage.
  • A man married to one wife does not yet need the rulings of dividing time fairly among several wives. If he takes a second wife, those rulings become obligatory for him to learn.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the obligation to learn comes before the obligation to act. Learn the rulings of fasting before Ramadan, and learn the rulings of Hajj once it becomes obligatory, not after you are already performing it.

(2)

The Student of Knowledge Is Different

An ordinary Muslim learns what currently applies to him. A student of knowledge studies every chapter, including rulings he does not personally need, because he may later teach others and give answers to their questions. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha estimated that an individual may need only about ten percent of fiqh in his personal life, while the student of knowledge studies both what he needs and what he does not. This again includes the historical chapters on slavery.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the student of knowledge studies broadly because others will come to him with their questions. Most people need perhaps ten percent of fiqh. The student needs all of it.

(3)

Knowledge of the Heart

Purifying the heart is also part of obligatory knowledge. The Shaykh named envy (hasad), arrogance (kibr), showing off (riya'), and self-admiration ('ujb) as diseases of the heart that every Muslim must learn to recognize and treat.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that obligatory knowledge is not only the outward rulings of worship. Learning how to treat the diseases of the heart is also part of what every Muslim must acquire.

(4)
