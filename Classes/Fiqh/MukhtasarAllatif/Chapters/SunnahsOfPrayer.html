The Sunnahs of Prayer سُنَنُ الصَّلَاةِ

Ab'ad and Hay'at

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي سُنَنِ الصَّلَاةِ]
وَسُنَنُهَا كَثِيرَةٌ، مِنْهَا:
رَفْعُ الْيَدَيْنِ عِنْدَ تَكْبِيرَةِ الْإِحْرَامِ، وَعِنْدَ الرُّكُوعِ، وَعِنْدَ الرَّفْعِ مِنْهُ، وَعِنْدَ الْقِيَامِ مِنَ التَّشَهُّدِ الْأَوَّلِ.
وَمِنْ سُنَنِهَا: دُعَاءُ الِافْتِتَاحِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَسُنَنُهَاand its sunnahs
  • كَثِيرَةٌare many
  • مِنْهَاamong them
  • رَفْعُ الْيَدَيْنِraising the hands
  • عِنْدَ تَكْبِيرَةِ الْإِحْرَامِat the opening takbir
  • وَعِنْدَ الرُّكُوعِand at ruku'
  • وَعِنْدَ الرَّفْعِ مِنْهُand on rising from it
  • وَعِنْدَ الْقِيَامِand on standing
  • مِنَ التَّشَهُّدِ الْأَوَّلِfrom the first tashahhud
  • وَمِنْ سُنَنِهَاand among its sunnahs
  • دُعَاءُ الِافْتِتَاحِthe opening supplication
Translation
A section on the sunnahs of prayer. Its sunnahs are many, among them: raising the hands at the opening takbir, at ruku', on rising from it, and on standing from the first tashahhud. And among its sunnahs is the opening supplication.
Commentary

Sunnah. What the Sharia asks for without making it binding: one is rewarded for doing it and not punished for leaving it. An integral, by contrast, is asked for bindingly.

(1)
KindExamples givenIf left out
Ab'adThe qunut; the first tashahhud; salawat on the Prophet ﷺ with itSujud al-sahw is recommended
Hay'atRaising the hands; a surah after al-Fatihah; the dhikrs of prayerNo sujud al-sahw

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha's example: saying the opening takbir without raising the hands. The takbir is an integral; raising the hands with it is a hay'ah, so the prayer is complete and no sujud al-sahw is called for.

He also cautioned against hearing "sunnah" as "unimportant." Calling an act sunnah is encouragement to do it, even though it is not obligatory.

Raising the Hands

Four places
  • With the opening takbir.
  • On going into ruku'.
  • On rising from ruku'.
  • On standing up from the first tashahhud.

Raising the hands when going into sujud is not sunnah. With the opening takbir, the basic sunnah is achieved by raising them at all, but the preferred way is to begin raising them as the takbir begins and finish as it ends, not to raise them after the takbir is over.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that there are four places for raising the hands, and going into sujud is not one of them.

(2)

The Opening Supplication and Seeking Refuge

Text
ثُمَّ التَّعَوُّذُ، وَيُسَنُّ فِي كُلِّ رَكْعَةٍ، وَالْأُولَى آكَدُ.
Vocabulary
  • ثُمَّthen
  • التَّعَوُّذُseeking refuge
  • وَيُسَنُّand it is sunnah
  • فِي كُلِّ رَكْعَةٍin every rak'ah
  • وَالْأُولَىand the first
  • آكَدُmore emphasized
Translation
Then seeking refuge, which is sunnah in every rak'ah, and in the first it is more emphasized.
Commentary

The opening supplication preferred here is the one Imam al-Shafi'i chose, which the jurists call al-tawajjuh ("the turning") after its opening words. Other transmitted openings also fulfill the sunnah. It takes its wording from Surat al-An'am, with "I am among the Muslims" in place of the verse's "I am the first of the Muslims":

وَجَّهْتُ وَجْهِيَ لِلَّذِي فَطَرَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ حَنِيفًا وَمَا أَنَا مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ، إِنَّ صَلَاتِي وَنُسُكِي وَمَحْيَايَ وَمَمَاتِي لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ.

It is said in obligatory and voluntary prayers, but not in the funeral prayer. Once a person has begun seeking refuge or reciting, the opening supplication is missed; he does not go back to it.

Any wording of seeking refuge fulfills the sunnah. The preferred form follows the command to seek refuge before reciting the Quran:

أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ
Opening supplicationSeeking refuge
WhereFirst rak'ah onlyEvery rak'ah, most emphasized in the first
Funeral prayerNot saidSaid
WhyIt opens the prayerIt belongs to recitation, and al-Fatihah is recited in every rak'ah

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the two are not interchangeable: the opening supplication is limited to the first rak'ah and left out of the funeral prayer, while seeking refuge is repeated every rak'ah and kept in the funeral prayer. Once seeking refuge or recitation has begun, the opening supplication is not returned to.

A Surah After al-Fatihah

Text
وَقِرَاءَةُ سُورَةٍ لِغَيْرِ الْمَأْمُومِ السَّامِعِ قِرَاءَةَ إِمَامِهِ بَعْدَ الْفَاتِحَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَقِرَاءَةُ سُورَةٍand reciting a surah
  • لِغَيْرِ الْمَأْمُومِfor other than the follower
  • السَّامِعِwho hears
  • قِرَاءَةَ إِمَامِهِhis imam's recitation
  • بَعْدَ الْفَاتِحَةِafter al-Fatihah
Translation
And reciting a surah after al-Fatihah, for anyone other than a follower who hears his imam's recitation.
Commentary

The surah belongs after al-Fatihah. Someone who recites a surah first and then al-Fatihah has not done this sunnah, though he may recite the surah again after al-Fatihah. A complete surah is generally better than a selection of verses; the Shaykh also reported a view that a longer passage of verses can be better than a short complete surah, and presented the question as open.

WhoRecites a surah?
Imam, or one praying aloneYes
Follower in a quiet prayerYes
Follower in a loud prayer who hears the imamNo; he listens
Follower who cannot hear the imam (a very large masjid, far back)Yes

The surah is recited in Fajr and in the first two rak'ahs of Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha. In the third and fourth rak'ahs al-Fatihah is recited alone.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the extra surah only counts after al-Fatihah, and that a follower who hears the imam in a loud prayer listens instead of reciting.

Reciting Aloud

Text
وَالْجَهْرُ فِي الصُّبْحِ وَفِي الرَّكْعَتَيْنِ الْأُولَيَيْنِ مِنَ الْمَغْرِبِ وَالْعِشَاءِ لِلرَّجُلِ وَالْمَرْأَةِ إِنْ لَمْ يَحْضُرْ عِنْدَهَا أَجَانِبُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْجَهْرُand reciting aloud
  • فِي الصُّبْحِin Subh
  • الرَّكْعَتَيْنِ الْأُولَيَيْنِthe first two rak'ahs
  • مِنَ الْمَغْرِبِ وَالْعِشَاءِof Maghrib and Isha
  • لِلرَّجُلِ وَالْمَرْأَةِfor the man and the woman
  • إِنْ لَمْ يَحْضُرْif there are not present
  • عِنْدَهَاwith her
  • أَجَانِبُnon-mahram men
Translation
And reciting aloud in Subh and in the first two rak'ahs of Maghrib and Isha, for a man, and for a woman if no non-mahram men are present with her.
Commentary

Israr (Quiet). Reciting so that you hear yourself.

Jahr (Aloud). Reciting so that another person can hear.

(5)

Besides the matn's places, recitation is aloud in Jumu'ah, the two Eids, the rain prayer (even by day), Tarawih, the lunar eclipse prayer (which is at night), and Witr in Ramadan. The solar eclipse prayer, by day, is quiet, and Witr outside Ramadan is not aloud.

Reciting aloud is sunnah for a man praying alone or leading; a follower does not recite aloud. For a woman, it is disliked when non-mahram men can hear her. Alone, with women, or with mahram men, it is sunnah for her in its places.

Placing the Hands and the Transition Takbirs

Text
وَوَضْعُ الْيُمْنَى عَلَى كُوعِ الْيُسْرَى تَحْتَ صَدْرِهِ.
وَالتَّكْبِيرَاتُ غَيْرَ تَكْبِيرَةِ الْإِحْرَامِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَوَضْعُand placing
  • الْيُمْنَىthe right hand
  • عَلَى كُوعِ الْيُسْرَىon the wrist of the left
  • تَحْتَ صَدْرِهِbelow his chest
  • وَالتَّكْبِيرَاتُand the takbirs
  • غَيْرَ تَكْبِيرَةِ الْإِحْرَامِother than the opening takbir
Translation
And placing the right hand on the wrist of the left, below the chest. And the takbirs other than the opening takbir.
Commentary

The hands rest below the chest, not on the stomach, with the right hand holding the left wrist.

The opening takbir is an integral; the rest are transition takbirs. A transition takbir accompanies the movement: it begins as one leaves a posture and ends on reaching the next. It is not delayed until the movement is over, nor finished before the movement begins. If a transition is long, the takbir is drawn out to cover it, including rising from sujud through the sitting of rest: that is one takbir, not two. The reason is that the prayer should not have an empty interval without dhikr; it has set places for brief silence, and silence should not be added without need.

An imam who reaches the ground in sujud and only then says "Allahu akbar" leaves followers unsure whether to follow his movement or his voice. The takbir belongs inside the movement.

The opening takbir, by contrast, is not drawn out. If the imam lengthens it, a follower may finish his own takbir first and enter the prayer before the imam, and a follower must never precede the imam in the opening takbir.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a transition takbir spans the whole transition, from leaving one posture to reaching the next, while the opening takbir is not lengthened, so that no follower enters the prayer before the imam.

The Qunut

Text
وَالْقُنُوتُ فِي اعْتِدَالِ الثَّانِيَةِ مِنَ الصُّبْحِ، وَفِي سَائِرِ الْمَكْتُوبَاتِ لِلنَّازِلَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْقُنُوتُand the qunut
  • فِي اعْتِدَالِin the standing upright
  • الثَّانِيَةِof the second (rak'ah)
  • مِنَ الصُّبْحِof Subh
  • وَفِي سَائِرِand in the rest of
  • الْمَكْتُوبَاتِthe obligatory prayers
  • لِلنَّازِلَةِfor a calamity
Translation
And the qunut, in the standing after ruku' of the second rak'ah of Subh, and in the other obligatory prayers for a calamity.
Commentary
Three occasions
  • Subh: in the i'tidal of the second rak'ah, all year.
  • Any obligatory prayer, after ruku', when a calamity (nazilah) strikes the Muslims or some of them. The Prophet ﷺ made qunut for a month against tribes who had killed Companions.
  • Witr in the second half of Ramadan. Some in the school allowed it all of Ramadan, as is common in Egypt, and that practice should not be condemned.

For the qunut of Subh the Shaykh cited Anas's report that the Prophet ﷺ continued it until he left this world, transmitted by Ahmad and al-Bayhaqi and declared authentic by Imam al-Nawawi.

The preferred wording begins:

اللَّهُمَّ اهْدِنَا فِيمَنْ هَدَيْتَ

Someone who does not know it may recite a verse that contains a supplication, intending supplication by it, such as:

رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Both conditions matter: a verse with no supplication in it (like the opening of Surat al-Kafirun) does not count, and neither does a supplicating verse recited without intending supplication. Any other supplication, transmitted or one's own, even for a worldly need, also achieves the basic sunnah. The best form joins supplication, praise of Allah, and salawat on the Prophet ﷺ.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a verse used as qunut must contain a supplication and be recited intending supplication; the familiar wording is better, but any supplication achieves the basis of the sunnah.

(7)

Qunut Behind an Imam Who Does Not Make It

Question: In Fajr behind an imam who does not make qunut, as happens in American masjids where people follow different schools, what should the follower do, and how should one answer those who call the Shafi'i qunut a bid'ah?

Answer: Condemning the Shafi'i qunut is not correct: it is a recognized position of the school and a valid position among the Sunni schools, and a matter of valid disagreement should not be condemned, especially in Western communities. Behind such an imam, the follower says the qunut quietly and quickly after rising from ruku' in the second rak'ah, after "sami' Allahu li-man hamidah" and "rabbana laka al-hamd," without falling behind the imam or raising his voice.

The imam's leaving the qunut is a defect that reaches the follower's prayer, so the follower may make the two prostrations of forgetfulness before his own salam. Since they are sunnah and can confuse a congregation, it may be better to leave them where they would cause disruption.

One school's bid'ah, another's sunnah. A practice can count as an innovation by one school's own principles and as sunnah by another's recognized position. A Hanafi should not impose the Hanafi ruling on a Shafi'i, and a Shafi'i should not condemn someone following another recognized position. The basmalah is the same: in this school, leaving it out makes al-Fatihah incomplete, but that is no basis for condemning someone who follows another school.

The Tasbih of Ruku' and Sujud

Text
وَيَقُولُ فِي الرُّكُوعِ: (سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ) ثَلَاثًا، وَفِي السُّجُودِ: (سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَى وَبِحَمْدِهِ) ثَلَاثًا.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَقُولُand he says
  • فِي الرُّكُوعِin ruku'
  • سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِglory be to my Lord, the Magnificent
  • وَبِحَمْدِهِand with His praise
  • ثَلَاثًاthree times
  • فِي السُّجُودِin sujud
  • سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَىglory be to my Lord, the Most High
Translation
In ruku' he says "Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent, and with His praise" three times, and in sujud "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High, and with His praise" three times.
Commentary
How manyRuling
NoneDisliked
OnceBasis of the sunnah achieved, but contrary to what is preferable (khilaf al-awla)
ThreeThe minimum of completeness
Up to elevenFor one praying alone; a follower whose imam lengthens the posture; or an imam of a limited, known group who consent to a longer prayer

A public masjid with changing, unknown attendance is not a "limited, known group"; a small group of familiar regulars is.

Going Down Into Sujud, and Between the Two Prostrations

Text
وَيَضَعُ فِي السُّجُودِ رُكْبَتَيْهِ، ثُمَّ يَدَيْهِ، ثُمَّ جَبْهَتَهُ وَأَنْفَهُ.
وَيَقُولُ بَيْنَ السَّجْدَتَيْنِ: (رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَارْحَمْنِي وَاجْبُرْنِي وَارْزُقْنِي وَاهْدِنِي وَعَافِنِي وَاعْفُ عَنِّي).
Vocabulary
  • وَيَضَعُand he places
  • رُكْبَتَيْهِhis knees
  • ثُمَّ يَدَيْهِthen his hands
  • جَبْهَتَهُ وَأَنْفَهُhis forehead and nose
  • بَيْنَ السَّجْدَتَيْنِbetween the two prostrations
  • رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِيmy Lord, forgive me
  • وَارْحَمْنِيand have mercy on me
  • وَاجْبُرْنِيand mend me
  • وَارْزُقْنِيand provide for me
  • وَاهْدِنِيand guide me
  • وَعَافِنِيand grant me well-being
  • وَاعْفُ عَنِّيand pardon me
Translation
In sujud he places his knees, then his hands, then his forehead and nose. Between the two prostrations he says: "My Lord, forgive me, have mercy on me, mend me, provide for me, guide me, grant me well-being, and pardon me."
Commentary

"And pardon me" was added by Imam al-Ghazali as a fitting supplication alongside the request for well-being; it is not part of the hadith's wording. Al-Rafi'i left it out of al-Muharrar.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the order of going down: knees, then hands, then forehead and nose; and that "and pardon me" is a permissible addition, not wording of the hadith.

The Sitting of Rest

Text
وَيُسَنُّ جَلْسَةُ الِاسْتِرَاحَةِ فِي الْأُولَى وَالثَّالِثَةِ مِنْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْرِبِ.
Vocabulary
  • جَلْسَةُ الِاسْتِرَاحَةِthe sitting of rest
  • فِي الْأُولَىin the first (rak'ah)
  • وَالثَّالِثَةِand the third
  • مِنْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْرِبِof other than Maghrib
Translation
The sitting of rest is sunnah after the first rak'ah, and after the third in prayers other than Maghrib.
Commentary

The test is a prostration followed by standing. So the sitting comes after the first rak'ah of every prayer, and after the third of Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha. Maghrib has none after the third, since it sits for the tashahhud instead. Fajr and Maghrib therefore have one; the four-rak'ah prayers have two.

  • It is brief, shorter than the sitting between the prostrations. Making it longer than that sitting can invalidate the prayer.
  • It is sunnah for imam and follower, in obligatory and voluntary prayers, and the books state it applies even to a fit person.
  • A follower who sits for it while the imam rises without sitting is not counted as falling behind.
  • It is not made after a prostration of recitation, such as an imam's sujud at the end of Surat al-'Alaq before returning to standing.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the sitting of rest follows a prostration after which one stands; it is not added after the third rak'ah of Maghrib or after a prostration of recitation, and it stays brief with a single drawn-out takbir.

(11)

The First Tashahhud, Iftirash, and Tawarruk

Text
وَيُسَنُّ التَّشَهُّدُ الْأَوَّلُ، وَالْقُعُودُ لَهُ، وَالصَّلَاةُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فِيهِ وَفِي الْقُنُوتِ.
وَالِافْتِرَاشُ فِي جَمِيعِ الْجَلَسَاتِ، وَالتَّوَرُّكُ فِي التَّشَهُّدِ الْأَخِيرِ.
Vocabulary
  • التَّشَهُّدُ الْأَوَّلُthe first tashahhud
  • وَالْقُعُودُ لَهُand sitting for it
  • وَالصَّلَاةُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّand salawat on the Prophet
  • فِيهِ وَفِي الْقُنُوتِin it and in the qunut
  • وَالِافْتِرَاشُand iftirash
  • فِي جَمِيعِ الْجَلَسَاتِin all the sittings
  • وَالتَّوَرُّكُand tawarruk
  • فِي التَّشَهُّدِ الْأَخِيرِin the final tashahhud
Translation
The first tashahhud is sunnah, and sitting for it, and salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it and in the qunut. And iftirash in all the sittings, and tawarruk in the final tashahhud.
Commentary

The final tashahhud was covered among the integrals; here it is the first. In the qunut, the supplication is the core, with praise and salawat as recommended additions.

The Shaykh preferred to state the sitting rule by what follows it: iftirash for any sitting not followed by salam, and tawarruk for the sitting followed by salam. "The final tashahhud" can mislead, because after what seemed the final tashahhud a person may make sujud al-sahw and then sit again before salam; that last sitting is the one followed by salam.

Iftirash. Sitting on the left foot, used in every sitting not followed by salam.

Tawarruk. The sitting used for the sitting followed by salam.

Gaze, Khushu', and Entering With Presence

Text
وَيُسَنُّ فِيهَا النَّظَرُ مَوْضِعَ سُجُودِهِ.

Translation
It is sunnah in it to look at the place of one's prostration; humility and reflection in recitation and dhikr; making the recitation of the first rak'ah longer than the second; entering it with energy and a heart free of distraction; and abundant supplication in sujud.
Commentary

Gaze. At the place of prostration throughout, even in the funeral prayer and even beside the Ka'bah. In tashahhud, the index finger is raised at "illa Allah" and the gaze rests on it, until salam in the final tashahhud and until standing in the first.

Khushu'. The relied-upon position is that khushu' is sunnah, not an integral or condition: the prayer is valid without it, though reward follows one's presence. Another position in the school requires some khushu' in part of the prayer.

Reflection. A general grasp of the meaning, not grammatical analysis that pulls one out of the prayer. Reciting Quran is worship even without understanding, though understanding increases the reward; for dhikr and du'a one should know the general sense, that this is glorification, praise, or a request.

Length. The first rak'ah is longer because one is freshest at the start, unless the Sunnah makes the second longer, as in Jumu'ah with Surat al-A'la and then Surat al-Ghashiyah.

Presence. Someone hungry or preoccupied should, where appropriate, take care of the need first. Supplication is good in ruku' and best in sujud.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that on the relied-upon position a lack of khushu' does not invalidate the prayer, though it strongly affects the reward, and that the reflection asked for is general understanding, not analysis.
