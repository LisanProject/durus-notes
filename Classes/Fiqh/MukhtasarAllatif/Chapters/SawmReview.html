Review of Bab al-Siyam مُرَاجَعَةُ بَابِ الصِّيَامِ

This page gathers the Book of Fasting and i'tikaf in one place: the lists to memorize, the conditions of validity and obligation, who owes a make-up or a fidya, the pairs most easily confused, and the points Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha stressed most.

How to use this page The chapter turns on three questions: is the fast valid, must he fast, and what does he owe if he breaks it. Sort every case into one of the three before answering it.

Validity, Obligation, and Breach

QuestionWhat answers it
Is the fast valid?The intention (at night and specified if obligatory); abstaining from the four; Islam, sanity, and purity all day; a day that can receive a fast
Must he fast?Sanity, puberty, ability (and the Shaykh added Islam and residence)
What does he owe?Make-up always; fidya for fear for the child alone, rescuing a life, or delay past the next Ramadan; the major expiation for deliberate intercourse

Numbers to Have Cold

  • Sha'ban completed as 30 days; the latter half begins on the 16th.
  • The Days of Tashriq: the 11th, 12th, and 13th of Dhu al-Hijjah.
  • Major expiation: free a slave; else 2 consecutive months; else feed 60 poor people.
  • Child: commanded at 7, disciplined at 10, both if able.
  • Travel: about 84 km, lawful, begun before dawn.
  • Fidya: 1 mudd per day of the land's staple, to the poor or needy, repeating each year of delay.
  • I'tikaf: remaining longer than one "subhan Allah."

Test Yourself: Cases to Work Through

Try each before opening the answer.

Case 1At 11 a.m. in Ramadan, a man realizes he never made the intention last night, though he has not eaten. Is his fast valid?

No. An obligatory fast needs the intention at night. He abstains for the rest of the day and makes it up.

Case 2A woman's period begins an hour before sunset. What happens to that day's fast?

It is invalid, since purity from menstruation is required for the whole day. She makes it up.

Case 3A man set out on a lawful 120 km trip at 3 a.m., before dawn. He eats at noon. What does he owe?

Only the make-up. His concession was valid, since he began the day traveling.

Case 4A man missed two days of Ramadan without excuse and has still not made them up three Ramadans later. What does he owe?

Two make-up days, urgently, and a fidya for each day for each year of delay: three years times two days, six mudds.

Case 5A man intends i'tikaf in a community center where prayers and classes are held, but which was never endowed as a masjid. Is it valid?

No. A place is a masjid only by endowment.

Questions to Bring to the Shaykh

  • The book requires a fidya from one who broke the fast to rescue a life; confirm this alongside the make-up.
  • The Day of Doubt was mentioned among the prohibited days; what defines it?
  • Sleeping the whole day is valid "from the side of validity"; what is the other side?
