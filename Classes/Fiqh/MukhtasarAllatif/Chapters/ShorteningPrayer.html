Shortening the Prayer While Traveling قَصْرُ الصَّلَاةِ لِلْمُسَافِرِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي قَصْرِ الصَّلَاةِ لِلْمُسَافِرِ]
وَيَجُوزُ لِلْمُسَافِرِ سَفَرًا طَوِيلًا مُبَاحًا، وَهِيَ مَرْحَلَتَانِ.. قَصْرُ الظُّهْرِ وَالْعَصْرِ وَالْعِشَاءِ رَكْعَتَيْنِ رَكْعَتَيْنِ.
وَمَنْ فَاتَتْهُ صَلَاةٌ فِي السَّفَرِ وَقَضَاهَا فِي الْحَضَرِ أَوْ عَكْسُهُ.. أَتَمَّهَا.
وَيُشْتَرَطُ قَصْدُ مَوْضِعٍ مُعَيَّنٍ؛ فَلَا قَصْرَ لِهَائِمٍ، وَأَلَّا يُصَلِّيَ خَلْفَ مَنْ يُتِمُّ الصَّلَاةَ أَوْ شَكَّ أَنَّهُ مُتِمٌّ أَوْ قَاصِرٌ، وَأَنْ يَنْوِيَ الْقَصْرَ عِنْدَ الْإِحْرَامِ.

Translation
A section on shortening the prayer for the traveler. A traveler on a long, permissible journey, which is two stages, may shorten Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha to two rak'ahs each. Whoever misses a prayer while traveling and makes it up at home, or the reverse, prays it in full. The conditions are: intending a specific destination, so there is no shortening for a wanderer; not praying behind someone who prays in full, or whom he doubts is praying in full or shortening; and intending to shorten at the opening takbir.
Commentary

Qasr is praying the four-rak'ah prayers as two; Fajr and Maghrib are never shortened. Its basis is the verse:

وَإِذَا ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَنْ تَقْصُرُوا مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ

"When you travel through the land, there is no blame on you for shortening the prayer." Praying in full is generally better. Shortening is better for someone others imitate, so they are not burdened or led to think it is not allowed, and for someone who dislikes taking the concession, as training the self to accept what the Sharia gave. That means disliking it, not denying it; denying a ruling in the Quran and Sunnah is a far graver matter.

Qasr (Shortening). Praying Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha as two rak'ahs each while traveling.

Itmam (Praying in Full). Praying them as four.

(1)

The Six Conditions

The matn gives five; the Shaykh added a sixth.

1. A long journey. Two stages (marhalatan), four barids, or sixteen farsakhs: the old measures of a moderate day's or night's travel by loaded camel, about 82 km today. The intended journey must be that long, but one need not cover 82 km before shortening: it begins once one leaves the connected built-up area of one's town. In old cities that was the wall; today it is the edge of the continuous urban area on a map. Leaving for a place 100 km from Seattle, one may shorten once past Seattle and what is joined to it.

2. Not a prohibited journey. Any journey that is not forbidden, whether obligatory, recommended, permissible, or disliked (traveling alone), such as paying a debt, visiting relatives, recreation, or trade. A journey made to kill or steal gets no concession, since concessions are not for one who disobeys by the journey itself. Sinning during a lawful trip, such as looking at what is prohibited, does not remove it. But changing the trip's purpose midway to a prohibited one makes it a prohibited journey.

3. A known destination. Knowing one is driving to New York qualifies. One who gets in a car and drives with no destination is a wanderer (ha'im) and does not shorten, even after two days or a thousand kilometers.

4. Not behind one who prays in full. Behind a resident imam praying four, the traveler prays four. If unsure whether the imam will shorten, he prays in full. To shorten, he prays alone.

5. Intending to shorten at the opening takbir. One who began intending only Dhuhr and remembered later may no longer shorten that prayer.

6. Knowing that shortening is permitted. Someone traveling with a group who shorten, but who does not know the concession exists, prays in full.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that shortening begins once one leaves the town's built-up area, not after covering the full distance; that a journey made for a prohibited purpose differs from a lawful journey in which a sin occurs; and that doubt about whether the imam will shorten means praying in full.

(2)

Making Up a Prayer Missed in Travel or at Home

At homeWhile travelingFour

While travelingWhile traveling, even on a later tripMay be two

Someone who missed Dhuhr at home, then set out and makes it up in the time of Asr, prays that Dhuhr as four but may still shorten the current Asr. A prayer missed on a November trip and remembered on a December trip may be made up as two.
