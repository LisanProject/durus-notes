Zakah on Mined Gold and Silver, and Buried Treasure الْمَعْدِنُ وَالرِّكَازُ

Mined Gold and Silver

Text
وَيَجِبُ فِي الْمَعْدِنِ مِنَ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ: رُبْعُ عُشْرِهِ إِذَا كَانَ نِصَابًا فِي الْحَالِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْمَعْدِنِthe mine, or what is mined
  • مِنَ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِof gold and silver
  • رُبْعُ عُشْرِهِa quarter of its tenth
  • إِذَا كَانَ نِصَابًاif it is a nisab
  • فِي الْحَالِimmediately
Translation
On gold and silver from a mine, a quarter of a tenth is due immediately, if it reaches the nisab.
Commentary

Ma'din can mean the place something is extracted from or the extracted substance itself. Allah says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنْفِقُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا كَسَبْتُمْ وَمِمَّا أَخْرَجْنَا لَكُمْ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ

"O you who believe, spend from the good things you have earned and from what We bring forth for you from the earth." The same verse returns under trade goods.

  • Only gold and silver. Petroleum, copper, lead, and other minerals owe no zakah under this ruling, even when worth more than gold.
  • The nisab is required: 20 mithqals of gold, about 85 g. Mined gold is added to gold he already owns: 50 g owned plus 40 g extracted is 90 g, so zakah is due.
  • No year is required. It is due the moment the nisab is extracted.
  • Paid after refining. Ore mixed with rock is paid on once purified and the gold can be weighed, just as crop zakah becomes due at ripening but is paid after the harvest.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that mineral zakah is only on gold and silver, that it needs a nisab but no year, and that extracted gold is added to gold already owned to reach the nisab.

Buried Treasure (Rikaz)

Text
وَأَمَّا الرِّكَازُ، وَهُوَ دَفِينُ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ.. فَفِيهِ الْخُمُسُ فِي الْحَالِ، بِشَرْطِ أَنْ يَكُونَ ذَهَبًا أَوْ فِضَّةً، وَأَنْ يُوجَدَ فِي الْمَوَاتِ، أَوْ فِي مِلْكٍ أَحْيَاهُ.
Vocabulary
  • الرِّكَازُburied treasure
  • دَفِينُ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِwhat was buried in the pre-Islamic era
  • الْخُمُسُa fifth
  • فِي الْحَالِimmediately
  • فِي الْمَوَاتِin dead (unowned, unworked) land
  • فِي مِلْكٍ أَحْيَاهُin property he revived
Translation
As for buried treasure, which is what was buried in the pre-Islamic era, a fifth is due on it immediately, on condition that it is gold or silver, and that it is found in dead land or in property he himself revived.
Commentary
Ma'din (mined)Rikaz (buried treasure)
WhatGold or silver extracted from the earthGold or silver buried before the Prophet's mission ﷺ
EffortSought with work and expenseFound with little effort
Rate2.5%20% (a fifth)
NisabRequiredRequired
YearNot requiredNot required

The rate is higher for rikaz because finding it costs little. A sign such as a Pharaonic inscription shows it is pre-Islamic. An ancient stone statue is not rikaz, however valuable, because it is not gold or silver.

FoundRuling
In dead land belonging to no oneRikaz: a fifth is due
In land he revived (cultivated, built on, settled)His, as rikaz
Buried, then exposed by a floodStill rikaz
Lying exposed, not brought up by a floodA lost item (luqatah), with its own rules in Matn Abi Shuja'

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that mined gold and buried treasure differ in how they are obtained and so in their rates, 2.5% and a fifth, and that rikaz means buried pre-Islamic gold or silver, not every old or valuable object.
