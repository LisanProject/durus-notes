The Obligations of Prayer فُرُوضُ الصَّلَاةِ

Seventeen, Eighteen, or Thirteen?

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي فُرُوضِ الصَّلَاةِ]
فُرُوضُ الصَّلَاةِ سَبْعَةَ عَشَرَ.
Vocabulary
  • فَصْلٌa section
  • فِي فُرُوضِ الصَّلَاةِon the obligations of prayer
  • فُرُوضُ الصَّلَاةِthe obligations of prayer
  • سَبْعَةَ عَشَرَare seventeen
Translation
A section on the obligations of prayer. The obligations of prayer are seventeen.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that furud and arkan here name the same thing: the integrals of the prayer. A different count does not mean the scholars disagree about what is required. It means they count differently, treating something as its own integral or as part of another.

Tuma'ninah as the example. Stillness is required in ruku', in standing up from it, in sujud, and in sitting between the two prostrations. Some count it separately in each of the four, some count it as one integral found in four places, and some treat it as a description of the posture it belongs to. No one disputes that it is required.

(1)
BookCount
Al-Minhaj of Imam al-NawawiThirteen (the count the Shaykh called more correct)
Al-Rawdah of Imam al-Nawawi, and this bookSeventeen
Matn Abi Shuja'Eighteen, including the intention to exit the prayer
The seventeen in this book
  • (1) Niyyah. (2) Takbirat al-ihram. (3) Standing. (4) Al-Fatihah.
  • (5) Ruku'. (6) Tuma'ninah in it. (7) I'tidal. (8) Tuma'ninah in it.
  • (9) Two prostrations in every rak'ah. (10) Tuma'ninah in them. (11) Sitting between them. (12) Tuma'ninah in it.
  • (13) The final tashahhud. (14) Sitting for it. (15) Salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it. (16) The salam. (17) Order.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that differences in the number of integrals come from different ways of counting, not from disagreement over what makes the prayer valid, and that on the relied-upon position the intention to exit the prayer is not a rukn.

(2)

1. The Intention

الْأَوَّلُ: النِّيَّةُ؛ فَإِنْ كَانَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَرِيضَةً.. وَجَبَ قَصْدُ فِعْلِهَا وَتَعْيِينُهَا وَنِيَّةُ الْفَرْضِيَّةِ مِنَ الْبَالِغِ، وَإِنْ كَانَتْ نَافِلَةً مُؤَقَّتَةً كَالْوِتْرِ، أَوْ ذَاتَ سَبَبٍ كَالْكُسُوفِ.. وَجَبَ قَصْدُ فِعْلِهَا وَتَعْيِينُهَا، وَإِنْ كَانَتْ نَافِلَةً مُطْلَقَةً.. وَجَبَ قَصْدُ فِعْلِهَا فَقَطْ.
Vocabulary
  • الْأَوَّلُthe first
  • النِّيَّةُthe intention
  • فَإِنْ كَانَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَرِيضَةًso if the prayer is obligatory
  • وَجَبَit is obligatory
  • قَصْدُ فِعْلِهَاto intend doing it
  • وَتَعْيِينُهَاand to specify it
  • وَنِيَّةُ الْفَرْضِيَّةِand to intend its being obligatory
  • مِنَ الْبَالِغِfrom one who has reached puberty
  • نَافِلَةً مُؤَقَّتَةًa voluntary prayer tied to a time
  • كَالْوِتْرِsuch as witr
  • أَوْ ذَاتَ سَبَبٍor one with a cause
  • كَالْكُسُوفِsuch as the eclipse prayer
  • نَافِلَةً مُطْلَقَةًan unrestricted voluntary prayer
  • فَقَطْonly
Translation
The first is the intention. If the prayer is obligatory, one must intend doing it, specify it, and, from one who has reached puberty, intend that it is obligatory. If it is a voluntary prayer tied to a time, like witr, or to a cause, like the eclipse prayer, one must intend doing it and specify it. If it is an unrestricted voluntary prayer, one need only intend doing it.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha defined niyyah as intending something together with doing it. In prayer, the intention must accompany its beginning, the opening takbir. He asked that the three kinds of prayer be reviewed carefully, since what the intention must contain depends on which one it is.

Kind of prayerExampleThe intention must contain
FardDhuhrDoing it + specifying it (Dhuhr, not Asr) + that it is fard
Restricted nafl, by time or causeWitr, Duha, the sunnah before or after Dhuhr, the eclipse prayerDoing it + specifying it
Unrestricted naflSimply two rak'ahsDoing it only

Specifying. A restricted nafl must be named by its recognized name or description: intend Eid al-Fitr or Eid al-Adha, not just "an Eid prayer." Adding that it is nafl is valid but not required.

Once the time or cause is gone, the restricted prayer is no longer prayed as that prayer: no eclipse prayer once the eclipse ends.

"From one who has reached puberty." The matn requires intending the obligatory status from an adult, not a child. The Shaykh noted that the issue has detail and disagreement, mentioning Ibn Hajar and al-Ramli, to be taken up in a later book.

(3)

Where the intention is. In the heart. Saying it quietly is recommended because the tongue helps the heart, as in pronouncing the intention for ihram. It is not required, and if said, it may be in one's own language, since the intention is about meaning. Announcing one's intention loudly in the masjid is a different matter from quietly pronouncing it.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the intention must accompany the opening takbir, and that what it must contain depends on whether the prayer is fard, restricted nafl, or unrestricted nafl.

(5)

2. The Opening Takbir

الثَّانِي: تَكْبِيرَةُ الْإِحْرَامِ، وَهُوَ أَنْ يَقُولَ: (اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ)، وَيُجْزِئُهُ (اللَّهُ الْأَكْبَرُ)، وَيَجِبُ أَنْ تَكُونَ هَذِهِ النِّيَّةُ مُقَارِنَةً لِلتَّكْبِيرِ جَمِيعِهِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّانِيthe second
  • تَكْبِيرَةُ الْإِحْرَامِthe opening takbir
  • وَهُوَ أَنْ يَقُولَwhich is to say
  • اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُAllah is greater
  • وَيُجْزِئُهُand it suffices him
  • اللَّهُ الْأَكْبَرُAllah is the Greatest
  • وَيَجِبُ أَنْ تَكُونَand it must be that
  • هَذِهِ النِّيَّةُthis intention
  • مُقَارِنَةًaccompanies
  • لِلتَّكْبِيرِ جَمِيعِهِthe whole of the takbir
Translation
The second is the opening takbir: to say "Allahu akbar," and "Allahu al-akbar" also suffices. This intention must accompany the whole of the takbir.
Commentary

The name of Allah must come first; reversing the order is not valid. A short insertion does no harm, such as "Allah, the Most Merciful, is greater," but a long string of names in between breaks it. The takbir is said standing (for one who must and can stand), facing the qiblah, and out loud enough to hear oneself; forming it in the mind is not enough.

Israr (Quiet). Speaking so that you hear yourself while the person beside you does not.

Jahr (Aloud). Speaking so that another hears.

(6)

The intention with the takbir. The matn requires it to accompany the whole takbir, from the first letter to the last. The Shaykh noted that many later Shafi'is chose a more accommodating position: it is enough that the intention coincides with part of the takbir.

Translation. Someone unable to say the takbir in Arabic may say it in his own language, since it is not Quran. Al-Fatihah cannot be translated (see below).

(7)

3. Standing

الثَّالِثُ: الْقِيَامُ إِنْ كَانَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَرْضاً وَقَدَرَ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّالِثُthe third
  • الْقِيَامُstanding
  • إِنْ كَانَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَرْضاًif the prayer is obligatory
  • وَقَدَرَand he is able
Translation
The third is standing, if the prayer is obligatory and he is able.
Commentary

"Obligatory" here covers the five prayers, a vowed prayer, communal obligations, and a prayer repeated in the form of a fard: someone who prayed Dhuhr in congregation and joins a second congregation may pray with them, and though it is recommended for him, he prays it as a fard, standing. In voluntary prayer, standing is not required even of someone able: he may sit or lie on his right side, with half the reward of standing.

وَمَنْ صَلَّى قَاعِداً فَلَهُ نِصْفُ أَجْرِ الْقَائِمِ
Whoever prays sitting has half the reward of the one standing. (al-Bukhari)

This halving is only for someone able who chooses to sit in a voluntary prayer, not for someone who sits because he cannot stand.

In a fard prayer, if he cannot...Then he...
StandSits
SitLies on his side, preferably the right
Lie on his sideLies on his back and indicates the movements
Move his headIndicates with his extremities, such as his fingers
Do any of thesePerforms the prayer in his heart, as long as his mind is present

What counts as inability. Severe hardship that is not normally bearable. Pain or tiredness one can tolerate does not permit sitting in a fard, and someone able to stand who sits has an invalid prayer. The Shaykh said it troubles him to see so many chairs in masjids.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that tolerable discomfort is not incapacity, and that as long as a person's mind is present he must never abandon the prayer, praying however he can.

(9)

4. Al-Fatihah

الرَّابِعُ: قِرَاءَةُ الْفَاتِحَةِ، وَيَجِبُ تَرْتِيبُهَا، وَمُوَالَاتُهَا، وَتَشْدِيدَاتُهَا، وَإِخْرَاجُ الضَّادِ مِنَ الظَّاءِ، وَتَجِبُ فِي كُلِّ رَكْعَةٍ إِلَّا رَكْعَةَ الْمَسْبُوقِ.
Vocabulary
  • الرَّابِعُthe fourth
  • قِرَاءَةُ الْفَاتِحَةِreciting al-Fatihah
  • وَيَجِبُ تَرْتِيبُهَاand its order is obligatory
  • وَمُوَالَاتُهَاand its continuity
  • وَتَشْدِيدَاتُهَاand its doubled letters
  • وَإِخْرَاجُ الضَّادِ مِنَ الظَّاءِand distinguishing the dad from the za
  • وَتَجِبُ فِي كُلِّ رَكْعَةٍand it is obligatory in every rak'ah
  • إِلَّا رَكْعَةَ الْمَسْبُوقِexcept the rak'ah of the latecomer
Translation
The fourth is reciting al-Fatihah. Its order, its continuity, its doubled letters, and distinguishing the dad from the za are obligatory. It is obligatory in every rak'ah, except the rak'ah of the latecomer.
Commentary
لَا صَلَاةَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يَقْرَأْ بِفَاتِحَةِ الْكِتَابِ
There is no prayer for one who does not recite the Opening of the Book.

Al-Fatihah is seven verses, and in the Shafi'i school the basmalah is one of them, so leaving it out leaves the recitation incomplete. It must be recited in Arabic, while standing, and aloud enough to hear oneself; thinking it or moving the lips silently does not count.

Not having memorized it is not an excuse. Someone who can read it from a phone, or follow someone prompting him, must do so. Memorizing it is better.

(10)
If he truly cannot recite al-FatihahHe does this instead
1st substituteSeven other verses of the Quran, preferably consecutive, with at least as many letters as al-Fatihah
2nd substituteSeven Arabic dhikrs: subhan Allah, al-hamdu lillah, la ilaha illa Allah, Allahu akbar, la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah, and so on
3rd substituteThose dhikrs translated into his own language
If none is possibleStands silently for as long as al-Fatihah takes, since standing is a separate integral

Why al-Fatihah is never translated. It is Quran, and a rendering of its meaning in another language is a tafsir of the meanings, not the Quran. A dhikr or the takbir is not Quran, so it can be translated.

(11)

What breaks continuity (muwalah). A long silence without an excuse. A short silence intended as stopping. An unrelated dhikr between verses.

What does not. A long silence from an excuse such as coughing or dizziness. A short pause without intending to stop. Saying "amin" to your own imam's Fatihah while you are still reciting yours. Saying "amin" to the imam of a neighboring masjid does break it.

(12)

Shaddahs. Lightening a doubled letter means the word was not recited, so repeat it. Doubling a light letter is an addition: harmless if the meaning is unchanged, invalidating if the meaning changes.

Dad and za. They must be distinguished, as in reciting الضَّالِّينَ correctly and not with a za.

(13)

The latecomer (masbuq) here is someone who did not catch, with the imam, enough time to recite al-Fatihah. If he finds the imam in ruku', he says the opening takbir and joins him in ruku'. If the imam is standing but bows before he finishes, he follows the imam, and the imam carries the rest for him. He does not finish al-Fatihah while bowing, since it must be recited standing.

The imam must be in a state of purity for this. If it turns out after the prayer that the imam had no wudu, he did not carry the latecomer's Fatihah, so the latecomer makes up that rak'ah. A follower who recited al-Fatihah himself has a valid prayer even in that case.

(14)

When there is time, the follower recites al-Fatihah himself. It is recommended for the imam to pause after al-Fatihah and after the surah so followers can recite; if he does not pause, the follower recites along with him, and there is no harm in that, as the Shaykh answered a question about praying Maghrib behind an imam.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the basmalah is part of al-Fatihah, that al-Fatihah must be recited standing and never finished in ruku', and that the imam carries a latecomer's Fatihah only if he himself is in a state of purity.

(15)

5 to 8. Ruku', I'tidal, and Tuma'ninah in Each

الْخَامِسُ: الرُّكُوعُ.
السَّادِسُ: الطُّمَأْنِينَةُ فِيهِ بِحَيْثُ يَسْتَقِرُّ كُلُّ عُضْوٍ مَكَانَهُ.
السَّابِعُ: الِاعْتِدَالُ.
الثَّامِنُ: الطُّمَأْنِينَةُ فِيهِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْخَامِسُthe fifth
  • الرُّكُوعُbowing
  • السَّادِسُthe sixth
  • الطُّمَأْنِينَةُ فِيهِstillness in it
  • بِحَيْثُsuch that
  • يَسْتَقِرُّsettles
  • كُلُّ عُضْوٍevery limb
  • مَكَانَهُin its place
  • السَّابِعُthe seventh
  • الِاعْتِدَالُstanding upright after ruku'
  • الثَّامِنُthe eighth
Translation
The fifth is bowing. The sixth is stillness in it, such that every limb settles in its place. The seventh is standing upright after it. The eighth is stillness in that.
Commentary

The minimum ruku'. Bending far enough that the palms could reach the knees; actually placing them there is recommended, not required. The complete form straightens the back and grasps the knees.

It must be meant as ruku'. Bending for another reason, to pick something up, at a bird, or to strike a snake or scorpion, does not count. He must stand back up and then bow intending ruku'. The same applies to sujud.

(16)

Tuma'ninah (Stillness). Described in the books as stillness after movement, or between two movements; either way, every limb settles in its place. It is required in ruku', i'tidal, sujud, and the sitting between the prostrations. Flowing straight from one posture into the next without settling does not fulfill it.

(17)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a movement that looks like ruku' does not count if it was made for another purpose, and that tuma'ninah is a real, settled pause, not a continuous transition.
