The Two Sermons الْخُطْبَتَانِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ الْخُطْبَتَيْنِ]
وَشُرُوطُ الْخُطْبَتَيْنِ خَمْسَةٌ:
الْأَوَّلُ: حَمْدُ اللهِ فِيهِمَا.
الثَّانِي: الصَّلَاةُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فِيهِمَا.
الثَّالِثُ: الْوَصِيَّةُ بِالتَّقْوَى فِيهِمَا.
الرَّابِعُ: قِرَاءَةُ آيَةٍ فِي الْأُولَى أَوْ فِي الثَّانِيَةِ.
الْخَامِسُ: الدُّعَاءُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ، وَلَوْ (رَحِمَكُمُ اللهُ) فِي الثَّانِيَةِ.
وَشُرُوطُهُمَا: إِسْمَاعُ أَرْبَعِينَ جَمَاعَةً، وَالْمُوَالَاةُ بَيْنَهُمَا وَبَيْنَ الصَّلَاةِ، وَطَهَارَةُ الْحَدَثِ الْأَصْغَرِ وَالْأَكْبَرِ، وَطَهَارَةُ النَّجَاسَةِ فِي الثَّوْبِ وَالْبَدَنِ وَالْمَكَانِ، وَالْقِيَامُ إِنْ قَدَرَ، وَسَتْرُ الْعَوْرَةِ، وَالْجُلُوسُ بَيْنَ الْخُطْبَتَيْنِ بِقَدْرِ الطُّمَأْنِينَةِ، وَأَنْ تَكُونَ بِالْعَرَبِيَّةِ وَبَعْدَ الزَّوَالِ.

Translation
A section on the two sermons. Their essentials are five: praising Allah in both; salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in both; enjoining taqwa in both; reciting a verse in the first or the second; and supplicating for the believers in the second, even with "may Allah have mercy on you." Their conditions: that forty hear them; continuity between the two, and between them and the prayer; purity from minor and major hadath; purity from impurity in garment, body, and place; standing if able; covering the 'awrah; sitting between the two sermons for the length of stillness; and that they be in Arabic and after zawal.
Commentary

The matn calls the first five "conditions"; Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha treated them as the sermons' obligatory elements (arkan), with the second list as their conditions. The sermons themselves are a condition of Jumu'ah, which is why they come before it. The Eid sermon comes after the Eid prayer because it is not a condition of it; one may pray Eid and leave.

The Five Essentials

  1. Praise of Allah, in both. The word hamd or a form of it, joined to the name Allah: "al-hamdu lillah," "inna al-hamda lillah," "ahmadu Allah." "Thanks to Allah," or praise joined to another name, does not suffice. This matters for sermons outside the Arab world.
  2. Salawat on the Prophet ﷺ, in both. His name need not be said; a description such as "the bringer of glad tidings, the warner, the illuminating lamp" suffices. Adding his family and Companions is recommended.
  3. Enjoining taqwa, in both. No set wording: any call to obey Allah or avoid what He forbids. Merely warning against worldly life, without a call to obedience, does not suffice.
  4. A verse, in either. It need not be a whole verse if the portion conveys a complete meaning; "then he looked" alone does not. Reciting it in the first sermon is better, avoiding a view that requires it there.
  5. Supplication for the believers, in the second. It must include those present, specifically or within "the Muslims," and must be for the next life. A purely worldly request, a car, a phone, money, does not fulfill it, though such requests are not forbidden.

One phrase cannot fulfill two essentials at once by a double intention. A verse containing praise or a command to taqwa, intended as both recitation and that essential, counts only as recitation. The five need not be in a fixed order, but the supplication must be in the second sermon.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that praise, salawat, and taqwa are in both sermons, the supplication for the believers is in the second, and the praise must use the word hamd joined to the name Allah.

(1)

The Conditions of the Sermons

  • Forty hear the essentials, not necessarily every word.
  • Continuity: no long break between the sermons, or between the second sermon and the prayer. A long talk or problem in between means the sermon is restarted. The sunnah is to sit between them for at least the length of stillness, about one tasbih; some recommend reciting Surat al-Ikhlas then.
  • The preacher's purity from hadath: if he loses wudu mid-sermon, makes wudu, and returns, he restarts from the beginning, as with a prayer; a substitute may continue from where he stopped.
  • Purity of the preacher's garment, body, and place.
  • Standing if able; if a capable preacher is available, standing is better.
  • Covering the preacher's 'awrah. Purity and covering concern the preacher; listeners need them for the prayer, not for listening.
  • The essentials in Arabic. The body of the sermon may be in the listeners' language. A preacher with little Arabic can say short Arabic formulas for the five essentials and give the rest in another language.
  • After zawal.
  • A male preacher.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the essentials must be in Arabic while the explanation may be in another language, and that a preacher who loses wudu restarts the sermon after renewing it.

(2)

Questions and Answers

Question: Must the listeners understand Arabic?

Answer: No. The essentials must be in Arabic, but the subject may be explained in their language.

Question: Must the five essentials come in a set order?

Answer: No; recitation may even come at the end. The supplication for the believers must be in the second sermon.

Question: Why pray Dhuhr after Jumu'ah?

Answer: As a precaution where a town has several Jumu'ahs without a clear need, or it is unknown which was first.
