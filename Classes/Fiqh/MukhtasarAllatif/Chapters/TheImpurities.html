The Impurities النَّجَاسَاتُ

Defining Najasah

Since this is a text for beginners, the author lists the impurities instead of opening with a long definition. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha gave a definition and asked the student to write it down and memorize it:

النَّجَاسَةُ: مُسْتَقْذَرٌ يَمْنَعُ مِنْ صِحَّةِ الصَّلَاةِ حَيْثُ لَا مُرَخِّصَ
Vocabulary
  • مُسْتَقْذَرٌsomething regarded as filthy
  • يَمْنَعُprevents
  • مِنْ صِحَّةِthe validity of
  • الصَّلَاةِprayer
  • حَيْثُwhere
  • لَا مُرَخِّصَthere is no dispensation
Najasah is something filthy that prevents the validity of prayer where there is no dispensation.

Linguistic filthiness and legal impurity are not the same. Nasal mucus and semen may be regarded as filthy in ordinary terms, yet they are legally pure. Wine is legally impure, even though some people do not find it filthy.

Najasah. Something filthy that prevents the validity of prayer where there is no dispensation.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes memorizing this definition, and separating what people find disgusting from what the Sharia rules impure.

(2)

Bodily Wastes and Blood

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي النَّجَاسَاتِ
وَالنَّجَاسَةُ هِيَ: الْبَوْلُ، وَالْغَائِطُ، وَالرَّوْثُ، وَالْقَيْحُ، وَالدَّمُ، وَالْقَيْءُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالنَّجَاسَةُand impurity
  • هِيَis
  • الْبَوْلُurine
  • وَالْغَائِطُand feces (human)
  • وَالرَّوْثُand dung (animal)
  • وَالْقَيْحُand pus
  • وَالدَّمُand blood
  • وَالْقَيْءُand vomit, what returns from the stomach
Translation
A section on impurities. Impurity is: urine, feces, dung, pus, blood, and vomit.
Commentary

Urine is impure in general; the Shaykh mentioned the urine of the Prophet as an exception. Gha'it is human waste and rawth is animal dung. Vomit means food that reached the stomach and came back out. If it only reached the throat and came back, it is not the vomit meant here.

Asked whether blood is only impure when it flows, the Shaykh said the base ruling is that all blood is impure. Whether a given amount is excused is a separate question with its own conditions, for example that it is the person's own blood, has not spread beyond its place, and has not mixed with anything else. The details come in Matn Abi Shuja.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the difference between a thing being impure and its being excused. Blood is impure in principle; when it is excused is a separate matter with conditions.

(3)

Intoxicants

وَالْخَمْرُ، وَالنَّبِيذُ، وَالْمُسْكِرُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْخَمْرُand wine, from grapes
  • وَالنَّبِيذُand nabidh, an intoxicating drink made from something other than grapes
  • وَالْمُسْكِرُand any intoxicant
And wine, nabidh, and any intoxicant.

In this legal usage, khamr means an intoxicating liquid. Linguistically, khamr is from grapes and nabidh from other sources such as dates, but both fall in the legal category of liquid intoxicants. Solid intoxicants, such as hashish and opium, are not impure. They are still prohibited: prohibition and impurity are two separate rulings.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that being prohibited does not make something impure. Solid intoxicants are haram to consume but are not najis.

(4)

Dogs and Pigs

وَالْكَلْبُ، وَالْخِنْزِيرُ، وَفَرْعُ أَحَدِهِمَا.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْكَلْبُand the dog
  • وَالْخِنْزِيرُand the pig
  • وَفَرْعُand the offspring of
  • أَحَدِهِمَاeither of them
And the dog, the pig, and the offspring of either.

The dog is impure even if trained for hunting or kept for guarding. The pig is worse: it may not be kept, while a dog may be kept for guarding or hunting, and a dog is only killed when it is harmful. There is disagreement over the impurity of the dog, while the jurists agree on the impurity of the pig.

An animal born from an impure animal and a pure one, such as a dog and a sheep, follows the lower, impure one. The Shaykh compared this to zakah, where offspring of an animal on which zakah is due and one on which it is not follow the lighter category.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that offspring of a pure and an impure animal follow the impure one in the ruling of purity.

(5)

Carcasses

وَالْمَيْتَةُ، وَشَعْرُهَا، وَرِيشُهَا، وَجِلْدُهَا، وَجَمِيعُ أَجْزَائِهَا.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْمَيْتَةُand the carcass
  • وَشَعْرُهَاand its hair
  • وَرِيشُهَاand its feathers
  • وَجِلْدُهَاand its skin
  • وَجَمِيعُand all of
  • أَجْزَائِهَاits parts
And the carcass, its hair, feathers, skin, and all its parts.

Maytah in fiqh is not "any animal that died". It is an animal whose life ended without valid ritual slaughter (dhakah shar'iyyah). An animal that died of illness, electric shock, or a fall is maytah, as is one slaughtered by someone whose slaughter is not valid. A validly slaughtered animal is not maytah. The Shaykh told of explaining this to a young student who, hearing "dead animals are haram", wondered whether one had to eat animals alive.

Maytah. An animal whose life ended without valid ritual slaughter.

(6)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes reading maytah in its technical sense, not as the everyday "dead animal". A properly slaughtered animal is dead but is not maytah.

(7)

Fluids

وَالْمَذْيُ، وَالْوَدْيُ، وَمَنِيُّ الْكَلْبِ وَالْخِنْزِيرِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْمَذْيُand madhy, a thin white fluid that comes with desire
  • وَالْوَدْيُand wady, a cloudy fluid after carrying something heavy or after urinating
  • وَمَنِيُّand the semen of
  • الْكَلْبِthe dog
  • وَالْخِنْزِيرِand the pig
And madhy, wady, and the semen of the dog and the pig.

Madhy and wady were described in the section on ghusl. The Shaykh mentioned, with caution, that he had heard from some teachers that wady after urination may indicate illness, adding "and Allah knows best." The semen of dogs and pigs is impure; the semen of other animals is not.

Milk, and What Is Pure Even in Death

وَلَبَنُ مَا لَا يُؤْكَلُ لَحْمُهُ غَيْرَ الْآدَمِيِّ. وَمَيْتَةُ الْآدَمِيِّ وَالسَّمَكِ وَالْجَرَادِ طَاهِرَاتٌ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَبَنُand the milk of
  • مَاwhat
  • لَا يُؤْكَلُis not eaten
  • لَحْمُهُits meat
  • غَيْرَother than
  • الْآدَمِيِّthe human being
  • وَمَيْتَةُand the dead body of
  • وَالسَّمَكِand fish
  • وَالْجَرَادِand locusts
  • طَاهِرَاتٌare pure
And the milk of an animal whose meat is not eaten, other than the human. The dead bodies of humans, fish, and locusts are pure.

Cow's milk is pure; donkey's milk is impure. Human milk is pure even though humans are not eaten, including, the Shaykh noted, milk from a male or a young girl.

The dead bodies of humans, fish, and locusts are pure. When the Quran describes the polytheists as najas, it refers to impurity of belief, not of their bodies.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the impurity of the polytheists mentioned in the Quran is spiritual, relating to their belief, and not physical impurity of the body.

(8)

Parts Separated from an Animal

وَالْجُزْءُ الْمُنْفَصِلُ مِنْ غَيْرِ الْآدَمِيِّ وَالسَّمَكِ وَالْجَرَادِ نَجِسٌ، إِلَّا شَعْرَ الْمَأْكُولِ وَرِيشَهُ، وَوَبَرَهُ، وَصُوفَهُ، وَالْمِسْكَ، وَفَأْرَتَهُ، وَإِنْفَحَتَهُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْجُزْءُand the part
  • الْمُنْفَصِلُseparated
  • مِنْ غَيْرِfrom other than
  • نَجِسٌis impure
  • إِلَّاexcept
  • شَعْرَthe hair of
  • الْمَأْكُولِan animal that is eaten
  • وَرِيشَهُand its feathers
  • وَوَبَرَهُand its soft hair, as of camels and goats
  • وَصُوفَهُand its wool
  • وَالْمِسْكَand musk
  • وَفَأْرَتَهُand its pod, the sac that holds the musk
  • وَإِنْفَحَتَهُand its rennet
A part separated from anything other than a human, fish, or locust is impure, except the hair, feathers, soft hair, and wool of an edible animal, and musk, its pod, and rennet.

The principle comes from the hadith: what is cut from an animal while it is alive is like its dead body. A leg cut from a living cow is impure, because a dead cow is impure. A severed human arm is pure, because a dead human is pure.

The hair, feathers, and wool of edible animals are the exception: cow hair and chicken feathers are pure, while cat hair is not. Musk comes from the blood of a gazelle, which would normally be impure, but it is excepted, as is the skin-like pod that holds it.

Ma Qutia min al-Bahimah wa Hiya Hayyah. What is cut from a living animal takes the ruling of that animal's dead body: impure if its carcass is impure, pure if its carcass is pure.
