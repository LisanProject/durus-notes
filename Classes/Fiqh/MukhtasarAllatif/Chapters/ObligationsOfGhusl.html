The Obligations and Sunnahs of Ghusl فُرُوضُ الْغُسْلِ

The First Obligation: The Intention

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي فُرُوضِ الْغُسْلِ
فُرُوضُ الْغُسْلِ شَيْئَانِ:
الْأَوَّلُ: النِّيَّةُ، وَهُوَ أَنْ يَنْوِيَ رَفْعَ الْجَنَابَةِ، أَوْ رَفْعَ الْحَدَثِ الْأَكْبَرِ، أَوْ رَفْعَ الْحَدَثِ، وَنَحْوَ ذَلِكَ عِنْدَ غَسْلِ أَوَّلِ جُزْءٍ مِنْ بَدَنِهِ.
Vocabulary
  • فُرُوضُthe obligations of
  • الْغُسْلِghusl
  • شَيْئَانِtwo things
  • الْأَوَّلُthe first
  • النِّيَّةُthe intention
  • وَهُوَand it is
  • أَنْ يَنْوِيَthat he intends
  • رَفْعَremoving, lifting
  • الْجَنَابَةِjanabah, the major impurity from intercourse or emission
  • أَوْor
  • الْحَدَثِthe hadath
  • الْأَكْبَرِmajor
  • وَنَحْوَand the like of
  • ذَلِكَthat
  • عِنْدَat the moment of
  • غَسْلِwashing
  • أَوَّلِthe first
  • جُزْءٍpart
  • مِنْof
  • بَدَنِهِhis body
Translation
A section on the obligations of ghusl. The obligations of ghusl are two. The first is the intention: that he intends to lift janabah, or to lift the major hadath, or to lift the hadath, or the like, at the washing of the first part of his body.
Commentary

Valid intentions include lifting janabah, lifting the major hadath, performing the obligatory ghusl, or performing ghusl for menstruation, nifas, or childbirth. The intention is made when water first touches the first part of the body washed.

This differs from wudu. In wudu the intention is tied to the face, the first obligatory limb. In ghusl the whole body is one unit, so a person may begin with the head, an arm, a leg, the back, or anywhere else, and makes the intention when the water first reaches the part he starts with.

Intention is generally made at the start of an act of worship. Fasting, zakah, expiation, and sacrifice are exceptions where it may come before the act.

If someone mistakenly intends the wrong cause, for example intending to lift the impurity of intercourse when it was actually a wet dream, the intention is valid as long as the mistake was unintentional. Deliberately intending a cause that did not happen is not valid. What intention suffices when more than one cause of ghusl has occurred will be covered later.

One may intend to lift "the hadath" without saying "major", provided he means the major hadath that is upon him. As explained earlier, the books use unqualified "hadath" for the minor state, but a person's intention means what he means by it.

Intending simply "ghusl" is not enough, because ghusl can be ordinary washing for cleanliness as well as worship. Wudu in its specific form is only ever an act of worship, which is why intending "wudu" suffices there. The intention for ghusl must identify its ritual purpose.

Janabah. The major ritual impurity, from intercourse or the emission of semen, that requires ghusl.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that intending "ghusl" alone does not suffice, while intending "wudu" alone does. Ghusl can be a mere bath; wudu is only ever worship.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that in ghusl the body is a single unit. There is no required starting point, and the intention goes with whatever part the water touches first.

(3)

The Second Obligation: Washing All Hair and Skin

الثَّانِي: غَسْلُ جَمِيعِ شَعْرِهِ الْخَفِيفِ وَالْكَثِيفِ، وَجَمِيعِ بَشَرَتِهِ حَتَّى مَا تَحْتَ قُلْفَةِ الْأَقْلَفِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّانِيthe second
  • غَسْلُwashing
  • جَمِيعِall of
  • شَعْرِهِhis hair
  • الْخَفِيفِthe light, through which the skin can be seen
  • وَالْكَثِيفِand the thick, through which it cannot
  • وَجَمِيعِand all of
  • بَشَرَتِهِhis skin
  • حَتَّىeven
  • مَا تَحْتَwhat is beneath
  • قُلْفَةِthe foreskin of
  • الْأَقْلَفِthe uncircumcised man
Translation
The second is washing all of his hair, light and thick, and all of his skin, even what is beneath the foreskin of an uncircumcised man.
Commentary

Every hair, light or thick, and all the outer skin must be washed. In wudu there is a concession for the inside of a man's dense beard and side-whiskers, because wudu is repeated five or six times a day or more and requiring it would be a hardship. Obligatory ghusl is far less frequent, so the concession does not carry over.

Water must reach beneath the foreskin of an uncircumcised man. The foreskin is extra skin meant to be removed by circumcision, so what lies beneath it counts as outer body. This applies to the living and also when washing the deceased, if it can be reached. If a deceased person's body has stiffened and the foreskin cannot be moved, the Shaykh said it is not to be cut, since the deceased is treated with honor and no part of him removed; instead he is given tayammum. There is a disagreement here, and the Shaykh noted a view in Hashiyat al-Bujayrami permitting cutting, but he favored not cutting.

The obligation is the outer body, not the inside of the mouth or nose. But if there is najasah inside the mouth, nose, or ear, it must be removed, which is a separate requirement from ghusl itself. The armpits and the folds of the body are outer body and must be washed.

Qulfah (Foreskin). The skin removed in circumcision.

Aqlaf. An uncircumcised man.

Zahir al-Badan (Outer Body). The outer surface of the body, including the folds and armpits, but not internal cavities like the inside of the mouth and nose.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes why the beard concession of wudu does not apply in ghusl: wudu is frequent and the concession removes hardship, while ghusl is occasional, so every hair and the skin beneath it must be reached.

(5)

The Sunnahs of Ghusl

وَسُنَنُهُ: السِّوَاكُ، وَالتَّسْمِيَةُ، وَالْوُضُوءُ قَبْلَ الْغُسْلِ، وَتَعَهُّدُ الْمَعَاطِفِ، وَتَخْلِيلُ الشَّعْرِ، وَالدَّلْكُ، وَالتَّثْلِيثُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَسُنَنُهُand its sunnahs
  • السِّوَاكُthe siwak
  • وَالتَّسْمِيَةُand the basmalah
  • وَالْوُضُوءُand wudu
  • قَبْلَbefore
  • الْغُسْلِthe ghusl
  • وَتَعَهُّدُand attending carefully to
  • الْمَعَاطِفِthe folds and creases of the body
  • وَتَخْلِيلُand passing the fingers through
  • الشَّعْرِthe hair
  • وَالدَّلْكُand rubbing the body
  • وَالتَّثْلِيثُand doing it three times
Translation
Its sunnahs are: the siwak, the basmalah, wudu before the ghusl, attending to the folds of the body, passing the fingers through the hair, rubbing, and doing it three times.
Commentary

The siwak is recommended here by analogy with wudu. For the basmalah, a person in janabah must not intend Quran recitation, since reciting Quran is prohibited in janabah. He may intend dhikr, or have no particular intention. Intending recitation, alone or together with dhikr, is prohibited.

Wudu is recommended with ghusl and is fulfilled whether done before, during, or after it, even in parts at different times. Completing the wudu before the ghusl is best. The Shaykh mentioned that Imam al-Ghazali preferred to make wudu except for the feet, complete the ghusl, then wash the feet.

Attending to the folds means making sure water reaches places it does not reach easily, such as the ears, the armpits, and the folds of the abdomen. Passing the fingers through the hair helps the water reach its roots. Rubbing the body is recommended in the Shafi'i school in order to avoid the disagreement of scholars who hold it obligatory. Each washing is done three times.

The Shaykh said the next lesson would cover the ordered, recommended method of ghusl. The minimum ghusl is simply getting water to the entire outer body; the recommended ghusl follows a set sequence.

Ta'ahhud al-Ma'atif. Checking the folds and creases of the body so water reaches them.

Takhlil al-Sha'r. Passing the fingers through the hair so water reaches the roots.

Dalk. Rubbing the body, recommended to avoid the disagreement of those who require it.

(6)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that one in janabah says the basmalah intending dhikr, never Quran recitation, since reciting the Quran is prohibited for him.

(7)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha's advice: fiqh is "a lion in the form of a cat." It looks easy at first, but it is full of fine distinctions between similar cases. Connect related rulings and learn to tell similar issues apart, rather than memorizing each ruling on its own.

(8)
