What Makes Ghusl Obligatory مُوجِبَاتُ الْغُسْلِ

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي مُوجِبَاتِ الْغُسْلِ
وَيَجِبُ الْغُسْلُ مِنْ خَمْسَةِ أَشْيَاءَ:
مِنْ إِيلَاجِ الْحَشَفَةِ فِي الْفَرْجِ. وَمِنْ خُرُوجِ الْمَنِيِّ. وَمِنَ الْحَيْضِ. وَالنِّفَاسِ. وَالْوِلَادَةِ.

  • وَيَجِبُand it is obligatory
  • مِنْbecause of, from
  • خَمْسَةِfive
  • أَشْيَاءَthings
  • إِيلَاجِinsertion

Translation
A section on what makes ghusl obligatory. Ghusl is obligatory because of five things: insertion of the glans into a private opening; the emission of semen; menstruation; postnatal bleeding; and childbirth.

What Ghusl Is

Linguistically, ghusl is water flowing over something. Pouring water over a pen is ghusl in the linguistic sense. Legally, ghusl is water flowing over the entire body with a specific intention. A shower taken without that intention is washing in the ordinary sense, not the ritual ghusl.

"Five things" means any one of them is enough to make ghusl obligatory; they do not have to occur together. Washing a deceased Muslim is also obligatory, but it is an obligation on the living, not something the deceased does for himself, so it is not counted in this list.

Ghusl. Linguistically, water flowing over something. Legally, water flowing over the entire body with a specific intention.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a shower without the intention is not ghusl in the legal sense, however thoroughly it covers the body.

(2)

The First Cause: Insertion

مِنْ إِيلَاجِ الْحَشَفَةِ فِي الْفَرْجِ.
From insertion of the glans into a private opening.

Ghusl becomes obligatory once the glans is inserted, even if there is no emission. The wording is general, so the ruling of ghusl applies whether the act is lawful or unlawful; the Shaykh gave the prohibited cases of zina, an animal, or a corpse as examples covered by the wording, while making clear that they are prohibited. A deceased person who has already been washed is not washed again because of this. Farj here includes both the front and rear openings.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that insertion alone makes ghusl obligatory, with or without ejaculation.

(3)

The Second Cause: Emission of Semen, and How to Tell It Apart

وَمِنْ خُرُوجِ الْمَنِيِّ.
And from the emission of semen.

Semen is recognized by any one of three signs:

1. Pleasure. It exits with desire and is followed by relaxation (futur).

2. Gushing. It comes out in spurts (tadaffuq).

3. Smell. When wet it smells like date-palm pollen or dough; when dry, like egg white.

(4)

These signs help a person identify a fluid found on his clothing. One sign is enough; they do not all need to be present. If none is present, it is not semen.

Two other fluids are easily confused with it:

  • Madhy. A thin, white, sticky fluid that comes with desire, though its exit is not itself pleasurable and may go unnoticed.
  • Wady. A cloudy fluid, sometimes only a drop or two, that may come after carrying something heavy or after urinating.

Mani (Semen). Pure. Makes ghusl obligatory. Washing it from clothing is not required, though one may.

Madhy and Wady. Impure. Break wudu but do not require ghusl. Clothing they touch must be washed.

(5)

This is why the distinction matters. If a person finds something on his clothing and cannot tell whether it is mani or madhy, he may choose which to treat it as. If he treats it as mani, he does ghusl. If he treats it as madhy, he does wudu and washes the clothing. Best is to combine both: do ghusl and also wash the clothing.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that when unsure whether it is mani or madhy, the safest course is to do ghusl and wash the clothing, satisfying both rulings.

(6)

The Third to Fifth Causes: Menstruation, Postnatal Bleeding, and Childbirth

وَمِنَ الْحَيْضِ. وَالنِّفَاسِ. وَالْوِلَادَةِ.
And from menstruation, postnatal bleeding, and childbirth.

Hayd is blood that comes from the womb. Nifas is blood that comes after the womb has been emptied by childbirth. With twins, blood between the first and second birth is not nifas, since the womb is not yet empty. Hayd and nifas share many rulings, which come in their own chapter.

Ghusl for menstruation is not valid until the bleeding stops. Once it stops, she has time to perform it: if the bleeding stops at 7 a.m. and Dhuhr comes in at noon, she has until the prayer time. Once the prayer is due, she must perform ghusl in order to pray it.

Childbirth itself requires ghusl even if no blood comes out. The jurists discuss a "dry" birth, though the Shaykh said he was unsure whether it occurs medically. A Caesarean birth also counts. If a woman gives birth without bleeding, does ghusl, and then bleeding appears a day or two later, that blood may be nifas, a separate cause of ghusl.

Hayd (Menstruation). Blood that comes from a woman's womb.

Nifas (Postnatal Bleeding). Blood that comes after the womb has been emptied by childbirth.

Wiladah (Childbirth). Requires ghusl in itself, even with no bleeding, including by Caesarean section.

(7)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that childbirth is its own cause of ghusl independent of bleeding, and that blood between twins is not nifas because the womb is not yet empty.

(8)
