The Nullifiers of Wudu نَوَاقِضُ الْوُضُوءِ

The Four Nullifiers at a Glance

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي نَوَاقِضِ الْوُضُوءِ
وَيَنْقُضُ الْوُضُوءَ أَرْبَعَةُ أَشْيَاءَ:
الْأَوَّلُ: الْخَارِجُ مِنْ أَحَدِ السَّبِيلَيْنِ الْقُبُلِ وَالدُّبُرِ.
الثَّانِي: زَوَالُ الْعَقْلِ بِنَوْمٍ أَوْ غَيْرِهِ، إِلَّا نَوْمَ مُمَكِّنٍ مَقْعَدَتَهُ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ.
الثَّالِثُ: الْتِقَاءُ بَشَرَتَيِ الرَّجُلِ وَالْمَرْأَةِ الْكَبِيرَيْنِ الْأَجْنَبِيَّيْنِ.
الرَّابِعُ: مَسُّ قُبُلِ الْآدَمِيِّ أَوْ حَلْقَةِ دُبُرِهِ بِبَطْنِ الْكَفِّ وَبُطُونِ الْأَصَابِعِ.
Translation
A section on the nullifiers of wudu. Wudu is nullified by four things. First, anything that exits from one of the two passages, the front and the rear. Second, loss of reason through sleep or otherwise, except the sleep of one whose seat is firmly settled on the ground. Third, the meeting of the skin of a man and a woman who are both grown and non-mahram to each other. Fourth, touching a human's front private part or the ring of the anus with the inner palm or the inner surfaces of the fingers.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that some books title this chapter the causes of hadath (asbab al-hadath) instead of the nullifiers of wudu. Both refer to the same thing: causes that bring the state of wudu to an end. Once one of them occurs, the person's wudu is gone.

Nawaqid al-Wudu (Nullifiers of Wudu). Causes that end the state of wudu.

Hadath. A non-visible, legally recognized state that prevents prayer. Practically, it can be understood as the causes by which purification ends.

Hadath Asghar (Minor). A state lifted by wudu.

Hadath Akbar (Major). A state lifted by ghusl, such as after menstruation, postnatal bleeding, or childbirth.

(1)

The First Nullifier: Anything Exiting the Two Passages

الْأَوَّلُ: الْخَارِجُ مِنْ أَحَدِ السَّبِيلَيْنِ الْقُبُلِ وَالدُّبُرِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْأَوَّلُthe first
  • الْخَارِجُthat which exits
  • مِنْfrom
  • أَحَدِone of
  • السَّبِيلَيْنِthe two passages
  • الْقُبُلِthe front passage
  • وَالدُّبُرِand the rear passage
Translation
The first: anything that exits from one of the two passages, the front and the rear.
Commentary

Anything that exits either passage nullifies wudu, whether it is customary, like urine and feces, or unusual, like a kidney stone. What exits from the nose, mouth, or ear does not fall under this nullifier.

The exiting must be certain. If a person merely doubts whether something came out, his wudu remains. The ruling is for a living person: if something exits a deceased person after he has been washed, it is cleaned away, but the washing does not have to be repeated.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that doubt does not nullify. A person who is unsure whether anything exited still has his wudu; he must be certain.

(2)

There is one exception in the Shafi'i school. If a person who is awake and already has wudu has a seminal emission from thinking or looking, he must perform ghusl but his wudu is not broken. This is not the case of an emission in sleep, since the sleep itself already broke his wudu, nor of intercourse, which has its own ruling. The principle behind it:

مَا أَوْجَبَ أَعْظَمَ الْأَمْرَيْنِ بِخُصُوصِهِ لَا يُوجِبُ أَدْوَنَهُمَا بِعُمُومِهِ
What necessitates the greater of two matters specifically does not necessitate the lesser of them through its general description.

Ghusl is the greater purification and wudu the lesser. The emission requires ghusl specifically, so its general description as "something that exited" does not add a separate requirement of wudu.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes this maxim and wrote it out for the student to keep: when something specifically requires the greater ruling, the lesser one is not added on top of it.

(3)

The Second Nullifier: Loss of Reason

الثَّانِي: زَوَالُ الْعَقْلِ بِنَوْمٍ أَوْ غَيْرِهِ، إِلَّا نَوْمَ مُمَكِّنٍ مَقْعَدَتَهُ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّانِيthe second
  • زَوَالُloss, removal
  • الْعَقْلِreason; here meaning discernment and awareness
  • بِنَوْمٍthrough sleep
  • أَوْor
  • غَيْرِهِother than it
  • إِلَّاexcept
  • نَوْمَthe sleep of
  • مُمَكِّنٍone who firmly settles
  • مَقْعَدَتَهُhis seat, his buttocks
  • مِنَon, from
  • الْأَرْضِthe ground
Translation
The second: loss of reason through sleep or otherwise, except the sleep of one whose seat is firmly settled on the ground.
Commentary

Linguistically, 'aql is connected to the idea of restraining, since reason holds a person back from shameful acts. Technically it is a quality by which a person distinguishes good from bad. For this ruling, what is meant is discernment and awareness. Anything that removes it nullifies wudu: sleep, fainting, insanity, illness, or a seizure. Some books also mention magic.

The exception is sleeping while the buttocks are firmly settled on what one sits on, with no gap between the body and the support. The support need not be the ground; it can be a chair, an animal, or anything else. The Companions used to doze while waiting for the prayer, their heads nodding, then stand and pray without renewing wudu. The jurists reconciled this with the hadith "whoever sleeps, let him make wudu" by keeping sleep as a nullifier in general while excluding this one posture.

For the exception to apply, the person must wake up in the same position he fell asleep in. If he slumps over or wakes in a different position, his wudu is broken. The Shaykh also noted that some body types may not be able to sit without a gap, and the exception would not apply to them.

If a trustworthy person tells someone who slept seated that he heard a sound from him, Ibn Hajar holds that his wudu is broken, while al-Ramli holds that it is not. Either position may be followed.

'Aql (Reason). A quality by which a person distinguishes good from bad. For this ruling, its loss means loss of discernment or awareness.

Tamkin al-Maq'adah (Firmly Settled Seat). Sleeping with the buttocks settled on the support, with no gap between them.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes both conditions of the sleep exception: the seat must be firmly settled with no gap, and the person must wake in the same position. Losing either one breaks the wudu.

(5)

The Third Nullifier: Skin Contact Between a Man and a Woman

الثَّالِثُ: الْتِقَاءُ بَشَرَتَيِ الرَّجُلِ وَالْمَرْأَةِ الْكَبِيرَيْنِ الْأَجْنَبِيَّيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّالِثُthe third
  • الْتِقَاءُthe meeting, contact
  • بَشَرَتَيِthe two skins of
  • الرَّجُلِthe man
  • وَالْمَرْأَةِand the woman
  • الْكَبِيرَيْنِthe two grown ones, of an age to be ordinarily desired
  • الْأَجْنَبِيَّيْنِthe two non-mahram to each other
Translation
The third: the meeting of the skin of a man and a woman who are both grown and non-mahram to each other.
Commentary

"Grown" (kabir) here does not mean having reached puberty. Puberty is established by signs such as menstruation or a seminal emission, or by reaching fifteen years. In this ruling, kabir means reaching the age at which a person would ordinarily be desired by people of sound disposition, which can come before legal puberty.

The conditions for this nullifier:

  • The contact is skin to skin with no barrier. Touching hair, a nail, or a tooth does not nullify, and neither does touching through gloves.
  • The two are of different sexes. A man touching a man, or a woman touching a woman, does not nullify.
  • Both have reached the age described above.
  • They are not mahram to each other.

In the Shafi'i school, desire is not a condition, so accidental contact also nullifies. The Shaykh contrasted this with the Maliki school, where desire is required. In the relied-upon Shafi'i position, both the one who touches and the one touched lose their wudu. There is another opinion within the school that only the one who touches loses it, and the Shaykh said this can be useful in situations of real hardship, such as the crowds of Hajj and Umrah, where following another position or school may be needed.

Kabir (Grown), in this ruling. Having reached the age at which a person would ordinarily be desired by people of sound disposition. It does not mean puberty.

(6)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that in the Shafi'i school skin contact between a qualifying man and woman breaks both their wudu even without desire and even by accident, as long as there is no barrier.

(7)

Who Is a Mahram?

Touching a mahram does not nullify wudu. A mahram is someone one is permanently forbidden to marry. This excludes someone who is only temporarily forbidden, such as any woman for a man already married to four, or his wife's sister or aunt while he remains married to his wife.

Through Lineage (Nasab), Seven. Mothers and grandmothers, daughters and their descendants, sisters, paternal aunts, maternal aunts, brothers' daughters, and sisters' daughters.

Through Nursing (Rada'ah), Seven. The same seven categories established through nursing. The details come later in the book.

Through Marriage (Musaharah), Four. The wife's mother, the wife's daughter, the father's wife, and the son's wife.

(8)

The Fourth Nullifier: Touching the Private Part

الرَّابِعُ: مَسُّ قُبُلِ الْآدَمِيِّ أَوْ حَلْقَةِ دُبُرِهِ بِبَطْنِ الْكَفِّ وَبُطُونِ الْأَصَابِعِ.
Vocabulary
  • الرَّابِعُthe fourth
  • مَسُّtouching
  • قُبُلِthe front private part
  • الْآدَمِيِّof a human being
  • أَوْor
  • حَلْقَةِthe ring of
  • دُبُرِهِhis rear passage
  • بِبَطْنِwith the inner part of
  • الْكَفِّthe palm
  • وَبُطُونِand the inner surfaces of
  • الْأَصَابِعِthe fingers
Translation
The fourth: touching a human's front private part or the ring of the anus with the inner palm or the inner surfaces of the fingers.
Commentary

The part of the hand that counts is the inner palm and the pads of the fingers, which the Shaykh demonstrated. Touching with the fingertips does not count. Touching the testicles does not nullify, nor does touching an animal's private part.

Here only the one who touches loses his wudu, not the one whose private part is touched. This differs from the third nullifier, where both lose it.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the contrast between the last two nullifiers: contact between a man and a woman breaks both people's wudu, while touching a private part breaks only the toucher's.

(9)
