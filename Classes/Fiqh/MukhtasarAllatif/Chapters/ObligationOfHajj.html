The Obligation of Hajj and Umrah وُجُوبُ الْحَجِّ وَالْعُمْرَةِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي الْحَجِّ]
وَيَجِبُ الْحَجُّ وَالْعُمْرَةُ عَلَى الْمُسْلِمِ، الْبَالِغِ، الْعَاقِلِ، الْحُرِّ، الْقَادِرِ عَلَيْهِ بِنَفْسِهِ أَوْ بِغَيْرِهِ إِنْ عَجَزَ بِمَرَضٍ لَا يُرْجَى بُرْؤُهُ أَوْ كِبَرٍ.
Vocabulary

  • الْحَجُّ وَالْعُمْرَةُHajj and Umrah

  • الْعَاقِلِthe sane

  • الْقَادِرِ عَلَيْهِthe one able to do it

  • إِنْ عَجَزَif he is unable
  • بِمَرَضٍ لَا يُرْجَى بُرْؤُهُthrough an illness with no hope of recovery

Translation
A section on Hajj. Hajj and Umrah are obligatory on the Muslim who has reached puberty, is sane, free, and able to do it, himself or through another if he is unable through an illness with no hope of recovery or through old age.
Commentary

Linguistically, hajj means heading for something. In the Sharia it is heading for the Sacred House to perform the rites. Both hajj and hijj are read in the Quran:

وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا

"Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by people who are able to make the journey." It is one of the five on which Islam is built. Hajj was known to earlier nations through Ibrahim, peace be upon him, but its particular form, with the circuits and standing at Arafah, belongs to this Ummah. It expiates minor sins by agreement; the scholars differed over major sins and the rights of others.

The relied-upon position of the school is that Umrah is obligatory. Imam Malik and Imam Abu Hanifa held it is a sunnah.

Two Kinds of Obligation

IndividualCommunal
On whomEach person who meets the conditionsThe Ummah as a whole
How oftenOnce in a lifetime; any further Hajj is voluntaryEvery year; if no one performs it in a year, all are sinful, even in war or plague
TimingMay be delayed (tarakhi), not immediateThat year

A person who becomes able may delay Hajj, but sins if he dies able and without having performed it.

  • Islam: an original disbeliever is not asked to perform Hajj in this world, though he is punished in the next for disbelief and for leaving the branches, such as prayer, zakah, and Hajj. "Original" distinguishes him from an apostate, whose rulings differ.
  • Puberty: a child's Hajj is valid, rewarded, and voluntary, but does not fulfill the Hajj of Islam. He must go again once adult and able.
  • Sanity and freedom: not obligatory on the insane or a slave, whose time belongs to his master's service.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime and may be delayed, and that a child's Hajj is valid and rewarded but does not replace the obligatory Hajj after puberty.

(1)

Being Able Oneself

  • Provisions for the journey there and back.
  • Transport, for one two marhalahs or more from Makkah (about 82 to 84 km, two days' travel). One closer than that walks if otherwise able.
  • A safe route, free of highway robbers.
  • Time to arrive before the standing at Arafah ends. If the journey takes four days and he would arrive on the Day of Sacrifice, he is not able that year.
  • For a woman: her husband, a mahram such as a brother or son, or trustworthy women (two or more; some say three). Organized Hajj groups make this easier today.

Older Hajj journeys took three or four months, sometimes a year. The Shaykh recommended Muslim travel literature, such as the Rihlah of Ibn Jubayr, who went from al-Andalus through Morocco, Libya, and Egypt to Makkah, and back through Iraq and Syria.

Being Able Through Another

One permanently unable through incurable illness or old age (al-ma'dub) sends someone to perform Hajj for him, for a fee or as a volunteer. Whether this applies to one living closer than the distance of shortening was left for later, since the books differ.

  • The deputy must already have performed his own obligatory Hajj, and must be trustworthy.
  • A Muslim who died owing Hajj: if he left an estate, a Hajj is paid for from it; if not, it is recommended that an heir or anyone else perform it for him.
  • No Hajj is performed for one who died an apostate.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that ability is either personal or through another, and that personal ability includes provisions, transport where needed, a safe route, and time to reach the rites.

(2)

Questions and Answers

Question: I can afford Hajj now. If I delay and later become ill, am I sinful?

Answer: The obligation may be delayed; the sin is in dying able without having gone. If he becomes permanently unable, someone performs it on his behalf.

Question: I have saved for a flat before marriage, and the money could pay for Hajj. Must I go first?

Answer: Hajj has become obligatory on him, but since it may be delayed, he may buy the flat and marry, then go later. The obligation remains.
