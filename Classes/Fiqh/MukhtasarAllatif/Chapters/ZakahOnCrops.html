Zakah on Crops and Fruits زَكَاةُ الزُّرُوعِ وَالثِّمَارِ

The Text

Text
وَلَا يَجِبُ فِي الزُّرُوعِ وَالثِّمَارِ إِلَّا فِيمَا يُقْتَاتُ فِي حَالَةِ الِاخْتِيَارِ، فَيَجِبُ بِبُدُوِّ صَلَاحِ الثَّمَرِ وَاشْتِدَادِ الْحَبِّ بِشَرْطِ أَنْ تَكُونَ ثَلَاثَ مِئَةِ صَاعٍ، وَالصَّاعُ: أَرْبَعَةُ أَمْدَادٍ.
وَيَجِبُ فِي ذَلِكَ نِصْفُ الْعُشْرِ إِنْ سُقِيَ بِمُؤْنَةٍ، وَإِنْ سُقِيَ بِغَيْرِ مُؤْنَةٍ كَمَطَرٍ.. فَفِيهِ الْعُشْرُ، جَافًّا مُنَقًّى.
وَيُضَمُّ الزَّرْعُ بَعْضُهُ إِلَى بَعْضٍ فِي إِكْمَالِ النِّصَابِ إِذَا كَانَتْ جِنْسًا وَاحِدًا وَحُصِدَتْ فِي عَامٍ وَاحِدٍ.

Translation
Zakah is due on crops and fruits only in what is eaten as a staple in ordinary circumstances. It becomes due when the fruit begins to ripen and the grain hardens, on condition that it reaches three hundred sa', the sa' being four mudds. Due on it is half a tenth if it was watered at a cost, and if watered without cost, such as by rain, a tenth, given dried and cleaned. Crops are combined to complete the nisab if they are one kind and harvested in one year.
Commentary

This is also called zakat al-mu'ashsharat, "the tithed," since the amount is a tenth or half of one.

What It Is Due On

A staple (qut) is food that sustains the body and is ordinarily stored: wheat, barley, rice, chickpeas, corn. Something stored only in famine does not count. Of fruits, only dates and grapes, which are dried and kept. Tomatoes, bananas, apples, and other fruits and vegetables owe no zakah in the school.

When It Becomes Due

CropSign that it is due
DatesColor begins to change in some of the fruit, not all of it
GrapesThe Shaykh mentioned their filling with juice, and said it needed checking
GrainIt hardens

The obligation is fixed at that point, but it is paid after harvest, once dried and cleaned. Whoever owns the crop when ripening begins owes it: if the farm is sold before, the buyer pays; if sold after, the seller.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that ripening fixes the obligation while payment comes after the harvest, and that a staple is what is ordinarily eaten and stored, not what is stored only in need.

Combining Harvests

Harvests are combined to reach the nisab if they are one kind and harvested within twelve months. Varieties of wheat combine with each other; wheat does not combine with barley. Four wasq of wheat and four of barley owe nothing, though they make eight.

A harvest in Rabi' 1448 and another in Muharram 1449 fall within a year and combine; Muharram 1448 and Safar 1449 do not.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that harvests combine only when they are one kind and within one year.
