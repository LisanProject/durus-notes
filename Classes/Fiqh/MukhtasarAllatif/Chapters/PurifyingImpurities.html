What Can Be Purified and What Cannot مَا يَطْهُرُ وَمَا لَا يَطْهُرُ

The previous section listed the impurities; this one explains how they are removed. Most are washed away with water, but some become pure through istihalah, a change from one state into another.

Purification by Transformation

Text
فَصْلٌ فِيمَا يَطْهُرُ وَمَا لَا يَطْهُرُ
وَتَطْهُرُ الْخَمْرَةُ إِذَا تَخَلَّلَتْ بِنَفْسِهَا، وَكَذَلِكَ النَّبِيذُ مِنَ التَّمْرِ وَالزَّبِيبِ.
وَجِلْدُ الْمَيْتَةِ إِذَا دُبِغَ.
Vocabulary
  • فِيمَاon what
  • يَطْهُرُbecomes pure
  • وَمَا لَا يَطْهُرُand what does not
  • وَتَطْهُرُand becomes pure
  • الْخَمْرَةُwine
  • إِذَاif
  • تَخَلَّلَتْit turned into vinegar
  • بِنَفْسِهَاby itself
  • وَكَذَلِكَand likewise
  • النَّبِيذُnabidh
  • مِنَ التَّمْرِfrom dates
  • وَالزَّبِيبِand raisins
  • وَجِلْدُand the hide of
  • الْمَيْتَةِa carcass
  • دُبِغَit is tanned
Translation
A section on what becomes pure and what does not. Wine becomes pure if it turns into vinegar by itself, and likewise nabidh from dates and raisins. And the hide of a carcass when it is tanned.
Commentary

Wine is impure as long as it is wine. If it becomes vinegar on its own, with nothing added to make it change, it becomes pure, and the vessel and its lid become pure along with it. If something is added to make it turn, that is not the same as turning by itself; the Shaykh deferred the detailed cases, such as something added and then removed.

The hide of a validly slaughtered animal is already pure; tanning is not needed, though it is recommended if the hide will be used. The hide of a maytah is impure and becomes pure by tanning, according to the hadith "any hide that is tanned has become pure." Tanning strips away the remaining matter with suitable agents, such as the qaraz tree or pigeon droppings. Both surfaces and the inside become pure. A little remaining hair is excused, but not a lot. The hide should be washed after tanning before use. Dog and pig hides never become pure through tanning.

Istihalah (Transformation). A change from one state into another, such as wine becoming vinegar.

Dibagh (Tanning). Treating a hide to strip away its remaining matter, which makes the hide of a carcass pure.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes "by itself": wine becomes pure when it turns to vinegar on its own, not when something is put in it to make it turn.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a tanned hide should still be washed before use, and that dog and pig hides are excluded entirely.

(3)

Severe Impurity: The Dog and the Pig

وَإِذَا تَنَجَّسَ شَيْءٌ بِبَوْلِ كَلْبٍ أَوْ خِنْزِيرٍ أَوْ فَرْعِ أَحَدِهِمَا أَوْ لُعَابِهِ أَوْ رَوْثِهِ أَوْ عَرَقِهِ أَوْ بَدَنِهِ وَهُوَ رَطْبٌ.. غُسِلَ سَبْعًا إِحْدَاهُنَّ بِتُرَابٍ طَاهِرٍ طَهُورٍ.
وَإِذَا تَنَجَّسَ التُّرَابُ بِالْكَلْبِ.. فَيَكْفِيهِ سَبْعُ مَرَّاتٍ بِالْمَاءِ الْخَالِصِ.

Translation
If something becomes impure by the urine of a dog or pig or the offspring of either, or by its saliva, dung, sweat, or its body while wet, it is washed seven times, one of them with earth that is pure and purifying. If earth becomes impure from a dog, seven washings with plain water suffice.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha set out the three grades of impurity:

Mughallazah (Severe). Dog and pig. Washed seven times, one with earth.

Mutawassitah (Intermediate). Ordinary impurities like urine and feces. Washed until removed; repeating up to three times is recommended.

Mukhaffafah (Light). The urine of a qualifying infant boy. Purified by sprinkling.

(4)

Contact with a dog transfers impurity if either side is wet: a wet dog touching a dry hand, a wet hand touching a dry dog, or both wet. If both are dry, the hand stays pure. This is the Shafi'i position; the Maliki school holds the dog to be pure.

The ruling is from the hadith of the dog drinking from a vessel, and applies equally to a hand, garment, body, or anything else. The first counted washing must remove the impurity itself and its traits. If that takes several attempts, together they count as one; then six more are counted. For the earth, it is best to mix it with water before applying. The earth must be pure and unused, not impure and not already used in tayammum. If the earth itself is soiled by a dog, pour water over it seven times; earth is not added to earth.

Asked about touching a dog when both are dry, the Shaykh said no impurity transfers. Keeping the animal is a separate question: a pig may not be kept, while a dog may be kept for a real need such as guarding or hunting. Touching a dog to treat it, or out of compassion, is fine, and any impurity that reaches the person is washed according to its ruling.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the first of the seven washings is the one that actually removes the impurity, however many attempts that takes. Only then are the remaining six counted.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that dry touching dry transfers nothing. Impurity moves only when one side is wet.

(6)

Light Impurity: The Infant Boy's Urine

وَمَا تَنَجَّسَ بِبَوْلِ صَبِيٍّ لَمْ يَطْعَمْ غَيْرَ اللَّبَنِ نُضِحَ، وَهُوَ رَشُّهُ بِالْمَاءِ مَعَ الْغَلَبَةِ وَالْمُكَاثَرَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَمَاand what
  • بِبَوْلِby the urine of
  • صَبِيٍّa boy
  • لَمْ يَطْعَمْwho has not eaten
  • غَيْرَother than
  • اللَّبَنِmilk
  • نُضِحَis sprinkled
  • وَهُوَand that is
  • رَشُّهُsprinkling it
  • بِالْمَاءِwith water
  • مَعَwith
  • الْغَلَبَةِthe water overpowering the urine
  • وَالْمُكَاثَرَةِand outweighing it in quantity
Translation
What becomes impure from the urine of a boy who has eaten nothing but milk is sprinkled, meaning water is sprinkled over it so that it overpowers and outweighs the urine.
Commentary

This lighter ruling applies only to a boy's urine, and only when both conditions hold: he has eaten nothing but milk, and he has not passed two years of age. Sabi can include girls in other contexts, but here it means a boy. A common explanation is that boys are carried around a great deal, and washing every time would be a hardship. Once he eats other food or passes two, his urine is intermediate impurity. A girl's urine, and a boy's feces, blood, or vomit, are not included.

If the urine is still wet in a garment, squeeze it out first; otherwise the water mixes with the urine and washing becomes necessary. If it has dried, it can be sprinkled.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes both conditions together: nothing but milk, and under two years. And only urine, only from a boy.

(7)

Intermediate Impurity: Everything Else

وَسَائِرُ النَّجَاسَاتِ تَطْهُرُ بِالْغَسْلِ إِذَا زَالَ طَعْمُهَا وَلَوْنُهَا وَرِيحُهَا.
وَلَا يَضُرُّ بَقَاءُ لَوْنٍ أَوْ رِيحٍ عَسُرَ زَوَالُهُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَسَائِرُand all the rest of
  • النَّجَاسَاتِthe impurities
  • تَطْهُرُbecome pure
  • بِالْغَسْلِby washing
  • زَالَwent away
  • طَعْمُهَاtheir taste
  • وَلَوْنُهَاand their color
  • وَرِيحُهَاand their smell
  • وَلَا يَضُرُّand it does not harm
  • بَقَاءُthe remaining of
  • لَوْنٍa color
  • رِيحٍa smell
  • عَسُرَwas difficult
  • زَوَالُهُits removal
Translation
All other impurities become pure by washing once their taste, color, and smell are gone. A color or smell that is difficult to remove does no harm if it remains.
Commentary

Wash until taste, color, and smell are gone, up to three times, using soap or a cleanser like ushnan. If only a hard-to-remove color or smell remains after that effort, it is excused. A remaining taste is not excused and requires more washing.

Ta'assur (Difficulty). The trace remains after three washings with a cleanser. A remaining color or smell is then excused.

Ta'adhdhur (Impossibility). Even extensive washing fails, and removing it would require cutting away that part of the garment. One may pray in it and the remainder is excused.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes checking all three traits after washing. Color or smell may be excused when stubborn; taste never is.

(9)

Liquids Cannot Be Purified

وَلَا يَطْهُرُ الْمَائِعُ إِذَا تَنَجَّسَ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَطْهُرُand does not become pure
  • الْمَائِعُa liquid
  • إِذَاif
  • تَنَجَّسَit became impure
A liquid does not become pure once it becomes impure.

Ma'i' means any liquid, not just water: oil, juice, drinks. Once impurity falls into it, there is no way to purify it. A solid is different: the impurity and what surrounds it are removed and the rest is pure.

The Shaykh acknowledged the hardship of losing a large amount of expensive oil. In the school it may not be eaten or sold, though it can be used for other purposes. He said he would like to look at how other schools treat it, but cautioned against taking a convenient ruling from another school without learning its full treatment. The jurists follow the evidence; they do not seek hardship or ease for its own sake.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha's advice: following another school on an issue means learning its full treatment of that issue, not picking out the convenient answer.

(10)

A Note on Istinja

The details of istinja come later, but the Shaykh introduced it here. It can be done with water, a suitable solid, or both. The best is to wipe first with tissue or similar and then use water. A "stone" in fiqh means any solid that removes the impurity, not just a literal stone, so tissue serves the purpose. If only water is available, use water; if water is unavailable, a solid may be used, under conditions to be explained.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that "stone" in istinja means any suitable solid, and that combining it with water afterward is best.
