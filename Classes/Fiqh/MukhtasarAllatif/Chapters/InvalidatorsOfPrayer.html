What Invalidates the Prayer مُبْطِلَاتُ الصَّلَاةِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي مُبْطِلَاتِ الصَّلَاةِ]
وَتَبْطُلُ الصَّلَاةُ بِالْكَلَامِ الْكَثِيرِ وَبِالْفِعْلِ الْكَثِيرِ وَلَوْ سَهْوًا؛ كَثَلَاثِ خُطُوَاتٍ، وَثَلَاثِ ضَرَبَاتٍ مُتَوَالِيَاتٍ، وَالضَّرْبَةِ الْمُفْرِطَةِ، وَالْوَثْبَةِ الْفَاحِشَةِ.
وَإِنْ تَكَلَّمَ بِكَلَامٍ قَلِيلٍ نَاسِيًا، أَوْ أَكَلَ قَلِيلًا نَاسِيًا، أَوْ زَادَ رُكُوعًا أَوْ سُجُودًا نَاسِيًا.. لَمْ تَبْطُلْ بِذَلِكَ.

Translation
A section on what invalidates the prayer. The prayer is invalidated by much speech and much action, even by mistake, such as three steps or three strikes in succession, an excessive strike, or a gross leap. If he speaks a little forgetfully, eats a little forgetfully, or adds a ruku' or a sujud forgetfully, it is not invalidated by that.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha framed the section against what came before: conditions come before the prayer and continue through it; integrals are done within it; and the sunnahs are ab'ad and hay'at. This section covers what breaks the prayer, and the next covers the prostration that repairs a lapse.

Speech

The speech that invalidates is speech addressed to people, even if said for the prayer's benefit. If the imam stands for a fifth rak'ah and someone says to him "Sit," the speaker's prayer is invalid, though his intention was to correct the prayer. Dhikr, salawat on the Prophet ﷺ, and a third-person supplication such as "May Allah have mercy on him" are not address of this kind and do not invalidate.

In the jurists' technical sense of "letter," a single letter ordinarily does not invalidate and two letters do. A single letter does invalidate if it carries meaning, such as the one-letter Arabic command "protect yourself" (قِ), or if it is said for play.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that much speech, four words or more, invalidates whether deliberate or not, while little speech invalidates only when deliberate; and that "letter" here is the jurists' technical term.

(1)

Eating

Whether eating is little or much is judged by custom, not by a number: swallowing a bit of food left between the teeth is little; eating a sandwich is much. Much eating invalidates even forgetfully. Little eating invalidates if deliberate, and not if forgetful or out of ignorance.

This differs from fasting, where forgetful eating does not break the fast. The prayer's form, the takbir, recitation, ruku', and sujud, should itself remind a person that he is praying.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that forgetful eating in prayer is not like forgetful eating in a fast: much eating breaks the prayer even when forgotten.

(2)

Action and Movement

The measure of much action is three consecutive movements of a heavy limb, such as the hand, the leg, or the head. One or two do not invalidate. Movements separated by a pause, or made at different positions of the prayer, are not consecutive. A continuous motion without a break counts as one movement; a hand moved out and then back, as two distinct motions, counts as two.

Movements of a light limb, such as the eyelids, lips, tongue, or a finger, do not invalidate even when repeated.

Cases that invalidate before three
  • A single excessive movement, such as the matn's gross leap or excessive strike.
  • A single movement made for play.
  • Beginning a movement while intending to make three: the prayer is invalid from the start of the sequence.

Repeated movement that cannot reasonably be avoided is excused: intense fear in battle, shivering in severe cold, or trembling from illness.

In practice: someone stepping to straighten a row, or pulled back by another person, may move one or two steps, but a third consecutive step reaches the limit.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the test is three consecutive movements of a heavy limb, while a single excessive movement, a movement for play, or starting an intended series of three invalidates before three are complete.

(3)
