The Sunnahs of Wudu سُنَنُ الْوُضُوءِ

The Sunnahs at a Glance

Text
وَسُنَنُهُ:
السِّوَاكُ. ثُمَّ التَّسْمِيَةُ. وَغَسْلُ الْكَفَّيْنِ. ثُمَّ الْمَضْمَضَةُ. وَالِاسْتِنْشَاقُ. وَالِاسْتِنْثَارُ. وَالتَّثْلِيثُ. وَمَسْحُ جَمِيعِ الرَّأْسِ. ثُمَّ الْأُذُنَيْنِ وَالصِّمَاخَيْنِ. وَتَخْلِيلُ اللِّحْيَةِ الْكَثِيفَةِ. وَتَخْلِيلُ الْأَصَابِعِ. وَتَطْوِيلُ الْغُرَّةِ وَالتَّحْجِيلِ. وَالْمُوَالَاةُ. وَتَرْكُ الِاسْتِعَانَةِ فِي الصَّبِّ. وَتَرْكُ التَّنْشِيفِ بِخِرْقَةٍ.
Translation
Its sunnahs are: the siwak; then the basmalah; washing the hands; then rinsing the mouth; drawing water into the nose; blowing it out; doing each act three times; wiping the whole head; then the ears and the ear canals; passing the fingers through a dense beard; passing water between the fingers and toes; extending the ghurrah and the tahjil; continuity; not asking someone to pour the water; and not drying off with a cloth.
Commentary

Each sunnah is taken in turn below. One question from class applies to all of them: must a person make a separate intention for the sunnahs? Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha answered that a single intention of wudu covers the whole act, its obligations and its sunnahs together. It is sunnah to have that intention from the very beginning, from washing the hands, so that the earlier sunnahs are rewarded as part of wudu. The intention must still be present at the first moment of washing the face. This is one intention held from the start, not two intentions. If someone performs the early sunnahs with no intention of wudu at all, they do not earn the reward of those sunnahs.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes making the intention of wudu at the very start, with washing the hands, and keeping it until the face. Otherwise the siwak, basmalah, and rinsing are done without the reward of being part of wudu.

(1)

The Siwak

السِّوَاكُ.
Vocabulary
  • السِّوَاكُlinguistically rubbing; also the implement used to rub the teeth
Translation
The siwak.
Commentary

Scholars differ over whether the siwak or the basmalah comes first, and either order is acceptable. The sequence the Shaykh presented as best, following Ibn Hajar, is: say the basmalah, wash the hands, use the siwak, then rinse the mouth.

A twig of the arak tree is best, but the sunnah is fulfilled with any rough implement, including a toothbrush. A liquid mouthwash alone is not siwak, since it is not a rough implement, and the finger does not count.

The siwak is recommended on many occasions: for wudu, before prayer, before dhikr and Quran recitation, on waking, before study, after much talking, after eating, and after eating something with an unpleasant smell.

Recommended. Its original ruling, at the times listed above.

Obligatory. If a person vows to use it, just as vowing to give charity or pray two rak'ahs makes those obligatory. Also if an impurity in the mouth cannot be removed except by it.

Prohibited. Using a stolen or wrongfully taken siwak.

Disliked. For a fasting person after the sun passes its zenith (zawal), according to the relied-upon position of the school. Imam al-Nawawi held that it is not disliked even then.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that one act can take different rulings as circumstances change. The siwak is recommended in itself, but becomes obligatory, prohibited, or disliked in particular cases. An act whose base ruling is recommended does not fall to being merely permissible.

(3)

The Basmalah

ثُمَّ التَّسْمِيَةُ.
Vocabulary
  • ثُمَّthen
  • التَّسْمِيَةُsaying the basmalah
Translation
Then the basmalah.
Commentary

The minimum is "bismillah", and the better wording is the full basmalah. In wudu it is an individual sunnah (sunnat 'ayn): each person says it for himself. At a shared meal, by contrast, the basmalah is a communal sunnah (sunnat kifayah), so one person saying it covers the group. The Shaykh said this distinction would be explained further in a coming lesson.

If a person remembers during wudu, he says it then. If he remembers only after finishing, the chance has passed. Food is different: one who forgot may say it when he remembers during the meal, and even after finishing, as the Shaykh explained from the hadith about Satan's share in food eaten without the name of Allah.

Sunnat 'Ayn (Individual Sunnah). A recommended act each person performs for himself.

Sunnat Kifayah (Communal Sunnah). A recommended act fulfilled for a group when one member performs it.

(4)

Washing the Hands, Rinsing the Mouth and Nose

وَغَسْلُ الْكَفَّيْنِ. ثُمَّ الْمَضْمَضَةُ. وَالِاسْتِنْشَاقُ. وَالِاسْتِنْثَارُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَغَسْلُand washing
  • الْكَفَّيْنِthe two hands, up to the wrists
  • الْمَضْمَضَةُrinsing the mouth
  • وَالِاسْتِنْشَاقُand drawing water into the nose
  • وَالِاسْتِنْثَارُand blowing the water out of the nose
Translation
Washing the hands; then rinsing the mouth; drawing water into the nose; and blowing it out.
Commentary

Washing the hands at the start is sunnah when they are known to be clean. If a person knows there is najasah on his hands, it is prohibited to put them into the water in a way that would make the water impure.

The basic sunnah of rinsing is achieved simply by putting water in the mouth. The complete form is to move the water around. Drawing water deep into the nose is likewise part of the complete form, but only when not fasting, since a fasting person risks the water going further in.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that hands known to carry najasah must not be dipped into the water if that would make it impure. Washing the hands first is a sunnah only when they are clean.

(5)

Doing Each Act Three Times

وَالتَّثْلِيثُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالتَّثْلِيثُand doing it three times
Translation
Doing each act three times.
Commentary

Each washing is done three times, and the head and ears are also wiped three times. Wiping over the khuffs three times, however, is disliked. On wiping a turban three times there is a disagreement between al-Ramli and Ibn Hajar; al-Ramli holds that three times is recommended.

To wipe over a turban, a person first wipes some of the hair of the head, then continues onto the turban in one connected motion, without lifting the hand. Lifting the hand and taking new water for a separate part does not achieve this. The Shaykh demonstrated this with a head covering. Wiping over a turban is only valid if it is not worn sinfully.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the method for the turban: wipe some hair first, then carry on over the turban without lifting the hand.

(6)

Wiping the Whole Head and the Ears

وَمَسْحُ جَمِيعِ الرَّأْسِ. ثُمَّ الْأُذُنَيْنِ وَالصِّمَاخَيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَمَسْحُand wiping
  • جَمِيعِall of
  • الرَّأْسِthe head
  • ثُمَّthen
  • الْأُذُنَيْنِthe two ears
  • وَالصِّمَاخَيْنِand the two ear canals, the inner part of the ear
Translation
Wiping the whole head; then the ears and the ear canals.
Commentary

The obligation is to wipe only part of the head. Wiping all of it is the sunnah. The ears and their inner canals are then wiped, and these too may be done three times.

Passing the Fingers Through the Beard and Between the Fingers

وَتَخْلِيلُ اللِّحْيَةِ الْكَثِيفَةِ. وَتَخْلِيلُ الْأَصَابِعِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَتَخْلِيلُand passing the fingers through or between
  • اللِّحْيَةِthe beard
  • الْكَثِيفَةِdense, the skin cannot be seen through it
  • الْأَصَابِعِthe fingers and toes
Translation
Passing the fingers through a dense beard; and passing water between the fingers and toes.
Commentary

A dense beard is one the skin cannot be seen through; a thin beard (khafifah) is one it can. The sunnah of passing the fingers through a dense beard applies to someone who is not in ihram. For a muhrim it is not recommended, because the movement may cause hairs to fall out, which is one of the prohibitions of ihram.

Passing water between the fingers and toes is sunnah, but it becomes obligatory if the water will not reach between them otherwise, for example if the toes are pressed tightly together.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that takhlil of the fingers and toes is ordinarily sunnah, but becomes obligatory when water cannot reach between them without it.

(7)

Extending the Ghurrah and the Tahjil

وَتَطْوِيلُ الْغُرَّةِ وَالتَّحْجِيلِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَتَطْوِيلُand lengthening, extending
  • الْغُرَّةِliterally the white blaze on a horse's face; here, washing beyond the face
  • وَالتَّحْجِيلِliterally the white markings on a horse's legs; here, washing beyond the arms and feet
Translation
Extending the ghurrah and the tahjil.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained the terms from their everyday meaning first. The ghurrah is the white blaze on a horse's face and the tahjil is the white on its legs. In wudu, the ghurrah is washing a little beyond the boundary of the face, and the tahjil is washing beyond the elbows and ankles. The extra washing may extend over the top and sides of the face and the neck, up the arms toward the shoulders, and up the legs toward the knees. These describe how far one may go, not a fixed endpoint.

A small amount beyond the boundary is actually obligatory, because without it one cannot be sure the whole limb was washed. Anything beyond that small amount is the sunnah.

The intention matters here. The area before the boundary of the face is not part of the obligatory face, so if one starts washing there, that is not the point at which the wudu intention takes effect. For the arms, one may begin above the elbow while keeping the wudu intention, and so gain the extra washing.

What is needed to complete an obligation is itself obligatory (ma la yatimmu al-wajib illa bihi fa-huwa wajib). This is why washing a little past the edge of each limb is required: it is the only way to be certain the limb itself was fully washed.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes separating the two layers: the small margin needed to complete the limb is obligatory, and only what goes beyond it is the sunnah of ghurrah and tahjil.

(9)

Continuity

وَالْمُوَالَاةُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَالْمُوَالَاةُand continuity, washing each limb before the previous one dries
Translation
Continuity.
Commentary

Muwalah is sunnah in the Shafi'i school, not an obligation. It is not measured by minutes. It means washing the next limb before the water on the previous limb has dried. To make the point, the Shaykh gave deliberately extreme examples: a person could wash his face in Washington and his arms six hours later in New York, or do most of his wudu at home and wash his feet at the mosque to avoid a muddy path. As long as nothing nullified his wudu in between, it is valid. He has only missed the sunnah of continuity.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that muwalah is measured by whether the previous limb has dried, not by a fixed amount of time, and that breaking it does not invalidate wudu in the Shafi'i school.

(10)

Not Asking Someone to Pour, and Not Drying Off

وَتَرْكُ الِاسْتِعَانَةِ فِي الصَّبِّ. وَتَرْكُ التَّنْشِيفِ بِخِرْقَةٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَتَرْكُand leaving, refraining from
  • الِاسْتِعَانَةِseeking help
  • فِيin
  • الصَّبِّpouring
  • التَّنْشِيفِdrying
  • بِخِرْقَةٍwith a cloth
Translation
Not seeking someone's help in pouring the water; and not drying off with a cloth.
Commentary

It is better to perform worship oneself, so one should not have someone else pour the water when there is no need. An elderly person, or anyone who needs help, may ask for it. Asking someone simply to bring the water is fine; the sunnah concerns having them pour it over you.

Leaving the water on the limbs after wudu, rather than drying with a towel, tissue, or similar, is preferred. If there is a need, such as surroundings where impurity could stick to wet skin, for example when working around animals, then drying is appropriate.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha reminds that explaining the sunnahs in detail is not a call to make wudu burdensome. They are the fuller way to perform it, and the exceptions for need are part of the same teaching.
