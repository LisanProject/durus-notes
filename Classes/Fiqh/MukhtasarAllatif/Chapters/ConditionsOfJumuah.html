The Conditions of Jumu'ah شُرُوطُ الْجُمُعَةِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ الْجُمُعَةِ]
وَشُرُوطُ الْجُمُعَةِ:
أَنْ تَكُونَ كُلُّهَا فِي وَقْتِ الظُّهْرِ.
وَأَنْ تُقَامَ فِي نَفْسِ الْبَلَدِ.
وَأَلَّا تُقَامَ قَبْلَهَا جُمُعَةٌ فِي تِلْكَ الْبَلَدِ.
وَأَنْ تُصَلَّى فِي جَمَاعَةٍ.
وَأَنْ يَكُونُوا أَرْبَعِينَ ذَكَرًا مُكَلَّفًا حُرًّا مُسْتَوْطِنًا لَا يَظْعَنُ شِتَاءً وَلَا صَيْفًا إِلَّا لِحَاجَةٍ.
وَأَنْ يَتَقَدَّمَهَا خُطْبَتَانِ.

Translation
A section on the conditions of Jumu'ah. Its conditions are: that all of it be within the time of Dhuhr; that it be held within the town itself; that no Jumu'ah be held before it in that town; that it be prayed in congregation; that they be forty legally responsible free males, settled residents who do not move away in winter or summer except for a need; and that two sermons precede it.

Time

All of Jumu'ah falls within Dhuhr's time. If they began in time and Asr enters during the prayer, they complete it as Dhuhr, four rak'ahs, without stopping or renewing the intention; hence the riddle "he intended but did not pray, and prayed but did not intend." If too little time remains at the start for the sermons and the prayer, they pray Dhuhr instead.

Place, and One Jumu'ah per Town

It is held in a settled town, not in open country. The tribes living in the desert around Madinah did not each hold their own; those who wished came to Madinah.

The default is one Jumu'ah per town. More than one is allowed only for a need, and only as many as the need requires; some in the school forbade it even then.

CaseResult
People can gather but split without excuseNot permitted
Two without excuse, one after the otherThe earlier is valid, the later is not
Two without excuse, at the same timeBoth are invalid
Gathering is hard (distant parts of a town, fighting between groups)As many as the need requires: if three masjids suffice, six exceed it

In a city like Seattle, where all the Muslims cannot gather in one masjid, the Shaykh prays Dhuhr after Jumu'ah as a precaution, since without a need only the first is valid, it is often unknown which came first, and one view in the school forbids more than one.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that one Jumu'ah per town is the default, that more are allowed only to the extent of the need, and that praying Dhuhr afterward is a precaution where there are several.

Congregation and the Forty

ConditionFor how long
CongregationThe first rak'ah; if each then separates in the second and finishes alone, it is valid
The fortyFrom the start of the sermons to the end of the prayer; if one of the forty invalidates his prayer before the salam, leaving thirty-nine, the Jumu'ah fails for all

The forty must be people who constitute the Jumu'ah: legally responsible, free, male, settled residents. A hundred attendees may still fall short if many are women, boys, or travelers; twenty men and twenty women, or thirty men and ten boys, do not make forty.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that congregation is required for the first rak'ah, while the forty must remain from the sermons to the end of the prayer, and that they are forty who constitute the Jumu'ah, not forty attendees.
