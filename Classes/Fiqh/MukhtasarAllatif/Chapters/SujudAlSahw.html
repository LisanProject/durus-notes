The Prostration of Forgetfulness سُجُودُ السَّهْوِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي سُجُودِ السَّهْوِ]
وَيُسْتَحَبُّ سُجُودُ السَّهْوِ، وَهُوَ سَجْدَتَانِ قُبَيْلَ السَّلَامِ، عِنْدَ تَرْكِ التَّشَهُّدِ الْأَوَّلِ، وَالصَّلَاةِ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فِيهِ، أَوْ قُعُودِهِ، وَتَرْكِ الْقُنُوتِ فِي الصُّبْحِ، وَالصَّلَاةِ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فِيهِ، وَعَلَى الْآلِ، وَالْقِيَامِ فِيهِ، وَالْكَلَامِ الْقَلِيلِ نَاسِيًا، وَزِيَادَةِ فِعْلٍ نَاسِيًا، وَالْأَكْلِ الْقَلِيلِ نَاسِيًا.

Translation
A section on the prostration of forgetfulness. The prostration of forgetfulness is recommended. It is two prostrations shortly before the salam, made upon leaving the first tashahhud, the salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it, or sitting for it; leaving the qunut in Subh, the salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it and on his family, or standing for it; and for a little speech, an added action, or a little eating done forgetfully.
Commentary

In language, sahw is forgetfulness or inattention. Here it means a defect in the prayer, whether it came from forgetting or was deliberate, so deliberately leaving the first tashahhud or the qunut also calls for it. It repairs the defect and humiliates Shaytan. It is not prescribed in the funeral prayer.

  • Ruling: recommended. It becomes obligatory for a follower when his imam makes it, because following the imam is obligatory.
  • Form: two prostrations before the salam. Deciding from the outset to make only one is not valid; deciding to stop after one once the first has begun is.
  • Placement: the Hanafis generally place it after the salam, and the Malikis distinguish cases. The school places it before, because the Prophet's ﷺ later practice was before the salam, and a repair to the prayer fits inside the prayer.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that sahw here means a defect in the prayer, not only forgetting, and that its ruling is recommended except for a follower whose imam makes it.

(1)

Four Causes

When sujud al-sahw is made
  • Leaving one of the ab'ad.
  • Doing something that invalidates when deliberate but not when forgetful: a little speech, a little eating, or an added physical integral, done forgetfully.
  • Moving a spoken integral out of its place: reciting al-Fatihah in ruku' or sujud. The salam is excluded: giving salam after the third rak'ah of a four is not a moved integral but an unfinished prayer.
  • Doing an integral that may be extra: in the last sujud of Asr, unsure whether it is the third or fourth rak'ah, he builds on certainty (three), prays another rak'ah, and prostrates, since that rak'ah may be extra.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes keeping the four causes distinct, especially the forgetful act that would invalidate if deliberate, and the integral done with a possibility of excess.

(2)

Someone who remembers in ruku' that he left al-Fatihah goes back to standing, recites it, completes the prayer, and prostrates for the lapse. But someone who has gone down into sujud and then returns to stand for the qunut invalidates his prayer: an ab'ad is never returned to.

The ab'ad
  • The first tashahhud (its obligatory wording), sitting for it, and the salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it.
  • The qunut of Subh, and of Witr in the second half of Ramadan; the salawat on the Prophet ﷺ in it; and standing for it.
  • The salawat on the Prophet's ﷺ family.

The qunut for a calamity is not one of them; leaving it calls for no sujud al-sahw. The hay'at that call for none include raising the hands, the tasbih of ruku' and sujud, the opening supplication, seeking refuge, the surah after al-Fatihah, the supplication between the prostrations, placing the hands, and the sitting of rest.

For the tashahhud, what counts is leaving it or part of its obligatory wording, which the Shaykh counted as five sentences, not an addition like "al-mubarakat." For the salawat, it is leaving its minimum, "O Allah, send blessings on Muhammad"; the longer wording with the family and Ibrahim is better but not required.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a missed integral is made up, a missed ab'ad is repaired by sujud al-sahw without going back, and a missed hay'ah gets neither.

(3)

Following the Imam in It

Text
وَتَجِبُ مُتَابَعَةُ الْإِمَامِ فِي سُجُودِ السَّهْوِ؛ فَلَوْ سَجَدَ لِسَهْوِ نَفْسِهِ أَوْ تَخَلَّفَ عَنْ سُجُودِ إِمَامِهِ عَامِدًا عَالِمًا بِالتَّحْرِيمِ.. بَطَلَتْ صَلَاتُهُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَتَجِبُand it is obligatory
  • مُتَابَعَةُ الْإِمَامِfollowing the imam
  • فَلَوْ سَجَدَso if he prostrates
  • لِسَهْوِ نَفْسِهِfor his own lapse
  • أَوْ تَخَلَّفَor holds back
  • عَنْ سُجُودِ إِمَامِهِfrom his imam's prostration
  • عَامِدًاdeliberately
  • عَالِمًا بِالتَّحْرِيمِknowing it is prohibited
  • بَطَلَتْ صَلَاتُهُhis prayer is invalid
Translation
Following the imam in the prostration of forgetfulness is obligatory. If the follower prostrates for his own lapse, or holds back from his imam's prostration, deliberately and knowing it is prohibited, his prayer is invalid.
Commentary
  • The follower makes it with the imam even if he joined after the imam's lapse.
  • While following, he does not make his own separate sujud al-sahw; the imam carries his lapses in the ab'ad. A missed integral such as al-Fatihah is different: the follower must make it up himself.
  • A latecomer who made it with the imam repeats it, as a recommendation, at the end of his own prayer before his salam. If he has a lapse of his own while completing the prayer alone, he prostrates for it.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the follower must join the imam's sujud al-sahw even if he did not witness the lapse, and must not prostrate separately for his own lapse while still following.
