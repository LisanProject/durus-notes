Who Must Fast, and Who May Break the Fast شُرُوطُ وُجُوبِ الصَّوْمِ

The Conditions of Obligation

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ وُجُوبِ الصَّوْمِ]
وَشَرْطُ وُجُوبِ صَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ: الْعَقْلُ، وَالْبُلُوغُ، وَالْقُدْرَةُ عَلَى الصَّوْمِ.
وَيُؤْمَرُ بِهِ الصَّبِيُّ إِذَا أَطَاقَ لِسَبْعٍ، وَيُضْرَبُ عَلَى تَرْكِهِ لِعَشْرٍ.
Vocabulary
  • شَرْطُ وُجُوبِthe condition of obligation
  • الْعَقْلُsanity
  • وَالْبُلُوغُand puberty
  • وَالْقُدْرَةُ عَلَى الصَّوْمِand the ability to fast
  • وَيُؤْمَرُ بِهِ الصَّبِيُّthe child is commanded to do it
  • إِذَا أَطَاقَif able
  • لِسَبْعٍat seven
  • وَيُضْرَبُ عَلَى تَرْكِهِand disciplined for leaving it
  • لِعَشْرٍat ten
Translation
A section on the conditions of the obligation to fast. The conditions of the obligation to fast Ramadan are sanity, puberty, and the ability to fast. A child is commanded to fast at seven if able, and disciplined for leaving it at ten.
Commentary

Conditions of obligation answer "must he fast?"; conditions of validity answer "is his fast valid?" The Shaykh added Islam and residence. A traveler is not obliged, but his fast is valid if he fasts.

  • Insanity: no obligation, unless he caused it by transgression, such as taking something that brings it on; then he makes up the fasts.
  • The child: commanding at seven and discipline at ten are both limited by ability. A child is not obliged to make up fasts missed before puberty; making up those missed after discernment is recommended. A child discerning at nine and reaching puberty at fifteen makes up the years nine to fifteen as a recommendation.

Who May Break the Fast

Text
وَيَجُوزُ الْفِطْرُ لِلْمُسَافِرِ سَفَرًا طَوِيلًا مُبَاحًا، وَلِلْمَرِيضِ إِذَا خَافَ الضَّرَرَ عَلَى نَفْسِهِ، وَلِلْحَامِلِ وَالْمُرْضِعِ إِذَا خَافَتَا عَلَى أَنْفُسِهِمَا أَوْ عَلَى الْوَلَدِ، أَوْ لِإِنْقَاذِ حَيَوَانٍ مُشْرِفٍ عَلَى الْهَلَاكِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَجُوزُ الْفِطْرُbreaking the fast is permitted
  • سَفَرًا طَوِيلًا مُبَاحًاa long, permissible journey
  • إِذَا خَافَ الضَّرَرَif he fears harm
  • وَلِلْحَامِلِ وَالْمُرْضِعِand for the pregnant and nursing woman
  • إِذَا خَافَتَاif they fear
  • عَلَى الْوَلَدِfor the child
  • لِإِنْقَاذِto rescue
  • حَيَوَانٍ مُشْرِفٍ عَلَى الْهَلَاكِa living being about to perish
Translation
Breaking the fast is permitted for a traveler on a long, permissible journey; for a sick person who fears harm to himself; for a pregnant or nursing woman who fears for herself or for the child; and to rescue a living being about to perish.
Commentary
WhoCondition
TravelerAbout 84 km, a lawful journey, and he must set out before dawn. Leaving after dawn does not permit breaking that day's fast.
Sick personFears the kind of harm that permits tayammum: death, losing a limb or its use, serious visible harm. Mere discomfort, or fear of vomiting, is not enough.
Pregnant or nursing womanFears for herself, the child, or both: losing the pregnancy, or her milk decreasing and harming the baby
RescuerBreaks the fast to save a protected (muhtaram) life, such as emergency responders at a serious accident; not, for example, pigs from a burning farm

If dawn is at 5 a.m., a traveler who leaves at 3 or 4 a.m. begins the day traveling and may break the fast. One who leaves at 6 a.m. may not break that day's fast, though he travels 90 km.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the traveler's concession requires setting out before dawn, and that the sick person's concession is tied to harm of the kind that permits tayammum, not discomfort or the fear of vomiting.
