The Etiquette of Relieving Oneself آدَابُ دَاخِلِ الْخَلَاءِ

Recommended, Unless Stated Otherwise

فَصْلٌ فِي آدَابِ دَاخِلِ الْخَلَاءِ
A section on the etiquette of one entering the toilet.

Adab can sometimes refer to recommended conduct and sometimes to obligatory conduct. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that the practices in this section are recommended, and their opposites disliked, unless he specifically points out that something is obligatory or prohibited.

Entering with the Left, Leaving with the Right

يُقَدِّمُ دَاخِلُ الْخَلَاءِ يَسَارَهُ، وَإِذَا خَرَجَ يَمِينَهُ.
Vocabulary
  • يُقَدِّمُputs forward, begins with
  • دَاخِلُthe one entering
  • الْخَلَاءِthe toilet, the place for relieving oneself
  • يَسَارَهُhis left (foot)
  • وَإِذَاand when
  • خَرَجَhe exits
  • يَمِينَهُhis right (foot)
Translation
The one entering the toilet puts his left foot first, and when he leaves, his right.
Commentary

The general principle, which the Shaykh noted from al-Nawawi's al-Majmu', is to use the left for what is low or impure and the right for what is honored. So one enters the toilet with the left and leaves with the right, and enters the mosque with the right and leaves with the left. This does not mean every ordinary place is entered with the left. It concerns places like the toilet and places of sin. One's home is entered with the right.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the principle behind this sunnah: the left for low or impure places, the right for places of honor. It reaches beyond the toilet, but not to every ordinary place one enters.

(1)

Not Carrying the Name of Allah, Covering the Head, Distance and Concealment

وَلَا يَحْمِلُ ذِكْرَ اللَّهِ وَاسْمَ رَسُولِهِ وَالْقُرْآنَ.
وَيُغَطِّي رَأْسَهُ.
وَيَبْعُدُ وَيَسْتَتِرُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَحْمِلُand he does not carry
  • ذِكْرَthe remembrance, the mention of
  • اللَّهِAllah
  • وَاسْمَand the name of
  • رَسُولِهِHis Messenger
  • وَالْقُرْآنَand the Quran
  • وَيُغَطِّيand he covers
  • رَأْسَهُhis head
  • وَيَبْعُدُand he goes far off
  • وَيَسْتَتِرُand he conceals himself
Translation
He does not carry anything bearing the remembrance of Allah, the name of His Messenger, or the Quran. He covers his head. He goes far off and conceals himself.
Commentary

Carrying something with the remembrance of Allah, the name of the Prophet, or Quran into the toilet is disliked. The Prophet would remove his ring on entering the toilet, and it is narrated that it was engraved "Muhammad, Messenger of Allah." The Shaykh mentioned that al-Adhra'i held the stricter view that carrying the mushaf in is prohibited, not merely disliked.

A word that could be a divine name or the Prophet's name, when used simply as someone's personal name, does not take this ruling. Names like Aziz, Ahmad, or Muhammad written as a person's name are not disliked on this account. It is disliked only when the revered name itself is meant.

Covering the head follows the practice of the Prophet. The Shaykh mentioned something he had heard from some teachers, that going in bareheaded could cause baldness, adding "and Allah knows best."

Going far off keeps others from hearing or seeing. If the person relieving himself does not move away, those nearby should. Concealment is recommended in general, but becomes obligatory when the person is in view of someone for whom it is prohibited to look at him. These matters were more pressing in the open settings of earlier times, but remain worth studying.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that concealment moves from recommended to obligatory when someone who may not look at him is present.

(2)

Where Not to Relieve Oneself

وَلَا يَبُولُ فِي مَاءٍ رَاكِدٍ وَقَلِيلٍ جَارٍ، وَجُحْرٍ، وَمَهَبِّ رِيحٍ، وَظِلٍّ مَقْصُودٍ، وَطَرِيقٍ، وَنَقْبٍ، وَتَحْتَ شَجَرَةٍ مُثْمِرَةٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَبُولُand he does not urinate
  • فِيin
  • مَاءٍwater
  • رَاكِدٍstill, stagnant
  • وَقَلِيلٍand a small amount
  • جَارٍflowing
  • وَجُحْرٍand a hole, a burrow
  • وَمَهَبِّand the path of
  • رِيحٍwind
  • وَظِلٍّand shade
  • مَقْصُودٍsought out, where people sit
  • وَطَرِيقٍand a path
  • وَنَقْبٍand an opening in the ground
  • وَتَحْتَand beneath
  • شَجَرَةٍa tree
  • مُثْمِرَةٍfruit-bearing
Translation
He does not urinate in still water or a small amount of flowing water, in a hole, where the wind blows, in shade people seek out, on a path, in an opening in the ground, or beneath a fruit-bearing tree.
Commentary

Whatever applies to urinating applies even more to defecating. Before the details, the Shaykh set apart the cases that are prohibited outright, not merely disliked: relieving oneself in water that belongs to someone else, or in water endowed or made available for public use (ma' musabbal), such as a public drinking fountain or water set out for wudu. He mentioned the village water stations of Upper Egypt, historically called a sabil. The disliked cases below concern water that is one's own or freely available.

At night. Disliked in any water, little or much, still or flowing, even a vast body of water, since water at night may be a dwelling of the jinn.

Still water. Disliked, whether little or much.

A small amount of flowing water. Disliked.

A large amount of flowing water. Not disliked, though avoiding it is better, and only when no other reason applies, such as ownership, public use, or night.

(3)

The Shaykh said the Shafi'i measure of what counts as a small or large amount of water will be explained in a later lesson.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that these rulings overlap and must be taken together. A case being permitted under one heading does not cancel a prohibition or dislike that comes from another. Large flowing water is still prohibited if it belongs to someone else, and still disliked at night.

(4)

The other places listed:

  • A hole, burrow, or opening (juhr, naqb, also thaqb or sarb in other texts). Disliked because an animal may live there and be harmed, or harm the person. Some explain it as a dwelling of the jinn, but the well-known reason among the jurists is the animal. If a person strongly presumes an animal is inside, which the Shaykh put at around ninety percent confidence, it becomes prohibited.
  • Where the wind blows. Disliked.
  • Shade people seek out (zill maqsud). A place where people customarily gather to sit and talk: shade in the summer, and in winter the sunny spot people seek. Disliked when they gather for something permissible. If the gathering is for something prohibited, such as backbiting, slander, or drinking wine, this dislike does not apply.
  • A path. A path people actually use. An abandoned path nobody walks is not included.
  • Beneath a fruit-bearing tree. Disliked whether or not it currently has fruit. "Beneath" means the area where its fruit usually falls, not just the spot by the trunk; ten meters away would not normally be called beneath it. "Fruit" in the jurists' usage is anything sought from the tree, whether for eating, for its scent like roses or basil, or for use like the fruit of the qaraz tree, which was used in tanning hides.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the terms of fiqh must be read with the jurists' meaning, not the everyday one. "Fruit" includes what is sought for scent or use, and "beneath" is defined by where the fruit falls.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a strong presumption changes the ruling. Relieving oneself in a hole is disliked, but becomes prohibited when one strongly presumes an animal is in it.

(6)

Not Speaking

وَلَا يَتَكَلَّمُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَتَكَلَّمُand he does not speak
Translation
He does not speak.
Commentary

Many later Shafi'i jurists held that speech is disliked throughout one's time in the toilet, before, during, and after relieving oneself, whether it is dhikr or ordinary talk. The relied-upon position limits the dislike to the time one is actually relieving oneself.

So someone who enters a modern bathroom to make wudu, fetch something, or fix something is not doing anything disliked by talking normally. Dhikr and Quran recitation, however, remain disliked in a place built for relieving oneself. Avoiding speech there altogether is best.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that in the relied-upon position speech is disliked while actually relieving oneself, not merely for being in the bathroom; but dhikr and Quran are disliked in that place regardless, and staying silent is best.

(7)

Making Sure the Urine Has Stopped

وَيَسْتَبْرِئُ مِنَ الْبَوْلِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَسْتَبْرِئُand he makes sure he is clear
  • مِنَof
  • الْبَوْلِurine
Translation
He makes sure he is clear of urine.
Commentary

Istibra' is verifying, by a suitable method, that urination has finished and nothing remains. It is recommended in the school. The Shaykh noted that al-Qadi al-Husayn and al-Baghawi held it to be obligatory, as did al-Nawawi in his commentary on Sahih Muslim.

Istibra'. Making sure that urination has stopped and no urine remains.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the purpose of istibra' is to settle the matter, not to open the door to obsessive doubts (waswasah).

(9)

The Supplications on Entering and Leaving

وَيَقُولُ إِذَا دَخَلَ: بِاسْمِ اللَّهِ، اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْخُبُثِ وَالْخَبَائِثِ.
وَإِذَا خَرَجَ: غُفْرَانَكَ، الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَذْهَبَ عَنِّي الْأَذَى وَعَافَانِي.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَقُولُand he says
  • إِذَاwhen
  • دَخَلَhe enters
  • بِاسْمِ اللَّهِin the name of Allah
  • اللَّهُمَّO Allah
  • إِنِّيindeed I
  • أَعُوذُseek refuge
  • بِكَin You
  • مِنَfrom
  • الْخُبُثِthe male devils; read khubuth or khubth
  • وَالْخَبَائِثِand the female devils
  • خَرَجَhe exits
  • غُفْرَانَكَ(I ask) Your forgiveness
  • الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِall praise belongs to Allah
  • الَّذِيwho
  • أَذْهَبَremoved
  • عَنِّيfrom me
  • الْأَذَىthe harm
  • وَعَافَانِيand gave me well-being
Translation
On entering he says: In the name of Allah. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the male and female devils. On leaving: Your forgiveness. All praise belongs to Allah who removed the harm from me and gave me well-being.
Commentary

The entering supplication is said whenever one enters, whether to relieve oneself or for any other reason. Only "bismillah" is said here, without "al-Rahman al-Rahim", because this is not a place of dhikr and one keeps to what is transmitted. This differs from the start of wudu or a meal, where the full basmalah is better.

Al-khubth may be read with a dammah on the ba (khubuth) or a sukun (khubth). Citing al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar, the Shaykh affirmed that both readings are correct and rejected the claim that the sukun is wrong.

On why one asks forgiveness on leaving, the Shaykh shared two reflections, not legal rulings: it is asking forgiveness for the time spent without remembering Allah, since dhikr is disliked there; or it is asking forgiveness for falling short in thanking Allah for the blessing of eating, digesting, and expelling what harms the body.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes saying only "bismillah" on entering, without completing the basmalah, because the place is not one for dhikr.
