Menstruation and Postnatal Bleeding الْحَيْضُ وَالنِّفَاسُ

Why This Comes Last

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained why the author placed menstruation and postnatal bleeding at the end of the Book of Purification. Wudu, ghusl, tayammum, and removing impurity apply to men and women alike, while most rulings of menstruation concern women only. So the shared rulings came first, and this section closes Taharah before the move to Salah.

The Duration of Menstruation

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي الْحَيْضِ وَالنِّفَاسِ وَمَا يَحْرُمُ بِهِمَا
وَأَقَلُّ الْحَيْضِ: يَوْمٌ وَلَيْلَةٌ، وَأَكْثَرُهُ: خَمْسَةَ عَشَرَ يَوْمًا، وَغَالِبُهُ: سِتٌّ أَوْ سَبْعٌ.
Vocabulary
  • الْحَيْضِmenstruation; linguistically, flowing
  • وَالنِّفَاسِand postnatal bleeding
  • وَمَاand what
  • يَحْرُمُis prohibited
  • بِهِمَاbecause of them
  • وَأَقَلُّand the minimum of
  • يَوْمٌa day
  • وَلَيْلَةٌand a night, twenty-four hours
  • وَأَكْثَرُهُand its maximum
  • خَمْسَةَ عَشَرَfifteen
  • يَوْمًاdays
  • وَغَالِبُهُand its usual length
  • سِتٌّsix
  • أَوْ سَبْعٌor seven
Translation
A section on menstruation and postnatal bleeding, and what is prohibited because of them. The minimum of menstruation is a day and a night, its maximum fifteen days, and its usual length six or seven.
Commentary

Hayd linguistically means flowing, as in the Arab saying that a valley "flowed" with water. Legally, it is natural blood that comes from the depths of the womb at known times, in a state of health. Blood caused by illness is istihadah, not menstruation; blood connected to childbirth falls under nifas.

The earliest age for menstruation is nine lunar years. Blood before nine is not menstruation; after nine, it may be menstruation or istihadah according to the rules.

The minimum is twenty-four hours in total within a span of no more than fifteen days. It need not be one unbroken flow. The jurists' test is that a piece of cotton placed there would come out stained. Bleeding of an hour one day, three hours another, and so on counts if it adds up to twenty-four hours within fifteen days. One hour on each of fifteen days, totaling fifteen hours, does not reach the minimum. Blood beyond fifteen days is istihadah.

These limits come from Imam al-Shafi'i's observation of women. The Shaykh explained this as incomplete induction (istiqra' naqis): examining many cases, but not every member of the group, as in surveying a thousand people in Somalia rather than everyone there. Complete induction (istiqra' tamm) would examine every member.

Hayd (Menstruation). Natural blood from the depths of the womb at known times, in a state of health.

Istihadah. Blood from illness or outside the limits of menstruation.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the minimum is twenty-four hours in total within fifteen days, not twenty-four hours of uninterrupted flow.

(2)

Istihadah Is Different

A woman with istihadah does not stop praying or fasting, and none of the prohibitions below apply to her because of it. She may touch and carry the mushaf and recite Quran. She is treated like anyone with continuous hadath: she washes the area, uses a pad or similar to contain the blood, makes wudu, and prays, all in close succession. The Shaykh cautioned that inserting something into the private part while fasting breaks the fast.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that istihadah carries none of the prohibitions of menstruation. She prays and fasts, following the procedure for continuous hadath.

(3)

What Menstruation and Janabah Prohibit

وَيَحْرُمُ بِالْحَيْضِ وَبِالْجَنَابَةِ: الصَّلَاةُ، وَالطَّوَافُ، وَمَسُّ الْمُصْحَفِ، وَحَمْلُهُ، وَاللُّبْثُ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ، وَقِرَاءَةُ الْقُرْآنِ بِقَصْدِ الْقِرَاءَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَحْرُمُand is prohibited
  • بِالْحَيْضِbecause of menstruation
  • وَبِالْجَنَابَةِand because of janabah
  • الصَّلَاةُprayer
  • وَالطَّوَافُand tawaf
  • وَمَسُّand touching
  • الْمُصْحَفِthe mushaf
  • وَحَمْلُهُand carrying it
  • وَاللُّبْثُand remaining
  • فِي الْمَسْجِدِin the mosque
  • وَقِرَاءَةُand reciting
  • الْقُرْآنِthe Quran
  • بِقَصْدِwith the intention of
  • الْقِرَاءَةِrecitation
Translation
Menstruation and janabah make prohibited: prayer, tawaf, touching and carrying the mushaf, remaining in the mosque, and reciting Quran with the intention of recitation.
Commentary

The Shaykh preferred the author's wording "prohibited because of menstruation" over "prohibited for the menstruating woman", because some prohibitions fall on others. Divorce during menstruation is prohibited for the husband, because of his wife's menstruation.

  • Prayer in every form: obligatory, voluntary, the prostrations of recitation and gratitude, and the funeral prayer.
  • Tawaf of every kind.
  • Touching and carrying the mushaf. Carrying it among belongings follows the same four cases of intention as for minor hadath.
  • Remaining in the mosque. One in janabah may pass through, entering by one door and leaving by another, but may not stay or wander about in it. Anyone, man or woman, who fears soiling the mosque may not pass through. If someone falls asleep in a mosque, wakes in janabah, and finds it locked, he is excused; if possible he may wash there. The Shaykh also discussed a non-Muslim entering, which is allowed only with a Muslim's permission and a need, historically such as for a court case or a fatwa.
  • Reciting Quran with the intention of recitation. Reciting words of the Quran intending dhikr or seeking protection is treated differently.

Asked whether a Quran app on a phone takes the ruling of the mushaf, the Shaykh said it does not. It is not fixed writing but electronic data, comparable to the Quran held in memory. This is the position of most later Shafi'i jurists, though there is disagreement.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the difference between remaining in a mosque and passing through it. One in janabah may pass through; no one may remain.

(4)

What Menstruation Alone Prohibits

وَيَحْرُمُ عَلَى الْحَائِضِ وَحْدَهَا: الصَّوْمُ، وَعُبُورُ الْمَسْجِدِ إِنْ خَافَتْ تَلْوِيثَهُ بِالدَّمِ، وَالِاسْتِمْتَاعُ فِيمَا بَيْنَ السُّرَّةِ وَالرُّكْبَةِ.
وَالْجِمَاعُ فِي الْحَيْضِ مِنَ الْكَبَائِرِ.
Vocabulary
  • عَلَى الْحَائِضِfor the menstruating woman
  • وَحْدَهَاalone
  • الصَّوْمُfasting
  • وَعُبُورُand passing through
  • الْمَسْجِدِthe mosque
  • إِنْ خَافَتْif she fears
  • تَلْوِيثَهُsoiling it
  • بِالدَّمِwith blood
  • وَالِاسْتِمْتَاعُand enjoyment
  • فِيمَا بَيْنَin what is between
  • السُّرَّةِthe navel
  • وَالرُّكْبَةِand the knee
  • وَالْجِمَاعُand intercourse
  • فِي الْحَيْضِduring menstruation
  • مِنَ الْكَبَائِرِis among the major sins
Translation
Prohibited for the menstruating woman alone: fasting, passing through the mosque if she fears soiling it with blood, and enjoyment between the navel and the knee. Intercourse during menstruation is one of the major sins.
Commentary

Fasting, obligatory or voluntary, is prohibited, meaning intending a fast. Simply not eating or drinking for the day without intending to fast is not a fast.

On passing through the mosque, the Shaykh gave three cases:

She fears soiling it. Prohibited.

She is safe from that and has a need. Not disliked.

She is safe from that but has no need. Disliked.

(5)

Intercourse during menstruation is prohibited absolutely, based on the verse commanding to keep away from women during menstruation. It is a major sin, and deeming it lawful is disbelief. Giving charity afterward is recommended, not obligatory, since its hadith is weak. Enjoyment between the navel and the knee is prohibited in the school when there is desire, whether or not there is a barrier; enjoyment above the navel and below the knee is permitted. The Shaykh noted that al-Mawardi and some books speak of "direct contact" (mubasharah) instead of "enjoyment", and the two expressions differ.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that fasting is prohibited in the sense of intending a fast; not eating without that intention is not itself a fast.

(6)

Making Up Fasts but Not Prayers

وَيَجِبُ عَلَيْهَا قَضَاءُ صَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ دُونَ الصَّلَاةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَجِبُ عَلَيْهَاand it is obligatory on her
  • قَضَاءُmaking up
  • صَوْمِthe fast of
  • رَمَضَانَRamadan
  • دُونَbut not
  • الصَّلَاةِthe prayer
She must make up the Ramadan fast, but not the prayer.

'A'ishah reported that the women were ordered to make up the fast and not ordered to make up the prayer. This ruling is received from the Sharia, not derived by reasoning; one possible wisdom is that fasting comes far less often than the daily prayers.

Should she make up those prayers anyway? Ibn Hajar holds it is prohibited and invalid; al-Ramli holds it is only disliked. Asked about the difference between these scholars, the Shaykh explained that Ibn Hajar and al-Ramli both wrote commentaries on al-Nawawi's Minhaj, clarifying it, adding issues, and sometimes understanding his wording differently. Following either is acceptable.

The Shaykh added two rulings here:

  • A husband may not divorce his wife during menstruation after the marriage has been consummated. This is called bid'i divorce. There are three exceptions; one is divorce before consummation, and the others come later.
  • Purification with the intention of worship is invalid during menstruation. A woman who became junub and then began menstruating before her ghusl cannot do ghusl for janabah during it. She may wash for cleanliness or to cool off. The ghusl of Eid is an exception and may be done during menstruation.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that missed fasts are made up and missed prayers are not, and that Ibn Hajar considers making up those prayers prohibited while al-Ramli considers it disliked.

(7)

When the Bleeding Stops

وَإِذَا انْقَطَعَ الدَّمُ.. حَلَّ لَهَا الصَّوْمُ قَبْلَ الْغُسْلِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَإِذَاand when
  • انْقَطَعَstopped
  • الدَّمُthe blood
  • حَلَّ لَهَاbecomes permissible for her
  • الصَّوْمُfasting
  • قَبْلَbefore
  • الْغُسْلِthe ghusl
When the blood stops, fasting becomes permissible for her before the ghusl.

If the bleeding stops at eight and she bathes at ten, in between she still may not pray, touch or carry the mushaf, recite Quran, or pass through or remain in the mosque. But fasting becomes permissible as soon as the blood stops: if it stops an hour before dawn and she intends to fast, her fast is valid. The husband may also divorce her once the blood stops, without waiting for her ghusl.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that only fasting and divorce become permissible when the blood stops. Everything else waits for the ghusl.

(8)

Postnatal Bleeding

وَيَحْرُمُ بِالنِّفَاسِ مَا يَحْرُمُ بِالْحَيْضِ.
وَالنِّفَاسُ: هُوَ الدَّمُ الْخَارِجُ بَعْدَ الْوِلَادَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَحْرُمُand is prohibited
  • بِالنِّفَاسِbecause of postnatal bleeding
  • مَاwhat
  • بِالْحَيْضِbecause of menstruation
  • وَالنِّفَاسُand postnatal bleeding
  • هُوَis
  • الدَّمُthe blood
  • الْخَارِجُthat exits
  • بَعْدَafter
  • الْوِلَادَةِchildbirth
Translation
What is prohibited because of menstruation is prohibited because of postnatal bleeding. Postnatal bleeding is the blood that comes after childbirth.
Commentary

The rulings of nifas are generally those of menstruation, with some differences studied later. More precisely, nifas is blood after the womb has emptied. Blood before the child emerges, during the birth, or between twins is not nifas. With twins or more, nifas begins only after the last child is fully out. The Shaykh noted the school's position that a pregnant woman can menstruate, though it is rare.

Minimum. A single moment, unlike menstruation's twenty-four hours.

Usual. Forty days.

Maximum. Sixty days. Blood after sixty days is not nifas and may be menstruation.

(9)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that nifas begins only once the womb is empty, after the last child, not with the first child or the blood of delivery itself.
