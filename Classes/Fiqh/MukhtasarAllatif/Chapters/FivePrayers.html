The Five Prescribed Prayers الصَّلَوَاتُ الْمَكْتُوبَاتُ

Text
بَابُ الصَّلَاةِ
الصَّلَوَاتُ الْمَكْتُوبَاتُ خَمْسٌ، وَتَقْدِيمُ الصَّلَاةِ عَلَى وَقْتِهَا وَتَأْخِيرُهَا عَنْ وَقْتِهَا بِغَيْرِ عُذْرٍ مِنَ الْكَبَائِرِ.
Vocabulary
  • بَابُthe chapter of
  • الصَّلَاةِprayer; linguistically, supplication
  • الصَّلَوَاتُthe prayers
  • الْمَكْتُوبَاتُprescribed, made obligatory
  • خَمْسٌfive
  • وَتَقْدِيمُand putting forward
  • عَلَى وَقْتِهَاbefore its time
  • وَتَأْخِيرُهَاand delaying it
  • عَنْ وَقْتِهَاbeyond its time
  • بِغَيْرِwithout
  • عُذْرٍan excuse
  • مِنَ الْكَبَائِرِis among the major sins
Translation
The Chapter of Prayer. The prescribed prayers are five. Praying before the time or delaying past the time without an excuse is one of the major sins.

What Salah Means

Linguistically, salah is supplication; some books say supplication for good. The Shaykh cited the verse "wa salli 'alayhim", meaning supplicate for them. He noted that translating salah simply as "prayer" can mislead in English, since the word may bring to mind worship in a church. Islamic technical terms carry specific meanings that often need explaining rather than a one-word translation.

Legally, salah is statements and actions that begin with the opening takbir and end with the taslim. The statements and actions meant are its required pillars, not every word and movement that may occur in it. Reciting a surah after al-Fatihah, for example, is sunnah; leaving it is disliked but does not invalidate the prayer.

Salah. Linguistically, supplication. Legally, statements and actions beginning with the opening takbir and ending with the taslim.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that "statements and actions" in the definition means the required pillars, not every act that can occur in the prayer.

(2)

The Rank of Prayer

The Shaykh distinguished bodily worship (prayer, fasting, zakah, Hajj, Quran recitation) from the acts of the heart (sincerity, humility, purifying the heart of arrogance and its other diseases). The acts of the heart are generally superior. Among bodily acts, prayer is the best, both among obligations and among voluntary acts.

This is a general ranking, not absolute in every situation. A wealthy person who has paid his zakah and then meets someone in need may do better helping him than praying a voluntary prayer.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the acts of the heart rank above bodily acts, and that prayer is the best of the bodily acts, though circumstances can make another deed better in a given moment.

(3)

Why "Five"

The five prescribed prayers are those Allah made obligatory from the outset and on every individual. A vowed prayer is obligatory only because the person bound himself to it. The funeral prayer is a communal obligation: once some Muslims perform it, the others are released. The evidence includes the instruction given to Mu'adh ibn Jabal when sent to Yemen, that Allah has obligated five prayers in every day and night, and the hadith of the man who asked whether there was anything beyond the five.

Fard 'Ayn (Individual Obligation). Required of each qualifying person, such as Dhuhr.

Fard Kifayah (Communal Obligation). Discharged for everyone when some perform it, such as the funeral prayer.

(4)

Praying Out of Its Time

Allah fixed times for prayer: "Indeed, prayer has been prescribed for the believers at appointed times." A prayer cannot be prayed as the obligatory prayer before its time enters, and delaying it until the time ends without an excuse is a major sin. Jibril taught the Prophet the times by praying at the start and end of each, and the time lies between the two.

The Shaykh defined a major sin with Ibn Hajar's words in Tuhfat al-Muhtaj: a crime that shows its doer has little regard for the religion and little restraint. Examples: drinking wine, zina, theft, and false testimony.

Kabirah (Major Sin). A crime that shows its doer has little regard for the religion and little restraint. (Ibn Hajar, Tuhfat al-Muhtaj)

(5)

Once the time enters, a person must either pray or be resolved to pray within the time. If he is resolved and dies before the time ends, he is not sinful. If the time ends and he has not prayed, he is sinful even though he had resolved.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the resolve to pray. When the time enters, either pray or resolve to pray before it ends; people often overlook this resolve.

(6)

Valid Excuses

  • Sleep. An excuse if he fell asleep before the time entered. Sleeping after it entered is also excused if he reasonably expected to wake in time. Sleeping when he knows he will not wake in time is sinful.
  • Forgetfulness. Not an excuse if it came from something prohibited or disliked, like losing track of time playing PlayStation. Becoming absorbed in study, or in reconciling two people, can be excusable.
  • Combining prayers. A traveler on a journey that permits shortening may combine Dhuhr and Asr, either in Dhuhr's time (jam' taqdim) or in Asr's time (jam' ta'khir), under conditions that come later.

Jam' Taqdim (Advanced Combining). Praying both prayers in the time of the first.

Jam' Ta'khir (Delayed Combining). Praying both in the time of the second.

(7)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that sleep is not automatically an excuse; it depends on when one slept and whether one reasonably expected to wake in time. And forgetfulness caused by something prohibited or disliked is no excuse.

(8)
