I'tikaf الِاعْتِكَافُ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي الِاعْتِكَافِ]
وَشَرْطُ صِحَّةِ الِاعْتِكَافِ: النِّيَّةُ، وَاللُّبْثُ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ، وَالْإِسْلَامُ، وَالْعَقْلُ، وَالنَّقَاءُ عَنِ الْحَيْضِ وَالنِّفَاسِ، وَالطَّهَارَةُ عَنِ الْجَنَابَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • الِاعْتِكَافِi'tikaf, devotional retreat in the masjid
  • النِّيَّةُthe intention
  • وَاللُّبْثُand remaining
  • فِي الْمَسْجِدِin the masjid
  • وَالنَّقَاءُ عَنِ الْحَيْضِ وَالنِّفَاسِand being clear of menstruation and postnatal bleeding
  • وَالطَّهَارَةُ عَنِ الْجَنَابَةِand purity from janabah
Translation
A section on i'tikaf. The conditions of a valid i'tikaf are the intention, remaining in the masjid, Islam, sanity, being clear of menstruation and postnatal bleeding, and purity from janabah.
Commentary

Linguistically, i'tikaf is remaining in a place. In the Sharia it is a specific remaining, by a specific person, in a specific place: the masjid. It is valid fasting or not, in Ramadan or outside it. It is most emphasized in Ramadan, especially the last ten nights, and for one who is fasting; fasting with it outside Ramadan is recommended.

Pillars and Conditions

Three pillarsDetail
IntentionIntending i'tikaf; if vowed, intending the obligatory i'tikaf
RemainingLonger than the stillness of prayer, about one "subhan Allah"; sitting, standing, or moving about within the masjid all count
The masjidA place endowed as a masjid, with its area identifiable

The four conditions of validity: Islam and sanity, since it is worship needing a valid intention; being clear of menstruation and postnatal bleeding; and purity from janabah, since remaining in the masjid is prohibited for them. Adulthood is not a condition: a child's i'tikaf is valid.

A place becomes a masjid only by being endowed as one. A school, study center, or ribat where people pray, teach, and memorize Quran is not a masjid for i'tikaf. A man who declares 100 of his 1,000 square meters a masjid without specifying which part has not identified the masjid, so i'tikaf there is not valid.

I'tikaf is valid in any masjid, but a Jumu'ah masjid is better, to avoid the view that requires one.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that i'tikaf requires a masjid, and that praying or teaching in a place does not make it one without being endowed as a masjid, and that fasting is not a condition of i'tikaf.

The Rulings of I'tikaf

RulingWhen
RecommendedThe default; most emphasized in the last ten nights of Ramadan
ObligatoryWhen vowed
Prohibited but validA wife's i'tikaf without her husband's permission
Prohibited and invalidBy a woman in menstruation or postnatal bleeding, or a person in janabah
DislikedA young woman whose presence draws attention; for elderly women it is fine

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that prohibited and invalid are separate judgments: an act can be prohibited yet valid, or prohibited and invalid.
