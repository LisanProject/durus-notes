The Prostration of Recitation سُجُودُ التِّلَاوَةِ

The Text

Text
وَيُسْتَحَبُّ سُجُودُ التِّلَاوَةِ لِلْقَارِئِ وَالْمُسْتَمِعِ وَالسَّامِعِ فِي أَرْبَعَ عَشْرَةَ آيَةً، يَسْجُدُهَا الْإِمَامُ وَالْمُنْفَرِدُ، وَلَا يَسْجُدُهَا الْمَأْمُومُ إِلَّا إِذَا سَجَدَ إِمَامُهُ، فَإِنْ سَجَدَ دُونَ إِمَامِهِ.. بَطَلَتْ صَلَاتُهُ.
Vocabulary
  • سُجُودُ التِّلَاوَةِthe prostration of recitation
  • لِلْقَارِئِfor the reciter
  • وَالْمُسْتَمِعِand the one who listens
  • وَالسَّامِعِand the one who happens to hear
  • فِي أَرْبَعَ عَشْرَةَ آيَةًat fourteen verses
  • يَسْجُدُهَاperforms it
  • الْإِمَامُ وَالْمُنْفَرِدُthe imam and the one praying alone
  • وَلَا يَسْجُدُهَا الْمَأْمُومُand the follower does not perform it
  • إِلَّا إِذَا سَجَدَ إِمَامُهُunless his imam prostrates
  • فَإِنْ سَجَدَ دُونَ إِمَامِهِand if he prostrates without his imam
  • بَطَلَتْ صَلَاتُهُhis prayer is invalid
Translation
The prostration of recitation is recommended for the reciter, the one who listens, and the one who happens to hear, at fourteen verses. The imam and the one praying alone perform it; the follower performs it only if his imam does. If he prostrates without his imam, his prayer is invalid.
Commentary

It is a prostration caused by recitation, as sujud al-sahw is caused by a lapse. Its ruling is recommended, not obligatory: Zayd ibn Thabit recited Surat al-Najm to the Prophet ﷺ, who did not prostrate, though the surah ends with a command to prostrate.

The listener intends to listen; the hearer simply hears. Both are included. Following a live broadcast, such as the night prayer from the Haram, one prostrates; for an old recording, such as a recitation by Shaykh al-Husari, one does not.

The count is fifteen in some reckonings and fourteen in the school. The prostration in Surat Sad is a prostration of gratitude, not of recitation.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the prostration is recommended, not obligatory, and that a live transmission differs from a recording.

Conditions Outside the Prayer

Five conditions
  • A recitation the Sharia sanctions: not prohibited (a person in janabah or a menstruating woman) or disliked (reciting in ruku' or sujud).
  • Intentional recitation: not a sleeper's words or a parrot's repetition.
  • The whole verse of prostration is recited, not part of it.
  • By one reciter: not one person beginning the verse and another finishing it.
  • No long gap: the prostration follows directly, not half an hour later.

Inside the Prayer

  • The follower never prostrates on his own. If the imam recites a verse of prostration and does not prostrate, the follower does not either; prostrating without him invalidates the follower's prayer.
  • The imam and the one praying alone prostrate for their own recitation only, not for someone outside the prayer, and the imam not for a follower's recitation.
  • The verse must be recited after entering the prayer, and the imam or one praying alone should not choose a verse of prostration just to prostrate.
  • There is no prostration of recitation in the funeral prayer, since it has no recitation of that kind.

Surat Sad. Outside prayer, one makes a prostration of gratitude at it. Inside prayer it is prohibited and invalidates the prayer. Behind an imam whose school counts it as a prostration of recitation, the follower does not prostrate with him; he waits standing until the imam rises, which is better, or separates from him and completes alone. Where an imam's unexpected prostration would confuse a congregation, it may be better for him to delay it until after the prayer.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that in prayer the follower prostrates only with his imam, and that the prostration of Surat Sad is one of gratitude, never made inside the prayer.
