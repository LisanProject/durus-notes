The Times When Prayer Is Prohibited الْأَوْقَاتُ الَّتِي تَحْرُمُ فِيهَا الصَّلَاةُ

The Five Times

Text
[الْأَوْقَاتُ الَّتِي تَحْرُمُ فِيهَا الصَّلَاةُ]
وَتَحْرُمُ الصَّلَاةُ فِي خَمْسَةِ أَوْقَاتٍ:
عِنْدَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ حَتَّى تَرْتَفِعَ قَدْرَ رُمْحٍ.
وَعِنْدَ الِاسْتِوَاءِ فِي غَيْرِ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ حَتَّى تَزُولَ.
وَعِنْدَ الِاصْفِرَارِ حَتَّى تَغْرُبَ.
وَبَعْدَ صَلَاةِ الصُّبْحِ حَتَّى تَطْلُعَ.
وَبَعْدَ صَلَاةِ الْعَصْرِ حَتَّى تَغْرُبَ.

Translation
The times in which prayer is prohibited. Prayer is prohibited in five times: at sunrise until the sun has risen a spear's length; at the zenith, except on Friday, until it declines; at the yellowing of the sun until it sets; after praying Subh until the sun rises; and after praying Asr until the sun sets.
Commentary

Three of these are tied to the sun itself: its rising, its zenith (except on Friday), and its yellowing. Two are tied to having prayed: once a person has prayed Subh, he may not pray until sunrise, and once he has prayed Asr, not until sunset. Someone who has not yet prayed Subh or Asr must of course still pray the obligation within its time.

Which Prayers Are Meant

وَلَا يَحْرُمُ فِيهَا مَا لَهُ سَبَبٌ فِي الْحَالِ؛ كَتَحِيَّةِ الْمَسْجِدِ، وَالْكُسُوفِ، دُونَ رَكْعَتَيِ الِاسْتِخَارَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَحْرُمُand is not prohibited
  • فِيهَاin them
  • مَاwhat
  • لَهُhas
  • سَبَبٌa cause
  • فِي الْحَالِpresent now, preceding or accompanying it
  • كَتَحِيَّةِsuch as the greeting of
  • الْمَسْجِدِthe mosque
  • وَالْكُسُوفِand the eclipse prayer
  • دُونَbut not
  • رَكْعَتَيِthe two rak'ahs of
  • الِاسْتِخَارَةِistikharah
Translation
Not prohibited in them is a prayer that has a present cause, such as the greeting of the mosque and the eclipse prayer, but not the two rak'ahs of istikharah.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha warned that "prayer" here needs care. What is prohibited is the unrestricted voluntary prayer (nafl mutlaq), with no particular time and no cause. Prayers with a cause or a set time are a different matter. He gave the funeral prayer (a cause) and Duha (a time) as examples, and noted that someone who prayed Subh without first praying its two sunnah rak'ahs may pray them afterward, since they have a cause.

Preceding Cause. The cause comes before the prayer: entering the mosque, then praying its greeting. Permitted.

Concurrent Cause. The cause is happening during the prayer: the eclipse prayer while the eclipse is underway. Permitted.

Later Cause. The cause comes after the prayer: in istikharah the two rak'ahs come first, then the supplication. Prohibited.

No Cause. An unrestricted voluntary prayer. Prohibited.

(1)

Where a Shafi'i text calls such prayer "disliked" (makruh) in these times, it may mean prohibited in the technical sense, and a prayer prayed in the prohibited category does not count. The Shaykh advised the student to stay focused on the Shafi'i school for now rather than reading other schools at this stage, and said this section would be reviewed again in the next lesson.

Nafl Mutlaq (Unrestricted Voluntary Prayer). A voluntary prayer with no specific time and no cause. This is the prayer prohibited in these five times.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the prohibition is not on every prayer. Prayers with a preceding or concurrent cause are permitted; those with no cause or a later cause are prohibited and invalid.
