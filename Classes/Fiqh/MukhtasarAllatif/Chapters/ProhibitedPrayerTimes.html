The Times When Prayer Is Prohibited الْأَوْقَاتُ الَّتِي تَحْرُمُ فِيهَا الصَّلَاةُ

The Five Times

Text
[الْأَوْقَاتُ الَّتِي تَحْرُمُ فِيهَا الصَّلَاةُ]
وَتَحْرُمُ الصَّلَاةُ فِي خَمْسَةِ أَوْقَاتٍ:
عِنْدَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ حَتَّى تَرْتَفِعَ قَدْرَ رُمْحٍ.
وَعِنْدَ الِاسْتِوَاءِ فِي غَيْرِ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ حَتَّى تَزُولَ.
وَعِنْدَ الِاصْفِرَارِ حَتَّى تَغْرُبَ.
وَبَعْدَ صَلَاةِ الصُّبْحِ حَتَّى تَطْلُعَ.
وَبَعْدَ صَلَاةِ الْعَصْرِ حَتَّى تَغْرُبَ.

Translation
The times in which prayer is prohibited. Prayer is prohibited in five times: at sunrise until the sun has risen a spear's length; at the zenith, except on Friday, until it declines; at the yellowing of the sun until it sets; after praying Subh until the sun rises; and after praying Asr until the sun sets.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha divided the five into two groups. The first three are prohibited because of the time itself: the sun's rising, its zenith (except on Friday), and its yellowing. The last two are tied to the person's own action: once someone has prayed Subh, he may not pray until sunrise, and once he has prayed Asr, not until sunset.

"Prohibited" here means the prayer is forbidden and, if begun in the prohibited time, does not take effect as a valid prayer. Some books say prayer is "disliked" (makruh) in these times, but the Shaykh said that wording also means prohibited here.

Sunrise. The prohibition begins as the sun starts to rise and lasts until it has risen a spear's height. The jurists put a spear at seven cubits; taking a cubit as roughly fifty centimeters, that is about three and a half meters as it appears to the eye, not the sun's actual distance. The Shaykh related this to astronomical degrees, one degree of the sun's movement taking about four minutes, which puts the end of the prohibited time roughly sixteen to twenty minutes after sunrise.

Zenith. The sun's apparent position at the middle of the sky until it declines. The interval is very brief, yet even beginning the opening takbir in it means the prayer has begun in a prohibited time. On Friday there is no prohibition at the zenith, even for someone not attending Jumu'ah, such as a woman praying at home.

Yellowing. From the yellowing of the sun until it sets.

(1)

After Subh and after Asr, the restriction is personal. It begins only once that person has prayed. If the time of Subh has entered but someone has not yet prayed it, the restriction has not begun for him.

(2)

Which Prayers Are Meant

وَلَا يَحْرُمُ فِيهَا مَا لَهُ سَبَبٌ فِي الْحَالِ؛ كَتَحِيَّةِ الْمَسْجِدِ، وَالْكُسُوفِ، دُونَ رَكْعَتَيِ الِاسْتِخَارَةِ.

Translation
Not prohibited in them is a prayer that has a present cause, such as the greeting of the mosque and the eclipse prayer, but not the two rak'ahs of istikharah.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha warned that "prayer" here needs care. Prayer is either obligatory or voluntary, and obligatory prayer is not prohibited in these times: someone who delayed Subh until after sunrise still prays it. Voluntary prayer is either unrestricted (nafl mutlaq), with no particular time or cause, or restricted (nafl muqayyad), tied to a time, like the sunnah before or after Dhuhr, or to a cause, like the greeting of the mosque. What is prohibited is the unrestricted voluntary prayer, and a voluntary prayer whose cause comes after it, such as istikharah or the prayer of need. Someone who prayed Subh without first praying its two sunnah rak'ahs may pray them afterward.

Preceding Cause. The cause comes before the prayer: entering the mosque, then praying its greeting. Permitted.

Concurrent Cause. The cause is happening during the prayer: the eclipse prayer while the eclipse is underway. Permitted.

Later Cause. The cause comes after the prayer: in istikharah the two rak'ahs come first, then the supplication. Prohibited.

No Cause. An unrestricted voluntary prayer. Prohibited.

(3)

Some jurists call the cause of the mosque greeting "preceding," since one enters first, and others "concurrent," since one remains in the mosque. Likewise some call the eclipse a preceding cause and others a concurrent one. Either way the result is the same: the prayer is permitted. The eclipse prayer is prayed while the eclipse continues; once it ends, its time has ended.

The Shaykh also advised the student to stay focused on the Shafi'i school for now, rather than reading the other schools on this question at this stage.

Nafl Mutlaq (Unrestricted Voluntary Prayer). A voluntary prayer with no specific time and no cause.

Nafl Muqayyad (Restricted Voluntary Prayer). A voluntary prayer tied to a time, such as the sunnah prayers around Dhuhr, or to a cause, such as the greeting of the mosque.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the prohibition is not on every prayer. Obligatory prayers, and voluntary prayers with a preceding or concurrent cause, are permitted; those with no cause or a later cause are prohibited and invalid.

(5)

When the Khatib Ascends the Minbar

وَيَحْرُمُ ابْتِدَاءُ الصَّلَاةِ بَعْدَ صُعُودِ الْخَطِيبِ فِي الْجُمُعَةِ غَيْرَ التَّحِيَّةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَحْرُمُand is prohibited
  • ابْتِدَاءُ الصَّلَاةِbeginning a prayer
  • بَعْدَafter
  • صُعُودِ الْخَطِيبِthe khatib's ascending (the minbar)
  • فِي الْجُمُعَةِat Jumu'ah
  • غَيْرَ التَّحِيَّةِother than the greeting (of the mosque)
Translation
Beginning a prayer after the khatib has ascended the minbar at Jumu'ah is prohibited, other than the greeting of the mosque.
Commentary

A latecomer who arrives once the khatib has ascended should not sit before praying the two rak'ahs of the mosque greeting, but they should be light. The Shaykh reported two formulations: Ibn Hajar says to limit them to their obligatory elements, while al-Ramli says not to lengthen them beyond what is customary. He did not name one as the relied-upon position.

The Exceptions

A student asked about the phrase "outside the sanctuary of Makkah." The Shaykh confirmed that these prohibitions do not apply in the Haram of Makkah, citing the hadith:

يَا بَنِي عَبْدِ مَنَافٍ، لَا تَمْنَعُوا أَحَداً طَافَ بِهَذَا الْبَيْتِ وَصَلَّى أَيَّةَ سَاعَةٍ شَاءَ مِنْ لَيْلٍ أَوْ نَهَارٍ.
O Banu 'Abd Manaf, do not prevent anyone from circling this House and praying at any hour he wishes, of night or day.
