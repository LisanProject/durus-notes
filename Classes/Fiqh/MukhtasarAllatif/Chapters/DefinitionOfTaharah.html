The Definition of Taharah تَعْرِيفُ الطَّهَارَةِ

Text
بَابُ الطَّهَارَةِ
Vocabulary
  • بَابُchapter, literally a door
  • الطَّهَارَةِpurification
Translation
The Chapter of Purification.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha began with a language note. The word taharah can be read with different vowel patterns, and with some words a change in vowels changes the meaning, while with others the meaning stays the same. The subject of this chapter is legal purification: wudu, ghusl, and the removal of najasah.

The Legal Definition

The matn itself does not define taharah. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha gave the standard definition of the Shafi'i school and asked the student to memorize it.

الطَّهَارَةُ: رَفْعُ حَدَثٍ، أَوْ إِزَالَةُ نَجَسٍ، أَوْ مَا فِي مَعْنَاهُمَا، أَوْ عَلَى صُورَتِهِمَا
Vocabulary
  • الطَّهَارَةُpurification
  • رَفْعُlifting, removing
  • حَدَثٍa state of ritual impurity
  • أَوْor
  • إِزَالَةُremoving
  • نَجَسٍphysical impurity
  • مَاthat which
  • فِيin
  • مَعْنَاهُمَاthe meaning of the two, having the same legal effect
  • عَلَىupon, in
  • صُورَتِهِمَاthe form of the two, looking like them outwardly
Translation
Purification is the removal of ritual impurity, or the removal of physical impurity, or what has the meaning of either of them, or what is done in the form of either of them.

Taharah (Purification). Removing ritual impurity (raf' al-hadath), removing physical impurity (izalat al-najas), or an act that has the meaning or the form of either one.

(1)

The Four Parts of the Definition

1. Removing hadath. A person sleeps, wakes up, and performs wudu. The wudu removes the state of ritual impurity caused by sleep.

2. Removing najasah. A person washes physical impurity off his hand with water.

3. What has their meaning. An act that takes the legal place of removal without literally removing anything. Tayammum lets a person with no water pray after sleeping, but it does not actually lift the hadath. Istinja with a stone counts as removing impurity legally, even though traces remain that the stone does not take away.

4. What has their form. An act that looks like purification when there is nothing to remove. Renewing wudu while already in wudu does not lift any hadath. Washing a second and third time after najasah is already gone does not remove it again. Both are done in the form of purification and are recommended.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that tayammum permits prayer but does not remove the hadath itself. The person is still in a state of hadath; the tayammum only makes the prayer permissible.

(3)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that istinja with a stone remains permissible even when water is available. Using a stone first and then water is the better practice. The stone does not remove every trace of impurity, but when its conditions are met the person may pray.

(4)

Key Terms

Hadath. A state of ritual impurity, removed by the appropriate purification, such as the state caused by sleep and removed by wudu.

Najasah. Physical impurity, removed by washing, such as impurity on the hand.

Tayammum. Dry purification with earth, which permits prayer when water is unavailable but does not remove hadath.

Istinja. Cleaning oneself after using the toilet, with stones, water, or both.

(5)
