When Water Becomes Impure مَا يَنْجُسُ بِهِ الْمَاءُ

Small Water and Other Liquids

Text
فَصْلٌ فِيمَا يَنْجُسُ بِهِ الْمَاءُ
وَيَنْجُسُ الْمَاءُ الْقَلِيلُ وَغَيْرُهُ مِنَ الْمَائِعَاتِ وَلَوْ بِتَكْمِيلِهِ قُلَّتَيْنِ بِوُقُوعِ النَّجَاسَةِ فِيهِ، سَوَاءٌ غَيَّرَتْهُ أَمْ لَمْ تُغَيِّرْهُ.

  • بِتَكْمِيلِهِby completing it

Translation
A section on what makes water impure. Small water and other liquids become impure when impurity falls into them, even if the impurity is what brings it up to two qullahs, whether it changes the water or not.
Commentary

The Shafi'i school divides water into small (qalil), under two qullahs, and large (kathir), two qullahs or more. The basis is the hadith:

إِذَا بَلَغَ الْمَاءُ قُلَّتَيْنِ لَمْ يَحْمِلِ الْخَبَثَ
When water reaches two qullahs, it does not carry impurity.

Small water becomes impure the moment impurity touches it, even if nothing changes. A drop of urine in a container of a hundred liters makes it impure. Large water only becomes impure if the impurity changes its taste, color, or smell.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the different cause in each case. For small water, the cause of impurity is contact. For large water, the cause is change.

(1)

Other liquids are treated like small water whatever their quantity: oil, ghee, juice, Pepsi. If impurity falls into a hundred-liter barrel of oil, the oil becomes impure in the school, though it may still be used for other purposes.

How Much Is Two Qullahs?

A qullah is a large earthenware water jar. Ibn Jurayj estimated one qullah at two water skins and a bit more; Imam al-Shafi'i, being cautious, counted the "bit more" as half a skin, making a qullah two and a half skins and two qullahs five skins. An older measure gives five hundred Baghdadi ratl. Since that unit is no longer in use, the jurists also gave a measure by space: a cube one and a quarter cubits in length, width, and depth, with a cubit around 48 cm today.

Contemporary calculations give about 200, 204, or 216 liters. The Shaykh said taking the largest, 216 liters, is more cautious, and that this will be revisited when studying Matn Abi Shuja.

Qullah. A large earthenware water jar, used in fiqh as the unit for the threshold between small and large water. Two qullahs are roughly 200 to 216 liters; the larger figure is the cautious one.

(2)

What Is Excused

وَيُعْفَى عَنِ الْيَسِيرِ مِنَ الشَّعْرِ النَّجِسِ، وَعَنِ الْمَيْتَةِ الَّتِي لَا نَفْسَ لَهَا سَائِلَةٌ، وَالنَّجَاسَةِ الَّتِي لَا يُدْرِكُهَا الطَّرْفُ، وَمَنْفَذِ الطَّيْرِ وَالْفَأْرِ، وَالْيَسِيرِ مِنْ غُبَارِ السِّرْجِينِ، وَسُؤْرِ الْهِرَّةِ الَّتِي أَكَلَتْ نَجَاسَةً ثُمَّ غَابَتْ زَمَنًا وَاحْتَمَلَ وُلُوغُهَا فِي قُلَّتَيْنِ مِنَ الْمَاءِ.

Translation
Excused are: a small amount of impure hair; a dead creature with no flowing blood; impurity the eye cannot see; the outlet of a bird or a mouse; a small amount of the dust of dung fertilizer; and the leftover drink of a cat that ate something impure, then was gone for a while such that it may have drunk from two qullahs of water.
Commentary

A small amount of impure hair. A part separated from an animal takes the ruling of its dead body. A dead cat is impure, so a cat's shed hair is impure, but a few hairs are excused. The same goes for donkey hair and similar. What counts as small or large is decided by custom ('urf), not a fixed number. Dog and pig hair are not excused even in small amounts. A large amount of hair is excused from riding animals, horses, mules, donkeys, and camels, since people are constantly exposed to it.

A dead creature with no flowing blood. Nafs here means blood. This covers creatures with no blood and those whose blood does not flow: flies, mosquitoes, ants. The evidence is the hadith of the fly falling into a drink, where the Prophet said to dip it and then remove it; the jurists took from this that it does not make the drink impure. Two conditions: it must not change the water, and it must not be thrown in after death. It must fall in by itself or by the wind.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the analogy from the fly hadith is for the excusal only. It does not mean every insect must be dipped and removed; if an ant falls in, there is no need to dip it.

(3)

Impurity the eye cannot see. The standard is ordinary, moderate eyesight, not that of someone with unusually weak or unusually sharp vision. Something only visible under a microscope is excused, provided no one put it in deliberately.

The outlet of a bird or mouse. Birds do not clean themselves, so some droppings stay at the outlet. If a chicken falls into the water, that residue is excused.

Dust of dung fertilizer. When farmers carry or spread it, dust can rise and fall into water. A small amount is excused.

The cat's leftover drink. If a cat is seen eating a mouse and then goes away for a while, it may have drunk from a large body of water in the meantime and so had its mouth purified. If it then drinks from a vessel, the remaining water is excused.

Ma La Nafsa Lahu Sa'ilah. A creature with no flowing blood, either having no blood at all or blood that does not run.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that deliberately placing a dead insect or an invisible impurity in water removes the excusal.

(5)

Large Water and Impurity

فَصْلٌ فِي حُكْمِ الْمَاءِ إِذَا وَقَعَتْ فِيهِ نَجَاسَةٌ
وَإِذَا كَانَ الْمَاءُ قُلَّتَيْنِ وَوَقَعَتْ فِيهِ نَجَاسَةٌ.. فَلَا يَنْجُسُ إِلَّا إِذَا تَغَيَّرَ طَعْمُهُ أَوْ لَوْنُهُ أَوْ رِيحُهُ تَغَيُّرًا كَثِيرًا أَوْ يَسِيرًا.
Vocabulary
  • حُكْمِthe ruling of
  • إِذَاwhen
  • وَقَعَتْfell
  • كَانَwas
  • فَلَا يَنْجُسُit does not become impure
  • إِلَّا إِذَاexcept if
  • تَغَيَّرَchanged
  • طَعْمُهُits taste
  • لَوْنُهُits color
  • رِيحُهُits smell
  • كَثِيرًاa lot
  • يَسِيرًاa little
Translation
A section on the ruling of water when impurity falls into it. If the water is two qullahs and impurity falls in, it does not become impure unless its taste, color, or smell changes, whether a lot or a little.
Commentary

Any change in large water caused by impurity, even slight, makes it impure. Compare water changed by a pure substance, where a slight change does no harm.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the cause of the change matters. A slight change from tea leaves the water purifying. A slight change from impurity makes it impure.

(6)

Purifying Impure Water

وَإِذَا زَالَ التَّغَيُّرُ بِنَفْسِهِ أَوْ بِمَاءٍ.. طَهُرَ.
وَلَا يَطْهُرُ إِذَا زَالَ التَّغَيُّرُ بِمِسْكٍ أَوْ زَعْفَرَانٍ أَوْ جِصٍّ أَوْ تُرَابٍ.
Vocabulary
  • زَالَwent away
  • التَّغَيُّرُthe change
  • بِنَفْسِهِby itself
  • بِمَاءٍby water
  • طَهُرَit becomes pure
  • وَلَا يَطْهُرُand it does not become pure
  • بِمِسْكٍby musk
  • زَعْفَرَانٍsaffron
  • جِصٍّgypsum
  • تُرَابٍsoil
Translation
If the change goes away by itself or by water, it becomes pure. It does not become pure if the change disappears by musk, saffron, gypsum, or soil.
Commentary

Since large water became impure because of the change, it becomes pure again once the change is gone:

By itself. Time passes, or the wind carries off the smell.

Mukatharah (Adding Water). Add water in stages, checking after each, until the change is gone. Example: a 300-liter tank.

Taqlil (Reducing). Remove the impurity and the water around it, and check that what remains is unchanged.

(7)

Small water that became impure by contact is not purified just because a change disappears, since its impurity came from contact. The method is to add water until it reaches at least two qullahs, then check that no change remains.

Covering the change does not purify. Musk can hide a smell; saffron, gypsum, or soil can hide a color. One cannot be sure the impurity's effect is gone rather than merely concealed, so the original ruling of impurity stays.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that masking is not removing. When the change is covered rather than gone, the water remains impure.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that small water is purified by bringing it up to two qullahs and then checking. Adding a little water without reaching that amount does nothing.
