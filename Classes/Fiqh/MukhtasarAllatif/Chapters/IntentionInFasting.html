The Intention of Fasting النِّيَّةُ فِي الصَّوْمِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ صِحَّةِ الصَّوْمِ]
وَشُرُوطُ صِحَّةِ الصَّوْمِ: النِّيَّةُ، فَإِنْ كَانَ فَرْضًا.. اشْتُرِطَ التَّبْيِيتُ قَبْلَ الْفَجْرِ، وَالتَّعْيِينُ كَصَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ أَوْ نَذْرٍ.
Vocabulary
  • شُرُوطُ صِحَّةِ الصَّوْمِthe conditions of a valid fast
  • النِّيَّةُthe intention
  • فَإِنْ كَانَ فَرْضًاif it is obligatory
  • اشْتُرِطَit is a condition
  • التَّبْيِيتُmaking it at night
  • قَبْلَ الْفَجْرِbefore dawn
  • وَالتَّعْيِينُand specifying it
  • أَوْ نَذْرٍor a vow
Translation
A section on the conditions of a valid fast. Its conditions are the intention. If the fast is obligatory, the intention must be made at night before dawn, and must specify the fast, such as the fast of Ramadan or of a vow.
Commentary

The author says "conditions," but the list mixes conditions and integrals. The word is used broadly for everything a valid fast needs.

Obligatory fast (Ramadan, make-up, vow)Voluntary fast
WhenAt night: from sunset until before dawn (tabyit)May be during the day, before zawal
Specification (ta'yin)Required: Ramadan, a make-up, a vowNot required
Extra conditionn/aNothing that breaks the fast since dawn

Specifying is like naming Dhuhr or Asr in the intention of prayer. Someone who wakes after dawn, has eaten nothing, and intends a voluntary fast before zawal has a valid fast. Someone who ate after dawn and then decides to fast does not.

The intention is in the heart. Saying it aloud is not required but is recommended, so the tongue helps the heart: "I intend tomorrow's obligatory fast of Ramadan."

Tabyit. Making the intention at night, between sunset and dawn.

Ta'yin. Specifying which obligatory fast is intended.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that an obligatory fast needs an intention made at night and specified, while a voluntary fast may be intended before zawal if nothing broke the fast first, and that the intention is in the heart.
