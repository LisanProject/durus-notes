Preparing the Deceased تَجْهِيزُ الْمَيِّتِ

A Communal Obligation

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي تَجْهِيزِ الْمَيِّتِ]
تَجْهِيزُ الْمَيِّتِ وَهُوَ غُسْلُهُ وَتَكْفِينُهُ وَالصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِ وَدَفْنُهُ.. فَرْضُ كِفَايَةٍ.
Vocabulary
  • تَجْهِيزُ الْمَيِّتِpreparing the deceased
  • غُسْلُهُwashing him
  • وَتَكْفِينُهُand shrouding him
  • وَالصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِand praying over him
  • وَدَفْنُهُand burying him
  • فَرْضُ كِفَايَةٍa communal obligation
Translation
A section on preparing the deceased. Preparing the deceased, which is washing him, shrouding him, praying over him, and burying him, is a communal obligation.
Commentary

"Preparing the deceased" is the subject and "a communal obligation" its predicate. A communal obligation (fard kifayah) is one that falls from everyone once enough people perform it; if no one does, the whole community remains accountable. Carrying the body is a practical fifth act, but it is counted with burial, since the deceased is carried to the grave.

The general ruling is for a Muslim who is neither a battlefield martyr nor a miscarried fetus. Those two, and the non-Muslim, have their own rulings.

Fard Kifayah (Communal Obligation). An obligation on the community as a whole: when enough people carry it out, the demand falls from the rest; if no one does, all remain accountable.

The martyr here is one killed in fighting against non-Muslims, because of the fighting. Dying on the battlefield is not enough: a sick man who goes out with the army and dies of his illness on the field is not a martyr in this sense. Nor is one killed in fighting between Muslims.

The miscarried fetus (siqt): first ask whether a sign of life appeared, such as movement or a cry; if so, it is treated as an adult. If not, ask whether human form had begun: a nose, an eye, a finger. An early loss that is only a piece of flesh has no required funeral acts, though burying it is recommended.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a martyr is one whose death was caused by the fighting, not one who merely died on the battlefield; that funeral prayer is prohibited for every non-Muslim; and that the fetus's ruling turns first on a sign of life, then on human form.

Washing

Text
وَأَقَلُّ غُسْلِهِ: تَعْمِيمُ بَدَنِهِ شَعْرًا وَبَشَرًا بِالْمَاءِ الْخَالِصِ بَعْدَ إِزَالَةِ النَّجَاسَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَأَقَلُّ غُسْلِهِand the minimum of his washing
  • تَعْمِيمُ بَدَنِهِcovering his whole body
  • شَعْرًا وَبَشَرًاhair and skin
  • بِالْمَاءِ الْخَالِصِwith pure water
  • بَعْدَ إِزَالَةِ النَّجَاسَةِafter removing impurity
Translation
The minimum of his washing is covering his whole body, hair and skin, with pure water after removing impurity.
Commentary

This is the minimum; the complete washing, three times with wudu for the deceased, was covered in Matn Abi Shuja'. One washing can count both for removing impurity and for the washing itself, if it removes the impurity and reaches the whole body: blood washed away in the same pour that covers the body suffices. If an impurity needs more than one wash, remove it first, then wash the body.

  • Looking: looking at the 'awrah is prohibited; the rest of the body is looked at only as needed.
  • Touching: touching the 'awrah without a barrier is prohibited, so a cloth is used to wash it; a barrier is recommended for the rest of the body as well.
  • Hair and nails: removing anything from them is disliked. The deceased is left as he was at death.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that one washing suffices only when it both removes the impurity and covers the body, and that the 'awrah is neither looked at nor touched without a barrier.

Shrouding

Text
وَأَمَّا الْكَفَنُ.. فَأَقَلُّهُ: مَا يَسْتُرُ الْعَوْرَةَ.
وَالْأَفْضَلُ لِلرَّجُلِ: ثَلَاثُ لَفَائِفَ، وَلِلْمَرْأَةِ: إِزَارٌ وَخِمَارٌ وَقَمِيصٌ وَلِفَافَتَانِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْكَفَنُthe shroud
  • فَأَقَلُّهُits minimum
  • مَا يَسْتُرُ الْعَوْرَةَwhat covers the 'awrah
  • وَالْأَفْضَلُand the best
  • ثَلَاثُ لَفَائِفَthree wrapping sheets
  • إِزَارٌa lower wrap
  • وَخِمَارٌa head covering
  • وَقَمِيصٌa shirt
  • وَلِفَافَتَانِand two wrapping sheets
Translation
As for the shroud, its minimum is what covers the 'awrah. Best for a male is three wrapping sheets, and for a female a lower wrap, a head covering, a shirt, and two wrapping sheets.
Commentary

Covering the 'awrah is the minimum that removes the communal obligation, but the deceased's due is a cloth covering the whole body. "Male" and "female" here are by sex, young or old.

  • Dying in ihram: a man's head is left uncovered, and a woman's face.
  • Material: whatever the person could wear in life, so silk is not used for a man but may be for a woman or child.
  • New or used: a washed, used garment is better, though people usually buy new cloth.
  • Preparing one's shroud in advance is disliked, unless it is from wholly lawful wealth.
  • Writing Quran on the shroud or the body is prohibited, since the shroud decays and mixes with blood and discharge.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the minimum shroud covers the 'awrah while the deceased's due is a covering for the whole body, and that writing Quran on the shroud or body is prohibited.
