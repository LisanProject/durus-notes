Review of Bab al-Taharah مُرَاجَعَةُ بَابِ الطَّهَارَةِ

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha advised that once a whole book is finished, the student should set aside time to review it from beginning to end. This page gathers the Book of Purification in one place: the lists and numbers to memorize, the definitions, the principles behind the rulings, the pairs most easily confused, and the points he stressed most. Each heading links back to the full section.

How to use this page Read the list headings and try to recall each list before reading it. Then go through the "Commonly Confused" table, since the Shaykh said fiqh is a lion in the form of a cat: easy at first glance, but full of similar cases that must be told apart.

Definitions to Memorize

The Shaykh asked for these two to be memorized word for word:

الطَّهَارَةُ: رَفْعُ حَدَثٍ، أَوْ إِزَالَةُ نَجَسٍ، أَوْ مَا فِي مَعْنَاهُمَا، أَوْ عَلَى صُورَتِهِمَا
Purification is removing hadath, or removing impurity, or what has their meaning, or what is in their form.
النَّجَاسَةُ: مُسْتَقْذَرٌ يَمْنَعُ مِنْ صِحَّةِ الصَّلَاةِ حَيْثُ لَا مُرَخِّصَ
Najasah is something filthy that prevents the validity of prayer where there is no dispensation.
TermDefinition
FiqhKnowledge of practical legal rulings derived from their detailed proofs.
Shart (condition)Its absence entails the absence of the thing; its presence entails nothing.
HadathA non-visible legal state that prevents prayer. Unqualified in the books, it means minor hadath.
NiyyahAn intention joined to the act at its legally relevant moment.
GhuslWater flowing over the whole body with a specific intention.
TayammumConveying earth to the face and arms under specific conditions.
Unrestricted waterWater called simply "water" without a qualifier it cannot do without.
MaytahAn animal whose life ended without valid ritual slaughter.
HaydNatural blood from the depths of the womb at known times, in health.
NifasBlood after the womb has emptied from childbirth.
Kabir (in contact)Of an age to be ordinarily desired; not the same as puberty.
MahramOne permanently unmarriageable: 7 by lineage, 7 by nursing, 4 by marriage.

Wudu

Six obligations
  • Intention, made at the first washing of the face
  • Washing the whole face, hair and skin (except inside a man's dense beard and side-whiskers)
  • Washing the arms with the elbows
  • Wiping some of the head, even part of one hair within its boundary
  • Washing the feet with the ankles
  • Order
Fifteen sunnahs
  • Siwak, basmalah, washing the hands
  • Rinsing the mouth, drawing water into the nose, blowing it out
  • Three times each; wiping the whole head; the ears and ear canals
  • Takhlil of a dense beard; takhlil of the fingers and toes
  • Extending the ghurrah and tahjil; continuity (muwalah)
  • Not having someone pour; not drying with a cloth
Four nullifiers
  • Anything exiting the front or rear passage (with certainty)
  • Loss of reason, except sleep firmly seated and waking in the same position
  • Skin contact between a grown, non-mahram man and woman (both lose wudu)
  • Touching a human private part with the inner palm or finger pads (only the toucher loses wudu)
Prohibited without wudu
  • Prayer, tawaf, touching the mushaf, carrying it (and a tablet written for study)

Ghusl

Five causes
  • Insertion of the glans (even without emission)
  • Emission of semen (one of three signs suffices: pleasure, gushing, smell)
  • Menstruation, postnatal bleeding, and childbirth (even dry or by Caesarean)
Two obligations, seven sunnahs
  • Obligations: the intention at the first part washed, and washing all hair and skin
  • Sunnahs: siwak, basmalah, wudu first, attending to folds, takhlil of the hair, rubbing, three times

Water

CategoryStatusExamples
Tahur (unrestricted)Pure and purifiesRain, sea, river, well, melted snow
TahirPure, does not purifyChanged substantially by a mixing pure substance; used water
NajisImpureSmall water touched by impurity; large water changed by it
Change by a pure substance removes purifying status only if all four hold
  • In taste, color, or smell (not temperature)
  • Substantial, not slight
  • By a mixing substance (mukhalit), judged by ordinary sight
  • Something the water could easily be kept from
Changes that do no harm
  • Long standing, soil (while still water), naturally growing algae, its resting place and channel, an adjacent substance (mujawir)
Used water requires all three
  • Used in an obligatory purification (first washing, not sunnah repeats or renewed wudu)
  • Small in quantity
  • Separated from the limb
Small vs. large water
  • Two qullahs, about 200 to 216 liters (216 is the cautious figure)
  • Small water and all other liquids: impure by contact, even with no change
  • Large water: impure only by change, even a slight one
  • Large water is purified when the change goes: by itself, by adding water, or by removing some
  • Small water is purified by bringing it up to two qullahs and checking
  • Masking with musk, saffron, gypsum, or soil does not purify
Six things excused in water
  • A little impure hair (not dog or pig; a lot from riding animals)
  • A dead creature with no flowing blood (not changing the water, not thrown in)
  • Impurity invisible to ordinary sight (not put in deliberately)
  • The outlet of a bird or mouse
  • A little dust of dung fertilizer
  • A cat's leftover drink after eating impurity and being absent a while

The impurities in the matn
  • Urine, feces, dung, pus, blood, vomit (from the stomach)
  • Wine, nabidh, any liquid intoxicant (solid intoxicants are haram but not impure)
  • Dog, pig, and the offspring of either
  • The carcass and all its parts
  • Madhy, wady, and the semen of dogs and pigs
  • Milk of an inedible animal, other than human milk
  • A part separated from a living animal (except hair, feathers, wool of edible animals, musk and its pod, rennet)
Pure, even though one might assume otherwise
  • Human semen, nasal mucus, human milk
  • Dead humans, fish, and locusts
  • Wine that turned to vinegar by itself; a tanned hide (not dog or pig)

Tayammum

Five obligations
  • Transferring the earth
  • Intention to make prayer permissible, from placing the hand until wiping the face
  • Wiping the face
  • Wiping the arms with the elbows
  • Order
Conditions
  • Deliberately aiming for the earth
  • Pure, unused earth with dust, unmixed
  • Two strikes: one for the face, one for the arms
  • After the time has entered
  • Renewed for every obligatory prayer
  • Searching for water first, after the time enters
Three levels of intention (intention reaches down, never up)
  • 1. An obligatory prayer (including a vowed one)
  • 2. A voluntary prayer, unspecified prayer, or the funeral prayer
  • 3. Anything else: touching the mushaf, recitation, prostration of recitation
Searching distances
  • Certain there is none: no search
  • Water possible: about 150 meters (hadd al-ghawth)
  • Water certain: about 2.5 km (hadd al-qurb); beyond that, no obligation
Pray and repeat
  • One with neither water nor earth (obligatory prayer only)
  • Tayammum because of cold
  • One whose journey is itself sinful

Menstruation and Nifas

MinimumUsualMaximum
Hayd24 hours (in total, within 15 days)6 or 7 days15 days
NifasA moment40 days60 days
  • Earliest age for hayd: nine lunar years.
  • Hayd and janabah prohibit: prayer, tawaf, touching and carrying the mushaf, remaining in the mosque, reciting Quran intending recitation.
  • Hayd alone prohibits: fasting, passing through the mosque if she fears soiling it, enjoyment between navel and knee. Intercourse is a major sin.
  • Fasts are made up; prayers are not.
  • When the blood stops, only fasting (and divorce) become permissible before ghusl.
  • Istihadah carries none of these prohibitions.

Principles That Run Through the Book

What is needed to complete an obligation is itself obligatory. Why washing a little past each limb is required.

(1)

What necessitates the greater of two matters specifically does not necessitate the lesser through its general description. Why an emission while awake requires ghusl but does not break wudu.

(2)

Certainty is not removed by doubt. Doubting that something exited does not break wudu.

(3)

Intention separates worship from habit. The same washing is wudu or merely cooling off, depending only on the intention.

(4)

Read terms with the jurists' meaning. Maytah, kabir, "fruit", "beneath the tree", "stone" in istinja, and "hadath" all carry technical meanings that differ from everyday usage.

(5)

Masking is not removing. Covering an impurity's trace does not make something pure.

(6)

Rulings overlap. A case permitted under one heading may still be prohibited or disliked under another, as with large flowing water that belongs to someone else.

(7)

Concessions follow real need. Tayammum is a purification of necessity; the sinful traveler loses travel concessions; ibrad and other allowances exist only where the hardship exists.

A reminder from the Shaykh Following another position or school on an issue means learning its full treatment, not picking out the convenient answer. The jurists follow the evidence; they do not seek hardship or ease for its own sake.
