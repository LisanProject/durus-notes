Those Behind Whom Prayer Is Invalid مَنْ تَبْطُلُ الصَّلَاةُ خَلْفَهُ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِيمَنْ تَبْطُلُ الصَّلَاةُ خَلْفَهُ]
وَتَبْطُلُ الصَّلَاةُ خَلْفَ الْأَلْثَغِ وَالْأَرَتِّ، وَخَلْفَ الْمَأْمُومِ، وَخَلْفَ الْمُحْدِثِ وَالْجُنُبِ وَالْكَافِرِ، وَخَلْفَ مَنْ عَلَى بَدَنِهِ أَوْ ثَوْبِهِ نَجَاسَةٌ، وَخَلْفَ فَاقِدِ الْمَاءِ وَالتُّرَابِ، وَلَا يُصَلِّي الرَّجُلُ خَلْفَ الْمَرْأَةِ وَالْخُنْثَى.

Translation
A section on those behind whom the prayer is invalid. The prayer is invalid behind one who substitutes letters or merges them; behind a follower; behind one in minor hadath, one in janabah, or a non-Muslim; behind one with impurity on his body or garment; and behind one lacking both water and earth. A man does not pray behind a woman or a khuntha.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha grouped the examples under the principles below: each is a missing condition of leading the prayer.

Recitation of al-Fatihah

Ummi. In this chapter, one who cannot correctly produce a letter of al-Fatihah, whether he cannot pronounce it or cannot bring it from its proper point of articulation. Not simply one who cannot read.

Althagh. One who substitutes one letter for another.

Arat. One who merges letters so that a word, such as al-mustaqim, comes out wrong.

(1)
  • If he cannot learn, his own prayer is valid, and he may lead someone with the same inability, but not someone who recites al-Fatihah correctly.
  • If he can learn and has not, his own prayer is invalid, so his leading is too.
  • One who repeats letters (a stutter on waw, fa, or ta) may lead; following him is valid but disliked.
  • Entering a masjid where an imam is praying, the mere possibility that he recites badly is no reason not to follow him. Only clear evidence counts, such as hearing a missing or changed letter in a loud prayer.

Following a Follower

No one is a follower and an imam in the same prayer at once. But a latecomer who caught three rak'ahs of Asr and stood after the imam's salam to complete the fourth is now praying on his own, and a new arrival may follow him.

An Imam Whose Prayer Is Invalid

What matters is whether the follower knows the imam's prayer is invalid.

The follower learns...What he does
Before joining, that the imam is in hadathHis prayer does not begin validly behind him
During the prayer, that the imam is in hadathSeparates and completes alone; does not restart
After the prayer, that the imam was in hadathNothing; his prayer stands
During the prayer, that the imam is a non-MuslimEnds the prayer and begins it anew (yasta'nif)
That the imam has visible impuritySeparates from him

One lacking water and earth prays and must repeat later when he finds either. Someone who holds that this imam must repeat his prayer may not follow him.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that discovering the imam's hadath during the prayer means separating and completing, while discovering his disbelief means starting over; and that yasta'nif in the jurists' usage means to begin anew, not to continue.

(2)

Men, Women, and the Khuntha

Three categories give nine pairings of imam and follower. The principle: the follower may not be of a higher category than the imam. Five pairings are valid and four are not.

ImamMay leadMay not lead
ManMan, woman, khuntha
KhunthaWomanMan, khuntha
WomanWomanMan, khuntha

A khuntha may be male or female, so a khuntha following a woman or another khuntha might be a man behind a woman.
