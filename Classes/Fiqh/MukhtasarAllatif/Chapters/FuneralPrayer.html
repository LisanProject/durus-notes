The Funeral Prayer صَلَاةُ الْجَنَازَةِ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي صَلَاةِ الْجَنَازَةِ]
وَفُرُوضُ صَلَاةِ الْجَنَازَةِ سَبْعَةٌ:
الْأَوَّلُ: النِّيَّةُ، فَيَنْوِي فِعْلَ الصَّلَاةِ وَتَعْيِينَهَا؛ كَصَلَاةِ الْجَنَازَةِ، وَيَنْوِي فَرْضِيَّتَهَا.
الثَّانِي: أَرْبَعُ تَكْبِيرَاتٍ.
الثَّالِثُ: قِرَاءَةُ الْفَاتِحَةِ فِي الْأُولَى أَوْ غَيْرِهَا.
الرَّابِعُ: الصَّلَاةُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ بَعْدَ الثَّانِيَةِ.
الْخَامِسُ: الدُّعَاءُ لِلْمَيِّتِ بَعْدَ الثَّالِثَةِ.
السَّادِسُ: الْقِيَامُ لِلْقَادِرِ.
السَّابِعُ: السَّلَامُ.

Translation
A section on the funeral prayer. Its obligatory elements are seven: first, the intention, intending the act of prayer, specifying it as the funeral prayer, and intending that it is obligatory; second, four takbirs; third, reciting al-Fatihah after the first takbir or another; fourth, salawat on the Prophet ﷺ after the second; fifth, supplication for the deceased after the third; sixth, standing for one who is able; seventh, the salam.

Saying "obligatory" is enough; one need not say whether it is individual or communal. One may intend the prayer over whichever deceased Muslims are present without naming them.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that indicating the body preserves the intention even when the name is wrong, while a wrong name alone invalidates the prayer.

The Takbirs and Their Remembrances

AfterIts remembrancePlacement
First takbirAl-FatihahBest here; may be delayed to the second or third
Second takbirSalawat on the Prophet ﷺMust be after the second
Third takbirSupplication for the deceasedMust be after the third
Fourth takbirSalamAfter the fourth
  • Delaying al-Fatihah: if delayed to the second takbir, he recites it and the salawat; to the third, it and the supplication. No order is required between them.
  • Once begun, al-Fatihah is finished, even if he then remembers it could have been delayed.
  • Salawat: "O Allah, bless our master Muhammad" suffices; adding his family is better, and the Ibrahimic wording is fuller.
  • The supplication must be for the next life, since the deceased has moved on to it, as with the supplication in the Friday sermon.
  • Standing is an obligatory element for one able; one unable sits.
  • Adding "wa barakatuh" to the salam: Ibn Hajar recommends it in the funeral prayer; al-Ramli does not.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that al-Fatihah is best after the first takbir but may be delayed, and once started must be completed, while the salawat must follow the second takbir and the supplication the third.

(2)

A Fifth Takbir, and the Latecomer

Four takbirs are required and none are added. If the imam forgetfully makes a fifth, it does not harm a follower who has completed four, but he does not follow him in it. He either waits for the imam's salam and then makes his own, or intends to separate and makes salam.

A latecomer who arrives after the imam's second takbir makes his own takbir and recites al-Fatihah, then follows the imam.

Questions and Answers

Question: Must I name the deceased in my intention?

Answer: No. Intending whoever is present, or indicating the body, suffices. Indication with a name is valid even if the name proves wrong; a name alone is not.

Question: May the supplication for the deceased be about worldly matters?

Answer: No. It must concern the next life, because the deceased has moved on to it.
