Further Conditions, and Days Fasting Is Prohibited الْأَيَّامُ الْمَنْهِيُّ عَنْ صَوْمِهَا

Islam, Sanity, and Purity All Day

Text
وَشَرْطُ صِحَّةِ الصَّوْمِ أَيْضًا: الْإِسْلَامُ، وَالْعَقْلُ، وَالنَّقَاءُ عَنِ الْحَيْضِ وَالنِّفَاسِ فِي جَمِيعِ النَّهَارِ.
Vocabulary
  • أَيْضًاalso
  • الْإِسْلَامُIslam
  • وَالْعَقْلُand sanity
  • وَالنَّقَاءُand being clear
  • عَنِ الْحَيْضِ وَالنِّفَاسِof menstruation and postnatal bleeding
  • فِي جَمِيعِ النَّهَارِthroughout the whole day
Translation
Also a condition of a valid fast: Islam, sanity, and being clear of menstruation and postnatal bleeding throughout the whole day.
Commentary
State during the dayEffect on the fast
Apostasy, even for a moment, even if he returns to IslamInvalid
Insanity, even for a momentInvalid
Unconsciousness or intoxication covering the whole dayInvalid
Unconsciousness or intoxication for part of the dayNot invalid on that basis
Sleeping the whole day, before dawn until after sunsetValid
Menstruation or postnatal bleeding at any pointInvalid

A menstruating woman may not intend to fast; that intention is prohibited. Simply not eating, without an intention, is not a fast.

Days on Which Fasting Is Prohibited

Text
وَيَحْرُمُ صَوْمُ الْعِيدَيْنِ وَأَيَّامِ التَّشْرِيقِ، وَيَحْرُمُ صَوْمُ النِّصْفِ الْأَخِيرِ مِنْ شَعْبَانَ إِلَّا لِنَذْرٍ أَوْ قَضَاءٍ أَوْ كَفَّارَةٍ أَوْ وِرْدٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَحْرُمُand it is prohibited
  • صَوْمُ الْعِيدَيْنِfasting the two Eids
  • وَأَيَّامِ التَّشْرِيقِand the Days of Tashriq
  • النِّصْفِ الْأَخِيرِ مِنْ شَعْبَانَthe latter half of Sha'ban
  • إِلَّا لِنَذْرٍexcept for a vow
  • أَوْ قَضَاءٍor a make-up
  • أَوْ كَفَّارَةٍor an expiation
  • أَوْ وِرْدٍor a regular practice
Translation
Fasting the two Eids and the Days of Tashriq is prohibited, and fasting the latter half of Sha'ban is prohibited except for a vow, a make-up, an expiation, or a regular practice.
Commentary

A day that cannot receive a fast is itself a condition: a fast on such a day is invalid, not merely sinful. The Days of Tashriq are the three days after the Day of Sacrifice (the 10th of Dhu al-Hijjah). Fasting the Eids and the Days of Tashriq is prohibited and invalid, for pilgrims and everyone else. The Day of Doubt was also mentioned among the prohibited days.

The latter half of Sha'ban begins on the 16th. Fasting in it is allowed for:

  • A make-up of Ramadan, or of a voluntary fast he began and broke (recommended).
  • An expiation, such as for an oath, zihar, or intercourse in Ramadan.
  • A vow made before, not a new vow made once the latter half has begun.
  • A regular practice (wird), such as Mondays and Thursdays, or every other day.
  • Continuing a fast begun before the 16th: fasting from the 10th through the 15th and carrying on.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the Eids and the Days of Tashriq cannot receive a fast, and that fasting from the 16th of Sha'ban depends on whether it is a make-up, an expiation, a prior vow, a regular practice, or a continuation.

(1)

Questions and Answers

Question: What is the difference between a regular practice and a vow?

Answer: A regular practice is a habit, such as fasting Mondays and Thursdays. A vow is an obligation he places on himself, such as vowing to fast every Monday. Both allow fasting in the latter half of Sha'ban, if they came first.
