Upon Whom Jumu'ah Is Obligatory, and the Excuses مَنْ تَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ الْجُمُعَةُ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِيمَنْ تَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ الْجُمُعَةُ]
تَجِبُ الْجُمُعَةُ عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ مُكَلَّفٍ ذَكَرٍ حُرٍّ مُقِيمٍ بِلَا مَرَضٍ، وَيُعْذَرُ فِي تَرْكِ الْجُمُعَةِ وَالْجَمَاعَةِ عِنْدَ الْمَطَرِ، وَالْمَرَضِ، وَالتَّمْرِيضِ، وَإِشْرَافِ الْقَرِيبِ وَنَحْوِهِ عَلَى الْمَوْتِ، وَالْخَوْفِ عَلَى نَفْسِهِ أَوْ مَالِهِ أَوْ عِرْضِهِ، وَمُدَافَعَةِ الْحَدَثِ مَعَ سَعَةِ الْوَقْتِ، وَشِدَّةِ الْجُوعِ، وَشِدَّةِ الْعَطَشِ، وَشِدَّةِ الْحَرِّ وَالْبَرْدِ، وَشِدَّةِ الرِّيحِ بِاللَّيْلِ، وَشِدَّةِ الْوَحَلِ، وَسَفَرِ الرُّفْقَةِ.

Translation
A section on whom Jumu'ah is obligatory upon. Jumu'ah is obligatory on every Muslim who is legally responsible, male, free, resident, and without illness. One is excused from Jumu'ah and congregation in rain; illness; nursing the sick; a relative or the like being near death; fear for one's self, property, or honor; holding back the call of nature while time is ample; severe hunger; severe thirst; severe heat or cold; severe wind at night; deep mud; and one's traveling party setting off.
Commentary

Jumu'ah is a prayer in its own right, not Dhuhr shortened to two. It was prescribed in Makkah, but the Prophet ﷺ did not establish it there, because the call was hidden and Jumu'ah needs public assembly with an imam, a preacher, and a congregation.

"Legally responsible" means an adult of sound mind. It is not obligatory on a non-Muslim, a child, the insane, a woman, a slave, a traveler, or one whose illness makes attending hard. A mukatab, a slave paying for his freedom, stays a slave while anything remains: "a slave is a slave as long as a dirham remains."

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that Jumu'ah is its own prayer, and that being obliged to attend, having a valid Jumu'ah, and counting toward the forty are three different things. A woman or a slave is not obliged, yet their Jumu'ah is valid if they pray it.

The Excuses

The principle is hardship. Naming a condition is not enough if it causes no real difficulty.

ExcuseWhen it applies
RainRain that soaks one's clothes, with no cover (kinn) on the way; not light rain or a covered route
IllnessOnly illness that makes attending hard; a mild headache does not
Nursing the sickSuch as a doctor whose patients would be endangered if he left; preserving life comes first
Someone near deathBeing with a spouse, parent, relative, neighbor, or friend as death approaches
FearFor one's self, property, honor, or a thing one has a special right to though it may not be sellable, such as a dog
Holding back the call of natureWind, diarrhea, repeated need, while the time is ample
Severe hunger or thirstWhile time is ample: eat or drink enough to relieve it, not to fullness (a third for food, a third for drink, a third for breath)
Severe heat or coldAny season; the Shaykh did not restrict heat to summer or cold to winter
Severe windAt night (sunset to dawn), not by day
Deep mudNight or day
The traveling party leavingIf being left behind brings hardship, fear, or loneliness; otherwise attending is better

Where the excuse depends on ample time, it ends once only enough time remains for the prayer itself: then one prays, even with the need.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that hardship is what makes something an excuse, and that the excuses of bodily need, hunger, and thirst never justify letting the prayer's time run out.
