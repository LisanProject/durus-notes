The Obligations of Wudu فُرُوضُ الْوُضُوءِ

Before Starting: The Five Legal Rulings

Before reading the obligations of wudu, Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha reviewed the five legal rulings, since every act discussed in fiqh falls under one of them.

Fard / Wajib (Obligatory). Must be done. Leaving one of the obligations of wudu invalidates it.

Haram (Prohibited). Must not be done.

Mandub / Sunnah / Mustahabb (Recommended). Better to do, but not obligatory. Examples in wudu are wiping the ears, rinsing the mouth, and sniffing water into the nose.

Makruh (Disliked). Better to leave, but not prohibited.

Mubah (Permissible). May be done or left. Eating, drinking, and sleeping are normally permissible.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha warns that people often hear the word makruh and assume it means the act is simply fine. It does not. A recommended act is better to do, and a disliked act is better to leave.

(2)

Intention transforms the permissible. A good intention can turn an ordinary permissible act into a rewarded, recommended one. Sleeping in order to gain strength for worship, study, and obedience becomes an act of reward.

(3)

From the questions in class:

  • Eating garlic or onion is permissible in itself. It becomes disliked when a person intends to go to the mosque, because the smell harms others. The dislike is for an outside reason, not because of the food itself.
  • Examples of acts disliked in themselves are plucking gray hair and leaving a sunnah of wudu or prayer. The Shaykh also mentioned that shaving the beard is considered makruh in the Shafi'i school.

The Six Obligations at a Glance

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي فُرُوضِ الْوُضُوءِ
فُرُوضُ الْوُضُوءِ سِتَّةُ أَشْيَاءَ:
الْأَوَّلُ: النِّيَّةُ، إِمَّا نِيَّةُ رَفْعِ الْحَدَثِ، أَوِ الطَّهَارَةِ لِلصَّلَاةِ، أَوِ الْوُضُوءِ، وَتَكُونُ هَذِهِ عِنْدَ غَسْلِ الْوَجْهِ.
الثَّانِي: غَسْلُ الْوَجْهِ جَمِيعِهِ شَعْرًا وَبَشَرًا، إِلَّا بَاطِنَ اللِّحْيَةِ الْكَثِيفَةِ وَالْعَارِضَيْنِ الْكَثِيفَيْنِ.
الثَّالِثُ: غَسْلُ الْيَدَيْنِ مَعَ الْمِرْفَقَيْنِ.
الرَّابِعُ: مَسْحُ شَيْءٍ مِنْ بَشَرَةِ الرَّأْسِ أَوْ شَعْرِهِ، وَلَوْ بَعْضَ شَعْرَةٍ.
الْخَامِسُ: غَسْلُ رِجْلَيْهِ مَعَ الْكَعْبَيْنِ.
السَّادِسُ: التَّرْتِيبُ هَكَذَا.
Translation
A section on the obligations of wudu. The obligations of wudu are six. First, the intention: either the intention of removing hadath, or of purification for prayer, or of wudu, and this is made at the washing of the face. Second, washing the entire face, hair and skin, except the inside of a dense beard and of dense side-whiskers. Third, washing the arms along with the elbows. Fourth, wiping some part of the skin of the head or its hair, even part of a single hair. Fifth, washing the feet along with the ankles. Sixth, observing this order.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha tied the six obligations to the verse of wudu in Surat al-Ma'idah:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ
O you who believe, when you rise to prayer, wash your faces and your arms up to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet up to the ankles. (al-Ma'idah 5:6)

The intention is taken from "when you rise to prayer", meaning when you intend to pray. Then come washing the face, washing the arms to the elbows, wiping the head, and washing the feet. The order is taken from the sequence in which the verse presents them.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that omitting any one of the six obligations invalidates the wudu. If a person completes his wudu but forgets to wipe his head, he has no wudu.

(4)

Order (Tartib) is obligatory in the Shafi'i school. Other schools do not make it obligatory, which is why it appears in this list but not in every school's list.

(5)

The First Obligation: The Intention

الْأَوَّلُ: النِّيَّةُ، إِمَّا نِيَّةُ رَفْعِ الْحَدَثِ، أَوِ الطَّهَارَةِ لِلصَّلَاةِ، أَوِ الْوُضُوءِ، وَتَكُونُ هَذِهِ عِنْدَ غَسْلِ الْوَجْهِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْأَوَّلُthe first
  • النِّيَّةُthe intention, linguistically aim or purpose
  • إِمَّاeither
  • نِيَّةُintention of
  • رَفْعِremoving, lifting
  • الْحَدَثِthe state of ritual impurity
  • أَوِor
  • الطَّهَارَةِpurification
  • لِلصَّلَاةِfor prayer
  • الْوُضُوءِwudu
  • وَتَكُونُand it is
  • هَذِهِthis (intention)
  • عِنْدَat, at the moment of
  • غَسْلِwashing
  • الْوَجْهِthe face
Translation
The first is the intention: either the intention of removing hadath, or of purification for prayer, or of wudu, and this is made at the washing of the face.
Commentary

In ordinary language, niyyah is an aim or purpose, and a person can intend today to do something tomorrow. In fiqh, the intention must be joined to the act at the legally relevant moment. In wudu, that moment is the beginning of washing the face. The intention should be present from the start of wudu and still be present when the washing of the face begins.

Any one of three intentions suffices: removing hadath, purification for prayer, or simply wudu.

The intention is what separates worship from habit. A person who washes his face, arms, and feet on a hot day to cool down, without intending wudu, has not performed wudu, even though the actions look identical.

Among the conditions for a valid intention, the Shaykh listed Islam, discernment (tamyiz), sanity, and knowing what is being intended. In some acts the intention may come before the act itself, as in fasting, where making the intention exactly at the start of the fast would be impractical, so intending in advance is permitted.

Niyyah (Intention). Linguistically, aim or purpose. In fiqh, an intention joined to the act at the legally relevant moment.

(6)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the wudu intention is tied to the first moment of washing the face. If someone intended wudu at the outset but the intention was gone by the time he began washing his face, his wudu is not valid.

(7)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the intention is what distinguishes worship from habit. The same washing can be an act of worship or merely cooling off, depending only on the intention.

(8)

The Second Obligation: Washing the Face

الثَّانِي: غَسْلُ الْوَجْهِ جَمِيعِهِ شَعْرًا وَبَشَرًا، إِلَّا بَاطِنَ اللِّحْيَةِ الْكَثِيفَةِ وَالْعَارِضَيْنِ الْكَثِيفَيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّانِيthe second
  • غَسْلُwashing
  • الْوَجْهِthe face
  • جَمِيعِهِall of it, the entire face
  • شَعْرًاhair
  • وَبَشَرًاand skin
  • إِلَّاexcept
  • بَاطِنَthe inside, the inner part
  • اللِّحْيَةِthe beard
  • الْكَثِيفَةِdense, so thick the skin beneath cannot be seen
  • وَالْعَارِضَيْنِand the two side-whiskers, the hair on the sides of the face
  • الْكَثِيفَيْنِthe two dense ones
Translation
The second is washing the entire face, hair and skin, except the inside of a dense beard and of dense side-whiskers.
Commentary

The whole face must be washed, both the hair growing on it and the skin. The hair of the face has specific names, including the eyebrow, the moustache, the beard, and the hair on the sides of the face called the 'arid.

  • A light beard, through which the skin can be seen, must be washed so that water reaches both the hair and the skin beneath it.
  • For a man with a dense beard, only the outer surface must be washed. Passing wet fingers through it (takhlil) so the water reaches inside is recommended.
  • For a woman, or a khuntha, both the outside and inside of beard hair must be washed, even if dense. It is recommended for a woman to remove such hair.

Dense Beard (Lihyah Kathifah). A beard through which the skin cannot be seen.

Takhlil. Passing the fingers through the beard so that water reaches within it. Recommended for a man's dense beard.

(9)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a beard is "dense" based on whether the skin can be seen through it, not on its length or overall size. A long but thin beard is still a light beard, and the skin beneath it must be reached.

(10)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the exception for the inside of a dense beard applies to men. For a woman or a khuntha, the outer and inner parts must both be washed.

(11)

The Third Obligation: Washing the Arms with the Elbows

الثَّالِثُ: غَسْلُ الْيَدَيْنِ مَعَ الْمِرْفَقَيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّالِثُthe third
  • غَسْلُwashing
  • الْيَدَيْنِthe two hands, meaning here the arms
  • مَعَtogether with, so the elbows are included
  • الْمِرْفَقَيْنِthe two elbows; both mirfaq and marfiq are correct
Translation
The third is washing the arms together with the elbows.
Commentary

The verse says to wash the hands "ila al-marafiq", up to the elbows. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that "ila" here is understood as including the elbows, not stopping just before them. The author's wording "ma'a", together with, makes this explicit. The verse uses the plural marafiq because it addresses a group, just as telling a group to wash their hands refers to each person's two hands.

The word yad (hand) covers different extents of the arm depending on context. In wudu it is washed to the elbows. In the punishment for theft, it refers only to the hand up to the wrist. The Shaykh named the wrist and the forearm as distinct parts of the arm.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the elbows themselves must be washed. Stopping the water just short of the elbow leaves part of an obligatory limb unwashed.

(12)

The Fourth Obligation: Wiping Part of the Head

الرَّابِعُ: مَسْحُ شَيْءٍ مِنْ بَشَرَةِ الرَّأْسِ أَوْ شَعْرِهِ، وَلَوْ بَعْضَ شَعْرَةٍ.
Vocabulary
  • الرَّابِعُthe fourth
  • مَسْحُwiping, passing moisture over a surface, as opposed to washing
  • شَيْءٍsomething, at least a portion
  • مِنْof
  • بَشَرَةِthe skin
  • الرَّأْسِthe head
  • أَوْor
  • شَعْرِهِits hair
  • وَلَوْeven if
  • بَعْضَpart of
  • شَعْرَةٍa single hair
Translation
The fourth is wiping some part of the skin of the head or its hair, even part of a single hair.
Commentary

Wiping (mash) is different from washing (ghasl). Washing means water flows over the limb; wiping means passing moisture over it. The minimum obligation is that moisture reaches some part of the skin of the head, or some hair that lies within the boundary of the head. The example of a single point is only to show the minimum, not to discourage the sunnah of wiping the whole head. The water may reach the area by the hand or by any other means.

  • A person with long hair cannot pull a strand outward and wipe only the part hanging beyond the head. That part lies outside the boundary of the head and does not count.
  • A bald person wipes the skin of the head, since the text refers to the head itself, not only to its hair.

The Shaykh contrasted this with the prohibition of removing hair in ihram. For wudu, what matters is whether the wiped hair is within the boundary of the head. For ihram, what matters is the hair itself, so cutting a strand even where it hangs outside the head is still removing hair and requires a fidyah. Asked whether this invalidates the Hajj or Umrah, he answered that it does not. It is a prohibition of ihram that carries a fidyah, not a matter of validity.

A man in ihram may not cover his head with a turban, and wiping over a turban is not valid when wearing it is itself prohibited. In general, wiping over a turban is conditional on the turban not being worn sinfully.

Mash (Wiping). Passing moisture over a surface, as distinct from washing it.

Hadd al-Ra's (Boundary of the Head). The area belonging to the head. Hair that extends beyond it does not count for the obligatory wipe.

Fidyah. A compensatory offering owed for committing certain prohibitions of ihram, such as removing hair.

(13)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the wudu wipe and the ihram prohibition look at different things. In wudu, a hanging strand beyond the head does not count. In ihram, cutting that same strand still counts as removing hair. Same hair, different rulings, because each ruling is tied to a different description.

(14)

The Fifth Obligation: Washing the Feet with the Ankles

الْخَامِسُ: غَسْلُ رِجْلَيْهِ مَعَ الْكَعْبَيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْخَامِسُthe fifth
  • غَسْلُwashing
  • رِجْلَيْهِhis two feet
  • مَعَtogether with, so the ankles are included
  • الْكَعْبَيْنِthe two ankles, the protruding bones at either side of the foot
Translation
The fifth is washing the feet together with the ankles.
Commentary

The ankles must be washed. The verse's "ila al-ka'bayn", up to the ankles, includes the ankles, exactly as "ila al-marafiq" includes the elbows.

The Sixth Obligation: Order

السَّادِسُ: التَّرْتِيبُ هَكَذَا.
Vocabulary
  • السَّادِسُthe sixth
  • التَّرْتِيبُorder, sequence
  • هَكَذَاlike this, in the order just listed
Translation
The sixth is observing this order.
Commentary

The required order is the face, then the arms, then wiping the head, then the feet. It is not valid to bring one obligatory act ahead of another. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha gave two proofs. First, the Prophet never performed wudu except in this order. Second, the verse places the wiping of the head in between the washed limbs. If order did not matter, the natural wording would group the three washed limbs together and mention the wiped one separately. Breaking that grouping to put the head in its place shows the sequence is intended.

One exception: if a person fully submerges in water, such as in the sea, and intends wudu while submerged, his wudu is valid. Ordering the limbs is impossible when the whole body is underwater at once, so the order is considered to have occurred in a legally recognized way.

Tartib (Order). Performing the obligatory acts of wudu in their prescribed sequence: face, arms, head, feet.

(15)

Doubts During and After Wudu

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha then addressed what to do when a person doubts.

Doubt about a limb after finishing. The wudu remains valid and does not need to be repeated.

Doubt about the intention after finishing. The wudu is invalid, because the intention is the foundation of the whole act.

Doubt about a limb during wudu. Go back to the doubtful limb, wash it, then redo what comes after it in order. If while wiping the head he doubts whether he washed his face, he washes the face, then the arms, then wipes the head. If while washing the feet he doubts the arms, he washes the arms and continues from there; the face is not repeated.

(16)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the difference between doubting a limb and doubting the intention once wudu is finished. The first is ignored. The second invalidates the wudu, because without intention there was no act of worship to begin with.

(17)
