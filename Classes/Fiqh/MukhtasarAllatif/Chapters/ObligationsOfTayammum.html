The Obligations of Tayammum فُرُوضُ التَّيَمُّمِ

The Five Obligations at a Glance

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي فُرُوضِ التَّيَمُّمِ
فُرُوضُ التَّيَمُّمِ خَمْسَةٌ:
الْأَوَّلُ: نَقْلُ التُّرَابِ.
الثَّانِي: النِّيَّةُ، وَهُوَ نِيَّةُ اسْتِبَاحَةِ الصَّلَاةِ، فَإِنْ كَانَ فَرْضًا.. نَوَى اسْتِبَاحَةَ فَرْضِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَيَجِبُ قَرْنُهَا بِوَضْعِ الْيَدِ عَلَى التُّرَابِ، وَاسْتِدَامَتُهَا إِلَى مَسْحِ شَيْءٍ مِنَ الْوَجْهِ.
الثَّالِثُ: مَسْحُ الْوَجْهِ.
الرَّابِعُ: مَسْحُ الْيَدَيْنِ مَعَ الْمِرْفَقَيْنِ.
الْخَامِسُ: التَّرْتِيبُ هَكَذَا.
Translation
A section on the obligations of tayammum. The obligations of tayammum are five. First, transferring the earth. Second, the intention, which is the intention to make prayer permissible; if it is an obligatory prayer, he intends to make the obligatory prayer permissible. It must be joined to placing the hand on the earth and continue until wiping some of the face. Third, wiping the face. Fourth, wiping the arms with the elbows. Fifth, observing this order.

The First Obligation: Transferring the Earth

الْأَوَّلُ: نَقْلُ التُّرَابِ.
Vocabulary
  • الْأَوَّلُthe first
  • نَقْلُtransferring, moving
  • التُّرَابِthe earth, dust
The first: transferring the earth.

The earth is moved from where it lies to the face and hands. The person may do this himself, or someone else may do it for him.

The Second Obligation: The Intention

الثَّانِي: النِّيَّةُ، وَهُوَ نِيَّةُ اسْتِبَاحَةِ الصَّلَاةِ، فَإِنْ كَانَ فَرْضًا.. نَوَى اسْتِبَاحَةَ فَرْضِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَيَجِبُ قَرْنُهَا بِوَضْعِ الْيَدِ عَلَى التُّرَابِ، وَاسْتِدَامَتُهَا إِلَى مَسْحِ شَيْءٍ مِنَ الْوَجْهِ.

Translation
The second: the intention, which is the intention to make prayer permissible. If it is obligatory, he intends to make the obligatory prayer permissible. It must be joined to placing the hand on the earth and kept until wiping some of the face.
Commentary

Tayammum is worship and needs an intention. The intention is to make worship permissible, not to lift the hadath. Tayammum does not lift hadath; only water does. It permits certain acts while the hadath remains.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha set out three levels of intention:

Level 1. Permission for an obligatory prayer, including a prayer made obligatory by a vow.

Level 2. Permission for a voluntary prayer, or for "prayer" unspecified. The funeral prayer belongs here.

Level 3. Permission for acts other than prayer, such as touching or carrying the mushaf, reciting Quran, or the prostration of recitation.

(1)

The rule: intention does not climb upward. Intending a level permits that level and those below it, never a level above. Intending Dhuhr permits one obligatory prayer, plus voluntary prayers and level three acts. Intending Duha permits voluntary prayers and level three, but no obligatory prayer. Intending only touching the mushaf permits that level alone. Intending an obligatory prayer permits the funeral prayer, but intending the funeral prayer does not permit an obligatory one.

The intention is made when the hands are placed on the earth and must be present when wiping of the face begins. If it lapses briefly in between, there is disagreement between Ibn Hajar and al-Ramli; the easier position, which the Shaykh attributed to al-Rabi', counts the tayammum as valid as long as the intention was there at the strike and at the start of wiping the face.

In wudu, the intention must continue throughout; an interruption invalidates what was washed during it. If someone apostatizes in the middle of wudu and returns to Islam, he renews the intention. A completed wudu is not invalidated by later apostasy, but tayammum is, since it is a weaker purification.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the rule of levels: intention reaches down, never up. An intention for a voluntary prayer can never permit an obligatory one.

(2)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the intention of tayammum is to make prayer permissible, not to lift hadath, because tayammum does not lift it.

(3)

The Third Obligation: Wiping the Face

الثَّالِثُ: مَسْحُ الْوَجْهِ.
Vocabulary
  • الثَّالِثُthe third
  • مَسْحُwiping
  • الْوَجْهِthe face
The third: wiping the face.

In wudu, water must reach under light hair. In tayammum, getting earth beneath any hair is neither obligatory nor recommended, because it would be a hardship; wiping the face is enough. But one must attend to spots an ordinary wipe can miss, which the Shaykh showed with his fingers: the inner corner of the eye by the nose (al-ma'q) and the outer corner by the ear (al-lihaz). These terms appear in the chapters of wudu, ghusl, and tayammum.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that in tayammum the earth need not reach beneath the hair, but the corners of the eyes and similar spots must not be missed.

(4)

The Fourth Obligation: Wiping the Arms with the Elbows

الرَّابِعُ: مَسْحُ الْيَدَيْنِ مَعَ الْمِرْفَقَيْنِ.
Vocabulary
  • الرَّابِعُthe fourth
  • مَسْحُwiping
  • الْيَدَيْنِthe two arms
  • مَعَwith
  • الْمِرْفَقَيْنِthe two elbows
The fourth: wiping the arms with the elbows.

The relied-upon Shafi'i position is to wipe up to and including the elbows. It matches the apparent wording of the wudu verse, is supported by reports, and is the cautious course. Wiping only to the wrists is the position of other schools and an earlier Shafi'i position, and Imam al-Nawawi chose it; reports exist for it as well, hence the disagreement. Wiping to the elbows satisfies both views, while wiping to the wrists does not satisfy the relied-upon one. The Shaykh compared it to bringing an extra hundred dollars when buying a thousand-dollar phone, just in case.

Asked why al-Nawawi's choice can differ from the relied-upon position: al-Nawawi was a jurist able to weigh positions and prefer among them. His personal preference can differ from the position he himself identifies as the relied-upon one. Al-Rafi'i and al-Nawawi together clarified which positions are relied upon in the school.

Al-Mu'tamad (The Relied-Upon Position). The position treated as authoritative in the school, which can differ from a great scholar's personal preference.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes wiping to the elbows: it is the relied-upon position and it covers the shorter view as well.

(6)

The Fifth Obligation: Order

الْخَامِسُ: التَّرْتِيبُ هَكَذَا.
Vocabulary
  • الْخَامِسُthe fifth
  • التَّرْتِيبُorder
  • هَكَذَاin this way
The fifth: observing this order.

The face comes first, then the arms. If someone wipes the arms before the face, the wiping of the face still counts, but the arms must be wiped again after it.
