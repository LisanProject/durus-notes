Burial and Mourning الدَّفْنُ

The Text

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي كَيْفِيَّةِ الدَّفْنِ]
وَأَمَّا الدَّفْنُ.. فَأَقَلُّهُ: حُفْرَةٌ تَكْتُمُ رَائِحَتَهُ، وَتَحْرُسُهُ مِنَ السِّبَاعِ، وَيَجِبُ تَوْجِيهُهُ لِلْقِبْلَةِ.
وَأَكْمَلُهُ: حُفْرَةٌ قَدْرَ قَامَةٍ وَبَسْطَةٍ، وَهُوَ: أَرْبَعَةُ أَذْرُعٍ وَنِصْفٌ.
وَيَحْرُمُ النَّدْبُ بِتَعْدِيدِ الشَّمَائِلِ؛ نَحْوَ: وَاسَيِّدَاهُ، وَاكَنْفَاهُ. وَيَحْرُمُ النَّوْحُ وَهُوَ: رَفْعُ الصَّوْتِ بِالنَّدْبِ. وَيَحْرُمُ الْجَزَعُ بِضَرْبِ الصَّدْرِ وَالْخَدِّ، وَنَشْرِ الشَّعْرِ، وَشَقِّ الْجَيْبِ، وَطَرْحِ الرَّمَادِ عَلَى الرَّأْسِ، وَنَحْوِ ذَلِكَ.

Translation
A section on how burial is done. As for burial, its minimum is a pit that conceals his smell and guards him from predators, and turning him toward the qiblah is obligatory. Its most complete is a pit the depth of a standing man with arms raised, which is four and a half cubits. Lamenting by listing his qualities, such as "O my master! O my shelter!", is prohibited. Wailing, which is raising the voice in lamentation, is prohibited. Anguished protest is prohibited: striking the chest and cheek, loosening the hair, tearing the neck of the garment, throwing ashes on the head, and the like.

The Grave

  • A dug pit is required. Laying the body on the ground and heaping soil over it does not suffice.
  • The qiblah: a Muslim must face it; if buried facing away, the grave is opened and he is turned. A non-Muslim need not face it.
  • The right side is recommended; laying him on the left is disliked.
  • The complete depth is a man standing with his arms raised, four and a half cubits; at about 48 cm a cubit, roughly two meters. It is used even for a child of one or two years.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the minimum grave is an actual dug pit that conceals the smell and protects from predators, and that a Muslim must face the qiblah, preferably on his right side.

Mourning

ActRuling
Crying without wailingPermitted
Mentioning his good qualities, without raised voice, crying out, or exaggerationNot prohibited
Nadb: listing his qualities with crying out, raised voice, or falsehoodProhibited
Nawh: raising the voice in lamentationProhibited, a major sin
Jaza': striking the chest or cheek, loosening the hair, tearing the garment's neck, ashes on the headProhibited, major sins

The "jayb" is the opening of the garment at the chest; tearing it is the gesture meant.

Before death, seeking medical treatment is permitted, and remembering death often, visiting the sick, repenting, and writing a will are recommended.

Questions and Answers

Question: Is the deceased punished for his family's wailing?

Answer: Not unless he instructed them to do it. The living answer for their own deeds, as Allah says:

وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ

"No bearer of burdens bears the burden of another."

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that wailing and the acts of anguished protest are major sins, and that the deceased is not punished for them unless he instructed them.
