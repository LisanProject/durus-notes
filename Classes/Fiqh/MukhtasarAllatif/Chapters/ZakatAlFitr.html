Zakat al-Fitr زَكَاةُ الْفِطْرِ

Who Owes It, and When

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي زَكَاةِ الْفِطْرِ]
وَتَجِبُ زَكَاةُ الْفِطْرِ بِغُرُوبِ الشَّمْسِ آخِرَ يَوْمٍ مِنْ رَمَضَانَ وَأَوَّلَ جُزْءٍ مِنْ شَوَّالٍ، إِذَا كَانَ حُرًّا؛ فَيَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ فِطْرَةُ نَفْسِهِ وَفِطْرَةُ مَنْ عَلَيْهِ مُؤْنَتُهُ مِنْ زَوْجَةٍ وَوَالِدٍ وَوَلَدٍ وَعَبْدٍ إِذَا كَانُوا مُسْلِمِينَ وَوَجَدَ مَا يُؤَدِّي عَنْهُمْ.
وَيَحْرُمُ تَأْخِيرُهَا عَنْ يَوْمِ الْعِيدِ، فَإِنْ أَخَّرَهَا.. أَثِمَ وَصَارَتْ قَضَاءً.
Vocabulary
  • بِغُرُوبِ الشَّمْسِwith the setting of the sun
  • آخِرَ يَوْمٍ مِنْ رَمَضَانَon the last day of Ramadan
  • وَأَوَّلَ جُزْءٍ مِنْ شَوَّالٍand the first moment of Shawwal
  • فِطْرَةُ نَفْسِهِhis own fitrah
  • مَنْ عَلَيْهِ مُؤْنَتُهُthose whose maintenance is on him
  • وَوَجَدَ مَا يُؤَدِّي عَنْهُمْand he has what to pay for them
  • وَيَحْرُمُ تَأْخِيرُهَاdelaying it is prohibited
  • أَثِمَhe has sinned
  • وَصَارَتْ قَضَاءًand it becomes a make-up
Translation
A section on zakat al-fitr. It becomes due at sunset on the last day of Ramadan and the first moment of Shawwal, if he is free. He owes his own fitrah and the fitrah of those whose maintenance he bears, such as a wife, parent, child, and slave, if they are Muslim and he has the means to pay for them. Delaying it past the day of Eid is prohibited; if he delays it, he has sinned, and it becomes a make-up.
Commentary

It goes by several names: zakat al-fitr, sadaqat al-fitr, the zakah of Ramadan, of fasting, al-fitrah, and the zakah of the body. Knowing them helps when reading other books.

One must be alive for part of Ramadan and part of Shawwal. Someone who dies before sunset on the last day owes none that year, and a child born after that sunset owes none.

TimeRuling
From the first day of RamadanPermitted to pay early
Sunset on the last day of RamadanIt becomes obligatory
After Fajr, before the Eid prayerBest
After the Eid prayer, through the end of Eid dayDisliked, but still on time
After Eid dayProhibited; it becomes a make-up

A delay without excuse is sinful and must be made up at once. A delay with an excuse, such as searching on Eid day and finding no poor recipient until the next day, or waiting for a needy relative who is away, is not sinful and need not be made up immediately. As with a missed prayer, the obligation never drops.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that fitrah becomes obligatory at sunset ending Ramadan, though it may be paid from the start of Ramadan, and that delaying it never cancels it: whether he sins and must hurry depends on whether he had an excuse.

(1)

Dependents, and the Order When Funds Fall Short

The rule: one pays the fitrah of every Muslim whose maintenance one is obliged to provide, such as a wife, young children, and needy parents. A wealthy parent or adult child who needs no maintenance pays his own. A master pays for his slave.

If he cannot cover everyone, he pays in this order:

  1. Himself
  2. His wife
  3. His young child
  4. His father
  5. His mother
  6. His adult child

With three sa', he pays for himself, his wife, and his young child.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that one pays fitrah for those whose maintenance one bears, and the order when funds are short: himself, wife, young child, father, mother, adult child.

(2)

The Poor Person, the Amount, and the Food

Text
وَلَا فِطْرَةَ عَلَى مُعْسِرٍ، وَهُوَ مَنْ لَا يَجِدُ شَيْئًا، أَوْ لَا يَجِدُ إِلَّا مَا يَكْفِيهِ لَيْلَةَ الْعِيدِ وَيَوْمَهَا، وَلَا يَجِبُ بَيْعُ مَسْكَنِهِ وَخَادِمٍ يَحْتَاجُ إِلَيْهِ.
وَهِيَ: صَاعٌ، وَالصَّاعُ أَرْبَعَةُ أَمْدَادٍ بِمُدِّ النَّبِيِّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ، وَالْمُدُّ: رِطْلٌ وَثُلُثٌ، وَلَا يُجْزِئُهُ إِلَّا الْكَيْلُ، وَلَا يُجْزِئُهُ إِلَّا مِنْ غَالِبِ قُوتِ الْبَلَدِ.
Vocabulary
  • مُعْسِرٍone in hardship
  • مَا يَكْفِيهِwhat suffices him
  • لَيْلَةَ الْعِيدِ وَيَوْمَهَاthe night of Eid and its day
  • بَيْعُ مَسْكَنِهِselling his home
  • وَخَادِمٍ يَحْتَاجُ إِلَيْهِand a servant he needs
  • صَاعٌa sa'
  • أَرْبَعَةُ أَمْدَادٍfour mudds
  • رِطْلٌ وَثُلُثٌa ratl and a third
  • الْكَيْلُmeasuring by volume
  • غَالِبِ قُوتِ الْبَلَدِthe predominant staple of the land
Translation
There is no fitrah on one in hardship: one who has nothing, or has only enough for the night of Eid and its day. He need not sell his home or a servant he needs. It is one sa', the sa' being four mudds by the mudd of the Prophet ﷺ, and the mudd a ratl and a third. Only measuring by volume suffices, and only from the predominant staple of the land.
Commentary
  • The amount: one sa' per person, four mudds; a mudd is about two cupped hands. About 2.75 kg is a rough modern figure only, since weight varies by food. Old units were given in the measures of their time, as two qullahs were given as 500 Baghdadi ratls.
  • The food: the staple of the payer's land, such as wheat in Egypt, rice in Indonesia and Malaysia. It is the land of the one paying that counts, not where the food is delivered. A land with no staple looks to the nearest land with one.
  • Substituting: a higher staple suffices for a lower one, never the reverse. A familiar ordering: wheat, then barley, then corn, then rice. Where rice is the staple, wheat suffices; where wheat is the staple, rice does not.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the amount is one sa' per person and kilogram conversions are only approximate, and that a higher-ranked staple suffices for a lower one, not the reverse.

(3)

Questions and Answers

Question: Can cash be given instead of food?

Answer: Not in the Shafi'i school. One may follow a school that permits paying the value, such as the Hanafi school, and that payment is valid by that school. One might arrange rice or wheat for families in need abroad, or give money by the Hanafi position. The Shaykh urged that this never become a cause of argument.

Question: A person owes for five people but has only three sa'. What does he do?

Answer: He pays for himself, his wife, and his young child, the first three in the order.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that his school requires food, that following a school that accepts the monetary value is permitted, and that the issue should never become a cause of dispute.
