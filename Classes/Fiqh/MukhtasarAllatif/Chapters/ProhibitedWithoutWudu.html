What Is Prohibited Without Wudu مَا يَحْرُمُ عَلَى الْمُحْدِثِ

Translation
A section on what is prohibited for one in a state of hadath. Whoever's wudu has been nullified is prohibited from prayer, tawaf, touching the mushaf and carrying it, and the tablet written for study. It is permitted to carry it among belongings and coins. It is lawful for a discerning child to carry it and touch it for study.
Commentary

The section heading is found in this edition but not in the Shaykh's own copy, and he suggested it was likely added by an editor.

What "Hadath" Means When Unqualified

Hadath is a non-visible, legally recognized state that prevents prayer. It can also be understood simply as the causes that end purification, covering both wudu and ghusl. The four nullifiers are causes of minor hadath.

When a book of fiqh says "hadath" without qualification, it ordinarily means minor hadath; an author says "major hadath" when he means it. A person's own intention is different: it means whatever he intends by it. After sleep, intending "to lift hadath" refers to the minor state. After a seminal emission, the same words intended for ghusl refer to the major state.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the difference between the language of the books and the language of intention. In the books, unqualified "hadath" means minor hadath. In a person's intention, it means whatever that person intends.

(1)

Prayer and Tawaf

The prohibition of prayer covers both obligatory and voluntary prayer. The prohibition of tawaf covers every kind of tawaf, whether obligatory, required, or recommended, since the Prophet said:

الطَّوَافُ بِالْبَيْتِ صَلَاةٌ، إِلَّا أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَحَلَّ فِيهِ الْكَلَامَ، فَمَنْ تَكَلَّمَ فَلَا يَتَكَلَّمْ إِلَّا بِخَيْرٍ
Tawaf around the House is prayer, except that Allah has permitted speech in it, so whoever speaks should speak only good. (al-Tirmidhi)

Tawaf itself is covered in the chapter on Hajj. A person with continuous hadath (da'im al-hadath) is an exception: he may pray after taking the necessary precautions to protect his clothing and place of prayer.

Da'im al-Hadath (Continuous Hadath). One whose hadath is ongoing and cannot be stopped. He prays after taking precautions rather than being barred from prayer.

(2)

Touching and Carrying the Mushaf

لَا يَمَسُّهُ إِلَّا الْمُطَهَّرُونَ
None touch it except the purified. (al-Waqi'ah 56:79)

In the interpretation the Shaykh presented, "it" refers to the Quran as the written mushaf, and "the purified" are those in a state of purity through wudu or ghusl. Other interpretations take "it" to be the Preserved Tablet and "the purified" to be the angels.

The prohibition includes the cover or binding while it is attached to the mushaf. Ibn Hajar permits touching a cover once it has been separated from the mushaf. Carrying is prohibited even more clearly than touching: if touching is prohibited, carrying is prohibited a fortiori.

The matn adds the tablet or page on which Quran is written for study, reading, memorization, or explanation. It takes the ruling of the mushaf. Writing made for a different purpose, such as an amulet, is treated differently. The mushaf itself keeps its ruling whatever the reason for carrying it, even if carried for protection.

Carrying It Among Belongings

When a mushaf is carried in a bag along with other things, the ruling depends on the intention.

Intending only the mushaf. Prohibited.

Intending only the belongings. Permitted.

Intending both. Permitted.

No particular intention. Permitted.

(3)

Money with Quranic writing on it, such as coins or bills engraved with a verse, may also be carried without wudu.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the four cases of intention. Of the four, only carrying the bag with the intention of carrying the mushaf alone is prohibited.

(4)

The Discerning Child

A discerning child may carry and touch the mushaf without wudu for the purpose of study. A discerning child (sabi mumayyiz) is one who eats, drinks, and cleans himself after the toilet on his own. The permission is limited to study.

A child who has not reached discernment must not be given the mushaf, and the parent or guardian is responsible for preventing it. Very young children, around one to four years old, are examples, but discernment itself is the criterion, not a fixed age.

Sabi Mumayyiz (Discerning Child). A child who can eat, drink, and clean himself after the toilet independently.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the child's permission is tied to study only, and that keeping the mushaf from a child without discernment is the guardian's responsibility.
