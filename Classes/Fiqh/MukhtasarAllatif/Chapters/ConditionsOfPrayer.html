The Conditions of Prayer شُرُوطُ الصَّلَاةِ

Conditions, Integrals, and Sunnahs

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha began by separating two kinds of condition. The previous section gave the conditions of obligation (shurut al-wujub): who prayer is due from. He added two more to the four in the matn, soundness of the senses and that the call of Islam has reached the person, making six. This section gives the conditions of validity (shurut al-sihhah): without them the prayer is not valid. One condition can be both. Islam is his example: prayer is not obligatory on an original non-Muslim, and no prayer is valid without Islam.

Shart (Condition). Comes before the act and continues through it. Covering the 'awrah is in place before the prayer begins and stays in place throughout; the time must have entered and still be running.

Rukn (Integral). Part of the act itself, so it cannot precede it. Reciting al-Fatihah only happens once one is inside the prayer.

(1)
Four categories of what belongs to prayer
  • Conditions (shurut)
  • Integrals or obligations (arkan, furud)
  • Sunnahs of ab'ad
  • Sunnahs of hay'at

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a condition precedes the prayer and continues through it, while a rukn is performed within it; and that conditions of obligation and conditions of validity are different, even when one condition, like Islam, serves as both.

(2)

The Six Conditions

Text
[فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ الصَّلَاةِ]
وَشُرُوطُ الصَّلَاةِ سِتَّةٌ:

Translation
A section on the conditions of prayer. The conditions of prayer are six: (1) knowing its time, as discussed; (2) facing the qiblah, except in a voluntary prayer while traveling; (3) covering the 'awrah; (4) purity from the two hadaths; (5) purity from najasah in the garment, the body, and the place; (6) knowing the obligations of prayer and its sunnahs.

1. Knowing the Time

This repeats the earlier section (see Determining the Time). The Shaykh gave three levels, in order:

LevelHow
1. Direct knowledgeSeeing the signs yourself (sunset, the zawal, true dawn, the red twilight disappearing), or a trustworthy informant, or the adhan of someone who knows the times.
2. IjtihadA reasoned estimate from a known interval: a lesson that usually lasts an hour after Maghrib, a stretch of work, a regular portion of recitation.
3. Following a mujtahidOnly for someone unable to do either of the above.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the levels are in order: one who can see the red twilight disappear may not estimate 'Isha by how many ajza' he has read, and one who can make ijtihad may not simply follow another's. Not every adhan counts; the caller must know the times.

(3)

2. Facing the Qiblah

The proof is the command to turn one's face toward al-Masjid al-Haram: since facing it is not required outside prayer, it is required within it. Someone who can see the Ka'bah faces it with certainty; someone far away faces its direction by a considered presumption, and phone apps can help. The qiblah extends above the Ka'bah and below it, so a person in an airplane, or even in space, faces what is aligned with it above, and a diver faces what corresponds to it below.

Two exceptions. (1) A voluntary prayer while traveling, the one the matn names. (2) Prayer in intense fear when a lawful battle is closely engaged, to be covered later. The first needs both qualifiers: voluntary and traveling. It does not cover any obligatory prayer, the funeral prayer included, or a voluntary prayer at home.

(4)

The travel here is not the long journey of more than about 85 km that allows shortening, combining, breaking the fast, and three days of wiping over khuffs. It is enough to pass about a mile beyond the built-up area of one's town, for instance leaving the edge of Seattle on a trip of about 20 km. The concession is for praying while moving, not for arriving somewhere and then praying without facing the qiblah.

TravelerWhat he does
Rider who can face the qiblah and bow and prostrate fullyMust do so. If a train has room to stand and face the qiblah, he prays fully.
Rider who cannotFaces the qiblah for the opening takbir if he can, then follows the direction of travel.
Walker"Face the qiblah in four, walk in four."

Face the qiblah in four: the opening takbir, ruku', sujud, and tashahhud.

Walk in four: standing and its recitation, standing up from ruku', sitting between the two prostrations, and the salam.

This is for someone who must keep moving. The Shaykh recalled a report of a man the Prophet ﷺ sent on a mission and taught to pray while walking, and noted it can still apply today: a vehicle that breaks down, or a long walk.

(5)

By the chest. Facing the qiblah is with the chest. Turning only the face is disliked but does not invalidate the prayer; turning the chest away does.

Unable to turn. A paralyzed patient with no one to turn him prays in the direction he lies, then repeats the prayer once he can face the qiblah.

(6)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that someone able to stand, bow, and prostrate must not pray an obligatory prayer seated with gestures on a train, a common mistake.

(7)

3. Covering the 'Awrah

وَعَوْرَةُ الرَّجُلِ وَالْأَمَةِ مَا بَيْنَ سُرَّتِهِمَا وَرُكْبَتِهِمَا.
وَعَوْرَةُ الْحُرَّةِ فِي الصَّلَاةِ وَعِنْدَ الْأَجَانِبِ جَمِيعُ بَدَنِهَا إِلَّا الْوَجْهَ وَالْكَفَّيْنِ.
وَعِنْدَ الْمَحَارِمِ مَا بَيْنَ السُّرَّةِ وَالرُّكْبَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَعَوْرَةُ الرَّجُلِand the 'awrah of a man
  • وَالْأَمَةِand of a slave woman
  • مَا بَيْنَis what is between
  • سُرَّتِهِمَاtheir navel
  • وَرُكْبَتِهِمَاand their knee
  • وَعَوْرَةُ الْحُرَّةِand the 'awrah of a free woman
  • فِي الصَّلَاةِin prayer
  • وَعِنْدَ الْأَجَانِبِand before non-mahram men
  • جَمِيعُ بَدَنِهَاis all of her body
  • إِلَّا الْوَجْهَ وَالْكَفَّيْنِexcept the face and the hands
  • وَعِنْدَ الْمَحَارِمِand before her mahrams
Translation
The 'awrah of a man and of a slave woman is what is between the navel and the knee. The 'awrah of a free woman in prayer and before non-mahram men is all of her body except the face and hands, and before her mahrams what is between the navel and the knee.
Commentary

The 'awrah differs by situation, and this section concerns prayer; the rulings outside prayer come in Matn Abi Shuja'. For a man, the navel and knees are not themselves 'awrah, but part of each must be covered to be sure the area between is covered, since what is needed to fulfill an obligation is itself obligatory. Shorts that end at the knee can expose skin above it in ruku' or sujud. This is the minimum for validity, not a recommendation to pray with shoulders or legs bare; good, covering clothing is better.

For a woman in prayer, everything but the face and hands is 'awrah. The Shaykh specifically warned that the wrist area (the kaw') is included, and if it shows, the prayer is invalid, so her clothing should stay in place as she moves.

Before mahrams. A mahram is a relative she may never marry, such as her brother or her paternal or maternal uncle. The legal limit before them is navel to knee, but the Shaykh firmly rejected reading that as permission to uncover casually. His example was a mother nursing her child while her brother is present: the books state the ruling, they do not encourage exposure.

The slave woman. The classical texts mention her, and the Shaykh said these rulings are still studied to understand the books. Her 'awrah in prayer is navel to knee; outside prayer he described it as like a free woman's.

(8)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a legal definition of 'awrah, especially before mahrams, is not an invitation to abandon modesty, and that the 'awrah of prayer must not be confused with the 'awrah outside it.

(9)

What Counts as a Covering

Four conditions of the covering (satir)
  • It has substance (jirm), a physical body, not just a color.
  • It is pure.
  • It hides the color of the skin.
  • It covers the whole required area.

Henna, dye, or paint on the skin is not a covering, since it has no substance of its own. A pure but see-through garment fails. A covering that meets the first three but leaves part exposed fails. It need not be ordinary clothing: pure, opaque mud counts, and the Shaykh gave murky water that hides the skin as a theoretical case, though someone in water may be unable to bow and prostrate, so it is only conceivable for something like the funeral prayer, which is all standing.

4. Purity From the Two Hadaths

Purity from both the minor and major hadath: with water if it can be used, otherwise by tayammum (see Tayammum).

Faqid al-Tahurayn (One Who Lacks Both Purifiers). Someone with neither water nor earth prays out of respect for the time, then purifies and repeats the prayer once water or earth becomes available.

(10)

5. Purity From Najasah in the Garment, Body, and Place

This concerns najasah that is not excused; the excused kinds come below.

Thawb (the garment) is wider than clothing: what you wear, what you carry, and what is connected to what you carry. A lab worker with a sample of blood or urine in his pocket; carrying a pure child whose diaper holds najasah; holding a rope tied to a dog. In each, the prayer is not valid.

Badan (the body) includes inside the mouth, nose, eyes, and ears. Residue in the mouth from wine, pork, or carrion must be removed; closing the mouth over it does not help.

Makan (the place) is what the body touches: where the feet stand and where the forehead, hands, knees, and feet rest in sujud. Najasah in front of you that you do not touch does not matter, nor does a spot on the prayer rug under your chest that your body does not touch.

(11)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that "thawb" here includes what is carried and what is connected to it, and that "place" means only what the body actually touches.

(12)

6. Knowing the Obligations and Sunnahs of Prayer

Everything in the prayer is sunnahNot valid
There are both, but he cannot tell which is whichThe jurists differ; for an untrained 'ami it is valid without dispute
He has studied the rulings of prayerHe must distinguish the obligatory from the sunnah

'Ami here means someone who has not learned to tell the obligations from the sunnahs, not simply anyone who is not a scholar.

(13)

Further Conditions

The Shaykh added, beyond the matn's six: Islam (a non-Muslim's prayer is not valid), tamyiz (the prayer of one without discernment is not valid), and avoiding what invalidates prayer (mubtilat), such as deliberate speech or eating, which will be covered later.

Excused Najasah

وَيُعْفَى عَنْ مَيْتَةٍ لَا نَفْسَ لَهَا سَائِلَةٌ، وَعَنْ وَنِيمِ الذُّبَابِ، وَعَنْ دَمِ الْبَرَاغِيثِ قَلِيلِهِ وَكَثِيرِهِ، وَعَنْ قَيْحِ الدَّمَامِيلِ وَصَدِيدِهَا، وَعَنِ الْقَلِيلِ مِنْ دَمِ الْأَجْنَبِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِ، إِلَّا الْكَلْبَ وَالْخِنْزِيرَ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيُعْفَى عَنْand there is excused
  • مَيْتَةٍa dead creature
  • لَا نَفْسَ لَهَا سَائِلَةٌthat has no flowing blood
  • وَعَنْ وَنِيمِ الذُّبَابِand fly droppings
  • وَعَنْ دَمِ الْبَرَاغِيثِand the blood of fleas
  • قَلِيلِهِ وَكَثِيرِهِlittle or much of it
  • وَعَنْ قَيْحِ الدَّمَامِيلِand the pus of boils
  • وَصَدِيدِهَاand their discharge
  • وَعَنِ الْقَلِيلِand a small amount
  • مِنْ دَمِ الْأَجْنَبِيِّof blood from another
  • وَغَيْرِهِand other than it
  • إِلَّا الْكَلْبَ وَالْخِنْزِيرَexcept the dog and the pig
Translation
Excused are: a dead creature that has no flowing blood, fly droppings, the blood of fleas whether little or much, the pus and discharge of boils, and a small amount of blood from another and the like, except from a dog or pig.
Commentary

Excused najasah may be on the body, the garment, or the place, with different details for each. Creatures without flowing blood are those like flies and ants. Small and large are judged by custom ('urf); a tiny speck is small. Blood needs the most care, since depending on its source and circumstances a large amount may be excused, only a small amount, or none at all.

BloodExcused
Your own, came out by itself, unmixed, still where it came out (e.g. nose, ear)Little or much
Your own, but it spread beyond its place (e.g. from the nose onto clothing)Only a little
Your own, but you made it come out (e.g. picking the nose in prayer)Only a little
From another (ajnabi): another person, man or woman, or an animalOnly a little, by custom
Mixed with a foreign substanceNone, little or much
From a dog or pigNone, not even a speck

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that blood cannot be reduced to one rule: its source, amount, whether it stayed in place, whether it mixed, and whether it came out deliberately all change the ruling. "Ajnabi" means anything other than the worshipper himself.
