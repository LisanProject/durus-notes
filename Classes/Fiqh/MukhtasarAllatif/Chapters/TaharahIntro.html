Chapter 2: Taharah بَابُ الطَّهَارَةِ

This chapter covers purification, the gateway to prayer. It begins with the legal definition of taharah and its four parts, then moves into wudu: its obligations, its sunnahs, what breaks it, and what becomes prohibited for one who has lost it. It continues with the etiquette of relieving oneself, ghusl, the conditions of purification and the kinds of water, the impurities and how they are removed, tayammum, and closes with menstruation and postnatal bleeding. Since prayer is not valid without purification, this is one of the chapters every Muslim is personally obligated to learn.

Here is a list of the sections covered in this chapter.

The Definition of Taharah covers the legal definition of purification, removing hadath, removing najasah, and what shares their meaning or form, with tayammum, istinja, and renewed wudu as examples.

The Obligations of Wudu covers the five legal rulings, the six obligations of wudu and their basis in Surat al-Ma'idah, the intention and when it must be made, and washing the face including the rulings on the beard.

The Sunnahs of Wudu covers the fifteen sunnahs listed in the matn, from the siwak and basmalah to continuity and not drying off, with when each one changes ruling and the single intention that covers them all.

The Nullifiers of Wudu covers the four things that break wudu, the sleep exception, contact between a man and a woman, who counts as a mahram, and touching the private part.

What Is Prohibited Without Wudu covers prayer, tawaf, touching and carrying the mushaf, the four cases of carrying it among belongings, and the discerning child.

The Etiquette of Relieving Oneself covers entering and leaving, where one should not relieve oneself and why, speaking, istibra', and the supplications on entering and leaving.

What Makes Ghusl Obligatory covers the five causes of ghusl and how to distinguish mani from madhy and wady.

The Obligations and Sunnahs of Ghusl covers the intention of ghusl and how it differs from wudu, washing every hair and all the skin, and the sunnahs of ghusl.

The Conditions of Purification covers what a condition is, Islam and discernment, the extra conditions added in class, and why only unrestricted water purifies.

Changed Water and Used Water covers water changed by pure substances, the changes that do no harm, used water and wash-water, and the intention to scoop.

When Water Becomes Impure covers small and large water, the measure of two qullahs, the impurities that are excused, and how impure water is purified.

The Impurities covers the definition of najasah and each impurity in the matn, from bodily wastes and intoxicants to carcasses, fluids, milk, and separated parts.

What Can Be Purified and What Cannot covers transformation and tanning, the three grades of impurity and how each is washed, impure liquids, and a first note on istinja.

Tayammum and When It Is Allowed covers the meaning of tayammum, when it is obligatory, the literal and legal absence of water, illness, and wounds and bandages.

The Obligations of Tayammum covers the five obligations, the three levels of intention, wiping the face and arms to the elbows, and order.

The Conditions of Tayammum covers the earth used, the two strikes, the time, renewing for each obligation, searching for water, lacking both water and earth, cold, and the sinful traveler.

Menstruation and Postnatal Bleeding covers the durations of menstruation, istihadah, what menstruation and janabah prohibit, making up fasts but not prayers, and the rulings of nifas.

Review of Bab al-Taharah gathers the whole book in one place for revision: the lists and numbers to memorize, key definitions, commonly confused rulings, recurring principles, and where the scholars differ.
