The Book, the Author, and the Science of Fiqh

The Book and Its Titles

الْمُخْتَصَرُ الصَّغِيرُ فِيمَا لَا بُدَّ لِكُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ مِنْ مَعْرِفَتِهِ فِي الْعِبَادَاتِ عَلَى مَذْهَبِ الْإِمَامِ الشَّافِعِيِّ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ
The Small Abridgment, on what every Muslim cannot do without knowing in matters of worship, according to the school of Imam al-Shafi'i, may Allah be pleased with him.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha opened the course by introducing the book, known both as al-Mukhtasar al-Latif (the Gentle Abridgment) and al-Mukhtasar al-Saghir (the Small Abridgment). The edition being studied is the matn alone, without a full commentary, though it carries footnotes. The book is devoted mainly to acts of worship and includes only a small portion of transactions.

One book, many titles. Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha pointed out that a single work is often known by several names, and a student should not be confused when the same book is cited under different titles. His example was Matn Abi Shuja, which is also called Ghayat al-Ikhtisar and Matn al-Ghayah wa al-Taqrib.

(1)

The Author

عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بَافَضْلٍ الْحَضْرَمِيُّ

The author is the jurist Abdullah ibn Abd al-Rahman Ba Fadl al-Hadrami, born in 850 AH and died in 918 AH, a lifespan of 68 years. Among his teachers were Ba Makhramah, a well known authority in Shafi'i jurisprudence, and Ibn Zahirah al-Makki.

Among his other works mentioned in class:

  • al-Mukhtasar al-Kabir, the larger abridgment, also called al-Muqaddimah al-Hadramiyyah and Masa'il al-Ta'lim
  • a book of supplications
  • a work on the rites of Hajj

The Hadrami "Ba". In Hadrami names, "Ba" is a shortened form corresponding to "Abu", as in Ba Fadl and Ba Alawi. So Ba Fadl is the family of Abu Fadl.

(2)

Understanding the Religion

مَنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ بِهِ خَيْرًا يُفَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّينِ
Vocabulary
  • مَنْwhoever
  • يُرِدِintends, wills
  • اللَّهُAllah
  • بِهِfor him
  • خَيْرًاgood, here meaning abundant or great good
  • يُفَقِّهْهُHe gives him understanding
  • فِيin
  • الدِّينِthe religion
Whoever Allah intends good for, He gives him understanding of the religion. (Bukhari and Muslim)
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that the good mentioned here is abundant, great good, and that the understanding of the religion in this hadith is broader than the technical discipline called fiqh. It includes understanding the religion as a whole.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the hadith is general. "Understanding the religion" here is not limited to the technical science of fiqh, so it should not be read as praise for jurists alone.

(3)

The Definition of Fiqh

الْفِقْهُ: الْعِلْمُ بِالْأَحْكَامِ الشَّرْعِيَّةِ الْعَمَلِيَّةِ الْمُكْتَسَبُ مِنْ أَدِلَّتِهَا التَّفْصِيلِيَّةِ
Vocabulary
  • الْفِقْهُfiqh, linguistically understanding
  • الْعِلْمُknowledge
  • بِالْأَحْكَامِof the rulings
  • الشَّرْعِيَّةِlegal, taken from the Sharia
  • الْعَمَلِيَّةِpractical, relating to action rather than belief
  • الْمُكْتَسَبُacquired, derived
  • مِنْfrom
  • أَدِلَّتِهَاtheir proofs
  • التَّفْصِيلِيَّةِdetailed, the specific evidence for each individual issue
Fiqh is knowledge of practical legal rulings, derived from their detailed proofs.
Commentary

In ordinary language fiqh simply means understanding. As a discipline, it is knowledge of practical legal rulings derived from their detailed proofs, which are the Quran, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogical reasoning (qiyas). Every individual issue in fiqh has its own evidence.

The word "practical" separates fiqh from creedal rulings, which concern belief in Allah, His attributes, the prophets, the angels, and what is necessary, possible, and impossible with respect to Allah. Those belong to the science of aqidah, not fiqh.

Fiqh. Linguistically, understanding. Technically, knowledge of practical legal rulings derived from their detailed proofs: the Quran, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy.

(4)

The Four Areas of Fiqh

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha outlined four broad areas that the discipline covers.

Acts of Worship ('Ibadat). Purification, prayer, zakah, fasting, and Hajj.

Transactions (Mu'amalat). Buying and selling, leasing, pledges, usury, and inheritance, which is also called fara'id.

Marriage and Related Matters (Munakahat). Marriage, divorce, waiting periods, financial maintenance, and khul'.

Criminal Law (Jinayat). Prescribed punishments, such as those relating to killing and theft, and the rulings on apostasy.

(5)

He also mentioned the classical chapters on manumission ('itq) and the umm al-walad, which address the historical institution of slavery and its legal rulings. Large, comprehensive fiqh works gather all of these areas together. Other works are devoted to a single subject, such as marriage, worship, inheritance, or menstruation and postnatal bleeding.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a student of knowledge studies the whole discipline, including chapters he may never personally need, such as the rulings of manumission. A chapter is not skipped just because it seems unlikely to come up in daily life.

(6)

Adab Before Reading the Text

قَالَ الْمُصَنِّفُ رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى
Vocabulary
  • قَالَsaid
  • الْمُصَنِّفُthe author or compiler
  • رَحِمَهُmay He have mercy on him
  • اللَّهُAllah
  • تَعَالَىthe Exalted
The author, may Allah the Exalted have mercy on him, said.
Commentary

Before the student read the author's text, Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha instructed him to begin with the basmalah, praise of Allah, and blessings on the Prophet, and then to say "the author, may Allah have mercy on him, said." The author taught beneficial knowledge, and the one who benefits from his knowledge should pray for him.

Musannif (Author). An author or compiler, from the verb sannafa, to classify or gather knowledge into categories.

(7)
