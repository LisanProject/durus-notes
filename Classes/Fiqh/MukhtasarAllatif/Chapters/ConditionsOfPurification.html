The Conditions of Purification شُرُوطُ الطَّهَارَةِ

What a Condition Is

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha began by defining a condition (shart) through the example of wudu and prayer. Wudu is a condition of prayer: without wudu there is no prayer. But having wudu does not mean one must pray; a person may make wudu simply to recite Quran. So the absence of a condition entails the absence of the thing, while its presence entails nothing either way.

Shart (Condition). That whose absence entails the absence of the thing, while its presence entails neither its presence nor its absence.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes this definition with the wudu example: wudu is required for prayer, but having wudu does not mean one has prayed or must pray.

(2)

The Conditions in the Matn

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي شُرُوطِ الطَّهَارَةِ مِنَ الْحَدَثَيْنِ
وَشُرُوطُ الطَّهَارَةِ عَنِ الْحَدَثِ الْأَصْغَرِ وَالْأَكْبَرِ: الْإِسْلَامُ. وَالتَّمْيِيزُ. وَالْمَاءُ الطَّاهِرُ الْمُطَهِّرُ.

  • وَشُرُوطُand the conditions of

  • الْأَصْغَرِminor
  • وَالْأَكْبَرِand major
  • الْإِسْلَامُIslam
  • وَالتَّمْيِيزُand discernment
  • وَالْمَاءُand water
  • الطَّاهِرُpure in itself
  • الْمُطَهِّرُpurifying others
Translation
A section on the conditions of purification from the two hadaths. The conditions of purification from the minor and major hadath are: Islam, discernment, and water that is pure and purifying.
Commentary

Wudu and ghusl are acts of worship, and worship requires intention. A valid intention requires Islam and discernment. So the ritual wudu or ghusl of a non-Muslim, or of a child who has not yet reached discernment, is not valid, because neither is able to make the required intention.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the chain of reasoning: purification is worship, worship needs intention, and intention needs Islam and discernment. That is why these two come first among the conditions.

(3)

Further Conditions Added in Class

The Shaykh noted that the conditions of purification are many and will be covered fully in a later book. He added several beyond those in the matn:

  • No menstruation or postnatal bleeding. A woman does not perform ritual purification from that state until the bleeding stops. Ordinary bathing, to wash away sweat for example, is not restricted.
  • The water must flow over the limb. Rubbing an ice cube over the body is not wudu; it is wiping, not washing. The ice must melt so that water runs over the limb.
  • Nothing on the limb that changes the water in a way that ruins it, such as powder that mixes with the water on contact.
  • No barrier that keeps water from reaching the skin.

A person with continuous hadath (da'im al-hadath), such as one who constantly passes urine with no break long enough to make wudu and pray, has two more conditions:

  • The prayer time must have entered before he makes wudu. Others may make wudu at eight in the morning for Dhuhr; he may not.
  • Continuity (muwalah). After cleaning himself, he makes wudu and then prays without undue delay, and washes the limbs of wudu one after another. For anyone else this continuity is only a sunnah.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that washing means water flowing over the limb. Wiping with unmelted ice or snow does not satisfy the obligation to wash.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the extra conditions of entering the time and continuity apply to the person with continuous hadath, not to everyone.

(5)

Only Unrestricted Water Purifies

فَلَا يَصِحُّ رَفْعُ الْحَدَثِ وَلَا إِزَالَةُ النَّجَسِ إِلَّا بِالْمَاءِ الْمُطْلَقِ، وَهُوَ: مَا نَزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ، أَوْ نَبَعَ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ.

  • أَوْor

  • مِنَ الْأَرْضِfrom the earth
Translation
So lifting hadath and removing impurity are only valid with unrestricted water, which is what descends from the sky or springs from the earth.
Commentary

The means of purification are four: water, earth (for tayammum), a stone or similar solid (for istinja), and tanning agents (for the hide of a dead animal). The book begins with water because it is the main means, and different waters have different rulings.

Water divides into three categories:

Tahur (Pure and Purifying). Pure in itself and purifies other things. Wudu and ghusl are valid with it. Also called unrestricted water (al-ma' al-mutlaq).

Tahir (Pure, Not Purifying). Pure in itself but does not purify. It cannot be used for wudu or ghusl, though it may be used for other things.

Najis (Impure). Water that has become impure, covered in the coming sections.

(6)

"Unrestricted" means it is called water without a qualifier it cannot do without. Watermelon water and rose water are named by their substance, so they are not unrestricted. River water or well water are named by a source, but that is not essential to calling them water, so they remain unrestricted.

The author's "what descends from the sky or springs from the earth" is an explanation by example, not the precise definition. From the sky: rain, snow, and hail, though snow must melt and flow over the limb. From the earth: rivers, seas, wells, and springs. Asked about coconut water, the Shaykh said it can be used if it is still unrestricted water, without a taste, color, or smell that makes it something other than water.

The evidence that purification is with water specifically is the verse "if you do not find water, then perform tayammum." If other liquids sufficed, the move to earth would not be tied to the absence of water. For removing impurity, the Prophet told the Companions to pour a bucket of water over the place where a Bedouin had urinated in the mosque.

Some purifying water is disliked to use even though purification with it is valid, such as sun-heated water and water that is extremely hot or cold. The conditions for this dislike come later.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that "unrestricted water" is the precise definition: water called simply "water". The description "from the sky or the earth" in the matn is an approximation by example.

(7)
