Changed Water and Used Water الْمَاءُ الْمُتَغَيِّرُ وَالْمُسْتَعْمَلُ

The second category of water, pure but not purifying, has two kinds: water changed by a pure substance mixed into it, and used water. This section covers both.

Water Changed by a Pure Substance

فَإِذَا تَغَيَّرَ طَعْمُ الْمَاءِ أَوْ لَوْنُهُ أَوْ رِيحُهُ تَغَيُّرًا فَاحِشًا بِمُخَالَطَةِ شَيْءٍ طَاهِرٍ يَسْتَغْنِي الْمَاءُ عَنْهُ؛ كَالزَّعْفَرَانِ وَالْأُشْنَانِ وَالْجِصِّ وَالنُّورَةِ وَالْكُحْلِ.. لَمْ تُجْزِ الطَّهَارَةُ بِهِ.
Vocabulary
  • فَإِذَاso if
  • تَغَيَّرَchanged
  • طَعْمُthe taste of
  • الْمَاءِthe water
  • أَوْor
  • لَوْنُهُits color
  • رِيحُهُits smell
  • تَغَيُّرًاa change
  • فَاحِشًاsubstantial, considerable
  • بِمُخَالَطَةِby mixing with
  • شَيْءٍsomething
  • طَاهِرٍpure
  • يَسْتَغْنِيcan do without
  • الْمَاءُ عَنْهُthe water, it (the water can be protected from it without hardship)
  • كَالزَّعْفَرَانِsuch as saffron
  • وَالْأُشْنَانِand ushnan, a plant used for washing like soap
  • وَالْجِصِّand gypsum, plaster
  • وَالنُّورَةِand lime
  • وَالْكُحْلِand kohl
  • لَمْ تُجْزِdoes not suffice
  • الطَّهَارَةُ بِهِpurification with it
Translation
If the taste, color, or smell of the water changes substantially by mixing with something pure that the water can be kept from, such as saffron, ushnan, gypsum, lime, or kohl, purification with it does not suffice.
Commentary

The Shaykh's example was soap falling into a container of water. For the change to take away the water's purifying status, all of these must hold:

1. In taste, color, or smell. A change in temperature alone does not count.

2. Substantial (fahish). A little tea that changes the water only slightly does not matter.

3. By a mukhalit. A substance that mixes in and cannot be told apart from the water by ordinary sight, like tea coloring it. People are judged by what is apparent, not by whether a laboratory could separate it.

4. Something the water can be kept from. Tea added to a cup could easily have been kept out. Leaves falling from an overhanging tree into a stream could not.

(1)

Once all four hold, the water is still pure but no longer purifies. "Lam tujzi" means purification with it is not valid and does not count.

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that mixing is judged by ordinary observation, not theoretical or laboratory separation.

(2)

Changes That Do Not Harm

وَلَا يَضُرُّ التَّغَيُّرُ بِالْمُكْثِ وَالتُّرَابِ وَالطُّحْلُبِ وَمَا فِي مَقَرِّهِ وَمَمَرِّهِ، وَلَا التَّغَيُّرُ بِالْمُجَاوَرَةِ؛ كَالْعُودِ وَالدُّهْنِ الْمُطَيِّبِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَضُرُّand it does not harm
  • التَّغَيُّرُthe change
  • بِالْمُكْثِby standing a long time
  • وَالتُّرَابِand soil
  • وَالطُّحْلُبِand algae, green growth in water
  • وَمَاand what is
  • فِي مَقَرِّهِin its resting place
  • وَمَمَرِّهِand its channel
  • بِالْمُجَاوَرَةِby an adjacent substance that stays distinct
  • كَالْعُودِsuch as oud wood
  • وَالدُّهْنِand oil
  • الْمُطَيِّبِscented
Translation
A change from standing a long time, from soil, from algae, or from what is in its resting place and channel does not harm, nor does a change from an adjacent substance, such as oud or scented oil.
Commentary
  • Standing (mukth). Water left in a bottle for two years may change, but this does not affect its status. The Shaykh added that it may still be wise not to use it if it could be harmful.
  • Soil. Cloudy or colored water is fine as long as it is still called water. If so much soil is added that it becomes mud, it is no longer water. Soil usually settles out.
  • Algae. Excused when it grows naturally in the water or its channel, but not if someone gathers it and throws it in.
  • Its resting place and channel. A well may give very salty, reddish, or yellowish water because of the earth around it. Water flowing through a new pipe may smell of it. Both are excused.
  • An adjacent substance (mujawir). Not merely something next to the water, but a substance that stays distinguishable from it, even if it affects the smell: iron placed in water and taken out, a bottle stored next to perfume, oud, or scented oil. This differs from tea, which mixes in and cannot be told apart.

Mukhalit (Mixing Substance). One that blends with the water and cannot be distinguished from it by ordinary sight. Its substantial change removes the water's purifying status.

Mujawir (Adjacent Substance). One that remains distinguishable from the water, even if it changes its smell. Its change is excused.

(3)

Used Water

وَلَا يَصِحُّ الطَّهَارَةُ بِمَا تُطُهِّرَ بِهِ مِنْ حَدَثٍ أَوْ نَجَسٍ.
Vocabulary
  • وَلَا يَصِحُّand it is not valid
  • الطَّهَارَةُpurification
  • بِمَاwith what
  • تُطُهِّرَ بِهِwas used to purify
  • مِنْfrom
  • حَدَثٍhadath
  • أَوْor
  • نَجَسٍphysical impurity
Translation
Purification is not valid with water already used to purify from hadath or impurity.
Commentary

Water used to lift hadath, through wudu or ghusl, counts as "used" (musta'mal) only under three conditions:

1. Used in an obligatory purification. Water from renewing wudu while already in wudu is not used water. In a wudu with three washings, only the first washing of an obligatory limb lifts the hadath; the second and third are sunnah.

2. A small quantity. A person in janabah who enters the sea intending ghusl does not make the sea used water. The measure of small and large is two qullahs.

3. Separated from the limb. While the water is still running over the face, it has not yet become used. Once it drips off, it has.

(4)

Water used to remove physical impurity is the issue the books call al-ghusalah, the wash-water. For the runoff from washing an impure item in a small amount of water to be pure:

1. The water is poured onto the item (warid). Putting the impure garment into a small vessel of water may make the water impure.

2. The place becomes pure. If the stain or smell is still on the garment, the runoff is not pure.

3. The runoff is unchanged. If its taste, color, or smell changed because of the impurity, it is impure.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that only the obligatory first washing produces used water. The water of the sunnah second and third washings, and of a renewed wudu, does not.

(6)

A Note: The Intention to Scoop

تَنْبِيهٌ: لَوْ أَدْخَلَ الْمُتَوَضِّئُ يَدَهُ بَعْدَ غَسْلِ وَجْهِهِ جَمِيعِهِ مَرَّةً، أَوِ الْجُنُبُ بَعْدَ النِّيَّةِ فِي مَاءٍ دُونَ الْقُلَّتَيْنِ، فَاغْتَرَفَ وَنَوَى الِاغْتِرَافَ.. لَمْ يَضُرَّ، وَإِنْ لَمْ يَنْوِ الِاغْتِرَافَ.. صَارَ الْبَاقِي مُسْتَعْمَلًا.
Vocabulary
  • تَنْبِيهٌa note, an alert
  • لَوْif
  • أَدْخَلَinserted
  • الْمُتَوَضِّئُthe one making wudu
  • يَدَهُhis hand
  • بَعْدَafter
  • غَسْلِwashing
  • وَجْهِهِhis face
  • جَمِيعِهِall of it
  • مَرَّةًonce
  • أَوِor
  • الْجُنُبُthe one in janabah
  • النِّيَّةِthe intention
  • فِي مَاءٍinto water
  • دُونَless than
  • الْقُلَّتَيْنِthe two qullahs
  • فَاغْتَرَفَand scooped
  • وَنَوَىand intended
  • الِاغْتِرَافَscooping
  • لَمْ يَضُرَّit does no harm
  • وَإِنْand if
  • لَمْ يَنْوِhe did not intend
  • صَارَbecame
  • الْبَاقِيthe remainder
  • مُسْتَعْمَلًاused
Translation
A note: if the one making wudu, after washing his whole face once, or the one in janabah after his intention, puts his hand into water of less than two qullahs and scoops, intending to scoop, it does no harm. If he does not intend scooping, the remaining water becomes used.
Commentary

Someone with a small vessel washes his face, then puts his hand back in to wash his arm. The hand is an obligatory limb, so if he puts it in to wash it, the water can become used. The solution is to intend, before putting the hand in, "I am taking water out", not "I am washing my hand in here".

For wudu, the time for this intention is after washing the whole face once. For ghusl, it comes right after the intention of ghusl. It only applies to small water, under two qullahs, not to the sea or other large bodies.

This is the relied-upon position. The Shaykh also reported that Imam al-Ghazali and Imam al-Baghawi held the intention to scoop is not obligatory, and that Bughyat al-Mustarshidin says the general public are not to be burdened with it because of its difficulty. He noted that this ruling mattered more when people made wudu from small vessels, but it is still to be studied.

Niyyat al-Ightiraf (Intention to Scoop). Intending to take water out of a small vessel, rather than to wash the limb inside it, so the remaining water does not become used.
