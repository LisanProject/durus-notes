Tayammum and When It Is Allowed التَّيَمُّمُ

When Tayammum Is Obligatory

Text
فَصْلٌ فِي التَّيَمُّمِ
وَيَجِبُ التَّيَمُّمُ عَنِ الْحَدَثِ الْأَصْغَرِ وَالْأَكْبَرِ عِنْدَ الْعَجْزِ عَنِ اسْتِعْمَالِ الْمَاءِ، بِسَبَبِ فَقْدِهِ فِي حَضَرٍ أَوْ سَفَرٍ، وَلِلْمَرِيضِ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَجِبُand is obligatory
  • التَّيَمُّمُtayammum; linguistically, aiming for something
  • عَنِin place of, for
  • الْحَدَثِhadath
  • الْأَصْغَرِminor
  • وَالْأَكْبَرِand major
  • عِنْدَupon, at the time of
  • الْعَجْزِinability
  • عَنِ اسْتِعْمَالِto use
  • الْمَاءِwater
  • بِسَبَبِbecause of
  • فَقْدِهِits absence
  • فِي حَضَرٍwhile resident
  • أَوْ سَفَرٍor traveling
  • وَلِلْمَرِيضِand for the sick
Translation
A section on tayammum. Tayammum is obligatory in place of the minor and major hadath when one is unable to use water, because it is absent, whether at home or traveling, and for the sick.
Commentary

Linguistically, tayammum means aiming for or heading toward something. Legally, it is conveying earth to the face and hands under specific conditions. It can stand in for wudu, ghusl, or washing a single limb, but never for removing physical impurity, which must still be removed. The Shaykh noted it is a gift particular to this ummah, legislated, he believed, in the fourth or sixth year after the Hijrah.

"Obligatory" is in relation to the prayer time. With time to spare, tayammum is permitted when its conditions hold; it becomes obligatory when only enough time remains to pray.

Absence of water can be literal, when there is none, or legal, when water exists but illness or danger prevents reaching or using it. Tayammum applies at home as well as on a journey; the verse mentions travel because that is when water is usually lacking.

Illness covers an existing illness as well as a real fear that water will cause illness, prolong it, or make it worse, or will harm life, a limb, or a limb's function such as sight or hearing. This fear can be established by a trustworthy doctor ('adl al-riwayah). Ibn Hajar allows relying on one's own past experience; where there is no such experience, there is disagreement.

Tayammum. Linguistically, aiming for something. Legally, conveying earth to the face and hands under specific conditions.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that tayammum replaces wudu, ghusl, or washing a limb, but never the removal of physical impurity.

(2)

Literal Absence. There is no water.

Legal Absence. Water exists, but something prevents reaching it or using it safely.

(3)

Wounds and Bandages

وَإِذَا كَانَ فِي بَدَنِهِ جِرَاحَةٌ يَضُرُّ بِهَا الْمَاءُ.. غَسَلَ الصَّحِيحَ وَتَيَمَّمَ عَنِ الْجَرِيحِ فِي الْوَجْهِ وَالْيَدَيْنِ، وَيَكُونُ التَّيَمُّمُ وَقْتَ غَسْلِ الْعَلِيلِ.
وَيَجِبُ مَسْحُ الْجَبِيرَةِ بِالْمَاءِ إِذَا لَمْ يُمْكِنْ إِخْرَاجُهَا.
Vocabulary
  • وَإِذَا كَانَand if there is
  • فِي بَدَنِهِon his body
  • جِرَاحَةٌa wound
  • يَضُرُّ بِهَاis harmed by, for which is harmful
  • الْمَاءُwater
  • غَسَلَhe washes
  • الصَّحِيحَthe healthy part
  • وَتَيَمَّمَand makes tayammum
  • عَنِ الْجَرِيحِfor the wounded part
  • فِي الْوَجْهِon the face
  • وَالْيَدَيْنِand hands
  • وَيَكُونُand it is
  • وَقْتَat the time of
  • غَسْلِwashing
  • الْعَلِيلِthe ailing limb
  • مَسْحُwiping
  • الْجَبِيرَةِthe bandage or splint
  • بِالْمَاءِwith water
  • لَمْ يُمْكِنْit is not possible
  • إِخْرَاجُهَاto remove it
Translation
If he has a wound on his body that water would harm, he washes the healthy part and makes tayammum for the wounded part on the face and hands, and the tayammum is done at the time of washing the ailing limb. Wiping the bandage with water is obligatory if it cannot be removed.
Commentary

A cut covered by a bandage is the classic case; the jurists call the bandage or splint a jabirah. It is not removed if that would be difficult or harmful. In wudu, on reaching the injured limb, the person washes the healthy part, wipes the bandage with water, and makes tayammum for the wound, finishing all three before moving to the next limb. Their order among themselves is free, though tayammum first is better, so that earth does not end up on top of water.

A wound on the head does not create this case in wudu, since the head is only wiped; a wound on the shoulder has no bearing on wudu at all. In ghusl, a wound anywhere matters, since the whole body is washed. Since the body is one unit in ghusl, tayammum can come first. Earth never replaces water for wiping the bandage.

The Shaykh cited the man with a head wound whose companions told him he had no option but to wash. He washed and died, and the Prophet said, "They killed him, may Allah kill them." This report is a foundation for the rulings of this section. If blood is on or seeping through the bandage, a second covering is placed over it before wiping, so the water does not mix with the blood; the blood is excused while separate, but once mixed with water the rules of removing impurity come in.

Jabirah. A bandage, splint, or covering placed over a wound or injury.

(4)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes the three acts for an injured limb in wudu: wash the healthy part, wipe the bandage with water, and make tayammum for the wound, all before moving on to the next limb.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that a wound only matters in wudu if it is on a limb that must be washed, while in ghusl a wound anywhere matters.
