Teaching Children What They Will Need تَعْلِيمُ الْأَوْلَادِ

What Parents Must Teach

Text
وَيَجِبُ عَلَى الْآبَاءِ وَالْأُمَّهَاتِ تَعْلِيمُ أَوْلَادِهِمُ الصِّغَارِ وَعَبِيدِهِمْ مَا يَجِبُ عَلَيْهِمْ قَبْلَ بُلُوغِهِمْ مِنَ الطَّهَارَةِ وَالصَّلَاةِ وَالصِّيَامِ وَغَيْرِ ذَلِكَ، وَيُعَرِّفُونَهُمْ تَحْرِيمَ الزِّنَا وَاللِّوَاطِ وَالسَّرِقَةِ وَشُرْبِ الْخَمْرِ وَالْمُسْكِرِ، وَتَحْرِيمَ الْكَذِبِ قَلِيلِهِ وَكَثِيرِهِ، وَالْغِيبَةِ وَالنَّمِيمَةِ وَشِبْهِ ذَلِكَ.
وَيُعَرِّفُونَهُمْ أَنَّهُمْ بِالْبُلُوغِ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي التَّكْلِيفِ.

Translation
Fathers and mothers must teach their young children, and their slaves, before they reach puberty, what is obligatory on them of purification, prayer, fasting, and the like, and make known to them the prohibition of fornication, sodomy, theft, and drinking wine and intoxicants, the prohibition of lying, little or much, and of backbiting, tale-bearing, and the like. And they make known to them that at puberty they enter into religious responsibility.
Commentary

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha explained that "fathers" includes the father, grandfather, and those above, and "mothers" the mother, grandmother, and those above. The duty is communal: if one responsible person teaches what is required, it falls from the others. It covers both the acts of worship a child will be responsible for and the prohibitions he must avoid, and the child must be told plainly that puberty brings taklif.

The Shaykh gave two proofs for the duty to teach and protect one's dependents:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَاراً
O you who believe, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire. (al-Tahrim 66:6)
كُلُّكُمْ رَاعٍ، وَكُلُّكُمْ مَسْئُولٌ عَنْ رَعِيَّتِهِ
Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock.

Ghibah (Backbiting). Citing Imam al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar: anything by which another understands a deficiency in a Muslim or a disparagement of him. It is not limited to open insults. Saying "may Allah improve his condition" about someone, in a way that conveys disparagement, is backbiting.

(1)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that guardians must teach both the obligations and the prohibitions, and must explain that puberty brings religious responsibility.

(2)

The Shaykh stressed this especially for families in Western countries, out of concern that later generations may lose their religion. He acknowledged that war, poverty, and hardship drive migration, but gave examples of Muslim grandparents, including some who built a masjid, whose grandchildren are not Muslim. He tied this to the hadith of the righteous child who supplicates for his parent.

The Signs of Puberty

وَيُعَلِّمُونَهُمْ فِي الصَّبِيِّ عَلَامَةَ الْبُلُوغِ، وَأَنَّهَا بِخَمْسَ عَشْرَةَ سَنَةً، أَوْ بِالِاحْتِلَامِ، وَبِالْحَيْضِ فِي الْمَرْأَةِ.

Translation
And they teach them the sign of puberty in the child: that it is at fifteen years, or by a wet dream, and by menstruation in the female.
Commentary
SignFromKind of threshold
Completing fifteen yearsFifteen lunar yearsExact (tahdidiyyah)
Wet dream (ihtilam)Nine years
Menstruation (girls)About nine lunar yearsApproximate (taqribiyyah)

Fifteen lunar years, exactly. A child born at noon on 1 Muharram 1440 AH reaches fifteen at noon on 1 Muharram 1455 AH.

Puberty during a prayer. If a child reaches puberty in the middle of a prayer, he completes it. Repeating it afterward is preferable but not obligatory, since part was prayed before puberty and part after.

(3)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the fifteen years are lunar, not solar, and are exact, while the nine years for menstruation are approximate.

(4)

Paying for a Teacher

وَيَجِبُ عَلَيْهِمْ؛ أَيْ: عَلَى الْآبَاءِ وَالْوَلِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِمْ أُجْرَةُ مَنْ يُعَلِّمُهُمْ مِنْ مَالِهِ، فَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ مَالٌ.. فَعَلَى مَنْ يَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ نَفَقَتُهُ.
Vocabulary
  • وَيَجِبُ عَلَيْهِمْand it is obligatory upon them
  • أَيْmeaning
  • عَلَى الْآبَاءِ وَالْوَلِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِمْupon the fathers, the guardian, and others
  • أُجْرَةُthe wage of
  • مَنْ يُعَلِّمُهُمْwhoever teaches them
  • مِنْ مَالِهِfrom the child's own property
  • فَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ مَالٌand if he has no property
  • فَعَلَىthen it is upon
  • مَنْ يَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ نَفَقَتُهُwhoever is responsible for his maintenance
Translation
And it is obligatory upon them, that is, the fathers, the guardian, and others, to pay the wage of whoever teaches them, from the child's own property; if he has no property, it is upon whoever is responsible for his maintenance.
Commentary

If a child needs a teacher to learn prayer and the rulings he must know, the cost comes first from the child's own money. If he has none, it falls on those responsible for his maintenance (nafaqah).

Who pays, in order. (1) The child's own property. (2) Those responsible for his maintenance: the father, and if needed the mother and others who must support him. (3) The Muslim treasury (bayt al-mal). (4) If there is none, wealthy Muslims.

(5)

Sidi Shaykh Abdullah Taha emphasizes that the required expense is for teaching the necessary obligations and prohibitions. It does not by itself oblige a father to pay for an expensive school or university.
