Al-Muqaddimah al-Khamisah (The Fifth Preliminary)

The Four Categories of Existing Things

وَالمَوْجُودَاتُ بِالنِّسْبَةِ إِلَى المَحَلِّ وَالمُخَصِّصِ أَرْبَعَةُ أَقْسَامٍ.
Existing things, in relation to their place and their specifier, are four categories.

Muqaddimah 5, opening classification of mawjudat.

1. Independent of both place and specifier: this is the Essence (Dhat) of Our Master, Exalted and Most High. Only Allah's Essence has this property; nothing else shares it.

(1)

2. Dependent on both place and specifier: this is al-'arad (accident), things like color or movement, which exist only inhering in a substance or body, never by themselves. Color exists only as attached to a surface or object.

(2)

3. Requires a specifier, but not a place: this is al-jawhar (substance). Al-jism (body) is a subcategory of jawhar, and al-jirm (bulk) is a subcategory of jism. A physical book is itself a jawhar/jism, and it continues existing even if one of its accidents, its color, changes.

(3)

4. Present in a place, but not needing a specifier: these are the eternal attributes (al-sifat al-azaliyyah) of Our Master, Exalted and Most High. This is a major area of theological debate specifically around whether the divine attributes are independent entities or not.

(4)

Aristotle's ten categories, and the Muslim philosophers' division into jawhar and nine kinds of 'arad, were discussed as background for this fourfold classification.

The Mu'tazilah-Ash'ari Debate on the Divine Attributes

The status of the divine attributes (sifat) relative to the essence (dhat) is a genuine point of divergence between the two schools.

أَثْبَتَتِ المُعْتَزِلَةُ أَنَّهُ لَا صِفَاتَ قَدِيمَةً زَائِدَةً عَلَى الذَّاتِ.
The Mu'tazilah affirmed that there are no eternal attributes additional to the essence.

The Mu'tazili position on the sifat.

أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ يُثْبِتُونَ الصِّفَاتِ، وَيَقُولُونَ: هِيَ لَيْسَتْ هُوَ وَلَا غَيْرُهُ.
Ahl al-Sunnah affirm the attributes and say: they are neither identical to Him nor other than Him.

The Ahl al-Sunnah position on the sifat.

The Mu'tazilah deny distinct eternal attributes to avoid multiplicity in what is eternal: on their view, Allah is "'Alim" (knowing) by virtue of His essence alone, not by a separate subsisting attribute.

(1)

Ahl al-Sunnah maintain Allah has real, eternal attributes, while clarifying these are not divisible entities or independent things, but genuinely inseparable from Him.

(2)

"Shu'ayb is tall" was used as an illustration: tallness is inseparable from the individual it describes, and it cannot subsist on its own apart from him, the same way a divine attribute cannot be pried apart from the essence it describes.

Why an Attribute Must Inhere in Its Subject

The reasoning for why a sifah cannot exist independently of its subject (mawsuf) proceeds in steps.

Premise 1: by definition, a sifah must describe a subject (mawsuf).

Premise 2: if a quality existed apart from any subject, it would no longer be a sifah at all, it would become a dhat (essence) in its own right.

Premise 3: if a sifah could exist detached from its subject, it could in principle attach to any subject, and would then no longer be unique to this one.

Conclusion: to preserve the uniqueness and truth of the ascription, an attribute must inhere in its own subject specifically (maqamuhu bi-mawsufihi).

(1)

An analogy was given to sharpen the distinction: "owning a pen" is external to a person and could be transferred to someone else, but "being tall" must be internal to the subject it describes, it cannot be handed off the way ownership can.

Al-Kalam and the Debate Over the Qur'an's Createdness

الكَلَامُ مِنْ صِفَاتِ اللَّهِ القَدِيمَةِ، وَالمُعْتَزِلَةُ يَقُولُونَ: القُرْآنُ مَخْلُوقٌ.
Speech is among Allah's eternal attributes, while the Mu'tazilah say: the Qur'an is created.

The dispute over the createdness of the Qur'an.

The Mu'tazili logic: admitting an eternal attribute of speech would mean positing something genuinely new arising within Allah, which they deny, so on their view all speech, the Qur'an included, must be created. The Ash'ari response distinguishes two senses of "speech":

1. Al-Kalam al-Nafsi (Internal Speech): an eternal meaning subsisting in Allah.

(1)

2. Al-Kalam al-Lafzi (Uttered Speech): the sequence of sound, letters, and words, which is temporal.

(2)

The meaning is eternal; the composed letters and sounds expressing it are temporal. An analogy from ordinary Arabic usage was given: speech is said to begin in the heart, with the tongue only translating it outward, and poetry saying the same. The Hanbali position that even the recited huruf themselves are eternal was raised and critiqued as an unsupported specification (tarjih bila murajjih), a preference without any actual basis for preferring it. (See: Al-Muqaddimah al-Sabi'ah, on al-Kalam al-Azali specifically)
