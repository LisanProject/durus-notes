Al-Muqaddimah al-Thaminah (The Eighth Preliminary)

Al-Khabar wal-Insha' الخَبَرُ وَالإِنْشَاءُ

Text
الكَلَامُ يَنْقَسِمُ إِلَى خَبَرٍ وَإِنْشَاءٍ.
Vocabulary
  • الكَلَامُ يَنْقَسِمُ إِلَىspeech divides into
  • خَبَرa report
  • إِنْشَاءan initiating utterance
Translation
Speech divides into khabar (a report) and insha' (an initiating utterance).

Al-Khabar الخَبَرُ

Text
الخَبَرُ هُوَ مَا يَصِحُّ أَنْ يُقَالَ لِقَائِلِهِ إِنَّهُ صَادِقٌ أَوْ كَاذِبٌ.
Vocabulary
  • الخَبَرa report
  • يَصِحُّ أَنْ يُقَالَit is correct to say
  • صَادِقٌ أَوْ كَاذِبtruthful or lying
Translation
Khabar is that of which it is correct to say of its speaker that he is truthful or lying.
Commentary

"Zaydun qa'imun" ("Zayd is standing") was given as the example, since you can verify externally whether Zayd is actually standing. Khabar is not limited to the present: "it is raining" and "dinosaurs existed" are both khabar, one about the present, one about the past.

Al-Insha' الإِنْشَاءُ

Text
الإِنْشَاءُ هُوَ مَا لَا يَصِحُّ أَنْ يُقَالَ لِقَائِلِهِ إِنَّهُ صَادِقٌ أَوْ كَاذِبٌ.
Vocabulary
  • الإِنْشَاءan initiating utterance
  • لَا يَصِحُّit is not correct
Translation
Insha' is that of which it is not correct to say of its speaker that he is truthful or lying.

Al-Amr (command)

Al-Nida' (calling)

Al-Tamanni (wishing)

Al-Raja' (hoping)

Al-Du'a (supplication)

Al-Iltimas (a request between equals)

(1)
Commentary

"Ya Zayd!" (O Zayd!) is insha', not information about Zayd but an act of calling upon him; "ya Allah!" (O Allah!) works the same way. "Layta al-shababa ya'udu yawman" (I wish youth would return one day) illustrates tamanni; hoping Allah forgives one's sins illustrates raja', since it initiates a meaning within possibility rather than reporting a checkable reality. Commands such as "ihdur!" (come!) are likewise insha'. The root of "insha'" itself points to creating or originating something at the very moment of speech. Amr, du'a, and iltimas were distinguished by the relationship between speaker and addressee: amr moves from higher to lower, du'a from lower to higher, and iltimas between equals.

A common writing mistake was also flagged: "in sha' Allah" must be written as two separate words, "in" (if) and "sha'a" (He willed). Writing it as one word changes the meaning toward "the creation of Allah," a serious lexical error.

Al-Sidq wa'l-Kadhib الصِّدْقُ وَالكَذِبُ

Text
الصِّدْقُ هُوَ مُطَابَقَةُ الخَبَرِ لِمَا فِي نَفْسِ الأَمْرِ. الكَذِبُ هُوَ عَدَمُ مُطَابَقَةِ الخَبَرِ لِمَا فِي نَفْسِ الأَمْرِ.
Vocabulary
  • الصِّدْقtruth
  • مُطَابَقَةcorrespondence
  • نَفْسِ الأَمْرthe actual state of affairs
  • الكَذِبfalsehood
  • عَدَمُ مُطَابَقَةlack of correspondence
Translation
Sidq (truth) is the correspondence of the report to what is actually the case (nafs al-amr). Kadhib (falsehood) is the lack of correspondence between the report and what is actually the case.
Commentary

"Nafs al-amr" here means the actual state of affairs outside the statement itself: sidq means the khabar matches that reality exactly, and any mismatch at all makes the report not true.

Does Truth Depend on Belief?

Text
الحُكْمُ بِالصِّدْقِ أَوِ الكَذِبِ عَلَى الخَبَرِ مَنُوطٌ بِمُطَابَقَتِهِ أَوْ عَدَمِ مُطَابَقَتِهِ لِلْوَاقِعِ فَقَطْ، لَا بِمَا يَعْتَقِدُهُ المُخْبِرُ. الصِّدْقُ لَا يَكُونُ إِلَّا إِذَا وَافَقَ اعْتِقَادَ المُخْبِرِ وَوَافَقَ مُطَابَقَةَ الخَبَرِ لِلْوَاقِعِ.
Vocabulary
  • مَنُوطٌ بِcontingent upon
  • اعْتِقَادَ المُخْبِرthe reporter's belief
  • وَافَقَmatched, agreed with
Translation
For Ahl al-Sunnah: judging a report true or false depends solely on its correspondence, or lack of it, to reality, not on what the reporter himself believes. For the Mu'tazilah (particularly al-Jahiz): truth only occurs when the report matches both the reporter's belief and reality itself.
Commentary

On the Mu'tazili view, a hypocrite's statement could never count as truthful even if it happened to match reality, unless he also inwardly affirmed it. Ahl al-Sunnah hold the statement is true regardless of the speaker's inner state, including testimonies given by the munafiqun (hypocrites) about the Prophet ﷺ himself. Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Yahya ibn Ma'in, and 'Ali ibn al-Madini were discussed in connection with the mihnah (inquisition): Imam Ahmad refused to take the legal rukhsah (concession) permitting a statement of falsehood under duress, while the other two did, and the differing scholarly reasoning behind when a rukhsah is or is not taken was discussed.

Al-Amanah wa'l-Khiyanah الأَمَانَةُ وَالخِيَانَةُ

Text
الأَمَانَةُ حِفْظُ القَلْبِ وَالجَوَارِحِ عَمَّا لَا يَنْبَغِي فِعْلُهُ شَرْعاً. وَمَا يُقَابِلُ الأَمَانَةَ هُوَ الخِيَانَةُ.
Vocabulary
  • الأَمَانَةtrustworthiness
  • حِفْظُ القَلْبِ وَالجَوَارِحsafeguarding the heart and the limbs
  • لَا يَنْبَغِي فِعْلُهُ شَرْعاًnot fitting to do, religiously speaking
  • الخِيَانَةtreachery
Translation
Amanah is safeguarding the heart and the limbs from doing what is not fitting to do, religiously speaking. What opposes amanah is khiyanah (treachery).

Avoidance of the prohibited (haram).

Avoidance of the disliked (makruh).

(1)
Commentary

The ayah on amanah in Surat al-Ahzab was discussed, distinguishing generic amanah from the Prophet's ﷺ own perfect amanah, so complete that even his enemies trusted him. Amanah was framed as the very foundation for accepting the Prophet's ﷺ message, analogous to accepting any historical fact: denying amanah as a valid basis for trust would undermine every chain of rational or legal proof, not only religious ones. The universal historical trust placed in a figure like Plato was compared to the certainty warranted in the Prophet's ﷺ character and message, to show that denying this kind of trust logically entails denying authority in every field, not just religion. "The Earth is round" and "one's mother is one's mother" were given as further analogies for the kind of necessary, undeniable certainty being described.

The Foundations of Accepting a Transmitted Report

Commentary

Imam al-Shafi'i was credited with codifying the legal principles used to accept or reject reports, establishing the categories below and demonstrating the systematic use of a sanad (chain of transmission).

Al-Hadith al-Mutawatir: mass-transmitted hadith.

Al-Hadith al-Ahad: singularly-transmitted hadith.

(1)

Imam Malik's Muwatta' was described here primarily as a record of the practical legal practice of the people of Madinah ('amal ahl al-Madinah), rather than simply a hadith collection in the ordinary sense.

Al-Ijazah

Text
الإِجَازَةُ فِي رِوَايَةِ النَّصِّ.
Vocabulary
  • الإِجَازَةauthorization
  • فِي رِوَايَةِ النَّصّto transmit the text
Translation
Authorization to transmit the text.
Commentary

The session concluded with the formal granting of ijazah: "I give you ijazah in narrating this matn". This was followed by a closing du'a for sincerity, sound intellect, and protection from the nafs and shaytan, and recommendations to read Al-Naba' al-'Azim by Shaykh 'Abdullah Daraz and Dala'il al-Nubuwwah by Imam al-Bayhaqi.
