Al-Muqaddimah al-Sabi'ah (The Seventh Preliminary)

Sifat al-Ma'ani al-Azaliyyah الصِّفَاتُ المَعَانِي الأَزَلِيَّةُ

Text
المُقَدِّمَةُ السَّابِعَةُ فِي الصِّفَاتِ المَعَانِي الأَزَلِيَّةِ.
Vocabulary
  • المُقَدِّمَةُ السَّابِعَةthe seventh preliminary
  • الصِّفَاتُ المَعَانِيthe attributes of meaning
  • الأَزَلِيَّةeternal
Translation
The seventh preliminary concerns the eternal attributes of meaning.

1. القُدْرَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ (eternal power)

2. الإِرَادَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ (eternal will)

3. العِلْمُ الأَزَلِيُّ (eternal knowledge)

4. الحَيَاةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ (eternal life)

5. السَّمْعُ الأَزَلِيُّ (eternal hearing)

6. البَصَرُ الأَزَلِيُّ (eternal sight)

7. الكَلَامُ الأَزَلِيُّ (eternal speech)

(1)
Commentary

These attributes are contrasted with the physical attributes some innovators falsely claim for Allah (sifat al-a'yan).

1. Al-Qudra al-Azaliyyah القُدْرَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ

Text
القُدْرَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ صِفَةٌ بِهَا يُوجَدُ كُلُّ مُمْكِنٍ وَيُعْدَمُ وَفْقَ الإِرَادَةِ.
Vocabulary
  • القُدْرَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةeternal power
  • يُوجَدُis brought into existence
  • وَيُعْدَمand made non-existent
  • وَفْقَ الإِرَادَةin accordance with the will
Translation
Eternal power is an attribute by which every possible thing is brought into existence or out of existence, in accordance with the will.

Al-Mumkin: the possible (contingent being).

Al-Mustahil: the impossible (rationally impossible being).

Al-Wajib: the necessary (absolutely necessary being).

(1)

Only al-mumkin (the possible) falls within the domain of divine power; the impossible and the necessary are both excluded from it entirely.

(2)
Commentary

This classification was used directly to refute the "stone too heavy for God to lift" argument, since such a scenario is rationally incoherent (mustahil), not something genuinely possible that divine power fails to reach. Physicality was negated from Allah in this same discussion, contrasted with Christian and Salafi literalist positions.

2. Al-Irada al-Azaliyyah الإِرَادَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ

Text
الإِرَادَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ صِفَةٌ بِهَا يُخَصَّصُ المُمْكِنُ بِبَعْضِ مَا يَجُوزُ عَلَيْهِ.
Vocabulary
  • الإِرَادَةُ الأَزَلِيَّةeternal will
  • يُخَصَّصُ المُمْكِنthe possible is specified
  • مَا يَجُوزُ عَلَيْهwhat is permissible for it
Translation
Eternal will is an attribute by which the possible is specified with some of what is permissible for it.
Commentary

This specification (takhsis) is what makes creation purposeful rather than random, illustrated with the specificity of human features and the form of angels. Any view ascribing randomness or purposelessness to creation was directly critiqued here.

3. Al-'Ilm al-Azaliyy العِلْمُ الأَزَلِيُّ

Text
العِلْمُ الأَزَلِيُّ صِفَةٌ يَنْكَشِفُ بِهَا كُلُّ مَعْلُومٍ انْكِشَافاً يُطَابِقُ المَعْلُومَ عَلَى مَا هُوَ بِهِ.
Vocabulary
  • العِلْمُ الأَزَلِيّeternal knowledge
  • يَنْكَشِفُis disclosed
  • كُلُّ مَعْلُومeverything knowable
  • يُطَابِقُmatching, corresponding to
Translation
Eternal knowledge is an attribute by which everything knowable is disclosed in a manner that perfectly matches its actual reality.

Al-Jahl: ignorance.

Al-Zann: speculation.

Al-Wahm: delusion.

(1)
Commentary

Allah's knowledge is perfect, unlike creation's, and it includes even non-existent things, such as a dragon, which we can know about despite the fact that it does not actually exist.

4. Al-Hayat al-Azaliyyah الحَيَاةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ

Text
الحَيَاةُ الأَزَلِيَّةُ صِفَةٌ بِهَا يَصِحُّ الِاتِّصَافُ بِسَائِرِ الصِّفَاتِ.
Vocabulary
  • الحَيَاةُ الأَزَلِيَّةeternal life
  • يَصِحُّ الِاتِّصَافascription becomes valid
  • سَائِرِ الصِّفَاتthe rest of the attributes
Translation
Eternal life is the attribute by which ascription of all the other attributes becomes valid.
Commentary

This attribute underlies all the others: without it, none of knowledge, hearing, sight, or power would even be possible. "Hadi is walking" was used as an illustration: ascribing the act of walking to someone necessarily entails that he has life, power, and the relevant faculties in the first place.

5. Al-Sam' al-Azaliyy السَّمْعُ الأَزَلِيُّ

Text
السَّمْعُ الأَزَلِيُّ صِفَةٌ يَنْكَشِفُ بِهَا كُلُّ مَوْجُودٍ ذِي صَوْتٍ كَمَا هُوَ بِهِ انْكِشَافاً يُبَايِنُ سِوَاهُ ضَرُورَةً.
Vocabulary
  • السَّمْعُ الأَزَلِيّeternal hearing
  • كُلُّ مَوْجُودٍ ذِي صَوْتeverything existing with sound
  • يُبَايِنُ سِوَاهُ ضَرُورَةnecessarily distinct from the others
Translation
Eternal hearing is an attribute by which everything existing with sound is disclosed exactly as it is, a disclosure necessarily distinct from the others.
Commentary

هُوَ يَسْمَعُ دَبِيبَ النَّمْلَةِ السَّوْدَاءِ فِي لَيْلَةِ الظَّلْمَةِ عَلَى صَخْرَةٍ صَمَّاءَ, "He hears the footsteps of a black ant, on a smooth solid rock, in the darkness of night," was cited to show this scope covering everything that exists with sound, used specifically to dispel any confusion suggesting this attribute implies anthropomorphism.

6. Al-Basar al-Azaliyy البَصَرُ الأَزَلِيُّ

Text
البَصَرُ الأَزَلِيُّ صِفَةٌ يَنْكَشِفُ بِهَا كُلُّ مَوْجُودٍ عَلَى مَا هُوَ بِهِ انْكِشَافاً يُبَايِنُ سِوَاهُ ضَرُورَةً.
Vocabulary
  • البَصَرُ الأَزَلِيّeternal sight
  • كُلُّ مَوْجُودevery existing thing
Translation
Eternal sight is an attribute by which every existing thing is disclosed exactly as it is, a disclosure necessarily distinct from the others.
Commentary

Sight, unlike knowledge, is limited to what actually exists: Allah knows a dragon despite its non-existence, but does not see or hear a non-existent image or sound. A student asked why taste and touch are not counted among the divine attributes.

Hearing and sight are perfections expressly affirmed in revelation, while taste and touch are inherently bodily qualities requiring physical organs like a tongue, which cannot apply to Allah. Allah knows every taste and texture through His eternal knowledge, but does not sense them the way a body does.

(1)

7. Al-Kalam al-Azaliyy الكَلَامُ الأَزَلِيُّ

Commentary

A meaning subsisting in the essence of Allah, expressed in different languages, not composed of discrete sounds or letters, and transcending sequence, division, and change altogether. The Qur'an and other revelations are expressions ('ibarat) of this one uncreated meaning, rendered in created languages and modes.

1. A meaning subsisting within the essence.

2. Expressed in different phrases and languages.

3. Distinct in nature from letters and sounds.

4. Transcendent above partiality, totality, sequence, regeneration, silence, and every kind of change.

5. Connected to everything knowledge itself pertains to.

(1)

This was contrasted with the Hanbali and Salafi view attributing sound, articulation, and sequence, all qualities of created, corporeal beings, to Allah directly. Ibn Taymiyyah's teachings on this point were discussed and critiqued, with the Ash'ari distinction drawn between the created letters and sounds we ourselves utter and the uncreated speech those utterances indicate. The existence of naskh (abrogation) and variation across the qira'at (recitations) were raised as evidence that divine speech itself cannot be identical to any one temporal, sequential form.

A student asked what 'A'ishah (may Allah be pleased with her) meant by saying "between the two covers of this mushaf is the speech of Allah."

For the Ash'ari, "the speech of Allah" in her statement refers to what is indicated (al-madlul), the created expression, not the uncreated subsisting meaning itself. Confusing the created expression with the uncreated internal reality is an error refuted by both rational and traditional proofs.

(2)

A second question asked why a word's literal and figurative meaning (haqiqah and majaz) cannot both be intended simultaneously. The example given: "there is a lion in the mosque" must mean either the actual animal or a brave man, never both at once, the same way each revealed phrase points to one single reality rather than multiple ones simultaneously.

The Point of All Seven: Negating Corporeality

Text
المَقْصُودُ مِنْ كُلِّ ذَلِكَ نَفْيُ الجِسْمِيَّةِ عَنِ اللَّهِ.
Vocabulary
  • المَقْصُودthe aim, the intent
  • نَفْيُ الجِسْمِيَّةnegating corporeality
Translation
The aim of all of this is to negate corporeality from Allah.
Commentary

Every distinction, denial, and rational exploration across these seven attributes serves this single intent. Salafis, Christians, and others who attribute physicality or accidental qualities, movement, limbs, or the like, to Allah were named and critiqued directly, with specific claims from Ibn Taymiyyah, Hanbali scholars, and Salafi preachers quoted and refuted. Reason ('aql) functions here to negate what is impossible for Allah; once the principle of divine transcendence is established, further speculation about the exact reality of the attributes is treated as both impossible and unnecessary.
