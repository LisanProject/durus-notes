Al-Muqaddimah al-Rabi'ah (The Fourth Preliminary)

Usul al-Kufr wa'l-Bida': The Seven Roots

أُصُولُ الكُفْرِ وَالبِدَعِ سَبْعَةٌ.
The foundations of disbelief and innovations are seven.

Muqaddimah 4, opening statement.

1. الإِيجَابُ الذَّاتِيُّ (al-Ijab al-Dhati)

2. التَّحْسِينُ العَقْلِيُّ (al-Tahsin al-'Aqli)

3. التَّقْلِيدُ الرَّدِيءُ (al-Taqlid al-Radi')

4. الرَّبْطُ العَادِيُّ (al-Rabt al-'Adi)

5. الجَهْلُ المُرَكَّبُ (al-Jahl al-Murakkab)

6. التَّمَسُّكُ بِمُجَرَّدِ ظَوَاهِرِ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ (Clinging to the bare apparent meanings of the Qur'an and Sunnah)

7. الجَهْلُ بِالقَوَاعِدِ العَقْلِيَّةِ وَلِسَانِ العَرَبِ (Ignorance of rational principles and the Arabic language)

(1)

1. Al-Ijab al-Dhati

الإِيجَابُ الذَّاتِيُّ، وَهُوَ إِسْنَادُ الكَائِنَاتِ إِلَى اللَّهِ تَعَالَى عَلَى سَبِيلِ التَّعْدِيلِيِّ أَوِ الطَّبِيعِيِّ مِنْ غَيْرِ اخْتِيَارٍ.
Al-Ijab al-Dhati is attributing created things to Allah Most High by way of causal necessity or natural process, without choice (ikhtiyar).

Muqaddimah 4, defining al-ijab al-dhati.

1a. Attributing it by way of causal necessity (ta'dili).

1b. Attributing it by way of natural/habitual process (tabi'i).

1c. Without any element of divine choice (ikhtiyar).

(1)

This was tied directly to the philosophers' (al-falasifah) doctrine that Allah is a "simple substance" and "first cause," from whom creation emanates necessarily rather than by will, denying the attribute of irada (will) altogether and implying deficiency in Allah. The consequence drawn out was that this doctrine makes the world eternal (qadim) and independent of divine choice, classified as kufr, refuted directly by the verse "wa rabbuka yakhluqu ma yasha'u wa yakhtar" (your Lord creates what He wills and chooses).

2. Al-Tahsin al-'Aqli

التَّحْسِينُ العَقْلِيُّ، وَهُوَ كَوْنُ أَفْعَالِ اللَّهِ تَعَالَى وَأَحْكَامِهِ مَوْقُوفَةً عَقْلاً عَلَى الأَغْرَاضِ، وَهِيَ جَلْبُ المَصَالِحِ وَدَرْءُ المَفَاسِدِ.
Al-Tahsin al-'Aqli is holding that Allah's actions and rulings are rationally contingent on purposes, namely attaining benefits and repelling harms.

Muqaddimah 4, defining al-tahsin al-'aqli.

2a. Jalb al-masalih: attaining benefits.

2b. Dar' al-mafasid: repelling harms.

(1)

This is the Mu'tazilah's doctrine, who held certain actions obligatory upon Allah when rational purpose dictated it. The debate between Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari and 'Ali al-Jubba'i over the fate of a child who died young was cited as exposing the inability of Mu'tazilite rationalism to fully account for divine action. Ahl al-Sunnah's position: nothing is obligatory upon Allah, and His will is not subject to human rational calculation.

3. Al-Taqlid al-Radi'

التَّقْلِيدُ الرَّدِيءُ، وَهُوَ مُتَابَعَةُ الغَيْرِ لِأَجْلِ الحَمِيَّةِ وَالتَّعَصُّبِ مِنْ غَيْرِ طَلَبٍ لِلْحَقِّ.
Al-Taqlid al-Radi' is following others for the sake of zealotry and partisanship, without seeking the truth.

Muqaddimah 4, defining al-taqlid al-radi'.

3a. Al-hamiyyah: zeal or partisan solidarity.

3b. Al-ta'assub: fanaticism.

3c. Without seeking the truth.

(1)

The people of Quraysh rejecting the Prophet ﷺ out of inherited pride and group solidarity, not because they failed to see the merit of his message, was given as the example. A distinction was drawn between this vile taqlid and good taqlid: following a qualified scholar out of one's own insufficiency in knowledge, supported by the verse "fas'alu ahl al-dhikr" (ask the people of remembrance). Modern political double standards driven by group loyalty were raised as a contemporary parallel.

4. Al-Rabt al-'Adi

الرَّبْطُ العَادِيُّ، وَهُوَ إِثْبَاتُ تَلَازُمٍ بَيْنَ أَمْرَيْنِ وُجُوداً أَوْ عَدَماً بِوَاسِطَةِ التَّكَرُّرِ.
Al-Rabt al-'Adi is asserting a necessary connection between two things, in existence or non-existence, purely on the basis of their repeated co-occurrence.

Muqaddimah 4, defining al-rabt al-'adi.

This is the error of confusing mere habit with genuine rational necessity, and it is directly the mechanism behind shirk al-asbab discussed in the third muqaddimah: attributing causal power to a means that is only habitually, not necessarily, connected to its outcome. (See: Al-Muqaddimah al-Thalithah)

5. Al-Jahl al-Murakkab

الجَهْلُ المُرَكَّبُ، وَهُوَ أَنْ يَجْهَلَ الحَقَّ وَيَجْهَلَ جَهْلَهُ بِهِ.
Compounded ignorance is to be ignorant of the truth, and ignorant of one's own ignorance of it.

Muqaddimah 4, defining al-jahl al-murakkab.

This is what prevents a person from listening to genuine experts in the first place, since he already believes he possesses true knowledge, illustrated with people who hold confident opinions on religious or scientific matters without the requisite expertise to actually back them up.

6. Clinging to the Bare Apparent Meaning of the Qur'an and Sunnah

التَّمَسُّكُ فِي عَقَائِدِ الإِيمَانِ بِمُجَرَّدِ ظَوَاهِرِ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ مِنْ غَيْرِ تَفْصِيلٍ.
Holding to the apparent wording of the Qur'an and Sunnah in matters of belief, without distinguishing what is rationally possible for Allah from what is rationally impossible.

Muqaddimah 4, defining the sixth root.

6a. What its apparent meaning would be, if taken literally, is rationally impossible for Allah.

6b. What its apparent meaning would be is not rationally impossible for Allah.

(1)

This critiques Salafi and Ibn Taymiyyah's tendencies toward strict literalism, specifically around divine attributes like "nuzul" (descent) and "istawa" (ascending/settling), warning that taking bodily language at face value leads to tajsim (anthropomorphism), directly contradicting "laysa ka-mithlihi shay'" (there is nothing like unto Him). Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal's own anathematizing of anyone who describes Allah as a jism (body) was cited here. The semantic range (bayan) of Arabic terms like "istawa" was discussed, along with the caution that translating divine attributes risks stripping them of their original nuance.

7. Ignorance of Rational Principles and the Arabic Tongue

الجَهْلُ بِالقَوَاعِدِ العَقْلِيَّةِ الَّتِي هِيَ العِلْمُ بِوُجُوبِ الوَاجِبَاتِ وَجَوَازِ الجَائِزَاتِ وَاسْتِحَالَةِ المُسْتَحِيلَاتِ، وَلِسَانُ العَرَبِ.
Ignorance of the rational principles, which is knowledge of the necessity of the necessary, the permissibility of the possible, and the impossibility of the impossible, and ignorance of the Arabic tongue.

Muqaddimah 4, defining the seventh root.

7a. Knowledge of the necessity of what is necessary (wajib).

7b. Knowledge of the permissibility of what is possible (ja'iz).

7c. Knowledge of the impossibility of what is impossible (mustahil).

7d. The Arabic tongue: the science of language, i'rab (syntax), and bayan (clarity of expression).

(1)

The Qur'an's own use of rational argument was cited, "law kana fihima alihatun illa Allahu la-fasadata" (had there been within the heavens and earth gods besides Allah, they both would have been ruined), as a proof of divine oneness from observed cosmic order. Those who claim "anything is possible for God," to the point of admitting a square circle or a logical absurdity, were critiqued directly, with a parallel drawn to Christian arguments for the Trinity or Incarnation as another instance of this same error. Ignorance of Arabic specifically, i'rab and bayan above all, was flagged as leading to serious error, since it obscures the clarity and nuance of revelation itself.
