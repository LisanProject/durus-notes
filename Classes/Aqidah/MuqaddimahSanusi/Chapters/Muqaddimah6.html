Al-Muqaddimah al-Sadisah (The Sixth Preliminary)

Al-Mumkinat wal-Mutaqabilat: Six Categories

المُمْكِنَاتُ وَالمُتَقَابِلَاتُ سِتَّةٌ.
The possible things and their opposites are six.

Muqaddimah 6, opening classification.

1. الوُجُودُ وَالعَدَمُ (Existence and non-existence)

2. المَقَادِيرُ (Quantities)

3. الصِّفَاتُ (Attributes)

4. الأَزْمِنَةُ (Times)

5. الأَمْكِنَةُ (Places)

6. الجِهَاتُ (Directions)

(1)

Evil (al-sharr) was defined here as the non-existence of good ('adam al-khayr), and darkness as the absence of light, both worked examples of the existence/non-existence pairing. Directions (jihat) were explained as inherently relative: a direction cannot exist unless there is more than one place or thing to be relative to, which underlies the Ash'ari refusal to assign Allah to any direction or place at all.

The Classification of the Soul (al-Ruh) in Ash'ari Theology

A student asked which of the six categories above the soul (al-ruh) would fall under.

The answer given: al-ruh is a jism (body). This point is debated; some Sufis claim the soul is uncreated, out of a kind of emotional reverence for it, but the actual consensus, citing al-Ghazali and Ibn Raslan, holds it to be a created, subtle body (jism latif), not uncreated.

(1)
وَالرُّوحُ مَا أَخْبَرَ عَنْهَا المُصْطَفَى، فَنُمْسِكُ المَقَالَ عَنْهَا أَدَباً.
The soul is as the Chosen One informed us of it, so we withhold speech about it out of propriety.

Ibn Raslan's poem, quoted on the soul.

Reflections on Brokenness and the Heart

The story of Sayyid Ahmad al-Rifa'i was shared as an illustration of humility: after allegedly kissing the Prophet's ﷺ hand, he had his own followers step over him to tame his ego. He is quoted as saying, "I found every path to Allah congested except the path of brokenness (al-inkisar)."

لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ بِهَا.
They have hearts with which they do not understand.

Verse cited on true understanding entering through the heart.

This verse was used to make the point that genuine understanding (ma'rifah) enters through the heart, not through intellect ('aql) alone, alongside the hadith, "istafti qalbaka wa in aftaka al-nasu wa aftawka" (consult your heart, even if people give you their opinions).
