Al-Muqaddimah al-Sadisah (The Sixth Preliminary)

Al-Mumkinat wal-Mutaqabilat المُمْكِنَاتُ وَالمُتَقَابِلَاتُ

Text
المُمْكِنَاتُ وَالمُتَقَابِلَاتُ سِتَّةٌ.
Vocabulary
  • المُمْكِنَاتthe possible things
  • وَالمُتَقَابِلَاتand their opposites
  • سِتَّةsix
Translation
The possible things and their opposites are six.

1. الوُجُودُ وَالعَدَمُ (existence and non-existence)

2. المَقَادِيرُ (quantities)

3. الصِّفَاتُ (attributes)

4. الأَزْمِنَةُ (times)

5. الأَمْكِنَةُ (places)

6. الجِهَاتُ (directions)

(1)
Commentary

Evil (al-sharr) was defined here as the non-existence of good ('adam al-khayr), and darkness as the absence of light, both worked examples of the existence/non-existence pairing. Directions (jihat) were explained as inherently relative: a direction cannot exist unless there is more than one place or thing to be relative to, which underlies the Ash'ari refusal to assign Allah to any direction or place at all.

The Classification of the Soul (al-Ruh)

Al-ruh is a jism (body). This point is debated; some Sufis claim the soul is uncreated, out of a kind of emotional reverence for it, but the actual consensus, citing al-Ghazali and Ibn Raslan, holds it to be a created, subtle body (jism latif), not uncreated.

(1)
Commentary

وَالرُّوحُ مَا أَخْبَرَ عَنْهَا المُصْطَفَى، فَنُمْسِكُ المَقَالَ عَنْهَا أَدَباً, "the soul is as the Chosen One informed us of it, so we withhold speech about it out of propriety," Ibn Raslan's poem, was quoted on the question of the soul.

Brokenness and the Heart

Commentary

The story of Sayyid Ahmad al-Rifa'i was shared as an illustration of humility: after allegedly kissing the Prophet's ﷺ hand, he had his own followers step over him to tame his ego. He is quoted as saying, "I found every path to Allah congested except the path of brokenness (al-inkisar)."

لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ بِهَا, "they have hearts with which they do not understand," was used to make the point that genuine understanding (ma'rifah) enters through the heart, not through intellect ('aql) alone, alongside the hadith, اسْتَفْتِ قَلْبَكَ وَإِنْ أَفْتَاكَ النَّاسُ وَأَفْتَوْكَ, "consult your heart, even if people give you their opinions."
