Al-Muqaddimah al-Thalithah (The Third Preliminary)

Mukhayyar and Musayyar: Free Will and Compulsion

الإِنْسَانُ مُخَيَّرٌ فِي كُلِّ مَا فِيهِ تَكْلِيفٌ وَمُسَيَّرٌ فِي غَيْرِهِ.
The human being is given free will in all that involves religious burden (taklif), and is compelled in what is outside of that.

Muqaddimah 3, on mukhayyar and musayyar.

Mukhayyar: free will exists in everything entailing religious duty.

(1)

Musayyar: compelled in whatever falls outside of that.

(2)

Falling sick was given as the illustrating case: a person does not choose to fall ill, so there is no religious burden (taklif) attached to feeling sick or not feeling sick, since taklif only attaches to what a person actually has free will over.

Anwa' al-Shirk: The Six Types of Shirk

قَالَ: أَنْوَاعُ الشِّرْكِ سِتَّةٌ.
He said: the types of shirk are six.

Muqaddimah 3, on the six types of shirk.

1. Shirk Istiqlal (Shirk of Independence): the affirmation of two independent gods, like the shirk of the Magians (al-Majus), who hold there are separate gods for good and for evil. The class discussed Zoroastrian dualism specifically in connection with this type.

(1)

2. Shirk al-Tab'id (Shirk of Division): making Allah into parts or divisions. Two examples were cited: the Christian Trinity (three persons in one God) and the LDS/Mormon belief in three separate divine beings. The philosophers' notion of an eternal matter (hayula) was also discussed critically in this connection, alongside the Greek/Arabic philosophical categories of substance (jawhar) and accident ('arad).

(2)

3. Shirk al-Taqrib (Shirk of Seeking Proximity): worshipping other than Allah in order to draw near to Allah. The pre-Islamic Arabs worshipping al-Lat, Manat, and al-'Uzza, calling them "daughters of Allah," were cited as the classic example, refuted directly by Qur'anic verses.

(3)

4. Shirk al-Taqlid (Shirk of Blind Following): worshipping other than Allah simply by following others. This was described as the practice of the later jahiliyyah pagans, who kept their ancestors' rituals without evidence or reasoning of their own.

(4)

5. Shirk al-Asbab (Shirk of Means): attributing real effect or influence to a means or cause in itself, such as believing fire itself burns rather than Allah creating the burning when fire is present. This type splits into two positions:

(5)

5a. Whoever says a means affects by its own nature: consensus (ijma') has been narrated on the disbelief of such a person.

5b. Whoever says a means affects by a power Allah placed within it: such a person is an immoral innovator (fasiq mubtadi'), and there are two opinions regarding whether this reaches disbelief. Ibn Taymiyyah was discussed as holding this second position, contrasted with the Ash'ari view that only Allah creates causation directly, never through a delegated natural power residing in the thing itself.

6. Shirk al-Aghrad (Shirk of Purposes): performing a deed for other than Allah, such as someone beautifying his prayer to impress another person. This is termed hidden shirk (al-shirk al-khafi).

(6)

The Rulings on Each Type

وَحُكْمُ الأَرْبَعَةِ الأُولَى: الكُفْرُ بِالإِجْمَاعِ.
The ruling on the first four: disbelief, by consensus.

Muqaddimah 3, ruling on shirk istiqlal, tab'id, taqrib, and taqlid.

وَحُكْمُ السَّادِسِ: المَعْصِيَةُ مِنْ غَيْرِ كُفْرٍ بِالإِجْمَاعِ.
The ruling on the sixth: sin, without disbelief, by consensus.

Muqaddimah 3, ruling on shirk al-aghrad.

وَحُكْمُ الخَامِسِ التَّفْصِيلُ.
The ruling on the fifth requires elaboration.

Muqaddimah 3, ruling on shirk al-asbab.

Types 1-4: kufr, by consensus.

Type 5: kufr by consensus if the means is held to act by its own nature; a fasiq mubtadi' with two opinions on takfir if held to act by a power Allah placed in it.

Type 6: sin, not kufr, by consensus.

(1)

Al-'Ibadah: What Actually Counts as Worship

Al-'Ibadah: absolute veneration of an entity, performing actions with the belief that the entity possesses benefit or harm in itself, and that it genuinely deserves worship.

(1)

This precise definition was used directly against a contemporary neo-Salafi/Wahhabi argument that calling upon the Prophet ﷺ or righteous individuals (as in tawassul or requesting shafa'ah) constitutes shirk or worship. The counter-argument: without belief in autonomous benefit or harm residing in that person, the act of calling upon or requesting does not meet this definition of 'ibadah at all. The Companions' continued use of the salam in the tashahhud phrasing after the Prophet's ﷺ death was raised in this context, along with the classical scholarly debate around it.

The Dead Hearing, and Intercession

خَيْرُ أَيَّامِكُمْ يَوْمُ الجُمُعَةِ، فَأَكْثِرُوا عَلَيَّ مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ فِيهِ، فَإِنَّ صَلَاتَكُمْ مَعْرُوضَةٌ عَلَيَّ.
The best of your days is Friday, so send many blessings upon me on it, for your blessings are presented to me.

Hadith cited in this session, on blessings being presented to the Prophet ﷺ.

This hadith, along with reports that the Prophet ﷺ hears and responds to blessings sent upon him, and that the dead hear the footsteps of their visiting companions, was used to support traditional practices around visiting graves and seeking intercession, directly addressing contemporary Salafi objections to these practices.

Dreams as a Source of Knowledge

أَوَّلُ أَمْرٍ... كَانَ بِالرُّؤْيَا الصَّالِحَةِ.
The first affair... began with the true dream.

Cited in connection with ilham and dreams as a channel of knowledge.

This was used to affirm the legitimacy of dreams as a genuine channel of knowledge (ilham), for prophets and for awliya' alike, with a direct rebuke of those who discount this kind of occurrence entirely.

Terminology: Kafir Asli vs. Murtadd

Kafir Asli: a person originally belonging to another religion, who never entered Islam.

(1)

Murtadd: an apostate, one who left Islam after having entered it.

(2)

A student asked whether an apostate must make up missed prayers and zakah from the period of apostasy. The answer: yes, a murtadd is required to make up all missed prayers and zakah covering the period of apostasy upon returning to Islam, while a kafir asli is not liable for anything missed prior to embracing Islam in the first place, since the two are in genuinely different legal positions with respect to what came before.
