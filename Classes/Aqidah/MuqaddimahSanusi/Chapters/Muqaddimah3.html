Al-Muqaddimah al-Thalithah (The Third Preliminary)

Anwa' al-Shirk أَنْوَاعُ الشِّرْكِ

Text
وَأَنْوَاعُ الشِّرْكِ سِتَّةٌ:
Vocabulary
  • وَأَنْوَاعand the types
  • الشِّرْكshirk, associating partners with Allah
  • سِتَّةsix
Translation
And the types of shirk are six:

1. Shirk Istiqlal (Shirk of Independence): affirming two independent gods, like the Magians (al-Majus), who hold there are separate gods for good and evil. Zoroastrian dualism was discussed specifically here.

2. Shirk al-Tab'id (Shirk of Division): making Allah into parts or divisions, as in the Christian Trinity and the LDS/Mormon belief in three separate divine beings. The philosophers' notion of an eternal matter (hayula) was discussed critically here too, alongside the categories of substance (jawhar) and accident ('arad).

3. Shirk al-Taqrib (Shirk of Seeking Proximity): worshipping other than Allah to draw near to Allah, as with the pre-Islamic Arabs worshipping al-Lat, Manat, and al-'Uzza, calling them "daughters of Allah," refuted directly by Qur'anic verses.

4. Shirk al-Taqlid (Shirk of Blind Following): worshipping other than Allah simply by following others, the practice of the later jahiliyyah pagans who kept their ancestors' rituals without evidence or reasoning of their own.

5. Shirk al-Asbab (Shirk of Means): attributing real effect to a means or cause in itself, such as believing fire itself burns rather than Allah creating the burning when fire is present. Splits into two positions, detailed below.

6. Shirk al-Aghrad (Shirk of Purposes): performing a deed for other than Allah, such as beautifying one's prayer to impress another person. Termed hidden shirk (al-shirk al-khafi).

(1)

Shirk al-Asbab in Detail شِرْكُ الأَسْبَابِ

a. Whoever says a means affects by its own nature: consensus (ijma') has been narrated on the disbelief of such a person.

b. Whoever says a means affects by a power Allah placed within it: such a person is an immoral innovator (fasiq mubtadi'), with two opinions on whether this reaches disbelief.

(1)
Commentary

Ibn Taymiyyah was discussed as holding the second position, contrasted with the Ash'ari view that only Allah creates causation directly, never through a delegated natural power residing in the thing itself.

The Rulings on Each Type

Text
وَحُكْمُ الأَرْبَعَةِ الأُولَى: الكُفْرُ بِالإِجْمَاعِ. وَحُكْمُ السَّادِسِ: المَعْصِيَةُ مِنْ غَيْرِ كُفْرٍ بِالإِجْمَاعِ. وَحُكْمُ الخَامِسِ التَّفْصِيلُ.
Vocabulary
  • حُكْمruling
  • الأَرْبَعَةِ الأُولَىthe first four
  • الكُفْرdisbelief
  • بِالإِجْمَاعby consensus
  • السَّادِسthe sixth
  • المَعْصِيَةsin
  • الخَامِسthe fifth
  • التَّفْصِيلdetailed elaboration
Translation
The ruling on the first four: disbelief, by consensus. The ruling on the sixth: sin, without disbelief, by consensus. The ruling on the fifth requires elaboration.

Types 1-4: kufr, by consensus.

Type 5: kufr by consensus if held to act by its own nature; fasiq mubtadi' with two opinions on takfir if held to act by a power Allah placed in it.

Type 6: sin, not kufr, by consensus.

(1)

Al-'Ibadah

Al-'Ibadah: absolute veneration of an entity, performing actions with the belief that the entity possesses benefit or harm in itself, and that it genuinely deserves worship.

(1)
Commentary

This precise definition was used directly against a contemporary neo-Salafi/Wahhabi argument that calling upon the Prophet ﷺ or righteous individuals, as in tawassul or requesting shafa'ah, constitutes shirk or worship. The counter-argument: without belief in autonomous benefit or harm residing in that person, calling upon or requesting does not meet this definition of 'ibadah at all. The Companions' continued use of the salam in the tashahhud phrasing after the Prophet's ﷺ death was raised in this context, along with the classical scholarly debate around it.

The Dead Hearing, and Intercession

Commentary

خَيْرُ أَيَّامِكُمْ يَوْمُ الجُمُعَةِ، فَأَكْثِرُوا عَلَيَّ مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ فِيهِ، فَإِنَّ صَلَاتَكُمْ مَعْرُوضَةٌ عَلَيَّ, "the best of your days is Friday, so send many blessings upon me on it, for your blessings are presented to me," along with reports that the Prophet ﷺ hears and responds to blessings sent upon him, and that the dead hear the footsteps of their visiting companions, was used to support traditional practices around visiting graves and seeking intercession, directly addressing contemporary Salafi objections to these practices.

Dreams as a Source of Knowledge

Commentary

أَوَّلُ أَمْرٍ... كَانَ بِالرُّؤْيَا الصَّالِحَةِ, "the first affair... began with the true dream," was used to affirm the legitimacy of dreams as a genuine channel of knowledge (ilham), for prophets and for awliya' alike, with a direct rebuke of those who discount this kind of occurrence entirely.

Kafir Asli vs. Murtadd

Kafir Asli: a person originally belonging to another religion, who never entered Islam.

Murtadd: an apostate, one who left Islam after having entered it.

(1)
Commentary

A student asked whether an apostate must make up missed prayers and zakah from the period of apostasy. The answer: yes, a murtadd is required to make up all missed prayers and zakah covering the period of apostasy upon returning to Islam, while a kafir asli is not liable for anything missed prior to embracing Islam in the first place, since the two are in genuinely different legal positions with respect to what came before.
