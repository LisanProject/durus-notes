Al-Sam'iyyat

What Is Sam'iyyat?

Al-Sam'iyyat are matters that cannot be established by pure reason alone, unlike al-Ilahiyyat (matters of Allah's essence and attributes) and al-Nubuwwat (matters of prophethood), which rely on rational and logical proofs. Sam'iyyat are known only through السَّمْع ("what is heard"), that is, what has come in the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah. Examples include the Sirat, Paradise, Hellfire, and the world of jinn and angels.

The role of the intellect here is limited to establishing mere possibility ('imkan), that these matters are rationally possible and not impossible (unlike, for example, the impossibility of a square circle). Establishing their actual occurrence depends entirely on revealed law (al-Shar').

Degrees of belief required, by source. Denying what is established in the Qur'an or mutawatir Sunnah is disbelief (kufr). Denying what is established in an authentic ahad hadith makes one an innovator (mubtadi'). Denying what is found only in a weak (da'if) hadith is permissible.

The Stages of the Last Day and the Reckoning

The stages of the Last Day begin with al-Ba'th (Resurrection). The scholars discussed whether resurrection means the gathering of the body's scattered parts, or creation anew from nothing, noting the existence of certain fixed parts of the human body that never change. Resurrection is followed by al-Hashr (the Gathering) upon a new earth different from the current one, then the taking of the records of deeds, leading up to al-Hisab (the Reckoning).

The three types of Reckoning. First, Allah's reckoning of the servant alone, the gentlest form, in which Allah conceals the servant's sins and forgives him. Second, the angels' reckoning while the servant is veiled from Allah, the harshest form of punishment. Third, the angels' reckoning followed by Allah's own reckoning, an intermediate stage.

(2)

Categories exempted from reckoning entirely are the prophets, the angels, and the seventy thousand of the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ who enter Paradise without reckoning or punishment, each accompanied by another seventy thousand.

Means of Forgiveness and the Fate of Servants

Eleven means by which sins are forgiven. Repentance, seeking forgiveness, righteous deeds, worldly afflictions, the agonies of death, the punishment of the grave, the terrors of the Day of Judgment, the intercession of the righteous, the intercession of the Prophet ﷺ, and the sheer mercy and pardon of Allah.

(3)

The believer's ultimate fate is Paradise, even if he is punished first. The disbeliever against whom the proof (al-hujjah) was established has Hellfire as his fate. It was stressed that one should not delve into the fates of specific named individuals, since that knowledge belongs to Allah alone.

Faith (iman) is a condition for entering Paradise, but we do not know what is in people's hearts, nor what occurs at the moment of death. Allah is the most just of the just and the most merciful of the merciful.

The States of the Barzakh and the Questioning of the Grave

The grave is either a garden from the gardens of Paradise, or a pit from the pits of Hellfire. Bliss and punishment occur to the soul and body together. Even if the body were burned or scattered in the sea, Allah is able to convey sensation to it regardless.

Dammat al-Qabr (the squeezing of the grave) is affirmed as true, gentle for the believer and severe for the disbeliever, followed by the two angels' questions: Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is your Prophet?

Categories exempted from the questioning of the grave. The angels, the prophets, the Siddiqin (e.g. Maryam, Abu Bakr, 'Ali), the martyrs, whoever regularly recites Surah al-Mulk every night, whoever recites Surah al-Ikhlas during their fatal illness, the plague victim, whoever dies during a time of plague, the insane, the profoundly disabled (such as those with Down syndrome), and whoever dies on Friday or during its night.

(4)

The Sirat and the Mizan

Al-Sirat is a bridge stretched over the back of Jahannam, between al-Mawqif (the standing place) and Paradise. Its true nature is known only to Allah. Everyone passes over it, including the prophets, while the disbelievers fall from its very start. The Sirat widens and narrows, and the speed of crossing differs according to people's deeds. Some cross in the blink of an eye, some like lightning, some like the finest horses, while others fall into the Fire and are later brought out.

Al-Mizan is a real scale, with two pans and a pointer, placed before the Sirat. The Shaykh favored the view that it is a single scale for all nations, in which deeds are weighed after being given luminous or dark forms, or in which the books of deeds themselves are weighed.

Term ExplainedWhat are al-Sunuj (الصُنُج), and why bring them up when discussing the Mizan?

Al-Sunuj (singular Sanjah) are the weights and metal standards historically used to weigh objects and determine their value. They were raised as a point of comparison for how the Mizan itself functions as a real, physical scale on the Day of Judgment.
